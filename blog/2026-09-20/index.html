The Next Deadlines Are Close—and the Best Comments Ask for the Missing Record

The seven days ending September 20 added a busy set of analyses and left several high-relevance comment periods closing this week. The practical lesson is simple: a useful comment does not need to be long, but it should identify the decision at issue, name the evidence that is missing or hard to test, and ask the agency for a concrete safeguard or alternative.

What Changed This Week

The repository added 13 summary files between September 15 and 19. New analyses covered air-quality plans in several Mid-Atlantic jurisdictions, Sacramento ozone attainment, federal employee health benefits, coal-ash permitting in Indiana, an FDIC disclosure extension, an FTC rulemaking petition, EPA's proposed repeal of power-plant greenhouse-gas findings and standards, pesticide-tolerance petitions, and the OPM employee-accountability proposal and reopening notice.

The immediate task for readers is to check deadlines. The local export lists several prominent comment periods ending September 22, followed by a Forest Service environmental-review notice on September 24, an IRS proposal on September 26, and the OPM employee-accountability reopening on September 30.

Dockets Worth Attention Now

1) SBA-2026-0199 — Small Business Size Standards

Why it matters: Size standards help determine which firms qualify for federal small-business programs and set-asides. A change can broaden eligibility while also changing who competes for limited opportunities.

What stands out in the visible record: The local analysis flags incomplete inspectability of the incorporated methodology and aggregate impact claims. It specifically warns that a broader eligibility rule could obscure concentrated displacement effects for smaller firms competing for set-asides. The current export estimates 1,627 public comments from a 336-comment analysis sample, with a negative net sentiment score, and lists September 22 as the deadline.

High-value comment angle: Ask SBA to publish the methodology it incorporated, show current-versus-proposed eligibility counts by industry and size band, and identify how the change affects firms that currently rely on set-aside access. Request a sensitivity analysis for the smallest firms and for sectors where a modest threshold change could move many competitors into or out of the program.

2) EPA-HQ-OPP-2026-0332 — Pesticide petitions for residues on various commodities

Why it matters: This notice opens comments on petitions involving pesticide tolerances, exemptions, revocations, commodities, and analytical methods. The later decision will depend on petition-specific residue, exposure, and enforcement evidence.

What stands out in the visible record: The new local analysis says EPA has not yet fully evaluated whether the submitted data support the petitions. It classifies the record as thin for independently assessing the scientific merits and notes that the deadline is September 22.

High-value comment angle: Do not rely on a general statement about pesticides. Identify the relevant petition or commodity and ask EPA to make the underlying studies, exposure calculations, residue data, detection limits, and method-validation results accessible. Where a petition leaves room for different commodity definitions or tolerance levels, ask EPA to explain the intermediate options and the basis for choosing among them.

3) FS-2026-0100 — Travel management on the National Forest System

Why it matters: This environmental-impact notice can shape how the Forest Service defines and evaluates travel-management choices on national forest lands. It is an opportunity to put concrete local uses, access needs, and environmental effects into the scope of the eventual review.

What stands out in the visible record: The current export classifies the item as an advance notice rather than a final rule and estimates more than 122,000 public comments, using a 417-comment analysis sample with a negative net sentiment score. The comment period is listed as closing September 24. That volume makes specificity especially important: a repeated position is less informative than a documented place, use, or impact.

High-value comment angle: Name the forest, route, season, or user group affected. Explain the current condition and the consequence of changing access, then ask the Forest Service to compare geographic, seasonal, maintenance, safety, and environmental alternatives with measurable criteria. Include maps, dates, photographs, or firsthand observations when available.

4) IRS-2026-0925 — Employer contributions to Trump Accounts and dependent-care nondiscrimination rules

Why it matters: The proposal reaches employer administration, employee eligibility, tax treatment, reporting, and nondiscrimination mechanics. A rule can look workable in principle while failing at the payroll, enrollment, validation, or correction points that employers and workers actually encounter.

What stands out in the visible record: The local scoring flags a high obligation-to-evidence gap, broad exclusions, and missing operational support. Its most important consequence is practical: if validation, reporting, or correction systems are not ready, eligible contributions could receive unfavorable treatment or employers could avoid offering the programs. The current export lists September 26 as the deadline.

High-value comment angle: Ask IRS and Treasury to publish end-to-end implementation examples for employers of different sizes, including enrollment, payroll coding, eligibility checks, error correction, and employee notices. Request distributional examples showing who benefits or loses under the nondiscrimination rules, and a transition period that protects employees when an employer or vendor makes a good-faith reporting error.

5) OPM-2025-0012 — Promoting Employee Accountability

Why it matters: The proposal would change procedures for performance-based reductions in grade, removals, and other adverse actions. Those changes affect both the government's ability to address poor performance and the predictability of review for federal employees.

What stands out in the visible record: This week's analysis found that the remedy is broader than the current empirical explanation. It asks for current data on removals, appeals, penalties, and settlements; a comparison with narrower alternatives; and guardrails for a broader totality-of-the-circumstances standard. The current record combines the underlying proposal with reopened comments directed to OPM-2025-0012-0678, and the export lists September 30 as the deadline. It estimates 593 public comments from a 244-comment analysis sample, with a strongly negative net sentiment score; that is a sampled signal, not a measure of public opinion.

High-value comment angle: Ask OPM to publish the current baseline that shows the size and source of the problem, then compare the full package with a narrower clarification, pilot, or phased approach. If the totality standard remains, request examples, factors, calibration rules, and appeal-safe guardrails that make outcomes more predictable and reviewable.

Comment Activity To Watch

The local export shows high activity around several deadlines, but it does not support a broad claim about public opinion. The largest estimated volume is attached to FS-2026-0100 at 122,254 public comments. Other near-term items include SBA-2026-0199 at 1,627, EBSA-2026-0331 at 740, and SBA-2026-0265 at 561. The OPM record shows 218 unique comment clusters in the sampled data, including 15 new clusters on September 17.

These figures are estimates based on the repository's representative samples and deduplication process. The displayed net sentiment values are lexical signals from those samples, not a validated poll and not a substitute for reading the comments. A docket with few captured comments may still deserve attention; a docket with many comments still benefits most from specific facts and usable alternatives.

Newly Published Analysis

The week's new files add both narrow procedural reviews and higher-impact analytical work. The EPA power-plant analysis for EPA-HQ-OAR-2025-0124 asks for updated baseline inputs, separate modeling of the full and narrower alternatives, and plausible-control scenarios rather than relying on a complete-elimination proxy alone. The pesticide analysis for EPA-HQ-OPP-2026-0332 focuses on making petition-specific evidence and analytical-method performance testable. The OPM analysis provides a concrete evidence-and-alternatives checklist for OPM-2025-0012 and its reopening notice.

The new state-plan reviews also show why seemingly narrow proposals can still benefit from comments: source-category descriptions, no-source findings, and future-change safeguards need to be reproducible before a later discovery creates confusion about regulatory coverage. The FDIC, FTC, Forest Service, and federal-benefits analyses add other current entry points for readers whose work or communities are directly affected.

Method Note

The current local site export was generated September 21 from data checked through September 20. It contains 85 docket records, but it is marked degraded because the live open-docket query was incomplete and a prior open snapshot was merged. Treat the ranking and comment counts as a practical triage aid, not a complete or definitive list. Scores describe visible relevance and analytical concern in the local record; they are not legal conclusions, forecasts, or findings that a proposal is unlawful.

If You Do One Thing This Week

Choose the deadline that affects your work, organization, or community and submit one specific comment before it closes. Identify the exact provision or decision, provide a fact the agency can evaluate, and request one concrete change—such as a missing analysis, a transition safeguard, a narrower alternative, or a method for correcting implementation errors.
