A Week of Fast-Moving Rules and Missing Evidence

The seven days ending September 13 brought new proposals on Arctic oil development, water coverage, postal services, robocall enforcement, and tax administration. The practical thread is clear: several agencies are asking the public to accept a faster or simpler system, while the most useful comments ask what evidence, safeguards, and transition plans make that change work.

What Changed This Week

The repository added analyses for 11 documents between September 7 and 12. New notices included BLM's proposed National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska approval pathway; FCC proposals on direct-to-device spectrum and robocall mitigation; EPA's supplemental “Waters of the United States” definitions and Wisconsin coal-ash permit program; USPS label discontinuation; an SEC political-contribution proposal; and two IRS tax notices. A North Dakota regional-haze reconsideration was also reviewed, alongside FDA's continuing terminology proposal.

One deadline is especially close: the current local export lists September 15 for the CMS Medicare payment and reporting proposal. If that rule affects your work, start there before drafting a broader comment.

Dockets Worth Attention Now

1) CMS-2026-2377 — Medicare payment, reporting, and interoperability changes

Why it matters: CMS's CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule proposal contains payment-formula changes as well as new reporting and interoperability mechanics. Even when the aggregate effect looks manageable, a formula or crosswalk choice can shift reimbursement among specialties, services, and places of service.

What stands out in the record: The local analysis finds that the proposal identifies several operational changes without consistently showing side-by-side distributional effects, low-volume sensitivity, or implementation readiness. The current local export lists comments as due September 15.

High-value comment angle: Ask CMS to publish current-versus-proposed payment outcomes for the most affected specialties and settings, explain low-volume code assignments with sensitivity checks, and provide readiness milestones, validation, fallback, and correction procedures for new reporting requirements.

2) FDA-2026-N-2886 — Modification of certain terminology in Title 21

Why it matters: FDA proposes to remove, replace, or delete terminology across 26 provisions covering clinical studies, devices, safety reporting, and tobacco products. Changes that look editorial on the page can still affect forms, databases, historical comparisons, and the meaning of required information.

What stands out in the record: FDA characterizes the amendments as having no measurable cost, industry-practice change, or public-health effect. The local analysis finds that the notice does not demonstrate semantic continuity across the different programs. The current export lists an October 3 comment deadline.

High-value comment angle: Request a provision-by-provision crosswalk showing the old text, new text, affected data field, and expected operational meaning. Ask FDA to explain updates to forms, systems, guidance, and training, and how it will preserve comparability with historical clinical and reporting data.

3) BLM-2026-0133 — National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska production-site development

Why it matters: BLM proposes a standardized pathway with predefined criteria and a 60-day decision window for qualifying production sites and rights-of-way in the NPR-A. This is more than paperwork reform: the accompanying economic analysis models more development under the proposal than under its baseline.

What stands out in the record: The rule relies on a common design envelope, the current Integrated Activity Plan, and standardized conditions, but the draft EIS chapters available for review do not yet include the full affected-environment, consequences, cumulative-effects, and consultation analysis. The current export lists comments as due November 10.

High-value comment angle: Ask BLM to complete and publish the environmental and subsistence record, show the geographic limits of the design envelope, and add clear triggers for project-specific or supplemental review. A phased pilot, sunset, or explicit exclusion for sensitive or materially different projects is a concrete alternative to evaluate.

4) EPA-HQ-OW-2025-0322 — Updated definition of “Waters of the United States”

Why it matters: The proposed definitions of perennial, relatively permanent, and continuous surface connection can determine whether waters receive Clean Water Act coverage and whether permits are required. That reaches landowners, farmers, developers, states, Tribes, and communities concerned about water quality.

What stands out in the record: The supplemental proposal uses bright-line temporal rules, including daily water presence, a 30-day limit for a temporary connection interruption, and a five-year treatment of drought. The notice says roughly 220,000 comments were received on the earlier proposal, but the local analysis finds no full sensitivity table showing how the selected thresholds work across regions and waterbody types. The notice identifies October 9; the local export displays October 10 after UTC normalization.

High-value comment angle: Test the proposed thresholds against local hydrology and borderline cases. Ask the agencies to publish regional sensitivity analyses, field and remote-data protocols, uncertainty rules, and a comparison with flow-duration, physical-indicator, regional, and hybrid alternatives.

5) USPS-2026-2179 — Discontinuation of Labels 888, 889, 891, and 892

Why it matters: USPS would discontinue legacy labels for tracking, signature confirmation, and insurance and move customers toward IMpb-compliant labels and other alternatives. The change could be routine for some offices but costly or confusing for metered customers, small mailers, Marketing Mail users, and locations that are still being upgraded.

What stands out in the record: USPS says the labels are incompatible with updated equipment and that discontinuation will improve service and customer experience, but the notice does not provide the underlying failure data or a service-by-service comparison. It proposes a January 17, 2027 implementation date while allowing existing stock through January 31, 2028; the current export lists October 10 as the comment deadline.

High-value comment angle: Ask USPS to identify the affected equipment, facilities, services, and failure rates for each label; publish baseline and projected performance metrics; and provide a label-by-label transition table. A targeted or phased discontinuation should be compared with universal removal.

Comment Activity To Watch

The local export shows activity concentrated in a few newly posted records, not a reliable broad trend. For the WOTUS docket, it estimates 10 public comments from a representative sample, with 50% classified as negative and a net sentiment score of -0.40. BLM shows four estimated public comments, with a net score of -0.25. FDA's terminology docket shows five estimated public comments and a smaller negative net score of -0.13.

These are small sampled counts, not a measure of public opinion. Several newly analyzed dockets show zero in the current export, which should be read as “no activity captured in this snapshot,” not proof that nobody has commented. The more useful signal is where a deadline is close or where a commenter can supply missing evidence.

Newly Published Analysis

This week's analyses also cover FCC's direct-to-device spectrum proposal, the FCC Robocall Mitigation Database proposal, North Dakota regional haze, IRS foreign-source deduction allocation and Qualified Opportunity Zone reporting, SEC political contributions, and EPA's Wisconsin coal-ash program. Across these reviews, the recurring public-facing opportunities are specific: request the missing calculations, make implementation rules reproducible, protect against false positives or abrupt transition costs, and ask agencies to compare narrower alternatives.
