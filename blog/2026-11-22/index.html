Bank Checks, Streaming Devices, and a Housing Deadline

Regulations Observer weekly update: November 16–22, 2026.

This week brought new opportunities to comment on business bank-account checks, streaming-device security, and hunting in two national monuments. There is also a more immediate task for readers following housing: Treasury's institutional-investor proposal has a November 23 comment deadline.

Start with the subject you know from experience. Use the site's summary to find the relevant provision, then read the notice and submission instructions. A useful comment explains what would happen in practice and asks for a specific improvement. The opportunities below were open at the end of the reporting window in the November 22 site snapshot.

What Changed This Week

Three newly posted items now have detailed reviews and public summaries:

  • November 16: proposed hunting access at Castle Mountains and Craters of the Moon, NPS-2026-0265.
  • November 19: proposed customer due-diligence changes for financial institutions, FINCEN-2026-0298.
  • November 20: an invitation to comment on a streaming-device rulemaking petition, FTC-2026-1849. This is a request from a petitioner; the FTC has not proposed its own device ban.

A new review also examines Utah's proposed takeover of carbon-storage permitting, EPA-HQ-OW-2026-0496, which was posted in October. A new analysis does not necessarily mean a new proposal.

For readers preparing a housing comment, TREAS-DO-2026-0529 closes November 23 according to the reviewed notice. The stored timestamp falls on November 24 in UTC and represents the same Eastern-time cutoff, not an extra filing day.

Dockets Worth Attention Now

Institutional home purchases — TREAS-DO-2026-0529

Comment deadline: November 23. Treasury proposes definitions and exceptions implementing the statutory prohibition on large institutional investors purchasing single-family homes. Those details matter to prospective buyers, renters, developers, and businesses arranging housing investments.

The review finds a coherent framework but questions how Treasury chose numerical thresholds and how its property-valuation method would work across jurisdictions. The notice acknowledges that reliable data on the number of affected institutional investors are unavailable. Later rulemaking phases would address additional implementation matters.

A useful comment: Provide a nonconfidential example showing how a local tax assessment would affect eligibility for a renovation exception. Explain any missing land or improvement values, then request a fallback valuation method and a worked example. Readers with homeownership-program experience can test whether the proposed timing requirements match actual transactions.

Business-account identity checks — FINCEN-2026-0298

Comment deadline: January 19, 2027. FinCEN proposes relief from repeatedly identifying a business's beneficial owners whenever it opens another account. Checks would remain at the first account, when prior information becomes questionable, and as ongoing risk-based diligence requires.

The same proposal would exclude Individual Taxpayer Identification Numbers as qualifying identification numbers and allow requests for immigration or employment-status information when justified by risk indicators or supervisory concerns. This could affect business owners, financial institutions, and compliance vendors. It does not create a universal immigration-document requirement.

The review's central question is whether the operative language keeps those requests within the limits described in the explanation. It also asks for evidence supporting the prediction that access to financial services would not be significantly affected.

A useful comment: Describe an anonymized verification case, the documents available, staff time, and any account-opening delay. Compare excluding an ITIN with accepting it alongside independent corroboration. Ask FinCEN to specify when additional status information is warranted and how a customer could correct an error.

Monument hunting and usable safety boundaries — NPS-2026-0265

Comment deadline: December 16. NPS proposes opening Castle Mountains and the original Craters of the Moon monument to hunting under state and federal law. Hunting already occurs on adjoining lands and in the Craters preserve. This proposal would change access in the specified monument areas; it does not currently authorize hunting there.

Hunters, other visitors, nearby mine workers, Tribes, and park staff have practical interests in clear boundaries. The review identifies specific drafting problems: the Castle Mountains authorization names a preserve, the intended mine-buffer prohibition is an incomplete sentence, and retrieval references do not match the restrictions they should address. Proposed protections also include watering-place restrictions at Castle Mountains and short-range weapons and a northern closure at Craters.

A useful comment: Ask NPS to correct the unit name, write a complete mine-buffer prohibition, align retrieval exceptions, and identify how the buffer is measured. Add local knowledge about trails, visitor overlap, or seasonal use that could help staff make the boundaries understandable on the ground.

Streaming-device security petition — FTC-2026-1849

Comment deadline: December 21. The FTC is seeking views before deciding whether to pursue an individual's petition. The petitioner requests informed consent for preinstalled software that routes others' internet traffic through a household connection, restrictions on deceptive device identity or certificate spoofing, and boot-verification and digital-rights-management measures.

Consumers and security researchers can help establish the problem; manufacturers, repair communities, and independent developers can help test the proposed remedies. The reviewed petition provides no representative prevalence data or reproducible testing to support its broad infection claims. Those are the petitioner's allegations, not FTC findings.

A useful comment: Supply one documented device or firmware example, explain testing conditions and limitations, and identify which requested measure it informs. Ask whether narrowly targeting undisclosed proxy operation would address the demonstrated harm, and what additional security benefit broader boot or DRM restrictions would provide.

Utah carbon-storage oversight — EPA-HQ-OW-2026-0496

Comment deadline: December 7. EPA proposes letting Utah issue and enforce Class VI underground carbon-dioxide storage permits outside Indian lands. EPA would retain oversight and enforcement authority. Approval would transfer program administration; individual storage projects would still need permits and technical review.

Groundwater users, nearby residents, landowners, Tribes, and storage operators have reasons to examine how that oversight would work. The review finds a supported technical framework, while identifying inconsistent document dates, questions about specialist staffing, and a need to explain long-term responsibility after a facility closes.

A useful comment: Ask EPA to identify the controlling versions of the program documents and map each reporting duty to a responsible actor. Readers with permitting or groundwater experience can describe what specialist capacity is needed at startup and ask how approved closure, financial assurance, and eventual state responsibility fit together.

What the Comment Activity Shows

November 22 checks recorded cumulative totals of 282 comments for the housing proposal, 25 for monument hunting, nine for bank due diligence, two for Utah permitting, and one for the streaming-device petition. These are snapshot totals, not submissions counted solely during November 16–22.

The cached housing sample contains comment groups dated each weekday from November 16 through 20, but includes only 186 records and is marked incomplete. That supports an observation of participation during the week, not a reliable growth rate or representative sentiment estimate. Low totals on a new docket likewise say little about its eventual importance. A concrete example can still add information the agency needs.

New Analyses to Start With

This week's additions offer four distinct entry points. The monument review identifies wording corrections readers can request directly. The FinCEN review separates repeat-check savings from possible identification barriers. The streaming-device review distinguishes the petitioner's claims from the FTC's procedural invitation. The Utah review examines supporting program materials and the commitments that would govern implementation.

This update uses local records and recent analyses, with the November 22 site snapshot keeping activity within the reporting window. Counts and cached samples have limits; rankings help readers find questions to investigate. They do not establish illegality or predict final agency decisions. Check the notice for submission instructions and the applicable deadline.

If You Do One Thing This Week

If housing purchases affect your work or community, check the November 23 deadline first. Pick one definition, exception, or valuation problem in TREAS-DO-2026-0529, show how it works in a real example, and request one specific clarification or change. Otherwise, choose the open docket closest to your experience and use that same focused format.
