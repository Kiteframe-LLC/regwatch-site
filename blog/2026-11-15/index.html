Patient Care, Pacific Fishing, and Two Deadlines Close to Home

Regulations Observer weekly update: November 9–15, 2026.

Reducing paperwork can free up time and money. It can also remove information people use to spot problems. Two proposals newly posted this week put that tradeoff in focus: changes to Medicare and Medicaid requirements, and reopening commercial fishing in Pacific monument expansion waters. Meanwhile, deadlines are approaching for offshore discharge exclusions and small-business eligibility standards.

Start with the issue you know best. Read its summary, then check the notice and submission instructions before commenting. These opportunities remained open at the end of the reporting window in the available local records. This update uses a November 16 snapshot; later collection times are identified below.

What Changed This Week

The Pacific fishing proposal, NOAA-NMFS-2026-2410, was posted November 9. The Medicare and Medicaid proposal, CMS-2026-3631, followed November 10. Both now have detailed analyses and public summaries. Neither proposal is a final rule.

The coming week also brings two practical deadlines. The water-permit proposal, EPA-HQ-OW-2026-6965, has a recorded cutoff corresponding to November 18 in Eastern time. Small-business size standards, SBA-2026-0199, follow on November 20 in Eastern time. Their stored UTC timestamps fall on the following dates, so check the notices for the applicable submission cutoff rather than assuming an extra day.

Dockets Worth Attention Now

Patient-care requirements — CMS-2026-3631

CMS proposes to remove selected training, obstetric quality-improvement, vaccine-education, and reporting requirements, while also updating facility safety codes and other participation standards. Patients, families, clinicians, and care facilities all have a stake. The notice gives a January 11, 2027 comment deadline.

The review identifies real burden-reduction arguments and substantial safeguards that would remain, including general quality and infection-control duties. Its central concern is whether replacement information would provide comparable oversight before existing reporting ends. The proposal also includes exploratory questions that do not themselves change policy.

A useful comment: Choose one reporting or training requirement you know. Explain the staff time it takes and what safety information it produces. Compare repeal with a narrower alternative, such as automated reporting, reduced frequency, or training focused on staff with obstetric responsibilities. Ask CMS to specify how it would test replacement coverage before ending the current requirement.

Pacific monument fishing — NOAA-NMFS-2026-2410

NMFS proposes reopening commercial fishing in roughly 50–200 nautical mile expansion waters around Wake and Jarvis Islands and Johnston Atoll. Inner monument closures would remain, along with existing fishery permits, catch limits, gear rules, and protected-species safeguards. Comments are due December 9.

The review includes the cached environmental and economic analysis. It finds substantial supporting analysis, but questions the interim monitoring plan: a targeted 2028 electronic-monitoring transition helps explain the agency's approach, yet does not by itself demonstrate adequate coverage before then. Shifting existing fishing effort can also change local wildlife exposure even without adding vessels.

A useful comment: Supply evidence about fishing locations, seasonal wildlife encounters, or cultural use. Ask what observer coverage, video review, and response triggers would apply before 2028, including a fallback if electronic monitoring is delayed. Operators can help by distinguishing equipment installation from the work needed to review recordings.

Offshore discharge exclusions — EPA-HQ-OW-2026-6965

EPA proposes changing which vessel discharges require authorization under the National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System. Under the proposed boundary, pollutant additions from vessels or floating craft not secured to the seabed in the contiguous zone or ocean would be excluded even during industrial or processing operations. The recorded deadline corresponds to November 18 in Eastern time.

This matters to seafood processors, other offshore operators, coastal communities, and people who use discharge data. The review includes EPA's draft economic analysis and permit examples. EPA anticipates compliance savings, while acknowledging that permit-required monitoring and reporting would end for affected operations.

A useful comment: Describe a specific operation and how it is moored or connected to the seabed. Ask EPA to explain its classification and what happens to existing permits. If you use discharge records, identify a concrete decision those records support and propose targeted monitoring that could preserve their value.

Small-business eligibility — SBA-2026-0199

SBA proposes 338 size standards using revised industry groupings and calculation methods, including more employee-based thresholds. The change affects access to federal small-business programs and contracting opportunities. The extended comment period's recorded cutoff corresponds to November 20 in Eastern time.

SBA estimates an overall increase in eligible firms, but that total does not explain every industry's experience. The review could not independently reconstruct the thresholds because the separate methodology paper and calculation files were not available in its prepared materials. That is a limit of this review, rather than proof those materials are absent from the full docket.

A useful comment: Identify your industry code and compare the current and proposed standards using nonconfidential business figures. Explain how the new grouping or measurement would affect eligibility and competition. Ask SBA for reproducible calculations and an industry-specific explanation where the result is difficult to follow.

What the Comment Activity Shows

Counts checked November 16 show 1,539 comments on Pacific fishing and 1,009 on the patient-care proposal. These are cumulative snapshot totals, not counts confined to November 9–15.

The cached samples contain newly identified groups of similar comments dated November 10 and 13 for fishing, and November 13 for patient care. The samples are incomplete: 60 cached records for fishing and 30 for patient care. They support an observation that comments are entering the analyzed record, but not a representative sentiment estimate or a claim about the week's overall pace.

New Analyses and How to Read Them

The two new-proposal reviews offer different starting points. The patient-care analysis compares retained safeguards with the information and training CMS would remove. The fishing analysis examines the supporting environmental assessment and focuses on monitoring during the transition. Both turn broad disagreements into questions an agency can answer with evidence.

This update draws only on local records and recent analyses. The November 16 docket export flags incomplete collection supplemented by an earlier open-docket snapshot, so it is not a complete inventory of federal activity. Concern scores help locate questions; they do not establish illegality or predict the final outcome. This post is a guide to participation, not legal advice.

If You Do One Thing This Week

Check the November 18 water-permit deadline first if offshore discharges affect your work or community. Otherwise, choose one provision in the docket closest to your experience. Describe what happens in practice, provide a measurable example, and ask for one specific change or explanation. A focused comment gives the agency something concrete to evaluate.
