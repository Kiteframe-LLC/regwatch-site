Failure scenario: If off-hours trading is thin but still informationally useful, a yes-or-no framing may miss intermediate safeguards short of rejecting continuous trading. Procedural implication: Commenters can push for conditional criteria, weekend safeguards, and product-specific thresholds rather than binary approval or disapproval.

Claim: Energy perpetuals may not fit existing Core Principle 3 and position-limit logic. Underlying assumption: A no-expiration product linked to a physically delivered benchmark creates distinct incentive and manipulation problems. Procedural implication: The best comments will test whether the agency should specify a design screen instead of asking only open-ended questions.

Claim: Settlement, margin, and customer-protection questions are central because traditional payment rails do not operate 24/7. Failure scenario: If participants can pre-fund, pre-margin, or use robust intraday liquidity arrangements, the notice may underdevelop feasible mitigations. Procedural implication: Commenters can offer concrete operational models and identify which participants would still be disadvantaged.
