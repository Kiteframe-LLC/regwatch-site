Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

  • Claim: C1: general oversight can replace targeted OB requirements

    Underlying Assumption: Broad QAPI, accreditation, professional education and voluntary quality initiatives reach the same staff, facilities and disparities.

    Negation Test: If coverage differs, removing targeted duties does not merely remove duplicate paperwork.

    Counterfactual: An unaccredited rural facility or infrequently exposed emergency clinician may not receive the cited specialty training.

    Adversarial Interpretation: A facility may treat general QAPI as permission to stop OB-specific disparity tracking.

    Procedural Implication: Request a duty-by-duty coverage crosswalk and outcomes monitoring; do not assume loss of all OB standards.

  • Claim: C2: reporting capacity remains ready during a PHE

    Underlying Assumption: Facilities maintain trained personnel, accounts and interoperable data despite ending routine submission.

    Negation Test: If capacity decays, reporting starts late exactly when coordinated response is needed.

    Counterfactual: High turnover or an outbreak preceding a formal declaration defeats the assumed response-ready foundation.

    Adversarial Interpretation: Facilities can stop routine reporting entirely while meeting local infection-control requirements.

    Procedural Implication: Request activation tests, coverage benchmarks and a low-frequency alternative.

  • Claim: C3: existing data can approximate Medicaid compensation shares

    Underlying Assumption: Payroll, resident mix, cost reports, wage estimates and claims can be linked at facility level and comparable periods.

    Negation Test: If the approximation is biased, rescission removes transparency without an adequate substitute.

    Counterfactual: Contract staffing, mixed payers, Medicaid-only facilities and managed-care encounters complicate allocation.

    Adversarial Interpretation: No new reporting is required even if replacement feasibility work never produces a public metric.

    Procedural Implication: Pilot and publish validation before relying on replacement estimates.

  • Claim: C4: equal-or-superior safety justifies future code waivers

    Underlying Assumption: CMS can show equivalence for specified provisions and interactions with the adopted edition.

    Negation Test: If provisions depend on a broader code package, selective substitution can weaken safety.

    Counterfactual: A waiver safe in one occupancy or system configuration may fail in another.

    Adversarial Interpretation: Optional guidance could become a broad alternative standard without ordinary public scrutiny.

    Procedural Implication: Define waiver limits, technical findings, election records, challenge paths and transition rules.

  • Claim: C5: beds proxy births

    Underlying Assumption: The share of all beds measures the share of delivery hospitalizations at OB facilities.

    Negation Test: If the denominator already represents deliveries in relevant facilities, multiplying by 60 percent understates affected encounters.

    Counterfactual: A maternity-heavy hospital and a hospital dominated by non-OB beds have different birth-to-bed ratios.

    Adversarial Interpretation: An understated denominator could make added consent duties appear cheaper than they are.

    Procedural Implication: Use delivery counts by eligible facility; identify existing consent practices and actual collection activity.

  • Claim: C6: emergency transfers need no Ombudsman copy

    Underlying Assumption: Appropriate initial transfer also implies no later discharge or readmission issue.

    Negation Test: A later refusal to readmit can escape early independent awareness.

    Counterfactual: An initially justified hospital visit becomes a contested permanent discharge.

    Adversarial Interpretation: An emergency label can remove routine external visibility.

    Procedural Implication: Test an exception that restores notice for readmission disputes and tracks transfers in a low-burden log.

V.C.7 applies 59 percent hospital and 1 percent CAH all-bed shares to 3,583,224 delivery hospitalizations from 2019, estimating 2,114,102 and 35,832 patients. V.C.12 acknowledges costs for electrical maintenance, heliports, extinguishers and high-rise sprinkler/relocation requirements. The preamble estimates a 12-year compliance horizon for affected existing ASC/ESRD high-rise buildings but lacks a count of exposed facilities.
