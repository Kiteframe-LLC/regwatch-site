Material Flaws

Flaw Severity Explanation Evidence Comment leverage
Evidence-to-Intrusion Mismatch 3 Broad payment and enrollment consequences may rely on aggregate or incomplete evidence. II.C: The rule proposes a temporary adjustment based on differences between assumed and actual behavior change, but the available record does not fully separate behavior from acuity and access effects. Require subgroup sensitivity and provider-specific evidence before intrusive action.
Remedy-Rationale Mismatch 3 A uniform adjustment may not match heterogeneous causes of observed spending or utilization. II.B-II.C: The rule reports multi-year utilization and cost comparisons, but the available record does not show why a uniform temporary adjustment is the least harmful remedy. Ask CMS to compare alternatives and access safeguards.
Threat Ambiguity 2 The breadth of enrollment-risk categories may leave regulated parties uncertain about triggers. V.B: The rule proposes adding grounds for denying or revoking enrollment and expanding retroactive effective dates; the available excerpt does not specify all trigger boundaries. Request precise triggers and examples.
Impact Denial 2 The stated protection and access benefits are not matched by full distributional analysis. I.A and Regulatory Impact Analysis: The rule states that changes would protect Trust Funds and beneficiaries, but the available record does not fully quantify rural, small-agency, palliative, or high-acuity access effects. Submit local access and patient-mix evidence.
Safeguard Absence 3 The available record does not fully state safeguards for retroactive revocation, reapplication bars, and payment disruption. V.B: The available record does not explain the complete notice, evidence-access, appeal, and timing safeguards for expanded enrollment sanctions. Ask CMS to establish meaningful pre- and post-deprivation review.
Externality Dumping 2 Potential access and administrative costs may fall on beneficiaries and small agencies. II.C and III.E: The available record does not fully allocate the access, reporting, and implementation costs of the proposed changes across agencies and beneficiaries. Request distributional estimates and mitigation.
Harm Before Review 3 Retroactive dates and enrollment actions can disrupt payment or service before review is complete. V.B: The rule proposes expanding reasons CMS can apply a retroactive effective date for provider and supplier revocations; the available record does not fully explain interim protections. Request stays, notice, and prompt review for disputed actions.
Procedural Gateway Control 2 Reporting and enrollment procedures can operate as gateways to payment and participation. III.E and V.B: The rule proposes revised reporting deadlines and expanded enrollment consequences, but the available record does not fully explain proportionality when compliance failures are minor or technical. Request cure periods and proportionality.
