Public Summary: NHTSA-2026-1519-0001

What This Document Does

NHTSA proposes to modernize lighting tests and driver-control requirements while preserving required external lighting. Integral-beam headlamps would be tested at their marked voltage; vertical high/low beam placement would become more flexible. Vehicles without manual driving controls could omit certain manual controls and routine driver telltales. Optional cyan ADS marker lamps would face uniform performance limits. This proposal does not authorize ADS driving generally or require every ADS vehicle to carry cyan lamps.

Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The modernization retains essential lighting functions, but its preamble and proposed visibility table reverse the intensity values for headlamps on and off. That narrow, repairable mismatch needs one consistent compliance requirement before finalization.

Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

  • Claim: C1: unchanged mounting-height limits ensure no negative safety outcome

    Underlying Assumption: Current photometry and permitted mounting heights adequately bound every newly permitted vertical arrangement.

    Negation Test: If lower mounting reduces seeing distance in some compliant designs, height limits alone cannot establish safety neutrality.

    Counterfactual Test: Compare low mounting, vehicle pitch, road crests and current minimum compliant output.

    Adversarial Interpretation: A design could meet minimum limits without reflecting the market-average performance cited in the rationale.

    Procedural Implication: Potential unacknowledged weakness: request a bounded engineering explanation rather than assert that injuries will increase.

  • Claim: C2: headlamp state selects a usable marker visibility minimum

    Underlying Assumption: The narrative and enforceable table specify the same mapping.

    Negation Test: They do not: narrative says 0.3 cd off/0.05 on, Table V-c says 0.05 off/0.3 on.

    Counterfactual Test: Daylight use of headlamps during rain also tests whether headlamp status is a sufficient ambient-light proxy.

    Adversarial Interpretation: A laboratory or manufacturer could apply the operative table while another follows the preamble.

    Procedural Implication: Potential unacknowledged weakness for the reversal; deliberate solicitation for rain/fog brightness.

  • Claim: C4: ADS monitoring replaces the need for routine driver telltales

    Underlying Assumption: Software receives accurate lamp-state and fault information and responds safely.

    Negation Test: An energized circuit does not necessarily prove useful emitted light; a failed sensor or stale state could undermine the premise.

    Counterfactual Test: Test partial outages, communications faults and stopped unoccupied vehicles.

    Adversarial Interpretation: Do not equate removal of driver controls with permission to omit required external lamps or fault displays.

    Procedural Implication: Deliberate solicitation on equipment approach and telltales; request validation examples without requiring a complete ADS performance standard in this lighting rule.

  • Claim: C6: no lead time is necessary

    Underlying Assumption: Every affected implementation can continue or promptly conform because equipment choices are optional.

    Negation Test: Already-installed cyan lamps could need hardware or software changes when new if-equipped limits begin.

    Counterfactual Test: Consider SAE-based preexisting lamps with different intensity or optical combinations.

    Adversarial Interpretation: Immediate effect could leave unclear treatment of vehicles built before finalization.

    Procedural Implication: Clarification-worthy prospective transition issue, not established retroactivity.

  • Claim: C7: software activation and cancellation make a steering-based substitute unnecessary

    Underlying Assumption: ADS commands reflect completed maneuvers and actual lamp output, not merely stored activation state.

    Negation Test: If the system retains a stale maneuver state, remembering an active signal does not ensure timely cancellation.

    Counterfactual Test: Consider an aborted lane change, a communications fault, or a maneuver that ends without the expected steering trajectory.

    Adversarial Interpretation: Avoid construing relief from steering-wheel cancellation as relief from traffic-law signaling or required operating equipment.

    Procedural Implication: Deliberate solicitation on the equipment approach. Ask for representative functional demonstrations; no observed ADS failure is established.
