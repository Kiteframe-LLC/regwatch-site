## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **C1 — assumption:** the proclamations compel repeal but leave sufficient scope to manage fisheries. **Negation:** if their legal effect or retained discretion differs, the alternatives boundary changes. **Counterfactual:** conditional access with monitoring could allow fishing without retaining a blanket monument prohibition. **Adversarial interpretation:** “no discretion” could be read to foreclose substantive management comments. **Implication:** explain precisely which choices remain open; legal effect is uncertain here, not adjudicated.
- **C2 — assumption:** current near-full-time operations, entry restrictions and catch/day limits make reopening a relocation of effort. **Negation:** extra trips, greater hooks per trip, permit activation or changed fishing efficiency would weaken that premise. **Counterfactual:** stronger tuna aggregation or fee savings could attract more concentrated effort than historical averages predict. **Implication:** test a higher-effort and higher-concentration scenario. The RIR discusses operational capacity, so the premise is supported but not guaranteed by vessel caps alone.
- **C3 — assumption:** low historical interactions predict effects under relocated effort and present ecological conditions. **Negation:** higher local encounter rates could increase protected-species take even without more fleetwide effort. **Counterfactual:** an aggregation or seasonal hotspot could matter locally despite a small share of regional effort. **Implication:** distinguish range-wide jeopardy, local resource care and detection uncertainty; do not equate no recorded interaction with zero risk.
- **C4 — assumption:** the electronic-monitoring transition is timely and sufficient to make a separate condition redundant. **Negation:** delayed equipment, incomplete video review or a gap before 2028 would undermine the rejection. **Adversarial interpretation:** installation could be treated as equivalent to analyzed coverage. **Implication:** specify interim coverage, review rates, funding and contingency triggers.
- **C5 — assumption:** low known use is a reliable indicator of low effects. **Counterfactual:** offshore voyaging without a special-use permit is acknowledged to be outside agency knowledge. **Implication:** invite practitioner evidence on routes, timing and nonphysical cultural values; the EA already recognizes possible changes to the voyaging experience.
- **C6 — assumption:** the displaced effort represents fee-paying days at comparable catch and travel costs. **Counterfactual:** multiple sets per day or added transit could reduce net benefits. **Implication:** publish a reproducible conversion from sets to days and treat the quoted savings as an upper bound, not an expected gain.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

NMFS proposes to reopen commercial fishing in the roughly 50–200 nautical mile expansion waters around Wake and Jarvis Islands and Johnston Atoll. Existing fishery permits, catch limits, gear rules and protected-species safeguards would apply. The inner monument closures would remain. **Observed Fact:** the proposal changes the prohibition, definitions and permit provisions. **Inference:** most activity would be longlining near Johnston and purse seining near Jarvis. The agency expressly requests comment on whether existing rules provide proper care for monument resources.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Observed Fact:** the EA compares no action with reopening and explicitly rejects enhanced monitoring and area-specific controls. It would be inaccurate to say these alternatives were ignored.

**Plausible refinement:** temporary enhanced longline monitoring through the fleetwide transition, with a sunset after a verified performance milestone. Feasibility is suggested by the EA’s discussion of observers, ongoing EM installations and the Council’s consideration of 100% monitoring; staffing, costs and authority remain to be established. Request comparison of phased coverage or risk-based sampling rather than presume immediate full coverage is feasible.

**Other refinement:** publish an early review of area-specific effort, bycatch and encounters, with defined thresholds for considering further action. Existing reporting, observer and vessel-monitoring systems suggest a possible data foundation. This need not preselect a new closure. A categorical prohibition may fall outside the agency’s interpretation of the proclamations; the scope of lawful narrower controls should be explained.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Observed Fact:** pelagic gear operates in the upper water column; the inner closures remain, supporting separation from shallow benthic habitat. EA §3.1.2 also addresses deepwater resources and why a precious-coral fishery is not expected to develop. This is a reasoned exposure argument, not proof that every authorized gear could never develop.

The EA distinguishes regional stock effects from transient local abundance changes and reviews mixed MPA literature (§3.3.5). Its negligible-impact conclusion should specify how local predator–seabird relationships are protected despite this uncertainty. Vessel caps constrain entrants rather than hooks or days directly; the RIR adds near-full-time operation as a capacity premise. Historical shares are not hard ceilings.

**Clarification needed:** the RIR uses historical hooks/days to project shares, translates recent activity into sets, then estimates daily access-fee savings. Show the sets-to-days conversion and sensitivity to transit and catch rates. This is a reproducibility issue; the available text does not establish a numerical error.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

1. **Monitoring transition delay:** fishing resumes before fleetwide EM is operational, leaving limited observations of rare interactions. Existing observer, logbook and take controls mitigate the risk; interim coverage and review plans would make them assessable.
2. **Concentrated effort:** historical fleetwide shares remain low while operations cluster near ecologically important features or during sensitive seasons. A local exposure check can test this without assuming a population decline.
3. **Changed incentives:** access-fee savings, fish distribution or operating efficiency generate more effort than projected. Periodic actual-versus-forecast reporting would detect divergence.
4. **Cultural-use undercount:** offshore voyages requiring no permit are poorly represented in administrative counts. Targeted practitioner input can improve the baseline.
5. **Boundary confusion:** coordinate-defined retained closures and cross-references are not clearly mapped. Published charts and corrected citations could improve compliance.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

Reopening domestic waters may shift effort away from fee-paying foreign EEZs and provide location flexibility, particularly around Jarvis and Johnston. These are acknowledged intended incentives. Benefits could be uneven by vessel location, operational profile and ability to travel; no evidence establishes improper favoritism or deliberate evasion. Retained permits, reporting and protected-species controls remain applicable. Incentives to concentrate effort matter analytically, even when no rule is violated. The proposal does not create a new individualized gatekeeping or viewpoint-based control surface.
