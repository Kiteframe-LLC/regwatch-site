## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

**Observed Facts**
- The notice identifies statutory reporting duties and gives QOF and QOZB burden estimates.
- It acknowledges incomplete data for identifying all QOZB tax returns and no data to predict the number of future filers or statement furnishers.

**Inferences**
- The stated need for information is directly tied to statutory administration, but the practical burden estimates should be tested against the added data elements, entity complexity, and coordination between QOZBs, QOFs, brokers, and investors.

**Questions**
- Does 1.25 hours capture data gathering, reconciliation, valuation, electronic submission, corrections, and professional review?
- What evidence supports treating added reporting and investor statements as not materially increasing start-up costs?

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**What Changes:** The proposal adds annual QOF information-return duties, QOZB-to-QOF statements, investor-disposition statements, related broker reporting, penalty rules, and procedures for inadvertent certification revocation and voluntary QOF decertification.

**Affected Entities:** QOFs, applicable QOZBs, QOF investors disposing of interests, brokers handling covered dispositions, and tax professionals or service providers that support those entities.

**Why It Matters:** The proposal translates new statutory reporting requirements into recurring operational duties and can affect access to opportunity-zone tax benefits, compliance costs, and the information available to the IRS and investors.

**Observed Fact:** The notice estimates annual paperwork burdens for both QOFs and QOZBs and proposes a $500-per-day penalty for specified QOF reporting failures, subject to stated caps and reasonable-cause relief.

**Inference:** The most useful comments will improve reporting design and implementation evidence without disputing statutory reporting obligations.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Observed Facts**

**Inferences**

**Comment Focus:** Ask for examples, schemas, validation rules, and a correction process that prevents minor format errors from causing disproportionate penalties.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Risks**
- Incomplete or inconsistent QOZB statements can propagate into QOF returns and investor records.
- A filing-format error that prevents processing may be treated as a late filing under the proposed penalty provision.
- Small entities may rely on outside professionals or software whose cost and availability are not captured by a per-form estimate.

**Constructive Response:** Specify validation, correction, notice, and reasonable-cause procedures; collect implementation data before finalizing burden assumptions.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Observed Fact:** The proposal combines reporting duties with daily penalties, caps, higher amounts for intentional disregard, and reasonable-cause relief.

**Inference:** The design encourages complete and timely reporting, but it also makes clarity about data fields, processing errors, and corrections especially important.
