## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Existing Illinois VOC rules remain RACT for the 2015 ozone standard.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The prior controls remain technologically and economically adequate despite changed technologies, costs, and emissions conditions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A newer feasible control or materially changed source mix could make the continued-certification conclusion incomplete.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask EPA to identify the control-technology search, date cutoff, and category-specific comparisons.
- **Claim:** The Pine Bend permit is equivalent to or better than the oil-and-gas CTG.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The external floating-roof requirement and associated permit conditions reliably achieve at least 95 percent VOC reduction and are federally enforceable as implemented.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The permit may omit inspection, testing, repair, recordkeeping, or applicability details needed to make the performance claim enforceable.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request the full permit conditions and a line-by-line comparison with the CTG.
- **Claim:** Negative declarations accurately show no covered sources exist in the two areas.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Illinois' facility searches used complete, current, and category-appropriate source inventories and thresholds.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** An overlooked or newly constructed source could leave the SIP without applicable controls for a CTG category.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for search methods, databases, dates, thresholds, and procedures for revisiting the declarations.
- **Claim:** The proposed NOx limits and voluntary averaging option satisfy Moderate and Serious RACT.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The selected limits are comparable across source categories and averaging cannot shift emissions in a way that undermines localized control or monitoring.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Averaging could allow high-emitting units to operate above unit-specific levels while the aggregate reduction is difficult to verify.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request unit-level baselines, averaging boundaries, monitoring equations, and compliance demonstrations.
- **Claim:** The out-of-state comparison supports the conclusion that Illinois rules are as stringent as relevant controls elsewhere.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The compared rules are functionally comparable in source coverage, averaging, testing, exemptions, and enforcement.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A comparison of nominal limits without implementation conditions could overstate stringency.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask EPA to publish the comparison table and explain differences in source characteristics and compliance provisions.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** EPA proposes to approve Illinois SIP revisions covering VOC and NOx RACT for Chicago and Metro-East St. Louis under the 2015 ozone standard. The proposal includes continued certification of existing VOC controls, new or revised NOx controls in 35 IAC 217, approval of a federally enforceable Pine Bend permit, and several negative declarations.

**Affected Entities**
- Major VOC and NOx stationary sources in the Chicago and Metro-East nonattainment areas
- The Flint Hills Resources Pine Bend facility
- Illinois EPA and regulated facilities implementing 35 IAC parts 217, 218, and 219
- Residents and communities exposed to ozone-forming emissions in the nonattainment areas

**Observed Facts**
- The proposal addresses Moderate VOC RACT and Moderate and Serious NOx RACT; Serious VOC RACT is reserved for a separate action.
- The NOx rules cover listed industrial boilers, process heaters, furnaces, kilns, and other units at specified potential-to-emit thresholds.
- The proposal would approve an emissions-averaging plan as a voluntary alternative and describes an additional 10 percent reduction requirement.

**Inference:** This is primarily an approval of a state implementation framework, but it has practical consequences for facility-level control obligations, monitoring, and future enforcement in two ozone nonattainment areas.

**Evidence Quotes**
- proposing to approve revisions to 35 Illinois Administrative Code parts 217, 218, and 219
- The EPA has not identified any new control technologies
- additional 10 percent emissions reduction
- generally consistent with or more stringent
