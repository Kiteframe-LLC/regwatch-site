## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Useful-life and warranty changes will reduce implementation pain without materially weakening the program.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The costs and benefits of a longer useful-life/warranty period are balanced correctly across engine classes.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the cost increase is larger or more uneven than EPA suggests, the same change could raise purchase prices or warranty burden without a proportionate benefit.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for class-specific cost and durability evidence.
- **Claim:** NCP availability will preserve compliance flexibility without distorting competition.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** EPA can set MY 2027 NCPs at levels that protect complying manufacturers while keeping near-compliant engines in the market.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the penalty rate is off, the rule could either distort competition or fail to give manufacturers a realistic compliance path.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for the exact methodology and sensitivity analysis used for NCP rates.
- **Claim:** Inducement revisions will improve operator experience without undermining DEF compliance.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Operators will continue to use quality DEF and tamper-resistant designs will still work under the revised settings.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the inducement rules are too weak, tampering risk rises; if too strong, field burdens and winter operation problems rise.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for field-test evidence and cold-weather guardrails.
- **Claim:** In-use guidance can be added later without needing a fuller record now.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The agency can safely defer in-use guidance while the new-manufacture rule proceeds.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If in-use engines are materially affected, deferring guidance could leave the record incomplete for a visible part of the rule.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to separate newly manufactured from in-use rationales.
- **Claim:** The retained 42%/90% emissions claim is enough to justify the bundle of revisions.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The emissions-retention claim applies cleanly across the changes that alter compliance burden and incentive structure.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the retained-emissions claim is driven mostly by the unchanged standards rather than the revised provisions, the burden/benefit tradeoff needs more unpacking.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to show the emissions contribution of each individual change.
