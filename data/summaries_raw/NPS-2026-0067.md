## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim: The 160-vehicle daily cap still balances demand and visitor experience

* **Underlying assumption:**
  The 2012 planning analysis and subsequent monitoring still support the same cap under current road conditions, visitation patterns, and wildlife-management needs.
* **Counterfactual test:**
  If current demand has changed materially, or if crowding, wildlife-stop congestion, or road-safety conditions differ from the 2012 baseline, the inherited cap may no longer be optimally calibrated.
* **Procedural implication:**
  Commenters can ask NPS to show current monitoring results rather than relying mainly on the existence of the older VMP.

### Claim: Existing monitoring is enough to justify codifying the VMP limit

* **Underlying assumption:**
  NPS has recent indicator data showing that the implemented cap is working as intended.
* **Failure scenario:**
  If the monitoring record contains mixed results, trend changes, or unresolved crowding and wildlife-disturbance concerns, the notice does not summarize them for public review.
* **Procedural implication:**
  A final rule would be easier to defend if it surfaced the concrete monitoring findings behind the proposal.

### Claim: Counting all vehicles toward the cap is the right measurement rule

* **Underlying assumption:**
  Administrative vehicles, concession buses, and permit-based vehicles impose materially comparable pressure on the relevant visitor-experience and resource indicators.
* **Adversarial interpretation:**
  Readers could question whether a single undifferentiated count masks meaningful differences between a bus carrying many visitors and a private vehicle carrying few.
* **Procedural implication:**
  NPS should explain whether one-for-one counting is an administrative simplification, an empirical conclusion, or both.

### Claim: The rule has no significant small-entity effect because it reflects long-running practice

* **Underlying assumption:**
  Existing concessioners, tourism businesses, and related operators have already absorbed any practical consequences of the 160-vehicle framework.
* **Failure scenario:**
  If the clarified counting rule or season endpoint changes alter access timing or available allocations in practice, some businesses could still experience meaningful operational effects.
* **Procedural implication:**
  The agency's certification is plausible, but commenters can ask for a more concrete explanation of why no direct or meaningful indirect impacts are expected.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

* NPS proposes to revise Denali special regulations so the rule states that no more than 160 motor vehicles may travel on the restricted section of the Denali Park Road per 24-hour period during the visitor season.
* The proposed rule would replace older regulatory language that used a seasonal permit total of 10,512 motor vehicle permits.
* The rule would define the visitor season as beginning on the Saturday of Memorial Day weekend and ending on the second Thursday following Labor Day.
* The proposal would also clarify that all vehicles count toward the cap, not only vehicles traveling under a permit.
* NPS says it has implemented the VMP's 160-vehicle standard since 2012 and that this rule is meant to conform the regulation to that practice.

### Inference

* The proposal is framed less as a new expansion and more as a codification-and-clarification rule, but it still matters because it hardens a specific daily cap and a particular counting method into regulatory text.
* The strongest public-interest question is whether the visible record adequately shows that the inherited 2012 management logic still fits current demand, access, wildlife, and road-use conditions.

### Evidence Quotes

* "The NPS has been managing vehicle use on the park road consistent with the VMP since 2012."
* "This regulatory text would be replaced with a statement that no more than 160 vehicles may travel on the restricted section of the road per 24-hour period during the visitor season."
* "The rule also would clarify that any vehicle traveling on the park road counts toward the vehicle limit, not just vehicles operating under a permit."
