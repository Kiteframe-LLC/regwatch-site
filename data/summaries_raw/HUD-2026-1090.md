  **Failure Scenario:** If restored standards materially reduce future flood losses or avoidance costs, the cost-savings claim overstates net benefit.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The notice proposes to rescind most of HUD's 2024 floodplain and wetlands final rule, restore the prior regulatory floodplain framework, keep several 2024 flexibilities, and revise parts 50, 55, 58, and 200 to conform with that rollback.

**Affected Entities**
- HUD program applicants and grantees
- Applicants for FHA-backed mortgages and one- to four-unit housing projects
- Public housing and multifamily property operators
- Responsible entities and environmental reviewers working under part 58
- Local jurisdictions and project sponsors that rely on floodplain determinations and NFIP coordination

**Statutory Authority:** The notice relies on HUD's existing regulatory authority in parts 50, 55, 58, and 200 and expressly ties the proposed changes to Executive Order 14148, Executive Order 11988, Executive Order 11990, and NFIP requirements.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice says the proposal would generally return HUD to the pre-2024 floodplain framework.
- It preserves several 2024 flexibilities, including floodway exceptions and certain categorical exclusions.
- It cites annual construction cost savings from rescission and says those savings are based on the earlier RIA.
- It separately explains NFIP-based thresholds for noncritical and critical actions.

**Inference:** This is a rollback-with-carveouts proposal: HUD is trying to reduce compliance burdens and return to earlier floodplain rules while keeping a few operational improvements from the 2024 rule.

**Evidence Quotes**
- This proposed rule generally would restore HUD's regulations to their state prior to the publication of the April 23, 2024, final rule, although it would maintain flexibilities from that rule related to floodways, categorical exclusions, exemptions from Part 55 applicability, and the decisionmaking process; it also proposes minor revisions for clarity.
- Rescission of the 2024 rule would result in millions of dollars in saved construction costs and help spur needed housing development, with expected annual construction cost savings of $4.5 million to $85 million.
- This language would align with existing regulatory NFIP requirements, established by FEMA under 44 CFR 60.3, which generally require newly constructed and substantially improved buildings in the 1 percent annual chance floodplain to elevate the lowest floor.
- All newly constructed critical actions within the 0.2 percent annual chance floodplain, including those within the 1 percent annual chance floodplain, must be designed and built at or above the 0.2 percent annual chance flood elevation and must include an emergency evacuation plan, an early warning system, and identification of evacuation routes.
