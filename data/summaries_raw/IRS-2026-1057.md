## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Observed Fact:** The proposal would make the refunded portion of the adoption tax credit, child tax credit, American opportunity tax credit, and earned income credit a Federal public benefit for PRWORA purposes. Non-U.S.-citizens, non-U.S.-nationals, and aliens who are not qualified aliens would be ineligible for that refunded portion, subject to the rule's definitions and exceptions.

**Inference:** The proposal converts part of the tax-return calculation into an immigration-status eligibility gate while leaving ordinary credit eligibility otherwise governed by the Internal Revenue Code.

**Regulatory Action:** Add rules under 26 CFR part 1 specifying the refunded portion of covered credits, eligible status categories, joint-return treatment, filing-date status, and examples.

**Affected Entities**
- Taxpayers claiming the adoption, child, American opportunity, or earned income credit
- Families with mixed immigration or citizenship status
- Tax return preparers and software providers
- IRS processing, compliance, and taxpayer-service personnel
- States, localities, and organizations assisting eligible taxpayers

**Statutory Authority:** Internal Revenue Code sections 23, 24, 25A, 32, and 7805(a); PRWORA sections 401, 404, and 431, codified in 8 U.S.C. 1611, 1614, and 1641.

**Key Regulatory Change:** Eligibility for the refunded excess of covered credits would depend on being a U.S. citizen, U.S. national, or qualified alien, with status generally fixed when the return first claims the credit.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Alternatives**
- Use a status determination closer to payment or allow a taxpayer to update status before payment.
- Provide prospective application with transition relief for returns filed before the final rule.
- Create a correction, appeal, and repayment process for erroneous status determinations.
- Limit the rule to specified credits or amounts after a documented analysis of the statutory categories and impacts.

**Comment Use:** Ask the agency to compare these alternatives on legality, administrability, error risk, burden, and effects on mixed-status households.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

The arithmetic concept is clear: covered refundable credits are aggregated and the excess over specified tax liability is treated as the Federal public benefit. The filing-date and amended-return examples are internally understandable, but operational details for identity/status verification, notices, appeals, payment timing, and corrections are not fully specified.

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

The proposal identifies Code sections 23, 24, 25A, 32, and 7805(a), PRWORA provisions, and two OLC memoranda. The principal legal question is whether the refunded excess of a tax credit falls within PRWORA's Federal public benefit definition and whether the proposed implementation follows from that authority. The notice should address competing interpretations, statutory exceptions, and the relationship between eligibility to claim a credit and eligibility to receive its refunded portion.

## Step 8 — Procedural Normalcy

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

The proposal may encourage taxpayers, preparers, and software providers to treat immigration-status reporting as a condition of receiving the refundable excess. The fixed filing-date rule may reduce some processing ambiguity but can also create incentives to delay or sequence amended claims and may impose high stakes on an early filing decision. Clear safe harbors and correction rules would reduce incentives for defensive over-reporting and inconsistent administration.
