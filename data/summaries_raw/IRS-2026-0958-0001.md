## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Observed Facts:** The proposal would allow a taxpayer to elect that a CFC generally not compute or recognize section 987 gain or loss for a QBU, while retaining section 987(1) and (2) income and earnings-and-profits rules. It would amortize pre-election unrecognized gain or loss over 120 months, impose consistency and revocation limits, and require gain recognition for certain inbound liquidations or reorganizations. It also proposes election timing relief through 2027 and a de minimis asset test.

**Inference:** The central tradeoff is lower recurring compliance complexity for CFC groups in exchange for more reliance on shareholder-level realization rules, transition tracking, and targeted anti-basis-shifting rules.

**Regulatory Action:** Create an elective CFC exemption from most section 987(3) recognition and add transition and inbound-transaction safeguards.

**Affected Entities**
- U.S. shareholders of CFCs
- CFCs with section 987 QBUs
- tax departments and tax advisers
- domestic corporations acquiring CFC assets in inbound nonrecognition transactions
- partnerships and exempt partnerships connected to CFCs
- IRS personnel administering section 987 elections

**Statutory Authority:** Sections 987(3), 989(c), and 7805(a), as cited in the notice.

**Key Regulatory Change:** A CFC generally may stop computing and recognizing section 987 gain or loss when the election applies, subject to specified exceptions.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Alternatives**
- An elective exemption with a periodic revocation window rather than Commissioner-consent-only revocation.
- A higher or lower threshold with sensitivity analysis rather than a single $50 million cliff.
- A simplified safe harbor for smaller QBUs combined with a targeted anti-fragmentation rule.
- Optional prospective treatment for taxpayers that cannot reconstruct pre-election pools, paired with a transition accounting method.
- A limited election by QBU or CFC class instead of a consistency rule across commonly controlled CFCs.

**Comment Use:** These alternatives are material because they could preserve burden relief while reducing lock-in, cliff effects, and reconstruction costs. The notice invites comment on the proposed mechanics but does not visibly compare all of these designs.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Failure Modes**
- Election lock-in: the consistency and Commissioner-consent revocation rules may make a choice difficult to reverse when currency exposures or group structures change.
- Threshold gaming or cliff effects: taxpayers near $50 million may have incentives to reorganize QBUs or face sharply different computation duties.
- Reconstruction burden: a later inbound transaction may require historical calculations even after recurring section 987 recognition is turned off.
- Timing mismatch: shareholder-level recognition may not align with the CFC's economic currency gain or loss in a taxable sale, liquidation, or reorganization.
