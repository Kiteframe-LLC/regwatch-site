## Phase 1 — Docket Integrity Check

**Classification:** Full analytical spine present

**Observed Fact**
The docket record for this OMB-wide proposed rule includes the NPRM itself, a separate Regulatory Impact Analysis, and a separate Initial Regulatory Flexibility Analysis. The reviewed local materials for this run included the NPRM text and the separate RIA, while the IRFA was referenced in the preamble but not separately ingested into the local review set.

**Observed Fact**
The supporting materials that appear most influential for first-pass review were already present in the local record: the rule text and the RIA. The missing IRFA matters for small-entity detail, but the available record is sufficient to identify the proposal's main theories, the major operational changes, and several areas where the record appears underexplained.

**Inference**
This is not a thin or routine housekeeping docket. It is a broad government-wide revision to 2 CFR assistance rules that would change how grants are structured, monitored, paid, terminated, and conditioned across many agencies.

**Why that matters**
The strongest comment opportunities are not about whether OMB has any record at all. They are about whether the record adequately supports the breadth of the changes, especially where the preamble and RIA rely on qualitative assertions, scattered examples, and broad Executive Branch policy statements rather than program-specific evidence.

## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Major claim: current grant rules are broadly deficient for oversight

**Underlying assumption**
The cited examples of waste, fraud, DEI-related activity, foreign collaboration, and uneven oversight are representative enough to justify a government-wide rewrite of multiple grant-management provisions.

**Failure scenario**
If the examples are episodic, politically selective, or concentrated in only a few programs, then a government-wide rule may rest on an underexplained generalization.

**Procedural implication**
Commenters can ask OMB to connect each major amendment to program-level evidence or a clearer baseline showing why existing authorities are inadequate.

### Major claim: eliminating fixed amount awards and subawards is the best oversight response

**Underlying assumption**
The transparency gains outweigh the administrative benefits of simplified payment structures.

**Failure scenario**
If many recipients use fixed amount structures for lower-risk work because they reduce paperwork while still delivering results, eliminating them could increase burden without a comparable risk reduction showing.

**Procedural implication**
The record would be stronger if OMB explained when fixed amount mechanisms fail, how often, and why narrower safeguards were rejected.

### Major claim: E-Verify and pre-payment screening can be imposed consistently across award programs

**Underlying assumption**
These controls are legally available, operationally feasible, and proportionate across many different program types.

**Failure scenario**
Programs with short timelines, volunteer-heavy work, research settings, or layered subcontracting may face implementation ambiguity, delays, or compliance mismatches.

**Procedural implication**
Commenters can press for definitions, exceptions, timing rules, and agency-by-agency feasibility examples.

### Major claim: the proposal reduces recipient burden overall

**Underlying assumption**
The removal of some prior requirements offsets new controls such as payment justifications, subaward reporting reinforcement, domestic preference flow-downs, E-Verify participation, and publication-cost restrictions.

**Failure scenario**
If the burden reductions are mainly rhetorical or concentrated in a few removed provisions while new controls apply broadly, the burden narrative may be incomplete.

**Procedural implication**
A commenter can ask for a more transparent burden ledger showing what is removed, what is added, and for whom.

### Major claim: small entities are not significantly directly affected

**Underlying assumption**
Most effects on small organizations are indirect because agencies and prime recipients mediate the rule's impact.

**Failure scenario**
If small nonprofits, local governments, research performers, or small contractors must change payroll, payment, reporting, procurement, or publication practices to stay eligible or competitive, the direct/indirect line may be doing too much work.

**Procedural implication**
The IRFA and the preamble would benefit from more concrete examples of why the direct-effects conclusion holds.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

The proposal would revise broad portions of 2 CFR subtitle A governing Federal financial assistance. The main changes described in the available record include:

- eliminating fixed amount awards and fixed amount subawards;
- adding payment-request justification expectations and Do Not Pay style pre-payment screening;
- requiring recipients and subrecipients to participate in E-Verify for covered workers under Federal awards;
- strengthening subaward reporting and treatment of transfers to affiliates or subsidiaries;
- clarifying suspension and termination authority, including discretionary termination tied to agency priorities and Federal interests;
- revising domestic preference requirements;
- restricting certain foreign collaborations and some publication-cost allowability;
- revising nondiscrimination and national-policy provisions; and
- clarifying that the subtitle A text operates as an OMB regulation.

### Inference

This proposal functions less like a narrow technical amendment and more like a government-wide governance reset for grants and cooperative agreements. The practical stakes are high because even modest-seeming text changes in 2 CFR can flow through many agencies, award terms, and recipient compliance systems.

### Evidence Quotes

- "OMB proposes to eliminate the use of fixed amount awards and subawards..."
- "OMB proposes to add § 200.303(f) to require all recipients and subrecipients... to participate in... E-verify..."
- "OMB proposes to revise § 200.461... to make publication costs unallowable unless such costs are expressly required by statute or approved in advance by the Federal agency..."

## Step 3 — Agency Argument Tree

**Regulatory objective**
Improve transparency, accountability, oversight, legal conformity, and burden reduction in Federal financial assistance.

**Problem definition**
Existing 2 CFR rules are said to permit waste, weak oversight, inconsistent implementation, and activities misaligned with current Executive Branch policy.

**Data used**
The preamble cites examples from White House fact sheets, agency reports, a Senate committee report, a Heritage Foundation report, existing grant-management practice, and the separate RIA's qualitative discussion of operational weaknesses.

**Analytical method**
OMB identifies current policy gaps, proposes revised regulatory text, considers alternatives in the RIA, and then asserts qualitative benefits with generally minimal-to-moderate implementation costs.

**Alternatives considered**
The RIA says OMB considered maintaining current policy, narrower versions of several reforms, or more restrictive categorical approaches in some areas.

**Agency conclusion**
The proposed package is portrayed as the best way to improve oversight and consistency across agencies while avoiding ineffective or uneven reliance on existing authorities alone.

### Missing links / unsupported premises / logical jumps

- The leap from illustrative anecdotes to a record-wide conclusion that broad 2 CFR revision is necessary is only partly shown.
- The alternatives discussion often explains why the status quo is inadequate, but less often compares narrower middle-ground options with concrete cost or burden consequences.
- The benefits discussion is overwhelmingly qualitative, while the costs discussion acknowledges new administrative steps in several areas.
- The burden-reduction claim is not mapped clearly against the proposal's new compliance obligations.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

### Regulatory objective: improve oversight without unnecessary burden

**Alternatives evaluated**

- rely on existing authorities and agency discretion;
- maintain current fixed amount structures;
- use less formal payment-integrity controls;
- retain current domestic preference language;
- use narrower or broader foreign-collaboration and publication-cost approaches.

**Plausible alternatives not fully analyzed in the reviewed record**

- retain fixed amount awards but add targeted safeguards for higher-risk programs;
- require E-Verify or pre-payment checks only for specified award categories, thresholds, or risk bands;
- phase in new payment and subaward controls with pilot testing or safe harbors;
- preserve publication-cost allowability with caps or documented-need standards rather than a near-ban;
- provide a more explicit program-by-program or recipient-type analysis for direct small-entity effects.

**Evidence suggesting feasibility**

The RIA itself describes alternative structures in several areas, which shows OMB recognized that intermediate options existed. What appears underdeveloped is the comparison between those options and the chosen approach.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Observed Fact**
The proposal is operationally coherent in the sense that its provisions largely point in one direction: more front-end eligibility screening, tighter payment and subaward controls, broader Federal discretion to terminate or suspend, and narrower cost allowability for some activities.

**Inference**
The weak point is not internal contradiction. It is compression. The reviewed record often states that a control will improve oversight or reduce risk without showing enough about implementation variance across agencies, recipients, and award types.

**Observed Fact**
The RIA acknowledges that eliminating fixed amount subawards will require "financial reporting and performance monitoring for all subawards," and that payment-accountability changes "will necessitate the development of new monitoring systems and financial review procedures."

**Inference**
Those acknowledgments cut against any broad assumption that the rule is mainly a burden-reduction exercise.

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

**Observed Fact**
The preamble relies heavily on 31 U.S.C. 503(a)(2), existing grant-management authorities, and statutory/programmatic cross-references. It also repeatedly invokes recent Executive Orders and administration policy priorities.

**Inference**
OMB likely has substantial authority to set government-wide financial management requirements, but some of the proposed conditions may invite comment on fit and limits. The biggest candidates in the reviewed text are the E-Verify mandate for assistance recipients and the breadth of termination, foreign-collaboration, and publication-cost restrictions when applied across many distinct statutory programs.

**Uncertain**
Without the full agency-by-agency statutory mapping and the separate IRFA, it is difficult to judge how much of the rule rests on OMB's general management authority versus specific award-program statutes.

## Step 8 — Procedural Normalcy

**Classification:** Complex but normal

**Observed Fact**

**Observed Fact**

**Inference**

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- Recipients may respond to the elimination of fixed amount structures by shifting toward more paperwork-heavy subaward or contract arrangements with little demonstrated fraud-prevention benefit.
- Broad E-Verify and payment-screening obligations may delay time-sensitive awards or create uneven compliance across programs with different staffing models.
- Discretionary termination standards tied to agency priorities or the Federal interest could create uncertainty for recipients planning multi-year projects.
- Publication-cost restrictions may discourage dissemination of award-supported research or evaluation where publication is useful but not expressly required.
- Domestic preference flow-downs may be interpreted inconsistently across non-infrastructure programs.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

- The proposal may incentivize agencies and recipients to over-document routine payments to avoid later scrutiny.
- It may incentivize award restructuring away from simple subaward designs even where those designs are currently efficient.
- It may incentivize conservative decisions on foreign collaboration, publication, and procurement because the compliance boundaries are not fully clear in the reviewed text.
- It may incentivize recipients to avoid marginal applicants, subrecipients, or activities that appear administratively complex even if they are lawful and programmatically valuable.

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

**Observed Fact**
The proposal has a substantial narrative record and a separate RIA. It is not unsupported in the absolute sense.

**Observed Fact**
The strongest visible support in the reviewed materials is qualitative and illustrative. The RIA repeatedly describes expected benefits as qualitative and expected costs as minimal to moderate, while often seeking comment rather than presenting firm quantification.

**Inference**
The evidentiary weakness is not absence of materials; it is that the materials reviewed here leave several core judgments under-quantified:

- how widespread the cited oversight failures are;
- why existing authorities are inadequate program by program;
- what aggregate burden the new controls create;
- why narrower alternatives were insufficient; and
- why small-entity and federalism implications remain limited despite broad operational changes.
