  **Consequence:** Smaller providers may struggle to build the required processes in time.
  
  **Comment Focus:** Ask for a phased schedule or size-based compliance path.

## Step 16 — Evidence Requests

- Ask for baseline data on upstream-provider problems and the current size of the bad-actor population.
  Why it matters: a mandatory baseline is hard to evaluate without knowing how common the problem is today.
  Ask the agency to: publish the data categories, provider classes, and complaint or traceback counts it relies on.
- Ask the FCC to compare the proposed baseline against a phased or risk-tiered alternative.
  Why it matters: a one-size-fits-all rollout may be more burdensome than needed for lower-risk or smaller providers.
  Ask the agency to: analyze phased compliance, size-based tiers, or narrower triggers targeted at known bad actors.
- Ask for an audit or sampling design that verifies KYUP and attestation claims.
  Why it matters: self-reporting can overstate compliance if the rule does not show how to test it.
  Ask the agency to: specify who audits, how often, what gets sampled, and what happens when records conflict with actual routing.
- Ask for provider-size-specific cost estimates and a transition plan for small entities.
  Why it matters: the same compliance architecture can have very different cost and staffing impacts across provider classes.
  Ask the agency to: provide a burden table by provider size and state whether the six-month one-time review should be phased in.
- Ask the agency to narrow and explain the special-case treatment for foreign-originated calls.
  Why it matters: special-case provisions can be overbroad if they are not tied to a concrete harm model.
  Ask the agency to: explain the specific failure mode, the affected call types, and the safeguards that prevent spillover to unrelated traffic.

## Step 17 — High-Leverage Comment Angles

- The FCC should justify the baseline problem size before making KYUP mandatory for everyone.
  Why it matters: a rule that expands duties across the ecosystem needs a clear problem baseline, not just a general assertion that bad actors exist.
  Ask the agency to: show the prevalence of bad actor upstream providers, the complaint or traceback data, and the specific evidence that flexible KYUP has failed.
  Practical question: what data supports the move from flexible KYUP to a mandatory baseline for all providers?
- The FCC should compare its rollout against phased or size-tiered alternatives.
  Why it matters: the notice sets transition timing, but it does not fully compare that approach against a slower or more targeted rollout.
  Ask the agency to: analyze whether a phased schedule, size-based tiers, or narrower triggers would achieve the same anti-robocall goals with less burden.
  Practical question: why is the proposed timetable better than a phased compliance model for smaller or lower-risk providers?
- The FCC should explain how KYUP and attestation compliance will actually be verified.
  Why it matters: self-reporting plus recordkeeping is not enough if the rule is meant to cut off bad actors in practice.
  Ask the agency to: spell out audit authority, sampling, record-retention rules, and correction procedures.
  Practical question: what independent check will show that providers are really doing the monitoring and verification the rule requires?
- The FCC should quantify the burden on smaller providers before finalizing the rule.
  Why it matters: the same KYUP stack may be routine for large providers but heavy for small ones.
  Ask the agency to: provide a size-stratified cost estimate and explain whether the six-month review should be phased in.
  Practical question: what evidence shows smaller providers can absorb the new workload without service disruption?
- The FCC should narrow the special-case rules for foreign-originated calls and other exceptional traffic.
  Why it matters: special-case language can overreach unless the agency shows the exact traffic types and harms it is targeting.
  Ask the agency to: define the special cases, explain the harm model, and state how public safety and legitimate cross-border traffic will be protected.
  Practical question: how will the FCC keep the special-case rules from spilling over into unrelated traffic?
- The FCC should make the Governance Authority revocation and appeal path more explicit.
  Why it matters: revocation tools work only if the review path is detailed enough to prevent arbitrary exclusion.
  Ask the agency to: explain the evidentiary standard, timing, and correction path for revocations and reconsideration.
  Practical question: what process prevents erroneous revocation from disrupting legitimate traffic?
