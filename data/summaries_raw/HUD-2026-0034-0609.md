## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_title_vi_scope
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The statutory text and cited precedent foreclose agency use of disparate-impact regulations under Title VI.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A court or commenter distinguishes private enforcement under Sandoval from the scope of agency regulations, or identifies statutory and regulatory functions that are not resolved by the cited cases.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** HUD should explain the distinction and address contrary authority and the limits of the proposed repeal.
- **Claim Id:** claim_constitutional_incentives
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Compliance with disparate-impact rules predictably causes recipients to adopt unlawful racial balancing.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Recipients instead use race-neutral investigation, narrowly tailored remedies, or existing safeguards; the claimed coercive mechanism may then be overstated.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The record would be stronger with concrete examples, enforcement files, or other evidence showing how the current provisions produce the asserted incentive.
- **Claim Id:** claim_compliance_costs
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Removing disparate-impact provisions will reduce net costs without shifting material costs to affected communities or recipients.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Reduced agency review increases private monitoring, litigation, remediation, or unequal-access costs that are not captured by HUD’s accounting.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** HUD should identify whose costs decrease, whose costs may increase, and how those effects were considered.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** HUD proposes to remove 24 CFR 1.4(b)(2)(i), remove the ‘or effect’ language in 24 CFR 1.4(b)(3), remove 24 CFR 1.4(b)(6), remove 24 CFR 1.4(c)(2), redesignate related paragraphs, and align the employment cross-reference with Executive Order 14173. It also reopens comments on related topics from the January 2026 proposal.

- Public housing agencies, State and local governments, and other HUD-funded entities
- HUD enforcement and compliance staff
- People and communities affected by decisions in HUD-assisted programs, including groups protected by Title VI

**Statutory Authority**

- HUD authority cited at 42 U.S.C. 3535(d) and 24 CFR part 1

**Observed Fact Vs Inference**

**Inference:** The practical result would likely reduce the role of disparate-impact analysis in HUD’s Title VI compliance work, although the notice does not specify every enforcement consequence.
