## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Following each TSCA Order with a SNUR will place similarly situated manufacturers and processors under the same protective standard.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The Order-derived restrictions are sufficiently clear, transferable, and accessible for persons who were not parties to the original PMN or Order.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A person may be unable to determine which confidential or chemical-specific restrictions apply, or may face different practical requirements across substances with different Orders.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request a chemical-by-chemical crosswalk from each proposed significant new use to the relevant Order restriction and the public notice or bona fide process that makes the restriction administrable.
- **Claim:** A 90-day SNUN notification and EPA review will prevent unreviewed significant new uses from commencing.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Potential manufacturers and processors can identify a covered use in time, submit a complete SNUN, and obtain a determination before operations begin.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Confidential use descriptions, import and article questions, or uncertain boundaries between ongoing and new uses could produce noncompliance or delayed lawful activity.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request examples, timing rules, and a clear process for borderline uses, confidential uses, and activities begun before the final rule.
- **Claim:** The proposed controls are supported by consideration of toxicity, exposure, release, volume, and disposal factors.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The underlying PMN, Order, hazard, exposure, and release evidence is sufficiently complete and chemical-specific to support each designation.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A broad or repetitive control may not track the evidence for a particular chemical, pathway, or use, especially where the public record does not expose the underlying analysis.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request the evidence table, assumptions, uncertainty treatment, and rationale for every listed chemical and significant new use.
- **Claim:** The proposed rule imposes only conditional costs because SNUN costs arise only if a person pursues a significant new use.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The conditional nature of the cost captures the real deterrence, delay, compliance, export, and opportunity costs created by the SNUR.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** Proposed TSCA section 5 SNURs would require a SNUN at least 90 days before a designated manufacture, import, or processing activity.

**Affected Entities**
- manufacturers, importers, processors, and downstream users
- exporters subject to TSCA section 12(b) notification
- small businesses facing SNUN fees and preparation costs
- workers, potentially exposed or susceptible subpopulations, and communities

**Statutory Authority:** TSCA sections 5(a)(2), 5(a)(1)(B), 5(e), 5(f)(4), 5(g), 12(b), and 13, with implementing regulations in 40 CFR part 721.

**Key Regulatory Change:** Deviations from underlying TSCA Order conditions would become significant new uses, extending pre-manufacture notification and EPA review beyond original PMN submitters.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice estimates per-SNUN costs of $45,496 for large businesses and $14,976 for small businesses.
- It estimates export notification cost at approximately $106 per unit but cannot estimate notification volume.
- It describes CBI significant new uses and a bona fide-intent identification process.
- It states that a SNUR is not itself a risk determination; EPA acts after receiving a SNUN.

**Inference:** The proposal is a notification and review gate whose fairness and feasibility depend on accessible, precise substance-specific Orders, evidence, and confidential-use procedures.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The issuance of a SNUR is not a risk determination itself, only a notification requirement.
- EPA believes it is necessary to follow a TSCA order with a SNUR.
- EPA is unable to estimate the likely number of export notifications.
