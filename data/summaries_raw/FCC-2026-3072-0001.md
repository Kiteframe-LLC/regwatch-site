## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_delay
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A single 120-day presumptive deadline can fairly identify delays that effectively prohibit service across varied authorization systems.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** An application requires multiple permits, contract negotiations, public-safety review, or correction of an incomplete submission, and the deadline expires before the government can act for reasons not equivalent to prohibition.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should define completeness, tolling, multi-agency coordination, extensions, and rebuttal evidence before assigning the presumption.
- **Claim Id:** claim_fee
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Actual direct rights-of-way management costs can be identified and allocated consistently by jurisdictions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Shared overhead, inspection costs, restoration obligations, or highly variable project conditions are excluded or allocated inconsistently, producing under-recovery or arbitrary charges.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters should provide cost-accounting examples and ask for a reproducible allocation method and review process.
- **Claim Id:** claim_safe_harbor
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A national safe-harbor level can approximate reasonable costs without distorting local conditions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The safe harbor is too low for complex urban or technically unusual projects, or high enough to become a de facto mandatory rate disconnected from actual costs.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The record should include sensitivity analysis, jurisdiction-size/project-type distinctions, and a mechanism for updating the harbor.
- **Claim Id:** claim_commingled
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Use of the same infrastructure for other services does not justify materially different public-rights-of-way conditions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A locality faces additional engineering, safety, restoration, or occupancy impacts from a facility’s broader use but cannot account for them under the proposed rule.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should distinguish impermissible service-based add-ons from legitimate, documented incremental impacts.
- **Claim Id:** claim_authority
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Section 253 and related provisions authorize prospective categorical presumptions rather than only case-specific adjudication or preemption.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A court treats the proposed deadline or fee ceiling as an impermissible expansion of agency authority or an intrusion on Section 253(c) functions.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should address statutory text, precedent, severability, and the relationship between presumptions and judicial review.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Observed Facts:** The NPRM would establish presumptions concerning when state and local authorization delays, fees, in-kind demands, and additional conditions for commingled facilities effectively prohibit wireline telecommunications service under Section 253.

**Inference:** The proposal seeks to convert a fact-specific statutory standard into clearer ex ante compliance benchmarks while preserving rebuttal and savings-clause arguments.

**Regulatory Action:** The FCC proposes a 120-day authorization presumption, actual-direct-cost fee limits and possible safe harbors, treatment of in-kind compensation within fee ceilings, and limits on additional requirements based on other services.

**Affected Entities**
- wireline telecommunications providers and infrastructure deployers
- state and local governments and agencies managing public rights-of-way
- small governmental jurisdictions
- residents, businesses, and communities awaiting broadband deployment
- cable franchisees and providers using commingled facilities

**Statutory Authority:** Sections 1, 4(i), 253, 303, and 403 of the Communications Act, as identified in the ordering clauses.

**Core Tradeoff:** The proposal may accelerate deployment and reduce entry barriers, but categorical presumptions could constrain legitimate local review, cost recovery, safety conditions, or varied project administration if the implementation lines are not precise.

**Evidence Quotes**

- actual, direct costs of managing the rights-of-way
- We tentatively conclude that the Commission possesses authority under Sections 253, 4(i), 201(b), and 303 of the Act.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- Deadline expiry could trigger a presumption before an application is complete or before all responsible agencies can coordinate.
- A safe harbor could under-recover legitimate variable costs or become a de facto mandatory price.
- Counting in-kind contributions may undervalue safety, restoration, or public-benefit obligations.
- The commingled-facility rule could create ambiguity over legitimate incremental engineering or occupancy requirements.
- Unclear rebuttal procedures could shift disputes into costly litigation rather than produce predictable compliance.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

- Providers gain incentives to use the presumptions and challenge delays or fees.
- Local governments may simplify requirements, but may also front-load conditions or characterize applications as incomplete.
- A fixed fee harbor may encourage standardization but could discourage unusual or high-cost projects if it is not calibrated.
- The proposal may shift disputes from local administration to FCC or federal-court proceedings.
