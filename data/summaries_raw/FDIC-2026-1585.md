## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_burden
  
  **Claim:** Prior approval creates unnecessary administrative burden and the proposed categories can be exempted.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The routine approvals reviewed by the FDIC are representative of the disclosures that would be made without prior review.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A disclosure category that is routine in ordinary cases creates a materially different confidentiality or competitive risk in a particular case.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FDIC should explain how category-level authorization preserves case-specific safeguards and whether a notice or recordkeeping backstop is needed.
- **Claim Id:** claim_good_cause
  
  **Claim:** A non-exhaustive good-cause list will improve transparency and consistency.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Authorized directors will apply the listed factors with enough consistency that parties can predict outcomes.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The factors point in different directions and the rule does not identify how conflicts are weighed or how decisions are documented.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request examples, a decision-record requirement, or a clearer hierarchy among the factors.
- **Claim Id:** claim_historical_threshold
  
  **Claim:** Confidentiality justifications generally diminish after twenty-five years.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Age is a reliable proxy for reduced privacy, supervisory, trade-secret, and safety-and-soundness risk across all information categories.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Historical information remains identifying, commercially sensitive, or useful to infer current supervisory practices even after twenty-five years.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The administrative record should compare the categorical threshold with information-specific review or longer protection for defined classes.
- **Claim Id:** claim_savings
  
  **Claim:** The proposal produces at least $19,807 in annual savings and meaningful unquantified efficiency benefits.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The number of requests eliminated, preparation time, and per-request cost are measured consistently and do not shift into compliance, contracting, or risk-management costs.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Institutions replace FDIC requests with additional internal screening, confidentiality agreements, or disclosure logs whose costs exceed the estimated savings.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters should ask for the underlying sample, time assumptions, and any expected offsetting compliance costs.
- **Claim Id:** claim_risk
  
  **Claim:** The risk of unintended disclosure will not increase materially.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Recipients authorized without prior FDIC review have sufficient incentives and controls to protect the information.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A merger counterparty, service provider, affiliate, or downstream recipient misuses or rediscloses information before the FDIC can review the event.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The proposal should clarify incident response, records of disclosures, recipient accountability, and the FDIC's ability to examine or enforce safeguards.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The substantive NPRM would reorganize and revise the FDIC's information-disclosure rules, expand specified no-prior-approval sharing of confidential information for business purposes, codify a good-cause standard for discretionary disclosures, revise FOIA procedures, move service-of-process rules to new 12 CFR part 306, and make conforming amendments to other FDIC regulations.

**Affected Entities**
- FDIC-supervised insured depository institutions
- parent holding companies of insured depository institutions
- affiliates, legal counsel, accountants, auditors, service providers, and potential merger counterparties
- FOIA requesters and submitters of confidential commercial information
- parties seeking FDIC information in non-party legal proceedings
- persons serving legal process on the FDIC or FDIC personnel
- small banking organizations and their customers

**Statutory Authority:** The proposed authority citations include 5 U.S.C. 552 and 12 U.S.C. 1819 (Seventh and Tenth), along with other listed banking statutes for part 303 amendments.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The most consequential change is shifting certain disclosures from a prior FDIC-approval model to a category-based authorization model conditioned on a necessary or appropriate business purpose and, for several recipient categories, a qualifying confidentiality agreement.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice proposes new subparts B, C, and D in part 309 and a new part 306.
- The proposal includes a twenty-five-year historical-information authorization and a written waiver for certain merger counterparties.
- The PRA table estimates 1,473 hours of annual burden for specified requests and waivers.

**Inference:** The proposal is primarily a procedural and confidentiality-governance rule whose practical effect depends on institution-level classification and documentation after prior FDIC review is removed.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Overbroad category-based disclosure
  
  **Mechanism:** A disclosure fits a listed recipient category but presents unusual confidentiality, privacy, or competitive risk.
  
  **Consequence:** The FDIC may learn of the problem only after disclosure, with limited ability to reconstruct the decision.
- **Failure Mode:** Ambiguous business-purpose boundary
  
  **Mechanism:** Necessary or appropriate for business purposes is applied differently across affiliates, service providers, and merger counterparties.
  
  **Consequence:** Institutions face inconsistent compliance decisions and recipients may receive more information than necessary.
- **Failure Mode:** Historical-information over-disclosure
  
  **Mechanism:** The twenty-five-year rule is applied without information-specific review.
  
  **Consequence:** Old records remain capable of identifying institutions, individuals, trade secrets, or supervisory methods.
- **Failure Mode:** Cost displacement
  
  **Mechanism:** FDIC-request savings are replaced by internal screening, contracting, logging, or incident-management costs.
  
  **Consequence:** Net benefits are smaller than the notice estimates, especially for smaller institutions.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Observed Fact:** The proposal reduces prior approval friction for institutions and recipients while retaining FDIC discretion for other disclosures.

**Incentives Created**
- Institutions have an incentive to classify disclosures within listed categories and document business purpose internally.
- Recipients have an incentive to negotiate qualifying confidentiality agreements and limit information sharing to preserve access.
- The FDIC has an incentive to develop consistent internal guidance because more decisions will occur outside individualized prior review.

**Risk:** If the rule does not require a disclosure record or meaningful feedback mechanism, the incentive to make conservative classifications may be weaker than the incentive to avoid delay.
