## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_rule204_approval
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The requirements in Rule 204 and the supporting documents are sufficiently specific and enforceable to support future credit transactions across varied nonroad fleets.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A fleet with unusual operating locations, mixed equipment, changing hours, or incomplete historic records could produce credits whose baseline or continued use is difficult to reconstruct.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** EPA can strengthen the record by identifying how the rule handles representative edge cases and by explaining the minimum records needed before a certificate is issued or maintained.
- **Claim Id:** claim_quantification
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Two-year average annual hours, rated horsepower, and the applicable emission-factor hierarchy provide a reliable baseline and post-project comparison.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Atypical operating years, manufacturer data that are not load-based, or a change in duty cycle could make the calculated reduction differ materially from actual emissions avoided.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** A comment can request examples, sensitivity calculations, and a clear explanation of how MCAQD will reject or adjust nonrepresentative baselines.
- **Claim Id:** claim_double_counting
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** MCAQD and MAG will maintain a reliable separation between reductions credited as NERCs and reductions already embedded in SIP inventories or RFP/ROP demonstrations.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Inventory updates, normal fleet turnover, or use of the same reduction in multiple planning products could make a credit appear surplus when it has already been relied upon elsewhere.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final record should identify the control ledger, update cadence, responsible entity, and reconciliation process for preserving surplus integrity.
- **Claim Id:** claim_permanence
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Permits, removal or disabling requirements, replacement standards, and ongoing monitoring will prevent the baseline engine or a higher-emitting substitute from returning to service in the nonattainment area.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** An engine is moved, sold, repurposed, or replaced through a pathway not captured by the captive-fleet records, or a permit condition is not updated after a fleet change.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for the enforcement and reporting steps that follow a transfer, fleet restructuring, or detected noncompliance, including whether already-issued NERCs are adjusted or revoked.
- **Claim Id:** claim_deferred_rule230
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The Rule 210 and Rule 220 pathways are sufficient for all eligible generators until EPA acts on Rule 230, and the deferred provision can remain severable without impairing the rest of Rule 204.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A generator that relies on a general-permit pathway cannot obtain equivalent federally enforceable conditions under the approved pathways, narrowing practical access or creating inconsistent treatment.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** EPA should identify the precise operational effect of the carve-out and the criteria or timing for resolving the Rule 230 dependency.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Observed Facts**
- EPA proposes to approve MCAQD Rule 204 into the Arizona SIP, except section 301.2 b.(3).
- The rule creates voluntary NOX and VOC credits from captive nonroad-engine retrofits or replacements for use as NNSR offsets.
- The approval relies on CAA sections 110, 110(k), 110(l), and 173, 40 CFR 51.165, and certification, permitting, monitoring, and recordkeeping safeguards.

**Inference:** This SIP approval expands offset supply from a nontraditional source. The main public-interest question is whether baseline, inventory, permit, and correction controls remain auditable over time.

**Regulatory Action:** Approve Rule 204 except its Rule 230-dependent provision and add the EIP support document to the non-regulatory SIP.

**Affected Entities**
- Nonroad-fleet generators
- Stationary sources seeking NNSR offsets
- MCAQD, ADEQ, MAG, and EPA
- Maricopa County communities

**Statutory Authority:** CAA sections 110, 110(k), 110(l), and 173; 40 CFR 51.165; and the Arizona SIP framework.

**Key Regulatory Change:** Recognize voluntary nonroad-engine reductions as NNSR credits subject to integrity safeguards, while deferring one permit pathway.

**Evidence Quotes**
- Rule 204 allows for the generation of ERCs from nonroad engines.
- Offsets must be real, surplus, permanent, quantifiable, and federally enforceable.
- The EPA is therefore deferring action on section 301.2 b.(3) of Rule 204.
