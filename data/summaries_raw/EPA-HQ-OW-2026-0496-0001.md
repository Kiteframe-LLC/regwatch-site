## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

EPA proposes to let Utah issue and enforce Class VI permits for underground carbon-dioxide storage outside Indian lands. It would amend 40 CFR 147.2250 and incorporate specified state laws and rules. EPA would retain oversight and its enforcement authorities; individual projects would still need permits, technical review and public participation. Approval would transfer program administration, rather than authorize a particular storage project or establish a new national carbon-storage standard.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Observed Fact:** R644 requires site characterization, calibrated/site-specific area-of-review analysis, corrective action, construction safeguards, mechanical integrity, testing and monitoring, response plans and financial assurance. The program description separates permission to construct from permission to inject. R644-17-1 sets a 50-year default post-injection monitoring period, allows supported alternative periods and requires longer monitoring when non-endangerment cannot be demonstrated. The MOA requires EPA consultation on alternative timeframes and excludes injection-depth waivers.

**Inference:** The framework is technically coherent on its face. Ten years since last injection is only a statutory prerequisite to requesting a completion certificate, not a substitute for approved closure. **Unknown:** The adequacy of any future project’s geology, model or financial estimate. No site-specific performance or climate-benefit finding follows from approving primacy. EPA Annex A’s emissions-reduction and dollars-per-ton diagnostics are inapplicable to this administrative transfer.

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

The notice consistently presents the proposed approval as a section 1422 action and retains EPA enforcement under section 1423 and 40 CFR 147.1(e). The Attorney General statement (-0003) certifies state authority under UC Title 40 Chapter 11 and R644 and explains that Utah’s environmental self-evaluation privilege does not cover DOGM’s program. These are affirmative supports, not an absence of legal analysis.

**Uncertain interpretation:** UC 40-11-16(2) broadly releases state regulatory obligations and bonds after certification. R644-5-3 and R644-17-1 link that event to approved site closure and non-endangerment. EPA should explain the interaction and the effect on any surviving federal authority without suggesting that state law can extinguish it. The program description repeatedly describes a revision of the section 1425 program while the notice proposes section 1422 approval; EPA should reconcile those descriptions. UC 40-11-6(3)’s Utah-origin preference warrants an explanation of federal-program scope and neutral technical criteria. These questions do not establish an unlawful delegation or constitutional violation.
