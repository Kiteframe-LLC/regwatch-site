## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim A

**Claim:** A fully approved second-period regional haze SIP is sufficient to satisfy Prong 4 for the 2015 ozone NAAQS.

**Underlying assumption:** The interstate consultation and visibility-protection obligations in `40 CFR 51.308(f)` capture the same practical concerns that CAA section `110(a)(2)(D)(i)(II)` addresses for this NAAQS-specific infrastructure submission.

**Failure scenario:** If the regional haze approval did not actually resolve all interstate visibility issues relevant to this infrastructure submission, the proposal's logic would look too categorical.

**Procedural implication:** A constructive comment could ask EPA to identify the specific findings in the January 26, 2026 regional haze approval that support this Prong 4 conclusion for Indiana.

### Claim B

**Claim:** Indiana's 2018 infrastructure SIP submission can be approved now because the necessary visibility predicate was supplied later through the 2026 regional haze approval.

**Underlying assumption:** EPA may reasonably treat the older infrastructure submission and the later regional haze approval as part of one continuous compliance story rather than needing a materially fuller explanation of the time gap.

**Failure scenario:** If the record does not clearly connect the 2018 submission to the 2026 regional haze action, outside readers may find the approval path harder to audit.

**Procedural implication:** EPA could strengthen the record by briefly explaining why the later regional haze approval fully cures the earlier “no action” posture on Indiana's visibility prong.

### Claim C

**Claim:** No additional technical showing is needed in this notice beyond the statement that Indiana now has a fully approved regional haze SIP.

**Underlying assumption:** The visibility analysis already happened in the regional haze action, so repeating that work here would be unnecessary.

**Failure scenario:** If commenters cannot easily locate the underlying haze analysis from this docketed record, the proposal may appear underexplained even if the legal result is ultimately correct.

**Procedural implication:** The final rule would benefit from a citation or short summary pointing readers to the underlying regional haze findings.
