## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The proposed definition is a clarification of existing law rather than a change in legal rights or obligations.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Market participants already treat the covered event contracts as swaps and existing CFTC obligations already apply.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A product falls within the non-exhaustive event-contract language but has been structured, marketed, or licensed under a state or another federal regime; the rule then changes practical jurisdictional exposure despite the stated baseline.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The Commission should identify which products are already covered, which would be newly clarified, and what transition or fair-notice consequences follow.
- **Claim:** Market practice is compelling evidence that event contracts are commonly known to the trade as swaps.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Self-certifications, CFTC treatment, and statements from several market participants represent the relevant trade as a whole.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The cited practice is concentrated among incumbent DCMs or products already designed for CFTC compliance and does not establish common treatment for political, cultural, weather, or novel products.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request a representative product taxonomy, source population, and explanation of how contrary state or industry treatment was weighted.
- **Claim:** The proposed rule produces negligible costs.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** No new CFTC requirements apply and any behavioral shifts are indirect or temporary.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Platforms move into the federal regime, incur fixed compliance costs, unwind state licenses, face parallel obligations, or change retail access and competitive conditions.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The cost analysis should quantify or bound transition, entry, state-revenue, retail-risk, and competition effects rather than rely mainly on qualitative treatment.
- **Claim:** No viable regulatory alternatives exist.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Only a broad codification can resolve uncertainty about the CFTC's jurisdiction.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A narrower definition, interpretive statement, product-specific guidance, phased application, or coordination process could resolve the identified uncertainty with less disruption.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The alternatives analysis should compare scope, legal certainty, implementation cost, federalism effects, and retail protections.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory action:** The CFTC proposes to add a broad, non-exhaustive class of event contracts to the swap definition in 17 CFR 1.3(2)(i)(G).

**Affected entities:** DCMs, SEFs, swap dealers, major swap participants, platforms, state-regulated operators, and market makers.

**Statutory authority:** CEA section 1a(47) and Dodd-Frank sections 712(d)(1), 712(d)(2)(A), and 721(b).

**Observed Fact:** The notice cites conflicting state actions and appellate decisions, reports $1.5 billion monthly, and identifies exclusions for futures, SEC instruments, and casino-style products.

**Inference:** The clarification may affect federalism, entry, competition, transition costs, and retail behavior.

**Evidence:** The proposed text adds event contracts to § 1.3; the notice says the list is non-exhaustive and that no viable alternatives were identified.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Overbroad classification
  
  **Mechanism:** A non-exhaustive definition could be applied to products whose structure or consequence profile differs from the examples.
  
  **Consequence:** Unclear compliance and litigation exposure for platforms and market participants.
  
  **Mitigation Comment:** Request examples, decision criteria, and treatment of borderline products.
- **Failure Mode:** Regulatory migration without measured safeguards
  
  **Mechanism:** Classification certainty shifts products from state regimes toward DCMs without a parallel monitoring or consumer-protection plan.
  
  **Consequence:** Higher retail exposure, transition friction, or reduced competition may occur before effects are evaluated.
  
  **Mitigation Comment:** Request implementation metrics, transition reporting, and retail-risk monitoring.
- **Failure Mode:** Incumbent advantage
  
  **Mechanism:** Fixed federal infrastructure costs may favor established DCMs over state-regulated or new entrants.
  
  **Consequence:** Reduced entry, service quality, innovation diversity, or competitive pressure.
  
  **Mitigation Comment:** Request an entry-cost analysis and less burdensome alternatives.
- **Failure Mode:** Boundary litigation
  
  **Mechanism:** Conflicting judicial interpretations and unresolved exclusions leave the classification question contestable.
  
  **Consequence:** Delayed enforcement and duplicative compliance while cases proceed.
  
  **Mitigation Comment:** Request a reasoned response to contrary interpretations and a transition rule for disputed products.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Evasion:** The proposed rule may reduce incentives to characterize covered products as state gambling or other products, but its broad boundary may also incentivize restructuring to fit exclusions such as futures or SEC-regulated instruments.

**Threshold Gaming:** The non-exhaustive category and product-specific exclusions create incentives to design contract terms, settlement mechanics, or marketing around the classification boundary.

**Strategic Compliance:** Incumbent DCMs may benefit from existing compliance infrastructure, while smaller or state-regulated operators may delay entry or seek costly parallel arrangements.

**Consumer Incentives:** The notice recognizes that a federal classification may be perceived as a safety signal and could increase retail risk appetite without parallel improvements in disclosure or education.

**Comment Use:** Ask the Commission to explain how it will detect migration, restructuring, and changes in retail behavior after adoption.

  **Why it matters:** Clear examples and limiting principles would reduce fair-notice risk and distinguish swaps from futures, SEC instruments, and casino-style products.
  
  **Ask the agency to:** Publish a product taxonomy, identify the statutory prong for each category, and explain treatment of borderline products.
- **Issue:** Compare the broad definition with narrower, phased, interpretive, and coordination alternatives.
  
  **Why it matters:** The proposal may have significant jurisdictional and market effects despite its clarification framing.
  
  **Ask the agency to:** Provide an alternatives matrix addressing certainty, transition, federalism, retail protection, competition, and enforceability.
- **Issue:** Pair classification certainty with safeguards and monitoring for retail risk.
  
  **Why it matters:** The notice warns that federal classification may be perceived as a safety signal and may increase loss-chasing or addictive-risk exposure.
  
  **Ask the agency to:** Explain disclosure, education, monitoring, and corrective mechanisms if participation increases.
