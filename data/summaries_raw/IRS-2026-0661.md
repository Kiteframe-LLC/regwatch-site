## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The later-of-date transition period is sufficient for both debt and effective-control rules.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A 90-day floor or first-taxable-year trigger is enough time for affected foreign governments to adapt their positions and documentation.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Entities with long settlement cycles, staggered taxable years, or complex portfolios may need more time or a different grandfathering structure.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask why this transition length is appropriate across different market structures and tax years.
- **Claim:** The binding commitment carveout will cleanly preserve pre-existing deals.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Affected parties can prove when a binding commitment was entered and can map it to the later-of-date test without dispute.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Close cases, staged investments, or amended commitments could generate classification disputes.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The notice should explain what documentary proof or safe harbor will govern close cases.
- **Claim:** Existing rules should continue to govern previously acquired interests until new controlling interests are acquired.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The distinction between legacy interests and new controlling interests is operationally clear.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Mixed holdings or incremental expansions could blur whether the old or new rule applies.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for examples showing how the rule applies to mixed and staged acquisitions.
- **Claim:** The NPRM is limited to applicability dates only.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Narrowing the rule to timing issues keeps the project focused and avoids reopening the substantive 2025 proposal prematurely.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A timing-only rule still creates substantive effects because it determines which holdings remain under the old regime.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask whether the agency should pair timing relief with clearer guidance or broader transition examples.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** Treasury and the IRS propose to withdraw the applicability dates in the 2025 section 892 proposed regulations and replace them with new transition dates for debt-acquisition rules and effective-control rules.

**Affected Entities**
- foreign governments
- international organizations
- foreign-government-controlled entities
- investment advisers and tax counsel handling cross-border portfolios
- market participants relying on binding commitments or staged acquisitions

**Statutory Authority:** Internal Revenue Code sections 892(c) and 7805(a).

**Key Regulatory Change:** The new rule would preserve pre-applicability treatment for existing holdings and for holdings acquired pursuant to pre-applicability binding commitments, while moving the new rules to the later of 90 days after publication of the final rule or the start of the first taxable year after publication.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice withdraws proposed §§ 1.892-4(d) and 1.892-5(e) from the 2025 proposal.
- The notice proposes new applicability dates for debt acquisitions and effective control.
- The notice says the Treasury Department and the IRS do not intend the 2025 proposed regulations to apply retroactively to existing holdings.

**Inference:** This is a transition-and-fair-notice rule designed to preserve legacy treatment while the substantive section 892 project continues in the next phase.
