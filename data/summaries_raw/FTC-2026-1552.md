## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Risk:** Overbroad conduct definition
  
  **Mechanism:** A future requirement could reach ordinary indexing, ranking, hosting, or lawful ad placement without a platform-specific contribution to deception.
  
  **Indicator:** The notice moves between ad optimization, non-advertising posts, curation, and other platform functions.
  
  **Mitigation:** Use a conduct taxonomy and require knowledge, participation, material contribution, or another articulated threshold.
- **Risk:** False positives and collateral removal
  
  **Mechanism:** Automated screening or complaint-triggered action could block legitimate businesses, government communications, or lawful speech.
  
  **Indicator:** The notice contemplates verification, monitoring, investigation, takedown, and discipline but does not specify appeal safeguards.
  
  **Mitigation:** Require notice, reason codes, human review where appropriate, restoration paths, and error-rate reporting.
- **Risk:** Data and market concentration burden
  
  **Mechanism:** Compliance costs or privileged access to identity and fraud data could disadvantage small businesses and smaller platforms.
  
  **Indicator:** The notice asks about small businesses but does not provide a common burden model or data alternative.
  
  **Mitigation:** Evaluate pass-through costs, phased coverage, interoperable standards, and data-minimizing options.
- **Risk:** Weak causal evaluation
  
  **Mechanism:** General scam-loss statistics could be used to justify duties without testing whether the specified platform practice caused or materially contributed to the harm.
  
  **Indicator:** The notice cites broad prevalence evidence and asks commenters to supply platform-specific evidence.
  
  **Mitigation:** Require platform-practice-level denominators, counterfactuals, and transparent coding of reported incidents.
- **Risk:** Safe-harbor gaming or underdeterrence
  
  **Mechanism:** A checklist safe harbor could encourage formal compliance while leaving high-risk practices unchanged, or become so demanding that it functions as a de facto prohibition.
  
  **Indicator:** The notice considers a safe harbor but does not specify performance outcomes or audit requirements.
  
  **Mitigation:** Tie safe-harbor protection to measurable outcomes, recordkeeping, testing, and periodic review rather than paperwork alone.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Observed Facts**
- The notice frames platform ad revenue and scam externalities as a central incentive problem.
- The notice considers monetary sanctions, prevention duties, complaint response, removal, discipline, recordkeeping, and safe harbors.
- The notice also asks about effects on legitimate businesses, consumers, platform innovation, and small-business advertisers.

**Likely Incentive Effects**
- Monetary exposure could increase investment in screening and reduce tolerance for high-risk advertisers.
- Strict liability or ambiguous duties could encourage over-removal, reduced access for new advertisers, or migration toward less transparent channels.
- A well-designed safe harbor could reward auditable prevention and response, but a checklist-only safe harbor could reward process over outcomes.
- Information-sharing requirements could improve detection while increasing privacy, competition, and error-correction risks.

**Comment Leverage:** Ask the FTC to model both intended and avoidance incentives, including false positives, advertiser exit, platform consolidation, and reporting transparency.
