## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Hearing loss claims constitute a significant portion of LHWCA claims.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The claim volume and case mix justify revisiting testing standards across the program.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The statement is true only for a narrow subset of claims, or the relevant cases have characteristics that differ from the population on which objective tests were studied.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** OWCP should provide or solicit claim-volume, case-mix, and outcome data by testing method and identify how representative the evidence is.
- **Claim:** Objective tests may offer more reliable evidence because they do not rely on subjective patient responses.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Physiological response measures correspond closely enough to functional hearing loss and impairment for LHWCA purposes.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A test reduces response bias but measures a different aspect of hearing, has frequency or condition limitations, or produces clinically meaningful disagreement with audiograms.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The record should distinguish response validity from functional impairment validity and identify acceptable disagreement ranges.
- **Claim:** Current regulations can be updated to consider objective tests within the statutory framework.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Objective results can be used as supporting, rebuttal, or primary evidence without displacing the statutory conditions for an audiogram's presumption.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A future standard gives objective tests weight that effectively changes the statutory presumption without clear authority or fair notice.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** OWCP should ask for concrete models of how conflicts would be resolved while preserving the statutory allocation of evidentiary weight.
- **Claim:** Updated standards can be administered fairly across practice settings.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Qualified personnel, calibrated equipment, and interpretive expertise are available at reasonable cost throughout the LHWCA claimant population.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Objective testing is concentrated in larger or urban practices, adds travel or delay, or creates cost barriers for small providers and claimants.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** OWCP should collect geographic availability, provider-capacity, wait-time, and cost evidence and consider alternatives for low-access settings.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** This is a Request for Information, not a proposed regulatory text package. OWCP is asking whether and how current LHWCA hearing-loss testing standards should be updated to account for objective tests such as ABR, ASSR, and OAE while retaining accurate and fair impairment determinations.

**Affected Entities**
- Employees and claimants with LHWCA occupational hearing-loss claims
- Employers, insurance carriers, and other parties involved in LHWCA claims
- Audiologists, otolaryngologists, medical professionals, and medical associations
- Trade associations and other commenters with clinical, operational, or economic evidence
- Small medical practices and providers that may administer or interpret hearing tests
- OWCP adjudicators and other decision-makers applying hearing-loss evidence

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites the LHWCA, 33 U.S.C. 901-950, including section 8(c)(13), 33 U.S.C. 908(c)(13), and identifies implementing regulation 20 CFR 702.441.

**Key Regulatory Change:** No immediate regulatory change is proposed in the selected notice. The inquiry could inform later changes to test administration standards, the evidentiary weight of objective tests, impairment calculations, and hearing-aid compensation questions.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice asks 28 questions across reliability and validity, clinical standards, quality control, weighting of tests, impairment calculation, economic feasibility, and OTC hearing aids.
- The notice specifically asks for peer-reviewed studies, standards, access and cost data, examples, and information about small-entity impacts.
- The notice states that qualifying audiograms have statutory presumptive status when specified conditions are met.

**Inference:** The strongest public contribution is likely to be evidence that separates clinical test validity, functional hearing loss, legal evidentiary weight, and feasible administration rather than treating objective testing as a single substitute for audiometry.

**Evidence Quotes**
- This request for information seeks the public's input on the comparative reliability and validity of audiograms versus objective tests.
- OWCP's goal is to adopt regulations that reflect current medical technology and practice while ensuring accurate, reliable, and fair determinations of hearing loss.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Construct validity mismatch
  
  **Mechanism:** A physiological response test is treated as a direct measure of functional hearing loss or AMA impairment without adequate correlation evidence.
  
  **Possible Effect:** The eventual rule could give weight to evidence that does not answer the legal impairment question.
  
  **Mitigation For Record:** Request method-specific studies, normal variation ranges, and explicit distinction between detection, threshold estimation, and impairment calculation.
- **Failure Mode:** Conflict-resolution ambiguity
  
  **Mechanism:** A later rule authorizes multiple tests but does not explain which result controls or how the statutory audiogram presumption is preserved.
  
  **Possible Effect:** Similar claims could receive inconsistent treatment and create avoidable disputes.
  
  **Mitigation For Record:** Ask for decision trees, triggers, documentation requirements, and examples involving conflicting results.
- **Failure Mode:** Access and delay burden
  
  **Mechanism:** Objective testing requires scarce equipment, specialized personnel, or travel that is not available to all claimants and providers.
  
  **Possible Effect:** Testing costs or claim processing time could rise unevenly.
  
  **Mitigation For Record:** Collect geographic capacity, wait-time, per-test, travel, and small-provider evidence.
- **Failure Mode:** False confidence in anti-bias benefits
  
  **Mechanism:** Reduced reliance on patient responses is treated as eliminating all validity problems.
  
  **Possible Effect:** Technical artifacts, interpretation error, or method limitations may be overlooked.
  
  **Mitigation For Record:** Request evidence on artifacts, operator qualifications, interpretation reliability, and false-positive and false-negative rates.
