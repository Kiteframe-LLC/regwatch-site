## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Failure Modes**
- Data-timing failure: a late certification, exceptional-event decision, or monitor correction changes an area's result after meaningful public review has closed.
- Boundary drift: a county or nearby contributing area is included or excluded without a clear, reproducible explanation.
- Record fragmentation: the area-level rationale is split across letters, datasets, website pages, and technical documents in a way that prevents commenters from tracing the decision.
- Tribal-process mismatch: a Tribe affected by a potential surrounding-area designation receives less usable information or time than is needed to evaluate the separate-area treatment.
- Classification ambiguity: the attainment/unclassifiable label is treated as equivalent for immediate burdens but creates confusion about what EPA found and how later planning or redesignation decisions should interpret it.

**Not Observed**
- No evidence of a correction, reopening, or extension notice.
- No evidence that the basic statutory designation authority is being replaced by another authority.
- No basis to infer bad faith or intentional use of ambiguity from the notice alone.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

The notice creates incentives for states, Tribes, local governments, and regulated parties to submit current monitor, exceptional-event, boundary, and contribution evidence quickly. It may also incentivize strategic emphasis on data treatments that move an area across the 9.0 threshold or alter the scope of a nonattainment area. EPA can reduce that incentive by publishing the same data dictionary, exclusion rules, sensitivity checks, and area-level decision factors for all jurisdictions. The court deadline may also favor administratively simple classifications; a transparent change log would help ensure speed does not displace traceability.

## Step 13 — Litigation Vulnerabilities

Potential vulnerabilities are chiefly record and explanation issues: a final designation could be challenged if EPA relies on 2023-2025 data without showing the decisive data treatment, changes an initial recommendation without a clear area-level explanation, or leaves the public unable to reconstruct boundary and nearby-contributor reasoning. The notice has stronger support for statutory authority, the existence of a 120-day mechanism, and the use of Appendix N design-value procedures. A constructive comment should request a consolidated, reproducible decision record rather than assume that dispersed docket materials are legally insufficient.

## Step 13A — Structural Integrity / Nonsense Detector

The proposal is structurally coherent at a high level: the revised standard creates a designation duty, monitoring data supplies the threshold, state and Tribal submissions provide context, and 120-day notifications precede final action. The material structural weakness is a traceability loop: EPA asks for public input on intended area decisions while placing the decisive area-level evidence across individual letters, datasets, and technical materials. The constructive fix is a consolidated designation table, data dictionary, change log, area-boundary rationale, and explicit treatment of exceptional events and Tribal options.
