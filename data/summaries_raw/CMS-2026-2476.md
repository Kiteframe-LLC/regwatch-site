## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The new threshold formula can be applied consistently across states and permissible classes.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** State tax collections, net patient revenue, and enactment/imposition dates can be aligned without major ambiguity.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** States report data on different schedules or with different interpretive assumptions, producing inconsistent threshold calculations.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask CMS to publish validation rules, correction procedures, and examples that show how edge cases will be handled.
- **Claim:** A zero threshold for classes with no qualifying tax on July 4, 2025, is administrable and fair.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The enactment/imposition cutoff cleanly separates existing from nonexisting tax programs.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A tax structure was amended, delayed, or partially implemented around the cutoff date and the line is harder to apply than the rule text suggests.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for examples and transition rules for late amendments, waiver timing, and local tax variation.
- **Claim:** Interim thresholds are a workable substitute while final data are pending.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Available reported data are accurate enough to guide the interim compliance period.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Interim thresholds are wrong because the reported data are incomplete or later revised, which can distort state compliance decisions.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** CMS should explain how interim errors will be corrected and whether states get relief if the final threshold differs materially.
- **Claim:** Sunsetting the secondary prong is enough to make the July 4, 2025 threshold the maximum permissible level.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The new formula captures the relevant hold-harmless risk without needing the older 75/75 backstop.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A tax arrangement could still produce a hold-harmless effect even if it falls below the new threshold, but the rule no longer tests that condition the same way.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask why CMS is comfortable removing the second prong entirely instead of keeping it as a backstop.
- **Claim:** The new permissible class improves oversight rather than creating a new boundary problem.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The added class is defined tightly enough that states and CMS can apply it consistently.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The new class invites boundary disputes or gaming around what counts as the permissible class or the denominator used in the threshold calculation.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** CMS should explain the class definition, why it is needed, and how it will be administered.
