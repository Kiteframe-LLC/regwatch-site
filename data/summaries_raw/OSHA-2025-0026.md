## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_insufficient_evidence
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A record that is weak on affirmative proof can still justify a categorical exemption rather than a narrower refinement.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the evidence is merely incomplete rather than affirmatively supportive, the better response may be to narrow or condition the exemption instead of removing screening altogether.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask OSHA to separate 'lack of proof of benefit' from 'proof that removing the requirement is safe.'
- **Claim Id:** claim_no_adverse_outcomes
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** If material harm were occurring, it would have surfaced in accessible COVID-era reporting or adverse-event channels.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Underreporting, incomplete surveillance, or limited visibility into nontraditional worksites could hide precisely the harms that the screening rule is supposed to prevent.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask OSHA to identify the datasets it actually checked and what those datasets can and cannot detect.
- **Claim Id:** claim_cost_savings
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The 2001 survey baseline and the later employment extrapolation still track current respirator use well enough to support the savings estimate.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If respirator use patterns changed after COVID or the compliance baseline is stale, the savings estimate could overstate the real effect of the carveout.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask OSHA for updated prevalence and sensitivity ranges.
- **Claim Id:** claim_align_standard
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Similarity to the Respiratory Protection standard is enough to justify streamlining the Formaldehyde standard's medical-evaluation requirement.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The line between respirator categories may not map cleanly onto actual worker risk, especially where work intensity, heat, or preexisting conditions dominate the health picture.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask why product type, rather than use conditions or medical risk, is the best administrative boundary.
- **Claim Id:** claim_acsch_consultation
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Reopening the record after ACCSH review is enough procedural response even if the notice itself does not summarize the committee's substantive criticism in detail.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the committee's objections were material, the final rule could be vulnerable if OSHA fails to explain why it did not adopt the committee's caution or best-practices approach.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask OSHA to squarely respond to ACCSH's recommendation and identify what changed, if anything, after the consultation.
