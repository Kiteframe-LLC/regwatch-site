## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim: DMM changes implement price adjustments
- Underlying assumption: The DMM text changes are necessary and appropriately matched to the PRC price adjustment filing.
- Failure scenario: A mailer cannot determine how a changed category, threshold, or discount maps to the price filing without consulting PRC materials not included in this docket.
- Procedural implication: Commenters can ask USPS to crosswalk the DMM amendments against the PRC price categories and effective dates.

### Claim: ADC/AADC removal simplifies market dominant mail classes
- Underlying assumption: Replacing ADC/AADC categories with 3-Digit or Mixed categories preserves workable preparation and pricing paths.
- Failure scenario: Mailers with software, documentation, or operational flows keyed to ADC/AADC categories experience conversion errors or unexpected price effects.
- Procedural implication: USPS should explain transition handling, software/documentation impacts, and whether mailers need implementation guidance before July 12, 2026.

### Claim: Periodicals restructuring should resemble Marketing Mail
- Underlying assumption: Periodicals mail can use a Marketing Mail-like structure without material service, publisher, or preparation problems.
- Failure scenario: Periodicals mailers face different economics or operational constraints than Marketing Mail mailers, especially after eliminating bundle and container rates.
- Procedural implication: A strong comment could request examples showing how representative Periodicals mailings would change under the new structure.

### Claim: Marketing Mail weight increases are operationally acceptable
- Underlying assumption: Automation, nonautomation, and carrier route flat operations can handle 20-ounce and 24-ounce thresholds without service or processing degradation.
- Failure scenario: Heavier flats affect tray handling, equipment compatibility, deflection, or delivery ergonomics differently across facilities.
- Procedural implication: USPS should describe operational evidence for the 20-ounce and 24-ounce thresholds and any safeguards.

### Claim: HPM is a coherent new category
- Underlying assumption: The 15-pound HPM category for non-fulfillment pieces is clearly distinguishable from other USPS Marketing Mail parcels and Package Services products.
- Failure scenario: Mailers misclassify parcels, or eligibility disputes arise around "non-fulfillment" pieces.
- Procedural implication: Commenters can ask for examples, boundary definitions, and transition instructions.

### Claim: Market Dominant parcel barcode fee can mirror Competitive parcel fee
- Underlying assumption: Barcode quality failures and compliance incentives are similar enough across Market Dominant and Competitive parcels.
- Failure scenario: Market Dominant parcels have different preparation patterns, mailer profiles, or data-quality constraints that make a mirrored fee over- or under-inclusive.
- Procedural implication: USPS should identify the barcode criteria, measurement process, notice/cure process, and basis for excluding Periodicals parcels and HPM.

### Claim: Eliminating bundling aligns with current processing operations
- Underlying assumption: Current processing operations no longer need bundled Marketing Mail flats in flat trays, and mailers can adapt without undue disruption.
- Failure scenario: Some mailers use bundling to manage quality control, co-mailing, transport, or documentation workflows.
- Procedural implication: A constructive comment could request a transition period, exception, or operational explanation for affected preparation methods.
