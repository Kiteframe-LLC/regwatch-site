## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** C1
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Institutions can distinguish illicit-finance concerns from document nationality and broader credit risks.
  
  **Negation Test:** If risk judgments lack boundaries, the asserted tailoring does not reliably limit collection.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** A lawfully present owner supplies only a permitted foreign passport; policies differ across institutions.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** A bank could read supervisory concerns broadly and seek status documents from an entire customer category.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for an objective nexus, documented reasons, and examples distinguishing permitted identification from independent risk evidence.
- **Claim Id:** C2
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Affected owners can obtain qualifying alternate documents without material delay.
  
  **Negation Test:** If lawful owners lack timely alternatives, the low-burden justification weakens.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** Expired, inaccessible, or delayed replacement documents make theoretical availability insufficient.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Institutions may treat the ITIN restriction as a categorical exclusion of customers rather than an identification-number rule.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Compare ITIN plus corroboration with exclusion and specify treatment of existing customers.
- **Claim Id:** C3
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Existing relief is the correct current-law baseline and adoption is sufficiently understood.
  
  **Negation Test:** If many institutions have not adopted relief, savings and transition costs differ.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** Separate banks with centralized customer files from smaller firms dependent on vendors.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Net savings could conceal distinct costs of the identification and status-information changes.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Provide both baselines and a component-level, institution-size sensitivity analysis.
- **Claim Id:** C5
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Permissive status inquiries do not materially affect lawful access to business accounts.
  
  **Negation Test:** If false flags cause delays or refusals, the access prediction needs revision.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** A passive owner who is not a U.S. employee may be asked for employment authorization.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Risk-averse institutions may over-collect or exit relationships to minimize examination uncertainty.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Measure delays, abandonment, corrections, and account refusals rather than inferring no impact from permissive wording.
- **Claim Id:** C7
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Existing recordkeeping language reliably captures oral confirmations.
  
  **Negation Test:** If confirmations are not reconstructable, later verification and examinations lose their audit trail.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** A call-center confirmation is stored separately from the original certification.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** An institution may retain only a checkmark without who confirmed what and when.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Clarify minimum record content and linkage; this is a solicited implementation question, not proof records are being abolished.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

- FinCEN proposes to codify relief from repeating beneficial-owner identification at every new account. Checks would occur at the first account, when facts call prior information into question, and when risk-based ongoing diligence requires them.
- It would specify identifying information in the rule, exclude ITINs as qualifying numbers, permit third-party sources subject to customer certification, and allow oral or written confirmation of previously certified information.
- Institutions could request additional information, including immigration or employment status, when justified by risk indicators or supervisory concerns. This is not a universal immigration-document requirement or a blanket account ban.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Observed Fact:** The rule separates minimum CDD identifiers from CIP verification procedures and preserves reasonable-and-practicable verification. Third-party sourcing still requires customer certification; oral confirmation is not permission to stop keeping records. No engineering model is involved.

**Material coherence question:** The preamble links status inquiries to necessary information resolving material concerns, while proposed paragraph (E) uses “useful” and “including but not limited to.” Request text that preserves the stated limits and distinguishes status from identity verification. Foreign passports are expressly acceptable for non-U.S. persons even though the preamble also suggests foreign documents may prompt inquiry; clarify when an additional independent fact is required.

**Implementation details:** Specify how certification and correction work for third-party-sourced status data; distinguish initial certification, risk-based reconfirmation under (i)(C), and a reliability-triggered fresh check under (i)(B). Explain whether existing ITIN-based files require immediate replacement or only updating at a later trigger. In IX.A.2.b.ii, 5,465,250 annual responses conflicts with 5,457,750; footnote 49 supports the latter (14,554 × 1.5 × 250). This small numerical discrepancy warrants correction, not a claim that the entire analysis fails.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Scenario:** A lawful owner cannot promptly replace an ITIN with a qualifying document.
  
  **Consequence:** Business-account opening or updating is delayed even if identity could be corroborated.
  
  **Mitigation:** Evidence-based alternative verification and clear transition treatment.
- **Scenario:** An institution uses a foreign passport or a vague supervisory concern as a broad proxy.
  
  **Consequence:** Disproportionate status requests and mistaken customer exit.
  
  **Mitigation:** Documented risk nexus, neutral triggers, escalation and correction procedures.
- **Scenario:** Third-party data conflict with customer certification.
  
  **Consequence:** Stale or incorrect records circulate across compliance workflows.
  
  **Mitigation:** Source provenance, reconciliation, correction ownership, and retained confirmation records.
- **Scenario:** Frequent account openings stop triggering routine refresh without effective ongoing monitoring.
  
  **Consequence:** Beneficial-owner changes are detected late.
  
  **Mitigation:** Examples for continuing accuracy and auditable risk-based update triggers; retain existing monitoring duties.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Beneficial:** Timing relief can reward reusable customer files and direct effort toward genuine risk.

**Adverse:** Uncertain supervisory thresholds can encourage defensive over-collection or account exit. Weak update controls can also let customers keep stale ownership information longer. These are plausible mechanisms, not observed effects.

**Limits:** The ownership threshold is not changed, so a new threshold-gaming mechanism is not established. No new universal status check, automatic SAR, or denial requirement is created.

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength
