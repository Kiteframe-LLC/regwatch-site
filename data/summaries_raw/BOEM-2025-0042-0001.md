## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Major claim: lowering the rating threshold from BBB− to BB− remains adequately protective

**Underlying assumption**

One-year default probabilities for BB-rated firms, combined with annual review authority and joint-and-several liability, are a sufficient proxy for decommissioning risk on long-lived offshore assets.

**Failure scenario**

If offshore default and asset-transfer risk is more tail-heavy than the annual rating tables suggest, BOEM may understate the chance that decommissioning liabilities migrate to weaker operators before BOEM can react.

**Procedural implication**

Commenters can ask BOEM to justify why a one-year corporate default metric is the right fit for multi-year decommissioning liabilities and whether stress scenarios would materially change the result.

### Major claim: predecessor credit strength materially reduces the need for supplemental assurance

**Underlying assumption**

Joint-and-several liability plus financially strong predecessors translates into real, collectible backstop protection in practice.

**Failure scenario**

If predecessor liability is legally complex, fact-specific, or slower to realize than BOEM assumes, then counting predecessor strength as a basis to waive present bonding may over-credit protection that is harder to collect than a surety instrument.

**Procedural implication**

The agency may need a clearer explanation of historical predecessor recoveries, collection timing, or legal constraints, not just a general statement that predecessor liability exists.

### Major claim: moving from P70 to P50 is an acceptable risk adjustment

**Underlying assumption**

The lower probabilistic estimate still leaves sufficient protection when combined with other safeguards.

**Failure scenario**

If actual decommissioning costs cluster in the upper tail, shifting from P70 to P50 could reduce coverage faster than BOEM’s expected-value framing suggests.

**Procedural implication**

This is a classic line-drawing issue. Commenters can ask why P50, rather than P60 or retaining P70, best balances burden reduction against public-loss risk.

### Major claim: removing the appeal-bond stay condition improves fairness without creating collection problems

**Underlying assumption**

BOEM can preserve enforcement leverage without requiring a bond equal to the demand during IBLA appeals.

**Failure scenario**

Appeals could delay realization of security while the financial condition of the lessee worsens.

**Procedural implication**

The record should explain why the old stay condition was too burdensome and what mechanism will prevent strategic delay under the new approach.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

BOEM proposes to revise its 2024 financial-assurance rule for OCS oil, gas, sulfur, RUE, and pipeline ROW obligations. The proposal would: lower the credit threshold from investment-grade `BBB−/Baa3` to `BB−/Ba3`; count predecessor financial strength; shift the decommissioning-cost probability level from `P70` to `P50`; explicitly allow dual-obligee instruments; add an alternative short-term decommissioning path; and remove the requirement to post an appeal bond equal to the demand to stay a supplemental-assurance demand during IBLA review. The RIA states that the 2024 baseline would otherwise hold roughly `$7.02 billion` in financial assurance and cost about `$566.65 million` in annual premiums, while the proposal would reduce burdens by about `$6.2 billion`.

### Inference

This is a meaningful deregulatory revision to a recently finalized risk-management regime. The strongest comment opportunities concern whether BOEM has chosen the right risk proxies and whether expected-value framing understates tail risk to the public.

## Step 3 — Agency Argument Tree

**Regulatory objective**
Reduce what BOEM views as over-burdensome supplemental financial assurance while preserving adequate protection for decommissioning obligations.

**Problem definition**
The 2024 final rule allegedly imposed excessive financial burden under current market conditions and constrained capital available for offshore operations.

**Data used**
Credit-rating default tables, decommissioning-liability estimates, BSEE probabilistic cost estimates, premium estimates, reserve-value ratios, and prior public comments on the 2023 NPRM.

**Analytical method**
Compare the proposal against full implementation of the 2024 rule; quantify savings in reduced bonding/premiums; estimate increased taxpayer-risk exposure using expected consequence calculations tied to one-year default rates.

**Alternatives considered**
The RIA appears to compare the 2024 baseline with the proposed package and some subcomponents, but the visible record is much thinner on intermediate options such as narrower threshold adjustments or partial retention of the appeal-bond stay.

**Agency conclusion**
The proposed package materially lowers burden while keeping risk at an acceptable level because predecessor liability, lower-tier credit ratings, reserve ratios, and annual review reduce the need for the 2024 level of bonding.

### Missing links / unsupported premises / logical jumps

The main jumps are:

1. from annual corporate default tables to long-horizon decommissioning risk,
2. from legal predecessor liability to practical collectability,
3. from a quantified industry-savings estimate to a conclusion that public-loss exposure remains acceptably low,
4. from dissatisfaction with the 2024 rule to the specific choice of `BB−` and `P50` rather than narrower alternatives.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

### Regulatory objective: reduce burden while preserving adequate decommissioning security

**Alternatives evaluated**

Full implementation of the 2024 final rule versus the proposed deregulatory package and its major components.

**Plausible alternatives not analyzed**

Use an intermediate credit threshold above `BB−`.
Use a middle probability value between `P70` and `P50`.
Count predecessor strength only when BOEM documents recent financial health plus a collection pathway.
Retain a reduced or tailored appeal-bond stay requirement instead of eliminating it entirely.
Phase in relief for categories where BOEM has stronger evidence that risk is overstated.

**Evidence suggesting feasibility**

The rule itself identifies several independent moving pieces, which suggests BOEM could have mixed and matched narrower options rather than combining multiple deregulatory changes at once.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Observed Fact**

The proposal is technically coherent in the sense that the RIA uses a consistent framework: baseline liability, rating-based risk, premium estimates, and probabilistic decommissioning costs. It also ties the chosen one-year default horizon to BOEM’s annual financial reviews.

**Inference**

The weak point is not internal contradiction. It is whether the chosen proxies are too stylized for the real risk profile of offshore decommissioning obligations, which can unfold over years, involve distressed asset transfers, and create collection challenges that expected-value tables may not capture well.

**Unknown**

The available record does not show a direct historical comparison between BOEM’s modeled expected losses and actual realized recovery experience when weaker operators fail.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

BOEM may underweight tail risk by using one-year default rates as the core measure for liabilities that may mature much later.

Predecessor strength may be easier to count on paper than to convert into timely recovery when a current lessee fails.

The combined effect of lowering the rating threshold, lowering the probabilistic cost level, and removing the appeal-bond stay may stack risk faster than each change appears to do in isolation.

Smaller or weaker operators may benefit from lower bonding in the short term but leave stronger co-liable parties or the public exposed if asset quality deteriorates.

---

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Observed Fact**

The rule is designed to free capital and reduce premium costs for operators that otherwise would post supplemental assurance.

**Inference**

That creates an incentive to structure holdings and transfers so current lessees can rely on predecessor strength or just-clearer credit thresholds, potentially reducing near-term cash commitments without necessarily improving the collectability of decommissioning obligations.

This is not necessarily evasion, but it is an incentive effect BOEM should confront directly.

---

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

**Overall assessment:** Moderate

**Strengths**

The rule has a real RIA, a stated baseline, quantified savings, and an articulated method.

**Weaknesses**

The most contestable parts of the analysis are:

* the fit of one-year default rates to long-duration liabilities,
* the practical treatment of predecessor liability,
* the specific choice of `BB−` and `P50`,
* the cumulative effect of several deregulatory changes taken together,
* the comparatively compressed discussion of downside public-risk scenarios.

---

## Step 12 — Administrative Record Gaps

1. **Time-horizon gap**
   A clearer explanation of why annual default rates are the right metric for liabilities that may persist far beyond one year.

2. **Predecessor-recovery gap**
   Evidence on how predecessor liability has worked in practice, including recoverability and timing.

3. **Threshold-selection gap**
   A more explicit comparison of `BB−` and `P50` against plausible intermediate choices.

4. **Cumulative-risk gap**
   More transparent treatment of the combined effect of lowering the rating threshold, lowering the P-value, and easing appeal-stay conditions at the same time.

5. **Public-exposure framing gap**
   More direct explanation of how taxpayer exposure is bounded if assumptions about defaults, transfers, or cost overruns prove optimistic.

---

## Step 13 — Litigation Vulnerabilities

The likely vulnerability theme is not ultra vires action. It is underexplained line-drawing and risk balancing.

Potential pressure points:

* whether `BB−` is sufficiently explained as the right cutoff rather than simply a deregulatory preference,
* whether `P50` is justified as protective enough for public liabilities,
* whether predecessor liability is treated too abstractly,
* whether the appeal-bond change weakens enforcement leverage without adequate discussion,
* whether expected-value analysis masks material downside scenarios.

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

### Leverage Point 1: One-year default rates as the core risk metric

**Key NPRM sentences**
“BOEM uses the default rates to estimate the expected consequence of provisions analyzed in this RIA.”
“For this analysis, BOEM finds that 1-year default rates are an appropriate measure of risk….”

**Routine description**
BOEM is using annual corporate default rates as the main quantitative bridge from credit category to expected decommissioning-loss risk.

**Regulatory effectiveness / statutory compliance / procedural adequacy**
This is mainly procedural adequacy and analytical fit.

**Real-world consequence**
If the time horizon is too short, the final rule could understate risk from distressed transfers and delayed decommissioning exposure.

**Worth a comment?** High

### Leverage Point 2: The `BB−` threshold and predecessor-credit change

**Key NPRM sentence**
The proposal would lower the threshold to `BB−/Ba3` and allow predecessor strength to exempt current lessees from supplemental assurance.

**Routine description**
BOEM is broadening the set of operators who can avoid supplemental bonding and increasing reliance on predecessor balance-sheet strength.

**Regulatory effectiveness / statutory compliance / procedural adequacy**
This raises line-drawing and practical-risk questions more than pure legal-authority questions.

**Real-world consequence**
Current lessees may post less security upfront even where practical recovery against predecessors is slower or more uncertain than a bond.

**Worth a comment?** High

### Leverage Point 3: The move from `P70` to `P50`

**Key NPRM sentence**
“The Department is proposing to revise the probabilistic value used for determining the amount of supplemental financial assurance required from P70 to P50.”

**Routine description**
This directly lowers the probability-based amount of coverage BOEM would require.

**Regulatory effectiveness / statutory compliance / procedural adequacy**
This is a classic threshold-justification issue.

**Real-world consequence**
If decommissioning costs run high, BOEM may hold materially less security than under the 2024 rule.

**Worth a comment?** High

### Leverage Point 4: Removal of the appeal-bond stay requirement

**Key NPRM sentence**
The proposal would remove the requirement to provide an appeal bond equal to the demand to stay the demand during IBLA review.

**Routine description**
BOEM is reducing the security required while a disputed supplemental-assurance order is under appeal.

**Regulatory effectiveness / statutory compliance / procedural adequacy**
This is partly fairness, partly enforcement design.

**Real-world consequence**
Commenters can ask whether the new process preserves BOEM’s leverage if a lessee’s financial condition deteriorates during appeal.

**Worth a comment?** Moderate to high

## Bottom line

This NPRM is not weak because it lacks an analytic record. It is vulnerable because it uses a visible but contestable analytic framework to support several meaningful deregulatory choices at once. The most productive comments will likely ask BOEM to show more work on time horizon, threshold selection, predecessor recoverability, combined-risk effects, and enforcement consequences of easing the appeal-stay rule.
