## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The current SNUR definitions are broad enough to capture some activities EPA says should not be treated as source construction.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** EPA can separate non-emitting work from regulated source construction without losing the permit gate.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the boundary is unclear, entities may sequence real construction as non-emitting work and start before review is complete.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA for tighter examples and a more objective carveout list.
- **Claim:** The proposal will reduce delays and burdens in preconstruction planning.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The affected projects are common enough that the burden is more than anecdotal.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the problem affects only a narrow set of cases, the rule may be broader than necessary.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for incidence and timing data supporting the burden claim.
- **Claim:** The 90-day notice requirement can function as a clean review gate.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** EPA can review the SNUN before construction starts and still preserve later emissions controls.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If construction begins in practice before the review window closes, the gate loses force.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for timing safeguards and examples of how the gate works for different projects.
- **Claim:** The same carveout can be applied across SIP, PSD, NNSR, and Tribal NSR programs.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Delegated permitting authorities can apply the same line-drawing rules consistently.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Different programs may interpret the carveout differently and create uneven enforcement.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for model permit language and implementation examples.
- **Claim:** EPA can rely on prior TSCA Orders and docketed analyses to support the proposal.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The docketed support is robust enough to carry the preamble's conclusions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the docketed support is thin or not easy to reconstruct, the record may not show the reasoning clearly.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to identify the decisive docket materials and summarize the support in the preamble.
- **Claim:** EPA can handle edge cases through existing TSCA procedures and case-by-case review.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Permit authorities will know when a preparatory activity crosses into regulated construction.
