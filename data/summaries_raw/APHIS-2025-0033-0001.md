## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Foreign certification and testing can demonstrate freedom from Plan disease infection.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The three import conditions and two-of-three laboratory conditions are comparable across countries and disease programs.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A certificate covers some Plan diseases but not the classifications used by the receiving hatchery, while a laboratory meets formal credentials without validated performance for the relevant assay.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request disease-by-disease criteria, validation evidence, and a decision record for equivalency determinations.
- **Claim:** Remote inspections increase biosecurity and efficiency.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Real-time audio/video access gives the OSA an adequate view of premises, records, and flock conditions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Connectivity, camera placement, blind spots, or inability to verify identity and records prevents detection of sanitation or segregation failures.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for inspection protocols, exclusion criteria, documentation requirements, and an in-person fallback.
- **Claim:** Simplifying Newcastle disease clean requirements removes an unnecessary barrier.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The deleted vaccination and monitoring provisions are no longer needed because virulent Newcastle disease is reportable and the United States is free of the disease.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Reduced program requirements weaken early detection or assurances for the linked H5/H7 AI compartment program.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request surveillance evidence and a comparison of current and proposed detection safeguards.
- **Claim:** An OSA-and-Service-approved surveillance program is a workable alternative to uniform testing for exhibition poultry.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Approval standards will be sufficiently specific and consistently applied across States and species.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Different OSAs approve materially different programs, leaving exhibitors uncertain about eligibility and creating uneven disease-control protection.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request minimum elements, approval timelines, public availability, and appeal or correction procedures.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** Amend NPIP rules in 9 CFR parts 145, 146, and 147 to update definitions, clarify foreign-product equivalency, authorize a remote-inspection concept, simplify Newcastle disease clean classifications, create a surveillance alternative for exhibition poultry, correct cross-references and terminology, and update slaughter-plant and NPIP Standards language.

**Affected Entities**
- NPIP participants and hatcheries
- primary breeders and importers of poultry or hatching eggs
- Official State Agencies and APHIS inspectors
- foreign exporting flocks and laboratories
- exhibition, exotic, game-bird, and waterfowl operators
- commercial slaughter plants and poultry producers
- diagnostic laboratories and poultry veterinarians

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites 7 U.S.C. 8301-8317 and 7 CFR 2.22, 2.80, and 371.4.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The most consequential changes are a new §145.4(f) pathway for foreign nonparticipant products, a defined remote inspection, and several program-classification changes that shift detail to OSA/AP HIS administration and NPIP standards.

**Core Tradeoff:** The proposal seeks operational flexibility and alignment with current industry practice while preserving disease-control assurances through certification, inspection, testing, reporting, and OSA/AP H​​IS approval.

**Observed Facts**
- The proposed foreign pathway requires APHIS import compliance, port inspection, additional testing where classifications are not covered, and at least two of three laboratory conditions.
- The proposed Newcastle provisions require hatchery sanitation, participation in an avian-influenza clean-compartment program, and reporting and examination for virulent Newcastle disease.

**Inference:** The proposal may improve supply-chain flexibility, but its practical safeguards will depend on standards and approval practices not fully specified in the available notice text.

**Evidence Quotes**
- For products from non-participant flocks located outside of the USA, importation may be permissible without the conditions listed under paragraph (d) ... only after freedom of infection from Plan diseases can be demonstrated.
- Remote inspections provide increased biosecurity and efficiency for both facilities and personnel.
