## Phase 1 — Docket Integrity Check

**Classification:** Full analytical spine present

**Observed Fact**

The selected document is the substantive proposal-stage document, not an extension, correction, reopening notice, or purely procedural notice. EPA describes the action as a "Notice of availability; request for comments," but the text proposes to approve Louisiana's partial coal combustion residuals (CCR) permit program under RCRA section 4005(d).

The available record includes the notice text and an ingested supporting-material spine. The required supporting documents identified in the export diagnostics were all ingested, with no missing required document IDs. The ingested materials include:

* `EPA-HQ-OLEM-2025-3324-0042` - EPA Technical Support Document for approval of Louisiana's CCR permit program.
* `EPA-HQ-OLEM-2025-3324-0045` - Louisiana solid waste regulations, October 2024.
* `EPA-HQ-OLEM-2025-3324-0015` - Louisiana regulations supporting the CCR program.
* `EPA-HQ-OLEM-2025-3324-0018` - 40 CFR part 257 checklist.
* `EPA-HQ-OLEM-2025-3324-0012` - CCR permit program application checklist.
* `EPA-HQ-OLEM-2025-3324-0041` - EPA's 2017 interim guidance for state CCR permit programs.
* `EPA-HQ-OLEM-2025-3324-0043` - Louisiana state-versus-federal requirements comparison.
* `EPA-HQ-OLEM-2025-3324-0017` - Guidance Manual for Environmental Boreholes and Monitoring Systems.

The notice states that EPA and LDEQ discussed state-federal differences including "uppermost aquifer" versus "uppermost permeable zone," groundwater protection standards, public participation requirements, and permitting procedures. EPA also identifies the federal provisions Louisiana is not seeking to replace, including legacy CCR surface impoundment requirements, CCR management unit requirements, several post-2020 amendments, and one Louisiana exclusion for CCR surface impoundments that no longer contain water or can no longer impound liquids.

**Inference**

This is not a thin record. The docket has the core documents one would expect for a state CCR program approval: state law/regulations, checklists, EPA technical support, and a crosswalk. The stronger comment opportunities are not about total absence of support. They are about whether the final approval record should better explain the partial-program boundary, the transition from federal self-implementation to Louisiana permits, the state groundwater terminology difference, and how communities can track major incident notification and overlapping federal/state authority.

**Why that matters**

The proposed approval would affect governance of CCR landfills and impoundments in Louisiana. It would not simply substitute one complete state rule for all federal requirements. EPA proposes a split system: approved state provisions would operate in lieu of corresponding federal provisions, while unapproved federal provisions would remain directly applicable.

## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Major claim: Louisiana's partial CCR permit program meets the approval standard

**Underlying assumption**

EPA's comparison of Louisiana law, regulations, and guidance accurately shows that the approved portion is identical to or at least as protective as the federal CCR requirements.

**Failure scenario**

If state-specific terminology or implementation discretion narrows groundwater protection, public participation, permit modification, or enforcement compared with federal expectations, the general approval conclusion may hide a practical protection gap.

**Procedural implication**

Comments can ask EPA to strengthen the final record on the state-specific differences that matter most, especially "uppermost permeable zone" and the excluded impoundment language.

### Major claim: the split federal/state system is manageable

**Underlying assumption**

Facilities, LDEQ, EPA, Tribes, and nearby residents can determine which requirements are state-permit requirements and which remain direct federal requirements.

**Failure scenario**

Legacy CCR surface impoundments, CCR management units, or facilities without Chapter 10 permits could fall into a confusing interim posture where the public cannot easily tell which regulator is responsible for a disputed obligation.

**Procedural implication**

EPA can be asked to include a clearer facility-facing applicability table in the final approval record or TSD.

### Major claim: no current Louisiana Chapter 10 CCR permits need to be reviewed for approval

**Underlying assumption**

Approving the framework without reviewing actual Chapter 10 permits is acceptable because federal CCR requirements continue to apply until permits issue under the approved state program.

**Failure scenario**

If the transition period is lengthy or permit applications are uneven in quality, communities may face a long period where state approval has been finalized but the practical permitting system remains incomplete.

**Procedural implication**

Commenters can ask EPA and LDEQ to describe expected timelines, prioritization, public notice steps, and status tracking for permit applications due within 365 days of approval.

### Major claim: tribal consultation is sufficient at the proposal stage

**Underlying assumption**

The informational session and opportunity for consultation satisfied EPA's consultation responsibilities, and the Jena Band request for notification of major incidents can be handled through program implementation.

**Failure scenario**

If major incident or plant failure notification expectations are not incorporated into a public, enforceable, or trackable mechanism, the consultation record may not translate into practical notice to affected Tribes.

**Procedural implication**

A constructive comment can ask EPA to explain in the final record how the Jena Band notification request will be handled in permits, state procedures, or EPA oversight.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

EPA proposes to approve Louisiana's partial CCR permit program under RCRA section 4005(d). Louisiana submitted its final application on March 19, 2026. EPA says the program would operate in lieu of the federal CCR program for approved provisions, while unapproved federal provisions would continue to apply directly.

EPA says Louisiana adopted much of 40 CFR part 257, subpart D, but did not adopt certain 2018, 2024, and February 2026 federal changes. EPA identifies unapproved federal areas including legacy CCR surface impoundment requirements and CCR management unit requirements. EPA also declines to approve Louisiana's exclusion for CCR surface impoundments that no longer contain water or can no longer impound liquids.

LDEQ identified 9 CCR disposal units at 4 facilities. Existing CCR landfills and surface impoundments must submit applications for a new solid waste permit or modification to an existing permit within 365 days of approval. LDEQ has not issued permits under LAC 33:VII.Chapter 10, so no such permits are part of this approval record.

### Inference

This proposal is primarily about regulatory governance and program substitution, not a new federal technical standard for CCR units. The practical stakes are whether Louisiana's permitting program will maintain federal-level protection, whether the state/federal boundary will be clear during transition, and whether affected communities can track compliance obligations for particular units.

### Evidence quotes

* "EPA is proposing to approve the Louisiana partial CCR permit program pursuant to RCRA section 4005(d)(1)(B)."
* "The State's CCR permit program does not encompass the full scope of Federal CCR requirements as presently constituted..."
* "LDEQ has not issued any LAC 33:VII.Chapter 10 CCR permits in the State."
* "Louisiana has one exclusion in the State CCR regulations that is not being approved..."

## Step 3 — Agency Argument Tree

**Regulatory objective**

Approve Louisiana to administer a partial CCR permit program in lieu of the federal program for covered provisions.

**Problem definition**

RCRA allows states to seek approval of state CCR permit programs. Louisiana has submitted a partial program built from state law, state solid waste regulations, Chapter 10 CCR provisions, guidance, and application materials.

**Data used**

EPA relies on Louisiana's application, revised narrative, state statutes and regulations, the 40 CFR part 257 checklist, the state-versus-federal comparison, the EPA Technical Support Document, the 2017 guidance, and information about the Louisiana CCR universe and LDEQ resources.

**Analytical method**

EPA evaluates two components: the adequacy of the permit program itself and the technical criteria to be included in permits. EPA uses 40 CFR part 239 criteria as guidelines for program adequacy and compares Louisiana's technical criteria against 40 CFR part 257 to decide whether state requirements are identical to or at least as protective as federal requirements.

**Alternatives considered**

The proposal acknowledges partial approval and continued federal applicability for provisions not approved. It gives less public-facing discussion of alternative transition mechanisms, such as staged approval, enhanced facility-specific applicability tables, or explicit permit-issuance milestones.

**Agency conclusion**

EPA preliminarily determines that Louisiana's partial CCR permit program meets the statutory standard and proposes to approve it.

### Missing links / unsupported premises / logical jumps

1. The record identifies the unapproved federal provisions, but a reader may still need a clearer facility-level map of which obligations remain federal and which shift to Louisiana permits.
2. EPA explains that no Chapter 10 CCR permits exist yet, but gives limited detail on the pace, sequencing, and transparency of the 365-day application and subsequent permit-issuance process.
3. EPA notes tribal consultation and the Jena Band's notification request, but the notice does not fully explain how that request will be operationalized.
4. EPA identifies the "uppermost aquifer" versus "uppermost permeable zone" issue as an important state-federal difference, but the notice's public-facing explanation is brief relative to the technical importance of groundwater monitoring.

## Step 4 — Analysis Obligations Review

**Adequate data:** Satisfied

For this kind of program approval, the docket contains the expected analytical spine: application materials, checklists, state regulations, EPA technical support, and a state/federal comparison.

**Transparent methodology:** Partially satisfied

EPA explains its legal and comparative methodology. The record is less transparent on the practical effect of some state-specific differences and on the transition from federal self-implementation to state permitting.

**Evaluation of reasonable alternatives:** Partially satisfied

EPA treats partial approval as the operative path and leaves omitted federal provisions in place. It does not deeply analyze alternative ways to manage transition clarity, facility-level public tracking, or notification procedures.

**Explanation of analytical thresholds:** Partially satisfied

The "at least as protective as" threshold is clear. The application of that threshold to state-specific groundwater terminology and implementation procedures could be explained more accessibly in the final record.

**Consideration of foreseeable impacts:** Partially satisfied

EPA discusses LDEQ resources, permit applications, continued federal applicability, and future program review. The foreseeable burden on public participants trying to navigate overlapping authority is acknowledged indirectly but not developed in detail.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

### Regulatory objective: approve a partial state CCR permit program while preserving federal coverage for omitted provisions

**Alternatives evaluated**

EPA evaluates partial approval and continued federal applicability for provisions Louisiana has not adopted or EPA will not approve.

**Plausible alternatives not analyzed in depth**

* Provide a facility-level applicability table for the 9 units at 4 facilities.
* Require or publish a transition schedule for permit applications, draft permits, final permits, and public comment periods.
* Clarify whether certain permits or units should be prioritized based on proximity to Tribes, groundwater issues, or surface impoundment status.
* Describe a public tracking page for major incidents, plant failures, permit status, and federal/state authority boundaries.
* Explain whether the Jena Band notification request will be reflected in specific permit terms, LDEQ procedures, or EPA oversight expectations.

**Evidence suggesting feasibility**

EPA already prepared a TSD, a checklist, and a state-versus-federal requirements comparison. The notice also identifies a discrete universe of 9 CCR units at 4 facilities. That makes a clearer public-facing transition and applicability map feasible.

**Stage-aware classification**

**Potential unacknowledged weakness.** Partial approval is lawful and normal under RCRA section 4005(d). The weakness is not the use of partial approval itself. The weakness is the limited practical explanation of how affected communities and facilities will navigate the partial approval during the pre-permit and early-permit period.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Observed Fact**

EPA says Louisiana's CCR regulations include location restrictions, design and operating criteria, groundwater monitoring and corrective action, closure and post-closure care, recordkeeping, notification, and CCR website posting requirements. EPA also says LDEQ adopted state-specific provisions and incorporated much of the federal CCR rule by reference.

EPA identifies the groundwater terminology issue as one area discussed with LDEQ: federal "uppermost aquifer" requirements versus Louisiana "uppermost permeable zone" requirements. EPA also identifies the state exclusion for surface impoundments that no longer contain water or can no longer impound liquids as not at least as protective.

**Inference**

The core technical framework is present, but the record would be stronger if the final TSD or preamble explained in plain language how Louisiana's groundwater-monitoring terminology will identify the same or more protective monitoring target in real facility settings. That is especially important for CCR units because groundwater monitoring and corrective action are central components of the federal rule.

**Unknown**

The available text does not provide a facility-specific groundwater demonstration for each of the 9 units. It also does not show how many of those units may involve legacy impoundment, CCR management unit, or formerly wet impoundment questions.

## Step 7 — Statutory Authority and Legal Fit

**Observed Fact**

EPA cites RCRA sections 4005(d) and 7004(b)(1). RCRA section 4005(d) allows EPA to approve state CCR permit programs, in whole or in part, if the state program requires CCR units to comply with federal CCR requirements or state requirements EPA determines are at least as protective. RCRA section 7004(b)(1) supports public participation in RCRA programs.

**Inference**

The proposal fits the basic statutory pathway. The principal legal-fit questions are not whether EPA can approve a partial state CCR program, but whether EPA has adequately explained the boundaries of approval and the reasons certain Louisiana differences satisfy the statutory protectiveness standard.

**Unknown**

The available materials do not show whether commenters will raise state-law authority objections or facility-specific arguments about particular units.

## Step 8 — Evidence Quality and Data Gaps

**Observed Fact**

The notice and supporting materials identify the application history, the covered universe of CCR units, the state legal framework, LDEQ resources, the state/federal differences, and the provisions EPA is not approving.

**Inference**

The strongest evidence gap is not lack of documents. It is public usability. A technically complete crosswalk can still be difficult for non-specialists to apply to a particular facility, especially when some federal provisions remain directly applicable and no Chapter 10 CCR permits have yet issued.

**Unknown**

The available text does not summarize current conditions at each of the four Louisiana facilities, including groundwater-monitoring status, closure status, or whether any units are near communities or Tribal areas with specific incident-notification concerns.

## Step 9 — Reliance Interests and Transition

**Observed Fact**

EPA states that existing CCR facilities must submit an application for a new solid waste permit or modification to an existing permit within 365 days of approval. EPA also states that, absent a permit issued under an approved state program, owners or operators must continue to comply with the federal CCR regulations.

**Inference**

The transition may be legally manageable but practically complex. Facilities remain under federal obligations until state permits issue, while the public may see a final approval and assume Louisiana has fully taken over all requirements immediately.

**Comment leverage**

EPA and LDEQ could clarify whether they will publish permit-application receipt dates, completeness determinations, draft-permit timelines, and final permit status for each CCR unit.

## Step 10 — Federalism, Preemption, and Intergovernmental Coordination

**Observed Fact**

The proposal is a state-program approval under a federal statute. EPA would retain inspection and enforcement authority under RCRA sections 3007 and 3008. Federal requirements not approved for state substitution would remain directly applicable.

EPA engaged four federally recognized Tribes in Louisiana and states that no Tribes sought further consultation after the informational session. The Jena Band of Choctaw Indians requested notification of major incidents and/or plant failure because Tribal boundaries are close to the Cleco Brame Power Plant.

**Inference**

The proposal reflects cooperative federalism, but that structure makes clarity essential. EPA should be explicit about which authority applies to each requirement and how intergovernmental notification will function.

**Unknown**

The notice does not identify a specific enforceable mechanism for the Jena Band notification request.

## Step 11 — Distributional and Environmental Justice Considerations

**Observed Fact**

The notice discusses Tribal consultation but does not provide a separate environmental justice analysis in the preamble text available for review. It identifies 9 CCR disposal units at 4 facilities, but the notice text does not provide demographic, groundwater, or risk-context summaries for nearby communities.

**Inference**

Because CCR units can affect groundwater, surface impoundment closure, and nearby communities, comments could constructively ask EPA to include more facility-level public-health and community-context information in the final record. The Jena Band request is a specific distributional signal that notification protocols matter.

**Unknown**

The available record does not show whether EPA conducted a separate EJScreen-style review or other community-impact analysis for the covered facilities.

## Step 12 — Enforcement, Monitoring, and Feedback Design

**Observed Fact**

EPA says LDEQ has permitting, information-collection, compliance monitoring, and enforcement authority. EPA also says EPA retains RCRA inspection and enforcement authorities and can enforce federal requirements that remain in effect.

The proposal says Louisiana's program includes groundwater monitoring, corrective action, recordkeeping, notification, and CCR website posting requirements. Federal provisions for legacy CCR surface impoundments and CCR management units would remain federal because Louisiana did not adopt them.

**Inference**

The enforcement framework is real, but public-facing enforcement clarity is a meaningful issue. A member of the public should not need to reconstruct the applicable law from multiple federal rules, state regulations, and crosswalk appendices to know who handles a concern about a given unit.

**Comment leverage**

EPA could explain how complaints, incident notices, website postings, permit violations, and federal-only requirements will be routed between EPA and LDEQ.

## Step 13 — Overall Administrative Record Risk

**Risk level:** Moderate

**Observed Fact**

The record includes substantial support and identifies the major state/federal differences. EPA is not ignoring the partial-program nature of the approval, and it expressly refuses to approve one Louisiana exclusion it finds not at least as protective.

**Inference**

The main administrative-record risk is not a missing legal authority or empty technical record. It is whether the final record will be clear enough on the practical consequences of partial approval: federal-only provisions, no current Chapter 10 permits, the 365-day application requirement, groundwater terminology, and Tribal/community notification.

## Phase 3 — Comment Generation

## Step 14 — Constructive Comment Themes

1. Ask EPA to add a facility-level table showing, for each of the 9 identified CCR disposal units, which requirements will be governed by Louisiana permits and which will remain direct federal requirements.
2. Ask EPA to explain the transition timeline after approval, including permit application deadlines, completeness review, draft permits, public comment, and final permits.
3. Ask EPA to expand the final record's explanation of how "uppermost permeable zone" will be applied in a way that is at least as protective as the federal "uppermost aquifer" requirement.
4. Ask EPA and LDEQ to describe how major incident or plant failure notifications will be provided to the Jena Band of Choctaw Indians and potentially to other affected communities.
5. Ask EPA to clarify public-facing enforcement routing for requirements that remain federal, especially legacy CCR surface impoundment and CCR management unit provisions.
6. Ask EPA to explain whether any of the 9 units may implicate the state exclusion EPA is not approving.

## Step 15 — High-Value Questions for Commenters

* Can EPA include a simple public crosswalk showing approved Louisiana provisions, federal-only provisions, and mixed provisions for each covered facility?
* How soon after final approval does EPA expect LDEQ to receive, review, and publicly notice permit applications or modifications?
* What happens if a facility misses the 365-day application deadline?
* How will LDEQ and EPA tell the public whether a specific compliance issue should be raised with LDEQ, EPA, or both?
* What technical basis supports treating Louisiana's groundwater-monitoring terminology as at least as protective as the federal text?
* How will the Jena Band's request for notification of major incidents and/or plant failure be implemented?
* Will EPA publish a list of any legacy CCR surface impoundments or CCR management units that remain subject to direct federal requirements?

## Step 16 — Evidence-backed Comment Drafting Notes

**Observed Fact**

Useful comments should quote the notice's statements that the program is partial, no Chapter 10 permits currently exist, applications are due within 365 days after approval, federal requirements remain applicable until approved state permits issue, and one Louisiana exclusion is not being approved.

**Inference**

The most credible comments will accept that EPA has a substantial record and focus on improving final-record clarity and implementation transparency. Comments that simply assert EPA has no evidence would be weaker because the docket includes a TSD, checklists, state regulations, and comparison documents.

**Possible comment framing**

"EPA should finalize approval only with a clearer public-facing implementation appendix that identifies, for each covered facility and unit, the applicable state permit pathway, the federal provisions that remain directly applicable, the expected permit transition dates, and the notification procedures for major incidents."

## Step 17 — Weak or Unsupported Comment Theories to Avoid

* Avoid claiming that EPA cannot approve partial CCR permit programs; RCRA section 4005(d) expressly allows approval in whole or in part.
* Avoid claiming that the docket has no supporting materials; the record contains a substantial analytical spine.
* Avoid treating ordinary NPRM requests for comment as defects by themselves.
* Avoid asserting facility-specific harm unless supported by facility records or local evidence not present in the available text.
* Avoid assuming Louisiana will immediately replace all federal CCR obligations upon final approval; the notice says federal requirements continue where not approved and until state permits issue.

## Step 18 — Bottom Line

EPA has built a meaningful record for approving Louisiana's partial CCR permit program, and the proposal appears to use the correct statutory framework. The best-supported concerns are practical and record-completion concerns: the public needs a clearer map of the split federal/state regime, a transparent permit-transition schedule, a stronger explanation of groundwater-monitoring terminology, and a concrete mechanism for incident notification where Tribes or nearby communities have requested it.
