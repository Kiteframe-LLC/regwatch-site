## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The FTC is receiving a petition and requesting comment before deciding whether to initiate rulemaking or take another action. The petition asks for a rule on deceptive acquisition-price representations in penny auctions and pay-per-bid auctions.

**Problem Addressed:** The petitioner argues that a displayed closing price can imply that it is the winner's total cost even when paid bids or mandatory charges are additional.

**Mechanism Requested:** The proposed text would prohibit misleading acquisition-price representations, require clear and proximate qualifications, address audiovisual disclosures and testimonials, and prohibit evasion through wording or presentation devices.

**Affected Entities:** Consumers; auction operators; advertisers and agencies; media distributors; platforms and influencers whose statements are adopted by advertisers; and smaller operators facing tracking or disclosure costs.

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites section 18(a)(1)(B) of the FTC Act and FTC Rule 1.31(f) for the petition process. The petition separately invokes section 5 and uses the fee rule as an analogy, while acknowledging that Part 464 does not generally cover penny auctions.

**Legal Effect:** The notice does not change existing law or begin a rulemaking proceeding; the petition's proposed language has no legal effect unless the FTC later acts.

**Inference:** The strongest comment posture is to test whether current evidence and workable definitions justify moving from general section 5 principles to a specialized rule.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Status:** The core distinction between a nominal closing price and acquisition cost is understandable, but the operational standard is not fully specified.

**Observed Facts**
- The proposed text covers paid bids and other mandatory charges and uses a reasonable-consumer net-impression test.
- The petition acknowledges that bid packages may have different effective per-bid prices and that bids may be used across multiple auctions.
- The petition proposes media-neutral requirements but does not provide worked examples across each listed medium.

**Inferences**
- A performance standard may be adaptable, but without examples or safe harbors it could produce uncertainty about when a closing-price claim becomes an acquisition-price representation.
- The phrase known or reasonably ascertainable could require data access or recordkeeping that differs substantially by operator and advertising relationship.

**Technical Questions**
- How should promotional or free bids be valued?
- How should costs be allocated when one package is used across multiple auctions?
- What information must an advertiser obtain from an operator before making a winner-specific claim?

**Failure Modes**
