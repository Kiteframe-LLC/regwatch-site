## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Codifying prior guidance will reduce uncertainty without creating new substantive risk.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The FAQ guidance already reflects the operative policy and market practice closely enough that codification is mostly a transparency change.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the codified language is narrower or broader than the prior guidance, plans could still face compliance uncertainty at the edges.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for examples showing where the codified text diverges from the earlier FAQ guidance.
- **Claim:** Short-period derivative positions are a workable bridge when physical securities are not immediately available.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Plans can tell when a derivative is genuinely transitional rather than a substitute for a different risk posture.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Without a clearer duration standard, a plan might either overhold derivatives or avoid them altogether to stay safe.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask PBGC to define the outer bound or provide examples that distinguish bridge positions from hedges.
- **Claim:** The prior-year valuation date is the right date for the settlement threshold.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The valuation date will reliably track the same economic picture that governs withdrawal liability allocation.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If market conditions move quickly late in the year, the chosen date may understate or overstate the threshold for approval.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask whether PBGC considered a more current date or a fallback when volatility is material.
- **Claim:** Aggregating related settlements will prevent threshold avoidance.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Plans can identify when settlements are related enough to aggregate and can document that judgment consistently.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If relatedness is unclear, the anti-avoidance rule can itself become a source of uncertainty and dispute.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask PBGC for a clearer definition of relatedness and a look-back example.
- **Claim:** Removing the reallocation exception is consistent with the statute and program purpose.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The statutory reading is narrow enough that no health-benefit exception should remain.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If real-world welfare-plan reallocations still occur for benign reasons, the repeal may be broader than needed.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask whether PBGC considered a narrower exception or a different safeguard.
