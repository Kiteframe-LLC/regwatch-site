## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_technology_supports_faster_revocation
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Callers can reliably route, authenticate, synchronize, and honor revocation requests within seven business days or another shorter period.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A caller uses distributed vendors, separate lines of business, or manual exception handling, so a request is received promptly but is not propagated to every message system before the new deadline.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should request baseline processing times, exception rates, and a record-based explanation for the proposed deadline or any safe harbor.
- **Claim Id:** claim_reply_texts_improve_revocation
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The consumer-facing benefit of reply-text revocation outweighs conversion costs and the loss of existing alternatives.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Schools, public institutions, or low-volume senders use one-way systems for notifications and cannot immediately migrate without interrupting useful messages or incurring disproportionate costs.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The agency should distinguish informational from advertising messages and analyze performance-based alternatives or transition periods.
- **Claim Id:** claim_revoke_all_benefits_consumers
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Consumers understand the scope of an all-message revocation and prefer a single broad control to category-specific control.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A consumer intending to stop marketing messages also loses wanted account, fraud, health, or public-service notifications, or a clarification workflow treats silence as broad revocation.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should define scope, confirmation, reversibility, and default behavior before proposing a mandatory automated mechanism.
- **Claim Id:** claim_affiliate_clarity_needed
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A clearer affiliate rule can be applied consistently across corporate structures and consumer expectations.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A caller cannot determine whether a separate brand, product, division, or service provider is covered, causing inconsistent suppression or repeated consumer requests.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The record should include concrete corporate examples and an administrable rule that avoids both underinclusive and overbroad suppression.
- **Claim Id:** claim_small_entity_burden_can_be_minimized
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Targeted exemptions, differentiated timelines, or message-type distinctions can reduce burden without weakening consumer control.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** An exemption becomes a loophole for high-volume callers, or a differentiated timeline produces consumer confusion and inconsistent enforcement.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should compare alternatives against measurable consumer-protection and compliance outcomes rather than leave the tradeoff at a general question level.

**Risks**

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Distributed-system deadline failure
  
  **Mechanism:** A request is received by one system but remains active in a vendor, affiliate, or separate business-line system beyond the new maximum.
  
  **Consequence:** Consumers continue receiving unwanted calls or texts and callers face inconsistent compliance exposure.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Request propagation metrics, clear receipt rules, exceptions, and a measurable safe harbor.
- **Failure Mode:** One-way-texting disruption
  
  **Mechanism:** Informational senders must replace systems that cannot receive replies.
  
  **Consequence:** Small entities, schools, and public institutions incur conversion cost or reduce useful notifications.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Ask for message-type distinctions, equivalent alternatives, and transition assistance.
- **Failure Mode:** Overbroad revoke-all result
  
  **Mechanism:** A consumer's category-specific request or ambiguous response is treated as stopping every message requiring consent.
  
  **Consequence:** Wanted fraud, account, health, or public-service communications stop unexpectedly.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Request explicit scope labels, confirmation, reversibility, and evidence on consumer understanding.
- **Failure Mode:** Affiliate inconsistency
  
  **Mechanism:** Different systems interpret the reasonable-expectation standard differently across brands and divisions.
  
  **Consequence:** Consumers must repeat requests or callers suppress communications more broadly than intended.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Request functional examples, a control-group rule, and sender-identification safe harbors.
- **Failure Mode:** Confirmation-message delay
  
  **Mechanism:** A caller uses a clarification text or call that creates an additional step before revocation takes effect.
  
  **Consequence:** A consumer's attempt to stop messages is delayed or confused by a new communication.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Ask for a default that honors clear requests immediately and limits clarification to genuinely ambiguous cases.
- **Failure Mode:** Differentiated-deadline confusion
  
  **Mechanism:** Different deadlines apply depending on the designated method, message category, or caller type.
  
  **Consequence:** Consumers and small callers cannot predict when a request is effective or which rule applies.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Request a simple public-facing rule and a compliance matrix.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Positive**
- A shorter and clearer deadline could encourage investment in reliable consent-management systems.
- Reply-text capability could make opt-outs easier to discover and reduce dependence on separate websites or phone numbers.
- A well-defined affiliate rule could reduce repeated consumer requests and inconsistent suppression.

**Negative**
- Callers may designate exclusive methods that are technically available but difficult to use, shifting friction to consumers.
- A broad revoke-all option may encourage overly cautious suppression of wanted communications or, if scope is unclear, repeated confirmation contacts.
- Complex differentiated rules may favor large entities with specialized compliance systems over small and public-institution senders.

**Design Question:** Can the FCC achieve reliable consumer control through outcome-based service levels, clear scope labels, and interoperable records rather than requiring one technical interface for every message and sender?

## Step 17 — High-Leverage Comment Angles

- **Id:** angle_measure_deadline
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Posture:** evidence request
  
  **Issue:** A shorter revocation deadline needs a measurable operational basis.
  
  **Why It Matters:** Without baseline and exception data, the deadline may be either too slow for consumers or too rigid for small and distributed systems.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** Publish processing-time evidence and compare seven days with phased alternatives.
- **Id:** angle_message_type_alternative
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Posture:** alternatives push
  
  **Issue:** A universal two-way-texting requirement may be broader than necessary.
  
  **Why It Matters:** Informational and public-institution messages may have different technical needs and lower consumer-protection risks than advertising messages.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** Compare message-type, equivalent-functionality, transition, and safe-harbor alternatives.
- **Id:** angle_revoke_all_scope
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Posture:** collaborative clarification
  
  **Issue:** A revoke-all method needs a precise, consumer-understandable scope and reversal design.
  
  **Why It Matters:** A broad control could stop wanted account, fraud, health, or public-service messages, while an unclear control could generate repeated clarification contacts.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** Define message categories, confirmation, reversal, and ambiguous-request defaults.

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

- **Id:** brief_deadline
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Key Sentences**
  - The Commission seeks comment on whether to reduce the maximum timeframe for honoring revocation requests.
  - The Joint Letter suggests reducing the deadline to seven business days.
  
  **Routine Description:** An opt-out must move from its receipt channel to every system that sends messages, including vendors and business lines.
  
  **Regulatory Effectiveness:** A shorter deadline could help if it matches actual system capability and has clear receipt and exception rules.
  
  **Statutory Compliance:** The record should connect the chosen deadline to TCPA consent and revocation purposes without making compliance unpredictable.
  
  **Procedural Adequacy:** The final record needs processing data, alternatives, and small-entity burden analysis.
  
  **Real World Consequence:** Consumers may continue receiving unwanted messages, or smaller callers may face avoidable violations, without propagation evidence.
  
  **Worth A Comment:** high
  
  **Comment Posture:** evidence request
- **Id:** brief_two_way
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Key Sentences**
  - We seek comment on whether to eliminate the provision ... that allows text senders to use a texting protocol that does not allow consumers to revoke consent by reply text.
  - Should we consider allowing one-way texting for informational messages?
  
  **Routine Description:** A sender uses a one-way or two-way protocol and maintains the suppression record for consent-required messages.
  
  **Regulatory Effectiveness:** Reply texting may improve usability, but message-type or equivalent-functionality options may reduce disruption.
  
  **Statutory Compliance:** The agency should explain why a technical mandate is needed for the TCPA purpose and why alternatives fail.
  
  **Procedural Adequacy:** Selection requires prevalence, reliability, cost, and public-institution evidence.
  
  **Real World Consequence:** Schools, public institutions, and small entities could incur migration costs or lose useful notification capacity.
  
  **Worth A Comment:** high
  
  **Comment Posture:** alternatives push
