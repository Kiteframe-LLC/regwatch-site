## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** A single nationwide framework will improve clarity and uniformity.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The same factor set can be applied consistently across diverse industries, workplaces, and statutes.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If program-specific facts matter more than the proposal allows, the cross-reference approach may create new uncertainty instead of reducing it.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for concrete examples showing the same standard working across FLSA, FMLA, MSPA, and agricultural settings.
- **Claim:** The proposed four-factor test captures the key control indicators.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The four factors reliably identify joint employment without needing a tighter threshold or a different factor hierarchy.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the factors are underinclusive or overinclusive in fringe cases, field enforcement and litigation outcomes may diverge.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask where the four-factor structure departs from the cited case law and why those departures are justified.
- **Claim:** The rule can be implemented by investigators and employers in real time.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Users can translate the regulatory text into consistent, fact-specific judgments without additional guidance.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If local offices and regulated entities need more examples or criteria, the rule may produce uneven application.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request model examples, checklists, or branch-specific guidance.
- **Claim:** The burden story is strong enough to justify the rewrite.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Qualitative burden claims and cost estimates are enough even though prevalence data are missing.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the affected universe is smaller or different than assumed, the rule may be broader than needed.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for prevalence, timing, and distribution data before accepting the claimed burden reduction.
- **Claim:** Cross-referencing Part 791 for FMLA and MSPA will preserve statutory fit.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The FLSA analysis can be transferred without losing program-specific differences in leave and agricultural contexts.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the incorporated statutes need different treatment at the margins, the cross-reference may flatten meaningful distinctions.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask where FMLA or MSPA should diverge from the FLSA analysis, if anywhere.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** Revise the Department's joint-employer analysis in Part 791 and cross-reference that analysis into the FMLA, MSPA, and related agricultural provisions.

**Affected Entities**
- Workers and employers whose relationships may be treated as joint employment under the FLSA, FMLA, or MSPA
- Staffing firms, subcontracting chains, franchise-related businesses, and other multi-entity work arrangements
- State, local, and Tribal wage-and-hour or agricultural enforcement personnel

**Observed Facts**
- The notice describes a proposed rule and explicitly invites public comments.
- The proposal would readopt and revise the Department's Part 791 framework, including vertical and horizontal joint employment and a business-practices section.
- The NPRM also proposes conforming changes to the FMLA, MSPA, and agricultural regulations so they cross-reference Part 791.
- The PRIA estimates one-time familiarization costs and otherwise treats most impacts qualitatively.

**Inference:** This is a broad definitional rewrite that aims to standardize joint-employment analysis across several wage-and-hour programs rather than a narrow housekeeping update.

**Evidence Quotes**

- cross-references to Part 791
- one-time regulatory familiarization cost
- the Department does not currently have data
