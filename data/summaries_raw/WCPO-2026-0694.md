## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** This RFI asks the public to develop the technical, clinical, legal, economic, and implementation record for possible future changes to LHWCA hearing-loss testing standards. It does not itself change 20 CFR 702.441(d) or decide whether objective testing will be required.

**Objective:** Identify reliable and fair ways to evaluate occupational hearing loss, including how behavioral audiograms and objective tests should be administered, compared, weighted, and translated into impairment determinations.

**Affected Entities:** LHWCA claimants and workers; employers and insurance carriers; audiologists, otolaryngologists, and other testing providers; medical associations; adjudicators; and small medical practices that may face equipment, training, or access burdens.

**Scope:** The inquiry spans test validity, clinical standards, calibration, masking, personnel qualifications, evidentiary weighting, AMA Guides calculations, economic effects, feasibility, and OTC hearing aids. It is broad and leaves the eventual regulatory option open.

**What The Agency Is Asking:** OWCP requests peer-reviewed evidence, professional standards, examples from other systems, equipment and per-test costs, access data, implementation recommendations, and concrete criteria for handling conflicting test results.

**Public Interest Relevance:** Moderate to high public-interest relevance within the LHWCA program because testing methodology can affect benefit eligibility, impairment percentages, litigation costs, and access to compensation, while the policy is specialized rather than economy-wide.

**Overall Assessment:** Promising and substantively relevant inquiry, but the record would be stronger if OWCP supplied a baseline, separated clinical measurement from legal impairment valuation, defined comparison criteria, and specified how it will weigh evidence from differently resourced commenters.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

- **Alternative Or Missing Option:** Retain audiograms as the sole impairment-measurement method while using objective tests only for defined validity disputes.
  
  **Why It Matters:** This is a meaningful lower-burden option and would separate test validation from wholesale replacement.
  
  **Question For Record:** Under what circumstances would targeted objective testing improve accuracy enough to justify its added burden?
- **Alternative Or Missing Option:** Use objective tests as corroborative or rebuttal evidence without converting them directly into impairment percentages.
  
  **Why It Matters:** A limited evidentiary role may avoid an unsupported conversion formula while still addressing disputed effort or inconsistency.
  
  **Question For Record:** What specific evidentiary threshold should trigger corroboration, and what finding would be sufficient to rebut a qualifying audiogram?
- **Alternative Or Missing Option:** Pilot or phase in standards with data collection and review before making a test mandatory.
  
  **Why It Matters:** A staged approach could reveal access, cost, and disagreement effects before they become program-wide requirements.
  
  **Question For Record:** What outcome measures and review date would determine whether a new testing standard is working?
- **Alternative Or Missing Option:** Adopt a technology-neutral performance standard rather than naming a fixed test list.
  
  **Why It Matters:** A performance standard may preserve innovation while requiring demonstrated reliability, validity, and reproducibility.
  
  **Question For Record:** What minimum validation and reproducibility evidence should any future method meet?

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Clinical-legal conflation
  
  **Mechanism:** A test that is useful for detecting physiological response could be treated as a direct substitute for the statutory impairment measure.
  
  **Potential Effect:** Unreliable ratings, inconsistent adjudication, or unsupported rebuttal of qualifying audiograms.
  
  **Mitigation:** Require a test-by-test endpoint map and a validated conversion or explicitly limit the test to corroboration.
- **Failure Mode:** Dueling-test escalation
  
  **Mechanism:** Routine second testing could create competing results rather than resolve exceptional cases.
  
  **Potential Effect:** Higher costs, delay, litigation, and pressure on claimants to undergo repeated testing.
  
  **Mitigation:** Define triggers, sequencing, neutral-review procedures, and cost allocation before authorizing additional tests.
- **Failure Mode:** Access-driven record bias
  
  **Mechanism:** Equipment and specialist availability may differ by geography and practice size.
  
  **Potential Effect:** The record overrepresents large carriers and institutions and underrepresents small practices and workers.
  
  **Mitigation:** Request public-data substitutes, geographic access data, and small-entity case studies.
- **Failure Mode:** Overbroad OTC substitution
  
  **Mechanism:** A general question about OTC hearing aids could be answered without distinguishing device suitability, fitting, maintenance, and worker choice.
  
  **Potential Effect:** Compensation may not match functional need or may shift costs to workers.
  
  **Mitigation:** Ask for separate evidence on clinical appropriateness, access, durability, and safeguards.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

- A requirement for objective testing could reduce incentives to rely on disputed behavioral results, but could also incentivize routine repeat testing or strategic presentation of whichever method produces a favorable outcome.
- A corroboration-only model could preserve access and reduce burden while still encouraging better-quality evidence in contested cases.
- Providers and carriers may have incentives to favor methods they already own or routinely use; a future rule should require disclosure of method limits and conflicts rather than assume neutrality.
- Small practices may be discouraged from serving claimants if equipment, calibration, or certification requirements are too costly or geographically concentrated.

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

**Strengths**

- It covers clinical validity, standards, costs, feasibility, and small-entity effects.
- It recognizes the need to address discrepancies and impairment calculations rather than stopping at test availability.

**Limitations**
- The notice supplies no studies, baseline claims data, current error rates, or cost estimates of its own.
- The question set does not define a hierarchy for clinical reliability, functional hearing, disability, and legal impairment.
- The requested evidence is demanding and may be difficult for individual workers and small providers to produce.

**Overall:** The RFI is evidence-seeking and reasonably disciplined, but the agency must provide a comparison framework and baseline in the next stage to avoid treating a collection of unequal submissions as a controlled evidence review.
