## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** A broader definitional inquiry can reduce ambiguity across swaps, security-based swaps, mixed swaps, and excluded instruments.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The relevant product categories can be distinguished by objective criteria that commenters can meaningfully compare.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the same product features map differently depending on market structure or use case, the definitional line may remain unstable even after additional guidance.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can push for concrete examples and decision rules rather than abstract category labels.
- **Claim:** Alternative compliance may be workable where requirements are substantially similar.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The two Commissions can identify comparable objectives, scope, and outcomes for some requirements without weakening either regime.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the agencies use different enforcement models or information-gathering tools, a broad substantial-similarity test could become either too permissive or too vague.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Comments should focus on which provisions can realistically be aligned and which must remain separate.
- **Claim:** Principled, objective criteria can be developed for event contracts and other innovative products.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The legal distinctions between event contracts, options, futures, and swaps can be operationalized without relying on case-by-case intuition alone.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If product innovation outpaces the criteria, the agencies may create definitions that are either overbroad or easy to game.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The most useful comments will test whether proposed criteria are administrable, not just conceptually neat.
- **Claim:** Cross-market surveillance and enforcement can support alternative compliance.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The Commissions can share enough information and coordinate enough oversight to preserve market-integrity goals.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If coordination depends on uneven information-sharing authority or uneven monitoring capacity, alternative compliance could create gaps rather than efficiency.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can identify where shared reporting, joint registration, or deemed filing would actually work and where it would not.
- **Claim:** The inquiry is exploratory rather than outcome-determinative.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The agencies are genuinely testing the boundaries before deciding whether to propose new rules or interpretations.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the question set is too broad, the record may favor well-resourced participants and leave smaller market users underrepresented.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Comments should ask for public-data substitutes, examples, and a narrower decision tree.
