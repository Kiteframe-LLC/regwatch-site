## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim A

**Claim:** EPA can appropriately generalize order-based restrictions into SNURs for all manufacturers and processors of the same substances.

**Underlying assumption:** The order terms negotiated with the PMN submitters are a sound proxy for the controls EPA would expect from any future manufacturer or processor of the same substances.

**Failure scenario:** If the order terms were tailored to submitter-specific manufacturing methods or exposure assumptions, extending them wholesale could overfit one factual setting.

**Procedural implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to explain which restrictions are substance-driven and which were shaped by site- or process-specific PMN facts.

### Claim B

**Claim:** The cited hazard concerns support the chosen engineering controls, respiratory thresholds, labeling obligations, and disposal restrictions.

**Underlying assumption:** The standardized assessment and PMN record support not just a general concern, but the exact thresholds EPA chose.

**Failure scenario:** If the supporting assessment shows more uncertainty than the preamble suggests, the precise line-drawing around APF levels, boundary concentrations, or labeling scope could look underexplained.

**Procedural implication:** Commenters can press EPA to connect the hazard evidence more explicitly to each major restriction.

### Claim C

**Claim:** Battery-only use is the right permitted use boundary.

**Underlying assumption:** EPA has enough evidence that other possible uses would materially alter exposure or release patterns in ways not addressed by the orders.

**Failure scenario:** If closely related non-battery uses could be managed with similar controls, the use restriction may look broader than necessary.

**Procedural implication:** This is a high-value place for commenters to request explanation of the agency's use-scope reasoning.

### Claim D

**Claim:** The proposal's economic discussion is adequate even though EPA cannot estimate likely export-notification counts.

**Underlying assumption:** A per-unit export-notification estimate and a per-SNUN filing estimate are enough to characterize the proposal's practical burden.

**Failure scenario:** If the number of affected exporters, likely notifications, or compliance behavior matters meaningfully, the economic discussion may be too abstract.

**Procedural implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to explain the expected frequency of these burdens or why it cannot do so.

### Claim E

**Claim:** Confidential significant new uses can still be administered fairly through the bona fide intent procedure.

**Underlying assumption:** Potential manufacturers and processors will be able to determine their obligations with reasonable certainty before beginning activity.

**Failure scenario:** If outside firms cannot reliably know whether a contemplated use crosses a confidential line without delay or ambiguity, fair-notice and compliance costs rise.

**Procedural implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to explain how practical and timely the bona fide process is for prospective market entrants.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

* EPA proposes SNURs under TSCA section `5(a)(2)` for two proposed CFR entries, `40 CFR 721.12219` and `40 CFR 721.12220`.
* Both entries cover battery-related mixed metal oxide substances generically described as cobalt lithium manganese nickel oxide, metals-doped.
* EPA says the substances were the subject of PMNs and TSCA section `5(e)` orders, and the proposed SNURs would extend the order protections beyond the original submitters.
* The proposed controls include battery-only use, battery/exterior packaging labels, `99%` dust-control efficiency, no water release, hazardous-waste landfill or incineration disposal, a property-boundary air limit, and a respiratory protection matrix tied to worker exposure levels.
* EPA also says manufacture or processing for more than one year becomes a significant new use unless there is no air release or inhalation exposure, or unless relevant limits are updated.
* EPA quantifies estimated SNUN submission costs and a per-export-notification cost, but says it cannot estimate the likely number of export notifications.

### Inference

This is a targeted, order-following TSCA proposal rather than a broad cross-market chemical rule. EPA is using the SNUR mechanism to convert submitter-specific new-chemical restrictions into generally applicable advance-notice obligations. The strongest public-comment opportunities concern whether EPA has shown enough visible reasoning for its precise thresholds, whether the confidential-use process provides adequate practical notice, and whether the economic discussion is too thin for outside readers to assess the real burden.

### Evidence Quotes

* “The proposed SNURs extend the protective measures from the signed consent orders... to any person intending to manufacture, process, use, distribute in commerce, or dispose of the same new chemical substances.”
* “No use of the PMN substance other than in the manufacture of batteries.”
* “No release of the PMN substance, or any waste stream containing the PMN substance, to water.”
* “If the 8-Hour TWA exceeds 5.3E+00 mg/m3, then manufacturing, processing, and use must cease.”

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Alternatives evaluated:** The public-facing notice mainly evaluates the order-following SNUR path and the specific restrictions that mirror the orders.

**Plausible alternatives not analyzed:** EPA does not visibly compare whether some restrictions could be framed more narrowly by processing type, cobalt content, release pathway, or downstream activity other than the battery-use limitation and the APF flexibility for substances with less than or equal to `3%` cobalt by weight.

**Evidence suggesting feasibility:** The notice itself recognizes at least one internal distinction by allowing APF values below `50` only when the PMN substance contains less than or equal to `3%` cobalt by weight. That suggests some finer-grained line-drawing is possible and worth explaining.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

The proposal is technically coherent at a high level. The worker-protection, release-control, disposal, and hazard-communication provisions fit together as a package aimed at inhalation, dermal, and environmental exposure. The inclusion of recycling and reclaiming within “processing” also reflects a coherent lifecycle view rather than a narrow manufacturing-only frame.

The weaker point is transparency rather than internal contradiction. The notice asks readers to accept a fairly exact package of `99%` dust controls, an aggregated boundary-air limit, a zero-water-release rule, a battery-labeling scheme, and a multi-level respirator matrix without exposing much of the intermediate assessment logic in the preamble.

**Observed Fact:** EPA ties the same broad concern set to both proposed CFR entries and uses nearly identical order-derived protections.

**Inference:** The rule is technically organized, but the available text gives more confidence that EPA has a framework than confidence that outside readers can independently evaluate every numeric choice from the notice alone.

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

**Observed Fact:** EPA grounds the action in TSCA section `5(a)(2)` and explains that a SNUR is a notification requirement rather than a risk determination itself.

**Observed Fact:** EPA also relies on TSCA section `5(f)(4)` to describe the proposal as extending order-based restrictions into significant new uses for all future actors.

**Inference:** The legal authority for using a follow-on SNUR after a TSCA order appears facially strong from the available notice text.

**Unknown:** The available materials do not show whether any case-specific facts in the underlying PMN negotiations would complicate EPA's effort to generalize those order terms to all future actors.

## Step 8 — Procedural Normalcy

**Classification:** **Normal but partly opaque**

**Observed Fact:** The notice follows a recognizable TSCA new-chemicals structure: PMN review, order issuance, then SNUR proposal to extend the same protections to others.

**Observed Fact:** EPA acknowledges that some significant new uses remain confidential and explains the `40 CFR 721.11` bona fide-intent process for obtaining them.

**Inference:** The process is procedurally normal for TSCA section `5` work. The main concern is not unusual procedure, but the degree to which confidentiality and off-notice supporting documents limit public visibility into the reasoning.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

* EPA may be relying on standardized-assessment conclusions without exposing enough of that assessment in the notice for outsiders to test the thresholds.
* The battery-only use restriction may be broader than necessary if closely related downstream uses could be managed with similar controls.
* The aggregated property-boundary air limit across other cobalt-containing MMO substances may be difficult to apply in mixed-product settings.
* The bona fide process for confidential significant new uses may create real notice and planning friction for non-submitter firms.
* The economic discussion may understate practical burden because it provides unit-cost estimates without estimating likely frequencies for some obligations.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

* The rule creates a strong incentive to remain inside the exact order-derived battery-use, exposure-control, labeling, and disposal conditions rather than test novel uses.
* It creates an incentive for firms to avoid triggering a SNUN by structuring operations to fit the existing protective conditions.
* It creates an incentive for recyclers and reclaimers to pay close attention because EPA expressly includes those activities within “processing.”
* It also creates an incentive for prospective entrants to seek early EPA engagement if confidential significant-new-use boundaries are unclear.

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

**Assessment:** **Moderate**

The available record is materially better than a bare rule text because EPA identifies the PMNs, describes the core hazard concerns, includes detailed restriction language, and surfaces sanitized PMN materials. But the strongest technical and economic support appears by reference more than by full reproduction. The public-facing record therefore supports a useful review, yet still leaves readers with limited visibility into why the exact thresholds and categorical boundaries were chosen.

## Step 13 — Litigation Vulnerabilities

* Potential underexplanation of the precise numeric and categorical thresholds used to convert hazard concerns into binding restrictions.
* Potential inadequacy in alternatives discussion, especially around whether narrower use, processing, or monitoring distinctions were considered.
* Potential fair-notice concerns in practice if confidential significant new uses materially affect non-submitter planning but are difficult to identify quickly.
* Potentially thin economic explanation because EPA presents unit burdens while admitting it cannot estimate some likely frequencies.

These are not obvious fatal flaws from the available text. They are constructive comment points about explanation, record visibility, and administrability.

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

**Key sentences from the NPRM**

* “The proposed SNURs extend the protective measures from the signed consent orders...”
* “While EPA is unable to make any estimate of the likely number of export notifications...”

**Routine description situated in context**

This is a conventional TSCA new-chemicals follow-on proposal. EPA reviewed PMNs for battery-related mixed metal oxide substances, imposed order-based protections, and is now proposing SNURs so future firms cannot step outside those conditions without filing a SNUN first.

**Regulatory effectiveness, statutory compliance, procedural adequacy**

The proposal looks legally plausible and structurally normal. The better comment targets are explanatory completeness and administrability. EPA appears to have support for its hazard concerns, but the public-facing notice does not fully expose how the exact restrictions and thresholds were derived or how confidential significant new uses work in practice for outsiders.

**Real-world consequence of invoking the leverage**

If commenters press these points effectively, EPA may make the final record more transparent about threshold-setting, use-scope line drawing, confidentiality administration, and likely burden frequency. That would improve public understanding even if the agency keeps most of the proposed restrictions.

**Worth a comment?** **Moderate to high value**
