## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim: “allowing lawful handguns to be mailed under the same terms and conditions as lawful rifles and shotguns”

* **Underlying assumption:** The safety, diversion, and operational risks of mailing lawful handguns are close enough to the risks of mailing rifles and shotguns that USPS can largely use the same framework.
* **Failure scenario:** Handguns may pose different theft, concealability, diversion, or verification risks than rifles and shotguns, making a largely parallel mailing framework incomplete.
* **Procedural implication:** USPS may need to explain why handgun-specific risks do not require additional safeguards, data collection, or phased implementation.

### Claim: “specify clear definitions of ‘mailable firearms’ and ‘nonmailable firearms’”

* **Underlying assumption:** The proposed text is sufficiently clear for ordinary mailers, retail personnel, inspectors, and enforcement staff to distinguish lawful mailable firearms from nonmailable firearms.
* **Failure scenario:** Because the rule ties mailability to a mix of federal firearms law, postal law, state law, and local ordinances, users may struggle to know ex ante whether a shipment is lawful and mailable.
* **Procedural implication:** Clarity comments are especially relevant because USPS specifically invites comments on understandability.

### Claim: comments on “the scope of firearms that are mailable and nonmailable under the Second Amendment” are outside scope

* **Underlying assumption:** USPS can wall off debate about the breadth of the new mailability regime because OLC supplied the constitutional premise.
* **Failure scenario:** The proposed regulatory text still makes operational line-drawing choices, definitions, and implementation decisions that materially shape which firearms can be mailed and under what conditions.
* **Procedural implication:** USPS may be understating the legitimate scope of comment, especially where commenters are not challenging the Constitution but are instead challenging USPS’s implementation logic and clarity.

### Claim: tracking and signature capture are sufficient controls

* **Underlying assumption:** Tracking plus signature capture meaningfully addresses diversion, loss, and accountability concerns for newly mailable handguns.
* **Failure scenario:** Tracking and signature capture may not address upstream theft, misdescription, employee screening burdens, or inconsistent retail acceptance practices.
* **Procedural implication:** USPS may need a stronger explanation of why those controls were selected and whether alternatives were considered.

### Claim: mailers can comply with federal, state, and local laws through the proposed framework

* **Underlying assumption:** The regulated public can accurately determine the legality of interstate and intrastate firearm transfers using the rule’s general instructions.
* **Failure scenario:** Non-FFL mailers may misread the interaction between USPS rules and firearms law, particularly for temporary custodians, intrastate transfers, curios and relics, or state-specific possession rules.
* **Procedural implication:** The rule may create enforcement and consumer-confusion risk unless USPS adds examples, clearer instructions, or limiting language.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

* USPS proposes to revise Publication 52, subchapter 43, governing firearms.
* The notice says the revisions are intended to conform USPS regulations to a January 15, 2026 OLC opinion on 18 U.S.C. 1715.
* The proposal would define “Mailable Firearms” to include lawful pistols, revolvers, shotguns, and rifles.
* The proposal would define “Nonmailable Firearms” to include firearms that are generally unlawful to possess, including National Firearms Act items described in 431.2 and firearms not detectable by metal detectors and x-ray machines after removal of grips, stocks, and magazines.
* USPS would continue to require unloaded firearms, no external content markings, secure packaging, and in most cases tracking plus signature capture at delivery.
* The proposal retains special affidavit, statement, and official-use pathways for certain otherwise nonmailable handguns and for certain government shipments.
* The proposal also includes mailing rules for curios and relics, museum shipments, air guns, and indemnity claims.

### Inference

* This is not just a minor wording clean-up. It is a meaningful expansion of ordinary mailability for lawful handguns combined with a complicated effort to preserve older exception structures for other categories.
* The central policy choice is not whether USPS must respond to OLC, but how broadly USPS should translate that constitutional view into retail mailing practice, compliance verification, and operational controls.

### Evidence Quotes

* “The proposed revisions expand the scope of mailable firearms compared to the existing regulations by allowing lawful handguns to be mailed under the same terms and conditions as lawful rifles and shotguns.”
* “Lawful firearms, including pistols, revolvers, shotguns, and rifles, are mailable (‘Mailable Firearms’).”
* “Mailers must comply with the Gun Control Act of 1968, all provisions of postal law, and all other federal and state regulations and local ordinances affecting the movement of firearms.”

The strongest public comments should press USPS to explain those implementation choices, correct drafting ambiguities, and provide a clearer operational record before finalizing the change.
