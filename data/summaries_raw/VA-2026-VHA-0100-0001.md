## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

Claim:
- The 65-percent nursing-home-rate proxy is an appropriate basis for MMADHC reimbursement.
Underlying assumption:
- Current MMADHC service intensity and cost structure are stable enough that a fixed percentage of nursing-home per diem is a reasonable cross-facility proxy.
Failure scenario:
- Transportation burdens, staffing models, or service intensity vary enough across State homes that the proxy over-pays some homes and under-pays others.
Procedural implication:
- Commenters can ask VA to justify why a fixed percentage remains appropriate instead of a direct-cost model, regional adjustment, or periodic recalibration.

Claim:
- The "lesser of 65 percent or daily cost" cap best implements the statutory duty to "adequately reimburse."
Underlying assumption:
- Adequate reimbursement means no more than documented daily cost, and the invoiced cost measure is comparable across facilities.
Failure scenario:
- Daily-cost accounting practices differ materially across State homes, producing reimbursement differences driven by bookkeeping rather than care needs.
Procedural implication:
- The record would be stronger if VA explained how daily cost will be calculated, documented, and audited.

Claim:
- MMADHC helps medically complex veterans remain in the community and avoid institutionalization.
Underlying assumption:
- The cited literature and current State-home experience translate well to future MMADHC programs.
Failure scenario:
- The three current MMADHC homes are not representative, or program benefits depend on local features not likely to hold elsewhere.
Procedural implication:
- Commenters can request clearer baseline outcome data and program metrics.

Claim:
- The definitional changes improve clarity without changing the statutory program in a problematic way.
Underlying assumption:
- "Medical model" and "medical supervision model" are functionally interchangeable, and the added phrase about "such other requirements" will be read narrowly.
Failure scenario:
- Regulated parties interpret the definition more broadly than the statute allows.
Procedural implication:
- Commenters can ask VA to explain the boundaries of the Secretary's discretion more clearly.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

Observed Facts:
- VA proposes to amend 38 CFR part 51.
- The proposal adds definitions for MMADHC and State Home Care Agreement.
- The proposal revises § 51.41(a) so VA and a State home "will" enter into a contract or agreement for eligible veterans, and it limits MMADHC per diem to veterans who would otherwise need nursing-home care.
- The proposal adds § 51.41(d), under which MMADHC payment is the lesser of 65 percent of the nursing-home prevailing rate or the daily cost of care shown on the invoice.
- The principal authority cited is 38 U.S.C. 1745(d), added by Public Law 115-159.

Inference:
- The proposal is less about creating new authority than about codifying the reimbursement formula and related definitions for a program Congress already authorized.
- The operational stakes are real because payment design can affect whether additional State homes decide to offer MMADHC.

Evidence quotes:
- "VA proposes to amend two of its State Veterans Home regulations."
- "A State home care agreement for MMADHC will provide for payments at the lesser amount of either: (1) sixty-five percent of the rate calculated for nursing home care under paragraph (c) of this section, or (2) the daily cost of care as provided on the invoice."

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

1. Facility variation failure:
- Homes with higher transportation burdens or different staffing mixes may find the formula under-reimbursing even if other homes do not.

2. Cost-accounting failure:
- If invoiced "daily cost" is not standardized, reimbursement may hinge on bookkeeping practices rather than service realities.

3. Access failure:
- Potential new entrants may conclude MMADHC is not financially viable under the capped formula.

4. Eligibility-application failure:
- The note limiting MMADHC to veterans who would otherwise need nursing-home care may be applied unevenly if VA does not explain documentation expectations.

5. Monitoring failure:
- The rule incorporates criteria from § 51.40(b) and (d), but the record does not explain whether those criteria fit MMADHC-specific attendance and cost questions.
