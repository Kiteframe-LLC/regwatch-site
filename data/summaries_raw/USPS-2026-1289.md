# Ballot Mail for Federal Elections

## Phase 1 — Docket Integrity Check

Classification: Thin record.

Observed Fact: The selected document is a Postal Service proposed rule titled "Ballot Mail for Federal Elections." The notice states that USPS proposes to amend the Domestic Mail Manual, incorporated by reference in 39 CFR part 111, for mail-in and absentee ballots in federal general, special, and runoff elections. The metadata supplied for this review reports zero supporting or related materials and zero ingested supporting materials.

Observed Fact: The notice describes OMB interagency review after Executive Order 14399 and before publication. It cites USPS statutory authority in 39 U.S.C. 401 and 404 and several federal election-law provisions, but it does not identify a separate regulatory impact analysis, privacy analysis, implementation-cost analysis, state/local implementation assessment, alternatives memorandum, or technical portal specification.

Inference: For a USPS mail-preparation standards rule, the core legal text may often be contained in the notice itself. Here, however, the rule would require election officials and authorized mail service providers to submit voter names, addresses, and unique outbound and return Intelligent Mail barcodes through a Federal Ballot Mail Portal before outbound federal ballot mailings. That makes implementation feasibility, privacy, state election-administration fit, and operational timing central to the proposal. The available docket spine is therefore thin for those issues.

Attachment assessment: The metadata reports no supporting materials. The notice cites existing public USPS resources, including Kit 600 and general Election Mail practices pages, but the proposed obligations are described in the notice text. Those cited resources would be useful background but are not required to understand the proposed rule's main obligations. No attachment download is blocking this summary.

### Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

Claim: Uniform standards will facilitate faithful execution of federal law.
Underlying assumption: Federal law-enforcement utility follows from a state-specific list of voters, addresses, and unique barcodes for federal ballot mail.
Failure scenario: A state election workflow, late ballot issuance window, privacy law, vendor process, or local office capacity makes timely complete list submission difficult, creating nonacceptance risks without a clear federal-law benefit.
Procedural implication: The final rule would be stronger if USPS explains how the participation list will be used, what safeguards apply, and how the rule avoids disrupting lawful state mail-ballot administration.

Claim: Unique IMbs facilitate tracking of individual pieces of ballot mail.
Underlying assumption: Every relevant outbound and return ballot mailpiece can practically carry a unique, properly embedded barcode tied to the correct voter record.
Failure scenario: A jurisdiction or mail service provider uses emergency reprints, replacement ballots, same-day corrections, small-volume retail entry, or mixed vendor workflows that create barcode mismatches.
Procedural implication: Commenters can ask USPS to test and explain edge cases, correction pathways, and acceptance consequences.

Claim: The participation list will help compare mailed ballots to received ballots to detect issues meriting investigation.
Underlying assumption: A comparison of mailed-ballot counts and received-ballot counts is a reliable screening method and will not be misread when ballots are spoiled, replaced, undelivered, surrendered, cured, or returned by nonmail channels.
Failure scenario: Normal election-administration events produce differences between outbound and returned ballots that appear suspicious without context.
Procedural implication: USPS should explain limits on the inference, required data fields, and whether the list is intended as an investigative lead rather than a compliance determination.

Claim: Noncompliant outbound federal ballot mail will not be accepted.
Underlying assumption: Acceptance personnel can verify compliance accurately and fast enough to avoid election-mail delay.
Failure scenario: A barcode, portal, certification, or data-file issue is discovered near a state mailing deadline, and the proposed rule gives limited detail on emergency acceptance, cure timing, escalation, or review.
Procedural implication: The record would benefit from service-standard and contingency detail.

### Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

Observed Facts:

- USPS proposes to add DMM 705.24, "Ballot Mail for Federal Elections."
- The rule would apply to mail-in or absentee ballots for federal general, special, and runoff elections, excluding primaries and UOCAVA ballots.
- Outbound and return federal ballot envelopes would need the official Election Mail logo, automation compatibility, unique IMbs with Delivery Point ZIP Code embedded and a Federal Ballot Mail Service Type Identifier, and USPS mailpiece design review.
- A state's chief election official could provide an optional notice at least 90 days before a federal election.
- Ballot Portal Users would submit voter name, voter address, unique outbound IMb, unique return IMb, and originating election-office state through a USPS Federal Ballot Mail Portal.
- Portal users would provide this information at least 30 days before a federal election to the extent practicable, or by the state-law date on which mail-in or absentee ballots may begin to be mailed, with supplemental submissions allowed until the last day ballots may be mailed under state law.
- USPS would provide each state's chief election official a state-specific Mail-In and Absentee Participation List on or about election day.
- USPS would verify outbound federal ballot mail before acceptance and return noncompliant mailings to the authorized ballot mailer.

Inference:

- The proposal would create a new federal postal acceptance condition for many outbound federal ballot mailings and would require election officials or their authorized providers to associate individual voters with both outbound and return barcode identifiers.
- The principal comment leverage is implementation detail: timing, privacy, authority fit, error correction, acceptance review, operational burden, and alternative ways to achieve mailstream visibility without risking late ballot-mail disruptions.

Evidence quotes:

- "Any state that intends to receive mail-in or absentee ballots from individual voters through the Postal Service must ensure that such individuals have been enrolled with the Postal Service for inclusion on the state's Mail-In and Absentee Participation List."
- "Mailings that do not comply with 24.5.1 and 24.5.2 will not be accepted and will be returned to the authorized ballot mailer."
- "The Postal Service does not purport to alter the eligibility of any individual to vote under state or federal law."

### Step 3 — Agency Argument Tree

Regulatory objective:
Protect federal election integrity and facilitate enforcement of federal law concerning ballots transmitted through the mail.

Problem definition:
The notice frames mail-in and absentee ballot transmission as requiring additional measures to reduce fraud risk, improve enforceability, and implement Executive Order 14399.

Data used:
The notice relies mainly on USPS operational knowledge of Election Mail logo use, automation compatibility, IMb scan data, design review, and existing extraordinary measures. No quantitative data are provided on fraud risk, barcode error rates, state readiness, portal capacity, acceptance delays, cost, or privacy impacts.

Analytical method:
USPS proposes standardized envelope design plus unique IMbs, data submission through a Federal Ballot Mail Portal, state-specific list compilation, and acceptance verification for outbound ballot mail.

Alternatives considered:
The notice does not present an alternatives analysis. It acknowledges primary-election and UOCAVA exclusions and requests comments generally, but does not evaluate phased implementation, pilot programs, alternative data fields, aggregate reporting, optional participation, lower-risk verification models, or emergency cure procedures.

Agency conclusion:
The proposed DMM amendments are within USPS authority, support law-enforcement use of mail-related information, and will not change longstanding processing and delivery practices for ballots that enter the mailstream.

Missing links:

- The notice does not explain the expected fraud-prevention or enforcement benefit relative to the operational burden and risk of ballot-mail nonacceptance.
- The notice does not explain how "to the extent practicable" interacts with the mandatory acceptance consequences.
- The notice does not provide privacy, records, access, retention, or data-sharing details for voter names, addresses, and barcode links, though it states a SORN is forthcoming.
- The notice does not explain whether small jurisdictions, vendors, or low-volume mailers can comply on the same timeline as large centralized election offices.

### Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

Regulatory objective: Improve ballot-mail visibility and enforcement support.

Alternatives evaluated: Required unique IMbs, Election Mail logo, automation compatibility, mailpiece design review, portal enrollment, state-specific participation lists, and outbound acceptance verification.

Plausible alternatives not analyzed:

- Phased implementation beginning with voluntary pilot states or high-volume jurisdictions.
- Aggregate ballot-mail reporting without voter-name and barcode pairing for all mailings.
- A safe harbor allowing acceptance of mailings with minor correctable portal or barcode errors when election deadlines are near.
- Post-mailing reconciliation rather than pre-acceptance rejection for certain low-risk errors.
- Different standards for small-volume mailers, retail-entry mailings, replacement ballots, emergency ballots, and late court-ordered ballots.
- Data minimization approaches, including hashed identifiers or limited retention.

Evidence suggesting feasibility: The notice states USPS already recommends the Election Mail logo, automation compatibility, IMbs, and mailpiece design review and will continue to provide technical support. That supports feasibility for some envelope standards, but not necessarily for individualized portal enrollment and pre-acceptance verification across all states and jurisdictions.

### Step 6 — Technical Coherence

The technical premise that unique IMbs improve mailstream visibility is coherent with ordinary USPS tracking practices. The proposed rule is less complete on implementation mechanics.

Observed Fact: USPS says technical instructions for barcode creation, service type identifiers, acceptance processes, file preparation, documentation submission, and portal entry will be provided with any final rule.

Inference: Because these details affect whether election officials can comply, deferring them creates comment difficulty. Commenters may not be able to evaluate whether portal submission formats, correction processes, small-volume acceptance, replacement ballots, and vendor workflows are workable.

Unknown: The record does not show portal capacity, cybersecurity controls, privacy architecture, data retention periods, user-authorization controls, barcode mismatch rates, or acceptance-review time estimates.

### Step 7 — Legal Grounding

Observed Fact: USPS cites 39 U.S.C. 401 and 404 as mail-regulation authority. It also references 52 U.S.C. 10307, 52 U.S.C. 20511, and 18 U.S.C. 3061(b) as examples related to election-law enforcement and mail-related crimes. The notice states that 39 U.S.C. 410(a) exempts USPS from APA notice-and-comment requirements but USPS is inviting public comment.

Inference: USPS has clear authority to set mail preparation, handling, acceptance, and delivery standards. The most important legal-grounding question is whether requiring state-provided individualized voter/barcode lists and using nonacceptance as the enforcement mechanism remains sufficiently tied to postal operations rather than state election administration.

Uncertain: The notice states that USPS does not alter voter eligibility and that states retain control of the list contents. The record does not yet explain how this boundary will operate if a state, local office, or authorized mailer fails to enroll a voter, submits an incorrect barcode, or faces a portal outage near a ballot-mailing deadline.

### Step 9 — Failure Modes

- Portal or account-registration failure close to a ballot-mailing deadline.
- Incorrect association between a voter's name/address and outbound or return IMb.
- Replacement ballots, spoiled ballots, emergency ballots, or late-added voters requiring supplemental submissions after ordinary production files are complete.
- Mailings rejected because of certification, barcode, or list-format errors, producing downstream election-timing problems.
- Small jurisdictions or retail-counter ballot mailings lacking staff or vendor systems needed for individualized barcode/list compliance.
- A comparison between mailed-ballot and received-ballot counts being misinterpreted without accounting for normal election-administration events.
- Privacy concerns if voter participation lists are retained, shared, accessed, or secured without clear limits.

### Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

The proposal could encourage election officials and vendors to standardize envelope design and barcode tracking, which may improve mailstream visibility. It could also create incentives to avoid USPS for certain ballot workflows if compliance uncertainty is high, to batch mailings in ways that minimize supplemental submissions, or to delay mail entry while resolving portal/list issues. If nonacceptance is the main enforcement tool, the incentive structure may prioritize technical compliance over timely ballot delivery unless USPS creates clear cure and escalation procedures.

### Step 13 — Litigation Vulnerabilities

The main APA-style vulnerability is not the existence of mailpiece standards. It is the possibility that USPS finalizes individualized portal enrollment and nonacceptance consequences without explaining feasibility, privacy safeguards, alternatives, state-election-administration effects, and the evidentiary link between the list requirement and the stated law-enforcement objectives.

Because USPS states it is exempt from APA notice-and-comment requirements under 39 U.S.C. 410(a), litigation posture may differ from ordinary agency rulemaking. Still, a stronger final record would explain why the final design is reasonable, operationally workable, and within postal rather than election-administration functions.

Score: 7 out of 10.

Rationale: The rule has a clear proposed regulatory text and a plausible postal-operations foundation for envelope design and barcode visibility. The concern score is elevated because the proposal imposes individualized voter/barcode list submission and outbound mail nonacceptance consequences while leaving important feasibility, privacy, timing, alternatives, and state/local capacity questions underdeveloped.

### Step 16 — Evidence Requests

- Portal technical specifications, including file layouts, validation rules, account controls, correction processes, and outage procedures.
- Estimated implementation costs for state election officials, local election offices, and authorized mail service providers.
- Data on current use of Election Mail logos, automation-compatible ballot envelopes, and unique IMbs across jurisdictions.
- Evidence supporting the 30-day timing standard and how it aligns with state-law ballot-mailing calendars.
- Expected barcode mismatch, rejection, and correction rates based on USPS experience with similar mailings.
- Privacy Act analysis, including SORN details, retention periods, access controls, permitted disclosures, correction rights, and law-enforcement sharing limits.
- Analysis of normal reasons mailed-ballot and returned-ballot counts differ.
- Alternatives considered, including phased implementation, pilots, safe harbors, post-mailing reconciliation, and data minimization.

### Step 17 — High-Leverage Comment Angles

1. Request a practical implementation record. Ask USPS to publish portal specifications, file formats, test procedures, acceptance timing, escalation pathways, and contingency plans before finalizing mandatory nonacceptance consequences.

2. Propose safe harbors and cure procedures. Suggest that minor barcode/list errors, portal outages, replacement ballots, and late emergency mailings should have clear correction paths that protect timely ballot delivery.

3. Ask for data minimization and privacy safeguards. Request that USPS explain why each data field is necessary, how long records will be retained, who can access them, and how law-enforcement uses will be limited and audited.

4. Request an alternatives analysis. Ask USPS to compare phased pilots, aggregate reporting, post-mailing reconciliation, optional early participation, and special rules for small-volume or local election offices.

5. Clarify the boundary with state election administration. Ask USPS to specify that mail acceptance decisions will not function as voter-eligibility determinations and to explain what happens when list errors affect otherwise eligible voters' ballots.

### Step 18 — Decision Brief

Strongest leverage point: Implementation feasibility and nonacceptance consequences.

Key sentences from the NPRM:

- "Mailings that do not comply with 24.5.1 and 24.5.2 will not be accepted and will be returned to the authorized ballot mailer."
- "Coinciding with publication of any final rule, to facilitate compliance by officials mailing ballots to voters, the Postal Service intends to provide further technical instructions regarding barcode creation, service type identifiers, acceptance processes, file preparation, documentation submission, and entry of data into the proposed portal."

Routine description situated in the context of the rule, law, and research: USPS is using its mail-preparation authority to condition acceptance of outbound federal ballot mail on envelope standards, individualized barcode/list submission, certification, and verification. Because election mail operates under strict statutory and practical deadlines, acceptance rejection can have a direct practical effect even if USPS does not decide voter eligibility.

Regulatory effectiveness: The rule could improve ballot-mail visibility if implementation is reliable. It could undermine effectiveness if compliance errors or portal issues delay outbound ballots.

Statutory compliance: USPS has strong postal authority for mailpiece design and handling. The record should better explain why individualized voter/barcode list requirements and rejection of noncompliant outbound ballot mail are necessary postal measures rather than election-administration controls.

Procedural adequacy: The notice provides proposed text but leaves important technical, privacy, and contingency details for later. Comments can ask USPS to publish those details and evaluate alternatives before finalizing.

Real-world consequence of invoking the leverage: A well-supported comment could lead USPS to add safe harbors, staged implementation, technical testing periods, emergency correction procedures, privacy limits, and clearer state/local coordination.

Worth a comment? High value.

Comment posture: evidence request | alternatives push | procedural concern
