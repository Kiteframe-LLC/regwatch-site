## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_4
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A written-and-spoken English proficiency requirement can be defined and tested in a way that fits customer-service work.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the standard is vague or culturally specific, providers may game the test or over-screen workers without improving service.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should explain how it would measure proficiency and what evidence would show that the requirement improves outcomes.
- **Claim Id:** claim_5
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A single percentage cap can capture the real compliance problem across all service channels and traffic types.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If inbound and outbound calls, transfers, and non-call communications differ materially, one cap may oversimplify the problem or miss the main risk.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask the FCC to justify the metric, the denominator, and the compliance period.
- **Claim Id:** claim_6
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Notice at call start and a right to transfer will materially improve consumer experience and information control.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If transferred calls take longer or create routing friction, the rule may add process without improving resolution.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should explain how transfer timing, routing, and customer choice would work in practice.
- **Claim Id:** claim_7
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Sensitive-information handling can be cleanly separated from ordinary customer-service interactions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If account and identity verification steps occur throughout a call or chat, the rule may be difficult to administer consistently.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should define the covered communications and explain how mixed-purpose interactions are classified.
- **Claim Id:** claim_9
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Self-tracking and reporting will produce accurate compliance information without major audit infrastructure.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If providers can count calls differently or rely on vendors with inconsistent records, reported compliance may be hard to compare or verify.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for audit standards, sampling rules, or reporting templates.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Observed Facts**
- The FCC is proposing rules for providers that use foreign call centers or related offshore customer-service operations.
- The proposed framework covers telecommunications services, CMRS, interconnected VoIP, cable television, DBS, and related affiliates, with possible expansion to other services.
- The proposal would require American Standard English proficiency, a cap on offshore call handling, customer notice and transfer rights, U.S.-only handling of sensitive-information communications, and a ban on representatives in foreign adversary nations.
- The notice also seeks comment on extending some or all of the rules to emails, texts, and online chats.
- The notice includes a PRA discussion, a Small Business Paperwork Relief Act request, and an IRFA.

**Affected Entities**
- Telecommunications, CMRS, interconnected VoIP, cable, and DBS providers
- Affiliates that provide internet access service
- Providers using offshore customer-service vendors or foreign call centers
- Small entities that may need new testing, routing, and reporting systems
- Consumers who rely on customer service by phone, text, chat, or email

**Inference**
- This is a consumer-protection and data-security proposal that tries to move customer service closer to the United States while also making offshore operations more transparent and more constrained.
- The key public-comment questions are not whether customer service should improve, but whether these specific tools are measurable, administrable, and proportionate.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The Commission proposes actions to encourage and facilitate the onshoring of foreign call centers.
- The proposal would improve customer service communications and better protect consumers' sensitive personal information.
- Customers would have the right to have the call transferred to a customer representative located within the United States.
- The proposal would bar use of customer representatives located in foreign adversary nations.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Mode:** Compliance by self-reporting only
  
  **Trigger:** Providers track and report their own offshore-call handling without independent audit hooks.
  
  **Consequence:** Reported compliance may not match actual routing behavior.
  
  **Comment Focus:** Ask for audit, sampling, or spot-check mechanics.
- **Mode:** Metric gaming
  
  **Trigger:** A single percentage cap is measured inconsistently across inbound, outbound, and transferred calls.
  
  **Consequence:** Providers can satisfy the rule on paper while the operational burden remains unchanged.
  
  **Comment Focus:** Ask the FCC to define the denominator and measurement period.
- **Mode:** Channel mismatch
  
  **Trigger:** The same rule covers calls, email, text, and chat, but those channels are operationally different.
  
  **Consequence:** Providers may overcomply in low-risk channels or undercomply in high-risk ones.
  
  **Comment Focus:** Ask the FCC to separate channel-specific rules if it wants to regulate them differently.
- **Mode:** Language standard drift
  
  **Trigger:** American Standard English is used as a compliance concept without a specific test standard.
  
  **Consequence:** Providers may choose inconsistent screening methods.
  
  **Comment Focus:** Ask for a test or benchmark that does not depend on subjective grading.
- **Mode:** Transfer delay backlash
  
  **Trigger:** Calls may have to be transferred on request.
  
  **Consequence:** If transfers are slow, customers may get longer waits instead of better service.
  
  **Comment Focus:** Ask for a transfer-time standard or a fallback rule.
