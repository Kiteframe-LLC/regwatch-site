## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** A tailored regime will facilitate capital formation while protecting investors.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Principles-based disclosures and antifraud liability provide information and deterrence comparable to more prescriptive registration requirements.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Early-stage projects provide broad narratives without reliable financial, governance, custody, or network-performance information.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request comparative evidence showing which investor-relevant information is gained and lost under each exemption.
- **Claim:** The $5 million and $75 million thresholds are appropriate.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The thresholds divide offerings by risk and issuer capacity without encouraging regulatory arbitrage.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** An issuer structures repeated offerings or affiliates around the limits, or a $75 million offering remains too large for the proposed disclosure package.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask the Commission to explain threshold selection, aggregation, anti-evasion controls, and sensitivity to alternative limits.
- **Claim:** The safe harbor can identify when an asset is no longer subject to an investment contract.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The conditions are objectively measurable and can be verified consistently over time.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Issuers self-certify changing managerial efforts or network conditions while investors and regulators lack timely information to test the certification.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request objective metrics, update duties, Commission review triggers, and consequences for mistaken status claims.
- **Claim:** State registration preemption is justified for qualified purchasers.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Federal disclosures and antifraud rules sufficiently replace state review for this class of offering.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** States lose the ability to address local fraud, merit-review, or enforcement concerns while federal information is incomplete.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for a documented comparison of federal protections and state protections displaced by preemption.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The SEC proposes Regulation Crypto Assets: a startup exemption capped at $5 million over four years, a fundraising exemption capped at $75 million per 12 months with enhanced disclosure and reporting, a conditional safe harbor from investment-contract status, and related state-registration preemption.

**Affected Entities**
- Crypto-asset issuers and project sponsors
- Investors, including retail investors and qualified purchasers
- Trading platforms, transfer agents, broker-dealers, and other intermediaries
- State securities administrators
- Auditors, counsel, and service providers supporting covered offerings

**Statutory Authority:** The notice invokes the Securities Act of 1933 and Exchange Act of 1934, including registration and security definitions, and proposes rules under the Commission's exemptive and rulemaking authority.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal would replace case-by-case reliance on existing securities-law frameworks with tailored exemptions and a conditional status safe harbor.

**Observed Facts**
- The proposal keeps antifraud and antimanipulation provisions applicable to exempt offerings.
- The second exemption requires financial statements and ongoing reporting.
- The proposal includes inflation adjustment, bad-actor disqualification, forms, recordkeeping, and a state-preemption provision.

**Inference:** The proposal is a substantial framework change whose practical effects depend on the boundary between covered investment contracts and assets that qualify for the safe harbor.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The first exemption would permit offerings of up to $5 million during a four-year period.
- Issuers that rely on these exemptions would remain subject to the antifraud and antimanipulation provisions of the Federal securities laws.
- If the conditions of that proposed safe harbor are satisfied, then a crypto asset would be deemed not to be subject to an investment contract.
