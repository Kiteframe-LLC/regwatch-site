## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **C1—SI scope.** Assumption: neither compute nor end-use distinctions can reliably separate sufficiently safe transactions. Negation: if a workable combination of characteristics can isolate lower-risk development, the case for the full prohibition weakens. Counterfactual: a covered foreign firm improving a narrow civilian application may satisfy the broad definition without frontier capability. Adversarial reading: claims of merely using a third-party model could mask capability-enhancing development. Implication: compare a multifactor approach and test the internal-use boundary with examples. Treasury explicitly solicits alternatives; the missing comparison is an open design issue, not proof that no risk exists.
- **C2—Biotechnology.** Assumption: the specified categories reliably capture acute national-security risk. Negation: ordinary biofuels or established medicines may produce burdens without comparable security gains. Counterfactual: an existing vaccine producer and an advanced biological design platform can fall under different risk profiles despite broad statutory implementation language. Adversarial reading: shift activity among equipment, software and biological-system descriptions. Implication: evaluate an advanced/frontier focus, equipment-linked limits and essential-health exclusions; Treasury asks about several of these.
- **C3—Cost population.** Assumption: doubling completed deals adequately captures the future population requiring regulatory diligence. Negation: abandoned prospects, indirect structures and newly covered civilian sectors could change that population. Counterfactual: notification volume may fall as more investments become prohibited while screening and feedback costs rise. Implication: split completed, screened, abandoned and feedback transactions; test several capture-rate scenarios.
- **C4—Small entities.** Assumption: investor sophistication, cross-border staffing and an industry mean predict few affected small firms. Counterfactual: a specialized fund or small strategic investor may be below its applicable size standard yet make a covered investment. Implication: tabulate entities by actual size and sector; do not infer the distribution from the average.
- **C5—Feedback.** Assumption: binding material terms are needed to filter hypothetical requests. Counterfactual: a detailed, good-faith nonbinding term sheet could support useful guidance earlier. Adversarial reading: limited answers or repeated completeness demands can leave a requester unable to plan a closing. Implication: evaluate staged eligibility, written completeness decisions and service targets without mischaracterizing feedback as a license.
- **C6—Proof.** The enforcement burden expressly remains with Treasury. Ordinary compliance inquiry should not be described as unlawful private self-policing solely because the investor classifies its own transaction.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

Treasury proposes a new Part 851 for outbound investment notification and prohibitions under the COINS Act’s addition of Title VIII to the Defense Production Act (50 U.S.C. 4581–4589). Part 850 would continue to govern activities during its specified earlier period.

The proposal broadens country and technology coverage, prohibits covered investments involving development of any defined “super intelligence” system (the proposal’s term for broadly defined AI), and adds biotechnology restrictions. Certain semiconductor, computing-interconnect and hypersonic activities would require notification. Exceptions remain important: these are restrictions on specified investments involving covered foreign persons, not a ban on all AI use, biomedical research, or trade.

**Observed Fact:** Proposed §851.226(h) says “Development of any SI system; provided that:” and includes third-party-system qualifications. **Inference:** The change can reach ordinary commercial applications as well as advanced models. Its practical reach depends on coverage, knowledge and exceptions.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Objective: restrict security-relevant investment while limiting unnecessary burdens.** IV.D evaluates fewer exceptions and broad sectoral definitions. The preamble also expressly solicits narrower SI and biotechnology designs, three definitions of maintaining existing operations, possible business-development-company and foreign-fund exceptions, and streamlined national-interest exemptions.

**Material comparisons still useful:** (1) a multifactor SI boundary combining capability, end use and development activity rather than a single training-compute threshold; (2) biotechnology coverage limited by advanced/frontier characteristics, specified equipment or sensitive uses, with separately justified treatment of ordinary medicines and biofuels; (3) early feedback on documented but nonbinding terms; (4) illustrative maintenance, safety-upgrade and capacity-expansion boundaries for intracompany transfers. These lie within the proposal’s stated design choices; their feasibility must be tested, not assumed. Treasury’s existing threshold distinctions, third-party-use qualifications and enumerated requests provide concrete evidence that alternative boundaries are administrable topics for review.

The public record does not yet compare residual security risk, compliance cost and enforcement feasibility across these narrower options. This is predominantly deliberate solicitation, not omission of all alternatives.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Observed Fact:** The rule specifies semiconductor parameters, including memory bandwidth density above 2 GB/s/mm², supercomputer capacities of 100 double-precision or 200 single-precision petaflops within a specified volume, and hypersonic speed at Mach 5 or greater. Notifiable technology is generally the remaining specified category, subject to prohibited-technology precedence. This is a parameter-based regulatory design, not an engineering model.

**SI boundary:** §851.232 includes predictive and generative systems, agent harnesses and embodied systems. The name “super intelligence” does not limit coverage to superhuman or frontier capabilities. Section 851.226(h) distinguishes enhancing a third-party system from strictly internal, noncommercial use. Examples should address third-party integration in commercial products and internal business operations; the exception should not be assumed to cover every form of customization.

**Two internal tensions warrant clarification.** II.B’s quantum discussion introduces an end-use limitation, but §851.226(j) covers development of any quantum-information-science algorithm or application; express end-use qualifiers appear in (k) and (l). Treasury should specify which activity the preamble’s limitation modifies. IV.D describes “all super intelligence” as a broad alternative creating uncertainty, while (h) prohibits development of any SI system. Development and third-party-use limits distinguish the texts, so this is a request to reconcile the reasoning, not a claim that the two formulations are identical.

**Unknown:** Technical validation of each parameter against underlying classified evidence or external export-control datasets. No such validation is inferred.

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

**Observed Fact:** Proposed Part 851 cites 50 U.S.C. 4581–4589 and 31 U.S.C. 321. The notice describes statutory notification and prohibition authorities, exceptions, technical-parameter discretion, additional-category authority subject to consultation and notice and comment, and feedback requirements. Biotechnology is an agency-added category under that described authority; additional countries are attributed to the statute. Country-specific statutory distinctions should not be confused with arbitrary geographic targeting.

**Uncertain interpretations requiring explanation:** How the breadth of SI/biotechnology restrictions satisfies the described national-security nexus and least-burdensome approach; the basis for including individuals and relationship-based entities beyond the enumerated statutory examples; and the reconciliation of overlapping bank-lending and other exceptions with equity-like debt coverage. The notice offers an anti-circumvention rationale for the relationship provisions; that rationale should be evaluated rather than ignored.

The statutory account has not been independently checked against the enrolled Act. No conclusion that Treasury lacks authority is justified from this review. Part 851 is prospective; Part 850’s continued applicability preserves earlier obligations. Removing a program-specific criminal penalty provision does not eliminate false-statement liability or penalties under other applicable laws; §851.701 expressly preserves them. No material federalism concern is identified in this record.

**Country coverage as written:** §851.207 lists China (including Hong Kong and Macau), Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, and Venezuela under the regime specified in that provision. The preamble says the named Venezuelan regime no longer exists; this review does not independently verify that geopolitical assertion. Treasury should explain the practical effect of retaining the regime-specific wording and provide clear applicability guidance rather than leaving investors to infer it.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Overinclusive scope:** a low-risk commercial SI application or routine biological product can draw the same investment prohibition as a high-risk technology; case-by-case exemptions may not offer timely relief.
- **Diligence uncertainty:** investor access to target data varies, especially in secondary or indirect transactions. Treasury’s information becomes an express knowledge-standard factor without a dedicated rebuttal timetable.
- **Timing mismatch:** binding prospective terms are needed for feedback, but no response is guaranteed; negotiation costs or deal deadlines may arrive before useful guidance.
- **Maintenance ambiguity:** a safety upgrade to an existing controlled-entity operation could be treated differently from maintenance or prohibited expansion depending on unresolved boundaries.
- **Coverage error:** related-party listings or downstream technology activity may be missed; an investor might incorrectly apply a publicly traded, fund, ordinary-business or banking exception.
- **Capacity pressure:** new feedback and tips processes compete for staff with monitoring and enforcement. The notice identifies IT and some staffing costs, so failure is a risk to test rather than a proven inability to operate.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

Prohibitions can redirect transactions toward non-U.S. sources, public securities, excepted fund interests or different structures. The proposal anticipates evasion and preserves knowledge-based and governance-right limits; these features reduce, but do not eliminate, incentive risks. Ordinary use of a lawful exception is not itself evasion.

The $50,000 notification-only exception could encourage transaction splitting; aggregation across the preceding 12 months is a meaningful safeguard. The $2 million limited-partner exception aggregates fund investment and co-investment vehicles and is distinct from the $50,000 rule. Investors may choose binding contractual assurances, but assurances do not replace all coverage analysis or Treasury’s enforcement burden. More commercial development may be described as internal third-party use, making examples and documentation expectations useful. No bad-faith conduct is inferred from these design incentives.
