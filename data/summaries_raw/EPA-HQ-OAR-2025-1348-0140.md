## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** No revision to current standards is necessary.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The current standards remain sufficient in light of developments in practices, processes, and control technologies.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** New control technologies, operational practices, or emissions patterns make the existing standards underinclusive for currently regulated emission points.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can test whether EPA has shown its technology-review analysis is up to date for each regulated emission point.
- **Claim:** The Agency has no obligation to regulate previously unregulated emission points in this 112(d)(6) review.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Section 112(d)(6) can be read more narrowly than a full MACT-floor analysis for previously unregulated sources.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The interpretation is too narrow and leaves identifiable HAP emissions without a persuasive statutory rationale for deferral.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Public comments can focus on whether the no-obligation theory fits the statute and case law.
- **Claim:** New standards for previously unregulated emission points would impose minimal costs and no emissions impacts.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Affected facilities already use controls or work practices capable of meeting the proposed levels.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Baseline controls vary materially across facilities, and the proposal underestimates compliance costs or overstates current performance.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to show the baseline distribution of controls, costs, and facility types for each new standard.
- **Claim:** No reported methanol emissions from transmission and storage facilities justify excluding subpart HHH from the methanol standard.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The ICR responses adequately capture methanol emissions and are representative enough for the source category.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Reporting gaps, categorical differences, or incomplete responses hide methanol emissions that should be considered.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Public comments can challenge the representativeness of the 2023 ICR response set.
- **Claim:** Removing the associated-equipment carveout will reduce burdens while aligning with the CAA.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The present carveout is inconsistent with the statute and the burden reduction does not create an under-regulation problem.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The reinterpretation changes regulated-universe boundaries without enough explanation of compliance and enforcement effects.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to explain how the new definition changes source counting, compliance scope, and enforcement in practice.
- **Claim:** The proposed compliance dates are workable.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Facilities can acquire equipment and implement the proposed changes within the proposed timeline.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Lead times, contractor availability, or internal capital cycles make the schedule too compressed for some facilities.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can submit concrete implementation timelines and equipment procurement evidence.
