### Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

Claim: The $10 billion threshold accounts for inflation or market changes since 1996.

Underlying assumption: Market growth, interagency consistency, and current regulatory threshold conventions are sufficient to identify $10 billion as the right line.

Failure scenario: If institutions below $10 billion can still create meaningful interlock-related competitive concerns in some regional or product markets, the bright-line threshold could be underinclusive.

Procedural implication: The agency can strengthen the final rule by explaining why $10 billion is preferable to inflation-adjusted, indexed, tiered, or intermediate thresholds.

Claim: Raising the thresholds reduces regulatory burden.

Underlying assumption: Exemption applications impose meaningful burden and the reduced filing universe will not create offsetting competition concerns.

Failure scenario: If exemption applications are rare, quick, or low cost, the burden-relief rationale may be directionally true but overstated.

Procedural implication: Commenters can ask for historical exemption application counts, approval rates, average processing time, and estimated cost savings.

Claim: Removing the minority/women presumption avoids Equal Protection issues.

Underlying assumption: Deleting the presumption is the appropriate way to avoid constitutional risk, rather than narrowing, evidentiary support, or race/sex-neutral replacement criteria.

Failure scenario: If the presumption served as a proxy for governance or recruitment challenges that remain relevant, deletion without replacement may reduce useful flexibility for affected institutions.

Procedural implication: The final rule should explain whether the remaining exemption factors adequately address newly chartered, community-based, low-income-area, or recruitment-constrained institutions.

### Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

Observed Facts:

- The proposal would amend 12 CFR 711.3(c) so the major-assets prohibition applies when both unaffiliated depository organizations have total assets exceeding $10 billion.
- Current NCUA rules use $2.5 billion and $1.5 billion thresholds.
- The proposal would remove current 12 CFR 711.6(b)(2), which creates a rebuttable presumption for a depository organization controlled or managed by persons who are members of a minority group or women.
- The proposal leaves the community and RMSA interlock prohibitions in place.
- The notice states that 309 credit unions exceeded $1.5 billion, 190 exceeded $2.5 billion, and 20 exceeded $10 billion as of December 31, 2024.

Inference:

- The proposal is principally deregulatory for credit unions below $10 billion that would no longer need exemptions from the major-assets prohibition.
- The competition-protection theory shifts more weight onto the community and RMSA prohibitions for institutions below $10 billion.
- The deletion of the presumption may matter most for institutions that would otherwise seek an exemption and rely on diversity-related governance facts as part of the application.

Evidence quotes:

- "Raising the $1.5 billion asset threshold to $10 billion would exempt 289 credit unions from the major assets prohibition as of December 31, 2024."
- "The proposal would amend current section 711.6(b)(2) of the NCUA's regulations to remove the presumption for institutions controlled or managed by persons who are members of a minority group or women and seeking to add a management official."

### Step 3 — Agency Argument Tree

Regulatory objective:

Reduce unnecessary regulatory burden while preserving DIMIA's competition-protection function.

Problem definition:

The 1996 thresholds no longer reflect the size of large institutions after asset growth and consolidation.

Data used:

The notice cites 341 percent total asset growth from Q4 1996 to Q4 2024, current credit union asset counts, and the 2019 OCC/Federal Reserve/FDIC threshold change.

Analytical method:

The agency relies on market growth, consistency with other banking regulators, and the use of $10 billion in other regulatory contexts.

Alternatives considered:

The notice does not present a formal alternatives analysis. It does not compare $10 billion with indexed thresholds, separate upper/lower thresholds, or intermediate values.

Agency conclusion:

Both thresholds should be set at $10 billion, and the minority/women presumption should be removed to avoid Equal Protection concerns.

Missing links:

- The notice does not show why market growth translates to $10 billion rather than another number.
- It does not quantify exemption burden.
- It does not analyze whether the remaining prohibitions sufficiently protect competition for institutions under $10 billion.
- It does not describe practical effects on institutions that might have relied on the deleted presumption.

### Step 9 — Failure Modes

1. Threshold underinclusion.

Institutions below $10 billion may still create competition concerns in specific regional or specialized markets, especially if community and RMSA prohibitions do not capture the relevant competitive relationship.

2. Threshold gaming.

Institutions near $10 billion may have incentives to structure timing, affiliations, or interlock arrangements around the bright line.

3. Burden-relief overstatement.

If exemption applications are rare or generally approved quickly, the burden relief may be less significant than the preamble implies.

4. Loss of useful exemption flexibility.

Deleting the presumption could unintentionally reduce clarity for community-based, newly chartered, or recruitment-constrained institutions unless the final rule explains how those facts remain relevant.

5. Future staleness.

A fixed $10 billion threshold may become outdated again absent indexing or periodic review.

### Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

Evasion: Low to moderate. The rule is a threshold-based deregulatory change. The main evasion risk is arranging interlocks around the $10 billion line or relying on geographic prohibitions not to trigger review.

Threshold gaming: Moderate. A single bright-line threshold is simpler, but it increases the importance of asset measurement timing and creates a discontinuity at $10 billion.

Strategic compliance: Moderate. Institutions below $10 billion may no longer need to present exemption justifications even when an interlock could raise localized concerns outside the community/RMSA framework.
