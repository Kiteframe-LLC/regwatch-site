## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** An 'effective' AML/CFT program can be defined well enough to guide supervision.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Supervisors and banks will read the new standard in a consistent way across different business models.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If 'effective' mostly functions as a retrospective label, the rule may produce uneven enforcement and more compliance-only documentation.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for operational criteria, examples, and examiner calibration guidance.
- **Claim:** Risk-based resource shifting will improve AML/CFT outcomes.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Institutions can reliably distinguish higher-risk from lower-risk customers and activities in practice.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If risk scoring is noisy or overcautious, the rule can push resources toward paperwork rather than better detection.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for evidence that the marginal resource shift changes outcomes, not just documentation.
- **Claim:** A U.S.-located AML/CFT officer improves oversight without adding much burden.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Large or internationally active banks can meet the requirement without major restructuring or continuity losses.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the bank's existing governance model is distributed globally, the location rule may force unnecessary reorganization.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask whether a functional-access standard would work as well as a location rule.
- **Claim:** Updating existing programs will be straightforward because the changes are mostly terminological.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Current Board-supervised programs already contain most of the needed elements.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If institutions need to revise controls, governance, training, and testing, the burden is larger than the notice suggests.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for transition-cost estimates by institution type.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The Board proposes to revise 12 CFR 208.63 so Board-supervised banks must establish and maintain effective AML/CFT programs that are risk-based, include customer due diligence, and are implemented in all material respects.

**Affected Entities**
- Board-supervised banks
- Large internationally active banking organizations with distributed compliance functions
- Community banks and other smaller supervised institutions
- Bank compliance officers, boards, and senior management

**Statutory Authority:** 12 U.S.C. 1818(s), the BSA as amended by the AML Act of 2020, and the Board's delegated and independent supervisory authority.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal adds an explicit effectiveness standard, a risk-assessment requirement tied to AML/CFT Priorities, a U.S.-located responsible officer, and a maintain-by-implementation requirement.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice is labeled a proposed rule and says the Board is inviting comment.
- The rule text repeatedly ties the program standard to risk-based internal policies, procedures, and controls.
- The rule text adds supervisory language about implementation 'in all material respects.'

**Inference:** This is a harmonization NPRM, but it also tightens how the Board will judge compliance and failure modes.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The proposed rule would require Board-supervised banks to establish and maintain effective AML/CFT programs.
- The rule requires implementation 'in all material respects.'
