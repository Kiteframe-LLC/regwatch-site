## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Access and amendment would compromise insider-risk investigations.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The relevant records are sufficiently connected to active or reasonably anticipated investigations that disclosure or amendment would create a material risk.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If CFTC-59 also contains routine, closed, stale, or weakly connected records, a categorical exemption could deny rights where the stated investigative risk is low.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for record-segmentation criteria, closed-case treatment, and partial-access or post-investigation review safeguards.
- **Claim:** The listed exemptions are necessary to protect the integrity of the investigative process.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Each listed Privacy Act provision creates a sufficiently similar investigative risk to justify the same system-wide treatment.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Different provisions serve different functions; a rationale adequate for access to investigative files may not independently establish why accounting, relevancy, agency-requirement, or agency-rule provisions must all be exempted.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The Commission should explain the distinct necessity, scope, and limiting principle for each subsection.
- **Claim:** Confidential-source protection requires restricting access to CFTC-59 records.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The source identities and source-derived material cannot be protected through redaction, segregation, delayed access, or other narrower controls.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A categorical exemption may conceal non-source material that could be disclosed without identifying a source or compromising an investigation.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request a segregability analysis and an explanation of why narrower protections would not work.
- **Claim:** The proposal does not impose a significant economic impact on a substantial number of small entities.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The rule's effects are limited to individual Privacy Act rights and do not impose meaningful compliance or operational costs on small entities.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Contractors or other organizations may interact with insider-risk investigations even if the Privacy Act rights at issue belong to individuals.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The Commission can clarify how contractor records and organizational interactions were considered in the RFA certification.
- **Claim:** The CEA section 15(a) market-impact considerations are not applicable because the rule is promulgated under the Privacy Act.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The proposal has no material effects on CFTC market-participant obligations or public-interest considerations beyond individual-record administration.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If implementation changes insider-risk reporting or contractor obligations in ways affecting Commission operations, the conclusion may be too categorical.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask the Commission to identify any operational or market-adjacent effects it considered before treating the CEA analysis as inapplicable.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The Commission proposes to add CFTC-59 to 17 CFR 146.12 and exempt it from specified Privacy Act access, amendment, disclosure-accounting, relevancy, and agency-rule provisions. The selected document reopens comments after a Regulations.gov availability error.

**Affected Entities**
- Individuals whose information is maintained in CFTC-59
- Confidential sources and witnesses
- CFTC investigators, records staff, counsel, contractors, organizations, and commenters

**Statutory Authority:** The NPRM relies on the Privacy Act, especially 5 U.S.C. 552a(k)(2), and proposes corresponding amendments to 17 CFR part 146. It also discusses the preliminary inapplicability of CEA sections 15(a) and 15(b).

**Key Regulatory Changes**
- Add CFTC-59 to 17 CFR 146.12(h).
- Exempt CFTC-59 from the listed Privacy Act provisions, subject to section 552a(k)(2).
- Apply corresponding exemptions to CFTC Privacy Act regulations and reopen comments through CFTC-2026-0893-0002.

**Observed Facts**
- The NPRM identifies CFTC-59 as a new Insider Risk Program system of records.
- It gives reasons involving investigative integrity, sources, relevancy, and agency requirements.
- The reopening notice attributes the added period to a Regulations.gov availability error.

**Inference:** The proposal prioritizes investigative confidentiality over several transparency and correction mechanisms, but does not fully specify safeguards for inaccurate, stale, overbroad, or closed-case records.
