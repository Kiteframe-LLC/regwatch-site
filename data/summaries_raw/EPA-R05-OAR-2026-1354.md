## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim A

**Claim:** EPA can rely on the exceptional-events exclusions to treat both areas as attaining.

**Underlying assumption:** The wildfire-influenced monitor readings were properly identified, the demonstrations satisfy the Exceptional Events Rule, and the excluded data are outcome-determinative in a defensible way.

**Failure scenario:** If commenters show that the wildfire causal showing is weaker than EPA suggests, or that one or more flagged days should remain in the regulatory data set, the resulting design values may no longer support the clean-data determination.

**Procedural implication:** This is the highest-value comment target because the planning suspensions follow only if the exclusions remain valid.

### Claim B

**Claim:** Design values of `0.070 ppm` in both areas are enough to support the proposed determination.

**Underlying assumption:** The data are complete, quality assured, and remain at or below the NAAQS through final action.

**Failure scenario:** Because both areas are exactly at the threshold, a small change in the included data set or an intervening violating design value could undo the proposal's premise.

**Procedural implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to show the attainment calculation transparently and explain the proposal's sensitivity to any restored excluded days or new monitoring data.

### Claim C

**Claim:** Suspending attainment demonstrations, RACM, RFP, and contingency submissions is warranted once EPA finds clean data.

**Underlying assumption:** EPA's Clean Data Policy and codified rule at `40 CFR 51.1318` fit this situation even though the areas were previously reclassified to Serious nonattainment.

**Failure scenario:** If commenters believe the record is too compressed on how the clean-data determination interacts with Serious-area obligations, the final action could appear underexplained even if the legal authority exists.

**Procedural implication:** EPA would benefit from a clearer explanation of why suspension is appropriate here and what planning obligations remain deferred rather than extinguished.

### Claim D

**Claim:** EPA's public-comment opportunity on the exceptional-events materials is sufficient.

**Underlying assumption:** The docketed materials expose enough of the exceptional-events reasoning for ordinary commenters to evaluate the exclusion decision.

**Failure scenario:** If the public-facing record summarizes the technical showing too heavily, commenters may not be able to test EPA's concurrence effectively before final action.

**Procedural implication:** Commenters can request clearer monitor-level tables, event narratives, and sensitivity explanations in the final record.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

* EPA proposes a clean-data determination for the Berrien and Muskegon, Michigan 2015 ozone nonattainment areas.
* EPA says both areas attain the `2015` ozone NAAQS for the `2023-2025` design-value period after excluding wildfire-influenced monitor readings under the Exceptional Events Rule.
* The Berrien monitor table shows `0.077`, `0.065`, and `0.070` for the annual fourth-high readings, averaging `0.070`.
* The Muskegon monitor table shows `0.073`, `0.071`, and `0.068`, averaging `0.070`.
* EPA says final approval would suspend attainment demonstrations, RACM, RFP plans, contingency measures, and related attainment-planning SIP elements for as long as the areas continue to attain.
* EPA also says this action is not a redesignation and that redesignation requests submitted on December 26, 2025 will be handled in a separate rulemaking.

### Inference

This proposal is a narrow but consequential EPA action. It does not redesignate the areas to attainment, but it would pause major Serious-area planning duties because EPA concludes that adjusted monitoring data now show attainment. The central comment opportunity is not broad statutory authority. It is whether EPA has fully justified the exceptional-events exclusions and fully explained the consequences of relying on them when both design values sit exactly at the standard.

### Evidence Quotes

* “For this proposed action, the EPA will rely on the calculated values that exclude the event-influenced data for the purpose of demonstrating attainment of the 2015 ozone NAAQS.”
* “The Berrien area's 3-year ozone design value for 2023-2025 is 0.070 ppm... Similarly, the Muskegon area's 3-year ozone design value for 2023-2025 is 0.070 ppm...”
* “As a result of this determination, the EPA is proposing to suspend the requirements for the Berrien and Muskegon areas to submit attainment demonstrations and associated RACM, RFP plans, contingency measures...”

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Alternatives evaluated:** The visible record does not present a classic alternatives section. EPA instead explains the legal consequences that follow if the adjusted design values show attainment.

**Plausible alternatives not analyzed in depth:** A more limited or staged approach to planning relief is not visibly explored in the NPRM text. Nor does the public-facing record discuss whether EPA considered retaining some planning submissions while separately evaluating redesignation.

**Evidence suggesting feasibility:** The NPRM itself distinguishes among clean-data determination, redesignation, and maintenance-plan approval. That distinction suggests EPA could explain more clearly why suspending multiple Serious-area planning duties now is the right procedural sequence.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

The proposal is technically coherent at a high level. EPA identifies the legal rule for exceptional events, cites state demonstrations, describes concurrence, and then shows adjusted design values that meet the `0.070 ppm` standard in both areas.

The main coherence issue is not contradiction but compression. Both areas land exactly at the threshold after the exclusions. That makes the quality of the exclusion showing unusually important, yet the NPRM presents only a condensed explanation of the underlying technical evidence and of how sensitive the attainment outcome is to those exclusions.

**Observed Fact:** The Berrien TSD says EPA reviews the demonstrations using a “weight of evidence approach” and that the air agency's package must satisfy all Exceptional Events Rule criteria.

**Observed Fact:** The cover letters state that, with the adjustments, both areas show attainment for `2023-2025`.

**Inference:** The technical rationale appears real and structured, but the public-facing proposal would be stronger if it translated more of that weight-of-evidence reasoning into plain-language explanation that commenters can test.

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

**Observed Fact:** EPA grounds the action in the Clean Air Act, the Exceptional Events Rule, and the clean-data regulation at `40 CFR 51.1318`.

**Observed Fact:** The notice carefully states what the action is not: it is not attainment-by-deadline, not redesignation, and not maintenance-plan approval.

**Inference:** The legal authority for EPA to propose a clean-data determination appears solid on the face of the notice. The more realistic legal-risk question is explanatory adequacy: whether the final record fully justifies the exclusion-driven attainment finding and the resulting suspension of Serious-area planning obligations.

**Unknown:** Whether commenters will surface contrary technical evidence on any flagged event days that materially changes the legal sufficiency of EPA's concurrence.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

* One or more excluded wildfire-influenced days could be less defensible than the notice suggests, changing the design-value outcome.
* Because both areas sit exactly at `0.070 ppm`, even a small data or methodological change could reverse the clean-data conclusion.
* Commenters may struggle to evaluate the exclusion logic if EPA leaves too much of the weight-of-evidence showing in dense technical attachments rather than the final explanation.
* Suspending Serious-area planning duties could be criticized as underexplained if EPA does not show why the clean-data rule fully fits these facts.
* If more recent monitoring data show renewed violations before final action, the proposal's premise may collapse.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

* The rule creates a strong incentive for states and regulated stakeholders to focus on exceptional-events demonstrations where attainment turns on a small number of high ozone days.
* A successful clean-data determination reduces immediate pressure to complete attainment-planning SIP submissions, which makes the exclusion and monitoring logic especially important.
* The proposal also creates incentives for commenters to concentrate on monitor-level causation and data-handling details rather than on broader anti-regulatory arguments.
