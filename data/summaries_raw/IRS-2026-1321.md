## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Automatic enrollment will increase access to qualified general contributions without creating unmanageable administrative burdens.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The Secretary, financial agent, trustees, and responsible parties can identify eligible children, maintain separate records, protect return information, and process claims at the projected national scale.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A data mismatch, delayed claim, or trustee-interface failure leaves an account funded but not controllable by the lawful responsible party, or causes a contribution or transfer to be attributed incorrectly.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request an end-to-end first-year workflow, error rates, service standards, correction process, and capacity assumptions for the 73-million-child population.
- **Claim:** The master group trust preserves separate account ownership while avoiding unnecessary disclosure of return information.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The group-trust recordkeeping boundary and the claim-authentication process reliably separate investment administration from beneficiary identity and tax data.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A vendor, trustee, or financial-agent handoff exposes more information than necessary or cannot reconcile account-level beneficial interests after a transfer, corporate action, or correction.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for data-minimization controls, access logs, reconciliation tests, breach response, and the legal basis for every disclosure in the claim and transfer process.
- **Claim:** Permitting qualified stock contributions will increase total funding enough to justify concentration and holding-period risks.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The incremental stock-funded contributions would not otherwise be made in cash and the benefits of additional funding exceed the risk borne by beneficiaries.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A concentrated stock position falls sharply, cannot be sold for five years, or creates a corporate-action problem, while the economic analysis overstates the incremental amount of funding caused by the rule.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request donor-behavior evidence, downside scenarios, concentration limits, diversification alternatives, and a clear liquidation/correction path.
- **Claim:** Immediate temporary rules are necessary because delay would prevent uniform account creation and contributions.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The need to start auto enrollment and accept contributions outweighs the costs of imposing immediately effective rules before public comments are resolved.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The temporary framework creates reliance, account transfers, or stock positions that are difficult to unwind after the final rule changes material definitions or safeguards.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask the agency to identify which provisions truly require immediate effect, how comments will be incorporated without retroactive disruption, and what transition protections apply.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The IRS proposes rules implementing section 530A by creating automatic initial Trump accounts, defining how accounts are claimed and transferred, facilitating qualified general contributions for broad classes, and permitting qualified stock contributions subject to holding-period and reporting rules.

**Affected Entities:** Children and families; responsible parties; trustees and financial agents; nonprofit and government donors; stock issuers; and IRS/Treasury.

**Statutory Authority:** The notice relies primarily on Code sections 530A, 408(a), 6103, and 7805(a).

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal creates auto accounts, a master group trust, authenticated claims, Treasury acceptance agreements, qualified classes, stock contributions, and related reporting duties.

**Observed Facts**

**Inference:** The main comment leverage is whether the immediate nationwide design has adequate implementation, privacy, transition, stock-risk, and small-trustee support.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Identity or authority mismatch
  
  **Mechanism:** Account claims require authentication and authority to receive return information.
  
  **Consequence:** A lawful parent, guardian, or adult beneficiary may face delay, while an incorrect claimant or record may receive control.
  
  **Comment Use:** Request error rates, correction deadlines, and escalation to a human reviewer.
- **Failure Mode:** Stock concentration and illiquidity
  
  **Mechanism:** Qualified stock generally remains in the account for five years.
  
  **Consequence:** Beneficiaries can bear issuer-specific loss or corporate-action risk before diversification.
  
  **Comment Use:** Request downside scenarios and alternatives that preserve donor participation without mandatory concentration.
- **Failure Mode:** Time-limited ABLE rollover failure
  
  **Mechanism:** A direct trustee-to-trustee ABLE rollover must occur in the calendar year the beneficiary turns 17.
  
  **Consequence:** A delay in verifying authority or coordinating trustees could eliminate a statutory opportunity.
  
  **Comment Use:** Request a safe harbor, notice process, and tolling or correction rule for timely requests.
- **Failure Mode:** Unwinding difficulty after final-rule changes
  
  **Mechanism:** Temporary rules apply immediately while comments are pending.
  
  **Consequence:** Accounts, contributions, stock positions, and reliance may be difficult to reconcile if the final rule changes.
  
  **Comment Use:** Request transition rules and prospective-only treatment for changed obligations.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives
