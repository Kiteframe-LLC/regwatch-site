## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim 1**
  - Claim: Prior approval language is inconsistent across the current rules.
  - Underlying assumption: Recipients need clear timing rules for when approval must be requested.
  - Failure scenario: If the current language is already clear enough, the revision may be unnecessary or over-scoped.
  - Procedural implication: Commenters can ask whether the ambiguity is real enough to justify text changes.
- **Claim 2**
  - Claim: The threshold increase to $50,000 will reduce burdens without weakening oversight.
  - Underlying assumption: Transactions between $25,000 and $50,000 are mostly low-risk or already covered by other controls.
  - Failure scenario: If that band includes meaningful risk, the threshold could remove useful screening.
  - Procedural implication: The agency should explain why the proposed number is the right break point.
- **Claim 3**
  - Claim: Fundraising proceeds should not count as derivative income.
  - Underlying assumption: The proposed reimbursement rule is enough to keep LSC funds limited to authorized purposes.
  - Failure scenario: If the reimbursement rule is hard to administer, the clarification may not reduce confusion.
  - Procedural implication: Commenters can ask whether alternative accounting treatment would be clearer.
- **Claim 4**
  - Claim: Flexible review timelines are needed for complex prior-approval requests.
  - Underlying assumption: A 20-day initial response window plus longer decisions is workable for recipients.
  - Failure scenario: If the timing changes create uncertainty, recipients may still face planning problems.
  - Procedural implication: Commenters can ask for clearer service standards and fallback rules.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

- The NPRM would revise parts 1630 and 1631 to clarify prior approval timing, raise the $25,000 threshold to $50,000, revise the fundraising-proceeds rule, allow more flexible review timelines, and move the accounting deadline to the annual audited financial statement deadline.
- The proposal applies to LSC recipients and the staff who prepare procurement requests, accounting reports, and related compliance files.
- LSC says the changes are meant to reduce administrative burden, improve consistency, and make oversight easier to administer.
