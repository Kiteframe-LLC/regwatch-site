## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim A

**Claim:** The SIP revisions are administrative and do not change substantive sulfur dioxide control obligations.

**Underlying assumption:** Updating the revoked state-rule reference and deleting duplicative federal citations does not alter what affected facilities must monitor, report, or emit in practice.

**Failure scenario:** If `10 CSR 10-6.261` differs materially from the revoked `10 CSR 10-6.260` in a way that changes reporting obligations or enforceability, EPA's “administrative changes” label would be too compressed.

**Procedural implication:** The strongest constructive comment would ask EPA to explain the practical equivalence of the old and new cross-referenced provisions more explicitly in the final record.

### Claim B

**Claim:** The Missouri SIP submission meets the substantive SIP requirements of CAA section 110 and implementing regulations.

**Underlying assumption:** No hidden conflict exists between the revised state rule text and the federally approved SIP framework for sulfur dioxide controls in the St. Louis area.

**Failure scenario:** If commenters identify an omitted cross-reference or an enforcement dependency that EPA did not discuss, the record could look underexplained even if the agency's ultimate conclusion remains correct.

**Procedural implication:** EPA should make the approval logic easier to audit by linking the key edits more directly to the relevant SIP requirements.

### Claim C

**Claim:** The area's ability to attain or maintain air quality standards is unaffected.

**Underlying assumption:** The revisions neither loosen emission limits nor reduce monitoring, reporting, or enforceability in any meaningful way.

**Failure scenario:** If the reporting cross-reference to `10 CSR 10-6.261` narrows obligations compared with the rescinded rule, the claim that there is no attainment or maintenance impact would need more support.

**Procedural implication:** The final rule would benefit from a short explanation of why the updated reporting reference preserves equivalent or adequate compliance oversight.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

* EPA proposes to approve Missouri SIP revisions to `10 CSR 10-5.570`, a St. Louis-area sulfur dioxide rule for stationary boilers.
* The rule applies to industrial, commercial, or institutional boilers and process heaters above `50 mmBtu` per hour in specified St. Louis-area counties and the City of St. Louis.
* The rule retains the core emission limit of `1.0 lb SO2 per mmBtu` over a `30-day` period, plus a `3,050-ton` twelve-month rolling limit for breweries.
* EPA describes the revisions as administrative, including cleanup of sulfur dioxide terminology, deletion of duplicative `40 CFR part 60` references, correction of an appendix citation, punctuation edits, and replacement of a reference to rescinded `10 CSR 10-6.260` with `10 CSR 10-6.261`.
* The Missouri submittal says the rulemaking “cleans-up reference information in this rule to address EPA concerns and includes other minor clarifications.”
* EPA says Missouri provided public notice from July 15, 2019 to September 5, 2019 and received one EPA comment related to `10 CSR 10-6.261`, which EPA says has since been approved into the SIP effective October 6, 2025.

### Inference

This is a routine SIP maintenance action rather than a policy shift. The real question for commenters is not whether EPA may approve editorial fixes in general, but whether the agency has explained clearly enough that the updated cross-reference to `10 CSR 10-6.261` preserves the same or adequate reporting and enforcement function once the older rule was rescinded.

### Evidence Quotes

* “This proposed action will amend the SIP to address administrative changes to the State rule in the Missouri Code of State Regulations (CSR).”
* “The most significant revision to 10 CSR 10-5.570 replaces a reference to 10 CSR 10-6.260... with 10 CSR 10-6.261...”
* “Missouri rescinded 10 CSR 10-6.260 and replaced the rule with 10 CSR 10-6.261.”
* “The revisions to 10 CSR 10-5.570 will not affect the area’s ability to attain or maintain any air quality standard because they are administrative in nature.”
