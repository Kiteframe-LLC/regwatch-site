## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Manufacturer-selected voltage produces inconsistent or weakly comparable compliance evidence.
  
  **Trigger:** Different integral-beam designs use different marked operating voltages or voltage behavior varies in service.
  
  **Consequence:** Cross-vehicle comparison, defect investigation, or minimum-performance assessment may be less clear.
  
  **Possible Mitigation:** Specify a common minimum or bounded test condition, document voltage tolerances, and explain post-market verification.
- **Failure Mode:** Automated lamp operation fails during an ADS state transition or degraded state.
  
  **Trigger:** The vehicle changes between expected human monitoring and no expected human monitoring, or the automation system faults.
  
  **Consequence:** Signals may be absent, stale, or misleading to other road users.
  
  **Possible Mitigation:** Require diagnostic, fallback, transition, and fault-recording evidence in compliance testing.
- **Failure Mode:** Cyan marker lamps are misunderstood or interact unpredictably with state lighting laws.
  
  **Trigger:** Color perception, ambient conditions, vehicle combinations, or non-identical state rules vary.
  
  **Consequence:** Road-user confusion, enforcement uncertainty, or delayed state-law compliance could occur.
  
  **Possible Mitigation:** Provide human-factors evidence, a state-law inventory, transition guidance, and a monitoring plan.
- **Failure Mode:** A redesigned vertical lamp arrangement changes glare or illumination in an untested configuration.
  
  **Trigger:** A vehicle uses a vertical arrangement with the lower beam below the upper beam and has unusual geometry or loading.
  
  **Consequence:** The assumed safety benefit or neutrality may not hold across all vehicle designs.
  
  **Possible Mitigation:** Show illumination and glare testing covering representative vehicle geometries and edge cases.

## Step 17 — High-Leverage Comment Angles

- **Issue (high; evidence request):** Comparative safety evidence. **Why it matters:** The proposal removes requirements while relying on safety neutrality. **Question:** Can NHTSA docket tests covering voltage, beam orientation, glare, loading, and edge cases?
- **Issue (high; alternatives push):** A phased or bounded alternative. **Why it matters:** The market is still developing. **Question:** How would bounded testing, phased lamps, and diagnostics compare with the proposal?
- **Issue (high; evidence request):** Human-factors basis for cyan lamps. **Why it matters:** The lamp's purpose is road-user communication. **Question:** What recognition, confusion, ambient-light, and color-perception evidence supports the proposal?
- **Issue (moderate; collaborative clarification):** Operating-state verification. **Why it matters:** Lamp meaning depends on the monitoring state. **Question:** What tests cover transitions, failures, maintenance, fallback, and mixed-control vehicles?
- **Issue (moderate; procedural concern):** Preemption and small-entity analysis. **Why it matters:** The market picture is unquantified. **Question:** Which laws are affected, and what fleet, supplier, operator, and small-entity assumptions support the final analysis?

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

- **Id:** brief_1
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Comment Posture:** evidence request
  
  **Key Sentences**
  - The proposed updates to the standard are intended to remove unnecessary requirements and to provide design flexibility without impact on vehicle safety.
  - At this stage, the agency has not quantified any potential benefits or costs.
  
  **Routine Description:** This is a routine proposal-stage modernization, but its safety case depends on engineering comparisons and documented operating conditions.
  
  **Regulatory Effectiveness:** Comparative tests could show whether flexibility preserves illumination, glare control, and signal reliability.
  
  **Statutory Compliance:** A developed record would connect the revised FMVSS to its cited safety authority.
  
  **Procedural Adequacy:** The NPRM process is normal; the final rule should respond to evidence and alternatives placed in the record.
  
  **Real World Consequence:** Without evidence, different vehicle designs may rely on different assumptions while claiming the same safety standard.
  
  **Worth A Comment:** High
