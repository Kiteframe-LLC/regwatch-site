## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The proposed levels are achievable while reducing exposure.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Historical samples are representative enough of current products and control measures can reduce elevated results without materially reducing product availability.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A category's sample distribution changes by ingredient, geography, supplier, or product format, so the reported percentage at or below the threshold no longer predicts current manufacturability or market availability.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request stratified achievability data and a plan for distinguishing ordinary variation from a persistent control failure.
- **Claim:** A 90th-percentile food intake combined with the lower TRV bound provides a health-protective basis.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The selected intake percentile and the lower end of the TRV range adequately represent high-exposure children and the relevant toxicological uncertainty.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Children with higher intake, multiple food categories, different body weights, nutritional vulnerabilities, or exposure from non-covered foods receive materially higher cumulative exposure than the scenario captures.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The record would be stronger if FDA showed sensitivity to intake percentiles, combined-category consumption, body weight, and the 0.21-0.36 TRV range.
- **Claim:** The 20 ppb yogurt/pudding/custard and 25 ppb meat levels are beneficial even though the reported exposure reduction is zero.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** An action level set above current observed concentrations will preserve low levels and provide a meaningful health-protective reference point without creating avoidable enforcement or market effects.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The levels become nominal enforcement reference points without measurable exposure benefit, or future products and sample distributions differ from the historical data used to justify 100% achievability.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** FDA can be asked to explain what monitoring signal would trigger adjustment and why these levels are preferable to a nonnumeric recommendation or a lower threshold.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The draft guidance proposes cadmium levels of 10 ppb for most covered produce and mixtures, 25 ppb for carrots and meats, 45 ppb for dry infant cereals, and 20 ppb for yogurt, pudding, and custard. The levels are nonbinding but may inform case-by-case enforcement.

**Affected Entities:** Covered baby-food manufacturers, processors, suppliers, importers, parents, and children under two.

**Statutory Authority:** The guidance cites 21 CFR 109.4 and 109.6, FD&C Act section 402(a)(1), and 21 CFR 10.115.

**Key Regulatory Change:** FDA would add category-specific cadmium action-level guidance under Closer to Zero.

**Observed Facts**
- The guidance is nonbinding and reports a 0.21-0.36 µg/kg bw/day TRV plus FDA monitoring, survey, consumption, exposure, and achievability data.
- The five proposed levels range from 10 to 45 ppb.

**Inference:** Practical effect may exceed a purely informational recommendation because FDA says the levels may inform case-by-case enforcement.
