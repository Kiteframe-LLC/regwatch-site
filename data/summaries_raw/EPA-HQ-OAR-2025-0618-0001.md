## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Current definitions are too restrictive for non-emitting components.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The restricted activities can be separated cleanly from source-construction decisions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the boundary is blurry, regulated entities may treat source construction as non-emitting work and weaken NSR control timing.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to define the non-emitting carveout more tightly.
- **Claim:** The old language creates unnecessary delay and burden.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The affected projects are common enough that the delay is material, not anecdotal.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the problem is limited to a few project types, the proposed rewrite may be broader than needed.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for incidence and timing data before accepting the burden claim.
- **Claim:** Allowing non-emitting work before permit issuance will not change emissions control outcomes.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Permitting authorities can still prevent emissions-unit construction and preserve later BACT or LAER decisions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Sunk costs from early construction could pressure later permit terms even if EPA says they should not.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can press EPA on reliance risk and whether the permit gate still functions cleanly.
- **Claim:** The new definitions can be applied consistently across SIP, PSD, NNSR, and Tribal NSR programs.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** State, local, Tribal, and Federal permitting authorities can operationalize the same boundary rules.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Different programs may interpret the carveout differently and produce uneven enforcement.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA for implementation examples and model permit language.
- **Claim:** The proposal reflects the best reading of the CAA.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The statutory text and prior policy guidance support the new interpretation.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If prior guidance or judicial precedent points the other way, the proposal may need a fuller legal bridge.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to identify the strongest statutory and precedential support.
- **Claim:** EPA can rely on case-by-case discretion to handle edge cases.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Permitting authorities will know when a preparatory activity crosses into regulated construction.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Case-by-case judgment can create inconsistent outcomes and weak notice to applicants.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for concrete examples or a more objective list of excluded activities.
