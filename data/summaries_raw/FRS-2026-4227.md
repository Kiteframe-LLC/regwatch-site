## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_objective
  
  **Claim:** The process can both support effective safety-and-soundness review and minimize unnecessary burden.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The information categories, existing supervisory information, and discretionary requests will be sufficient without producing repeated or open-ended supplementation.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Applicants receive repeated additional-information requests or divergent expectations across Reserve Banks, so the tailored process becomes less predictable and more burdensome than a structured baseline application.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final rule should identify minimum required content, supplementation standards, and a record of clock-affecting requests.
- **Claim Id:** claim_completeness
  
  **Claim:** An application is substantially complete when it contains sufficient information for the Board to decide the statutory factors.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Sufficiency can be determined consistently before the Board has completed its substantive review.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The Board treats a material question discovered during review as proof that the application was never complete, delaying the 120-day clock and weakening the deemed-approval protection.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters should ask for objective examples and a rule distinguishing ordinary review questions from true completeness defects.
- **Claim Id:** claim_timing
  
  **Claim:** The receipt-date, 30-day notice, 120-day decision, and deemed-approval rules implement the statute.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The proposed clock rules will operate predictably even when information is supplemented, submitted outside business hours, or affected by a material change.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A disputed submission date or material-change determination restarts the clock after an applicant reasonably believed the application was complete.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The Board should publish a clock-and-notice protocol and explain how disputes over completeness are resolved.
- **Claim Id:** claim_information
  
  **Claim:** Five respondents and 405 annual burden hours are a reasonable PRA estimate.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The small number of expected applications and the use of existing supervisory information adequately capture preparation, legal, technology, and remediation costs.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A complex consortium or first-of-its-kind application requires substantially more work than the average 80-hour estimate, while the application letter still requires extensive business, reserve, governance, and compliance material.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final analysis should disclose sensitivity cases for one, five, and higher application volumes and distinguish initial preparation from follow-up requests.
- **Claim Id:** claim_denial
  
  **Claim:** A single statutory-factor inconsistency may support denial when the activities would be unsafe or unsound.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The Board will explain how the factor-specific deficiency translates into an unsafe-or-unsound finding rather than treating any technical omission as independently dispositive.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A broad factor or discretionary condition becomes a stand-alone gateway for denial without a clear connection to the applicant's proposed activities.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The decision and hearing rules should require factor-specific findings, causal explanation, and actionable cure information.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The proposal would add a letter-based Board approval process for an insured State member bank's payment stablecoin subsidiary.

**Affected Entities**
- Insured State member banks that seek approval for a payment stablecoin subsidiary
- Proposed Board-supervised permitted payment stablecoin issuers and their officers, directors, and principal shareholders
- Consortium and multi-bank structures that may seek coordinated approval
- Customers and market participants affected by the safety, redemption, reserve, and compliance controls of approved issuers

**Statutory Authority:** The notice relies on section 5 of the GENIUS Act, 12 U.S.C. 5904, and related Federal Reserve authority.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The specialized track would include completeness notice, a 120-day decision period, deemed approval, denial reasons, and a hearing path.

**Core Tradeoff:** Predictable procedure versus enough discretion to evaluate safety, reserves, redemption, management, technology, and compliance.

**Observed Facts**
- The application must include business, financial, governance, compliance, and background information.
- The Board may request more information and impose approval conditions.
- The Board estimates 405 annual burden hours and $30,112 total cost.

**Inference:** The procedural gateway can determine whether an issuer reaches substantive review and begins operations.

## Step 13A — Structural Integrity / Nonsense Detector

**Why:** The proposal has a coherent purpose, statutory authority, operative application mechanism, factor review, and appeal path. Its principal structural weakness is that the decision gate is discretionary at exactly the points that control clock protection and individualized conditions.

**Observed Fact Vs Inference**
**Observed Fact:** The Board may request additional information at any time, defines substantial completeness by sufficient information, may treat a material change as requiring a new application, may impose conditions, and may waive or change procedures in exceptional circumstances.

**Inference:** Without clearer constraints and records for those powers, applicants may face a less predictable gateway than the statutory deadlines imply, although the available record does not show an intentional effort to evade review.
