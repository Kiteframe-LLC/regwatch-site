## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The new administrative-leave examples can be codified without changing the practical burden much.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Agencies already follow the same policy choices in practice, so the rule mainly converts guidance into binding text.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If agencies have been applying the policy unevenly or if the examples invite new claims for leave, the codification could materially change behavior.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** A commenter can ask OPM to show utilization history or examples of actual agency practice, not just policy intent.
- **Claim:** Broad agency discretion is sufficient because the rule still provides examples and prohibitions.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The examples and carveouts are specific enough to guide consistent administration across agencies.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If borderline cases turn on unwritten norms or ad hoc judgment, similar employees could be treated differently across agencies or even within the same agency.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** A commenter can ask for more concrete documentation standards and examples for edge cases.
- **Claim:** Acceptance of deferred-resignation benefits is a valid reason to deny withdrawal of a resignation.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Employees receiving paid administrative leave or similar benefits understand that the benefits are tied to the resignation commitment.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the employee-side notice or agreement language is unclear, the rule could produce disputes over whether the withdrawal denial is fair or expected.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** A commenter can ask for model notice language and minimum agreement terms before a withdrawal denial is treated as valid.
- **Claim:** The proposal will not materially change costs, so a detailed cost model is unnecessary.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The agency's current experience is a good proxy for future use, and the workforce realignment scenarios will remain limited.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If agencies use the new flexibility more often than expected, the no-cost conclusion may understate administrative and budget effects.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** A commenter can ask for sensitivity ranges or utilization scenarios rather than a single cost conclusion.
- **Claim:** The 10-workday and 1-workday thresholds are administratively workable as written.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** HR staff can cleanly distinguish investigative leave, sick leave, and other forms of administrative leave without frequent dispute.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If operational edge cases are common, the thresholds may generate inconsistent coding and leave disputes.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** A commenter can ask for implementation examples showing how agencies should code mixed or overlapping leave situations.
