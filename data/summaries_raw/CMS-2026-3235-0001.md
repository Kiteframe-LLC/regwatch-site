## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** No concerning citation trends were found for remote digital review.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Survey citations capture the relevant quality failures and the reviewed period is representative of future virtual-access use.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Low-frequency or unreported cybersecurity, interface, validation, or workflow failures could escape citation data.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request the underlying search terms, denominator, citation categories, and a prospective monitoring plan.
- **Claim:** Secure connections allow virtual access to operate under the primary laboratory certificate.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A secure connection, director responsibility, and staff listing are sufficient to preserve quality across remote locations and system boundaries.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A connection is technically encrypted but access controls, audit logs, validation, or local conditions are inadequate.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for minimum security, validation, audit, and incident-response standards.
- **Claim:** Digital-image cytology PT can maintain integrity and relevance.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Digital-image PT produces performance information comparable to glass-slide PT for all relevant users and systems.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Image quality, display conditions, platform differences, or proctoring weaknesses affect results or make scores non-comparable.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request validation evidence, equivalence criteria, and safeguards for failed or disputed events.
- **Claim:** Personnel qualification revisions respond to workforce and burden concerns without compromising quality.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The alternative qualifications and grandfathering or continuity provisions identify candidates who are comparably competent.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Relaxed or newly broadened pathways produce uneven competency across specialties or leave transition gaps.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask CMS to explain competency equivalence and monitor quality outcomes after implementation.
- **Claim:** The rule is economically positive for small entities.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Personnel-related savings offset implementation, technology, storage, validation, and reporting costs for most small laboratories.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Small or rural laboratories lack the infrastructure to realize savings or incur proportionally higher unquantified costs.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request a disaggregated RFA analysis and sensitivity testing.
- **Claim:** No feasible alternatives are apparent for other changes.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The reporting, enforcement, consultation, and technical amendments are sufficiently minor or interdependent that alternatives would not matter.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A narrower amendment, implementation guidance, phase-in, or separate rulemaking could reduce burden and improve clarity.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask the agency to identify the design space and explain why bundling and immediate implementation are preferable.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Remote system or connection failure
  
  **Mechanism:** A laboratory may rely on a secure connection, LIS, EHR, or encrypted communication that is unavailable, incomplete, or not validated for a particular workflow.
  
  **Consequence:** Delayed review, reporting error, or inconsistent handling of test data.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Fallback procedures, downtime documentation, access logs, and validation requirements.
- **Failure Mode:** Digital cytology PT non-equivalence
  
  **Mechanism:** Differences in image quality, display, platform, or proctoring may change performance independently of professional competence.
  
  **Consequence:** Misleading PT results or inappropriate loss of independent screening privileges.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Equivalence studies, display standards, integrity monitoring, and appeal/retest procedures.
- **Failure Mode:** Qualification transition gap
  
  **Mechanism:** Broader or alternative pathways may be implemented before laboratories and surveyors have consistent documentation and competency guidance.
  
  **Consequence:** Uneven oversight or workforce disruption.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Clear transition rules, examples, and post-implementation quality monitoring.
- **Failure Mode:** Cost savings fail to materialize for small or rural laboratories
  
  **Mechanism:** Fixed technology, storage, validation, and reporting costs may be higher for small laboratories than the aggregate model assumes.
  
  **Consequence:** Reduced participation, delayed modernization, or local service loss.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Small-entity sensitivity analysis and technical assistance or phased compliance.
