## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim

Improving measurement will better align surcharges with risk.

Underlying assumption
Current method 2 coefficients and score bands have drifted materially away from the intended risk calibration because of inflation, growth, and financial-system change rather than firm-specific risk changes.

Failure scenario
If the current framework is still roughly calibrated, the proposal could function less as a measurement fix and more as a substantive capital reduction.

Procedural implication
Commenters can press the Board to show why the chosen recalibration reflects risk more accurately rather than merely producing lower surcharges.

---

### Claim

Averaging daily or monthly values will reduce temporary year-end balance-sheet management.

Underlying assumption
Observed year-end patterns are meaningfully distortive and averaging is the correct way to neutralize them.

Failure scenario
Some quarter-end or year-end movements may reflect legitimate business patterns rather than gaming, and averaging could overcorrect or add reporting burden without comparable analytical gain.

Procedural implication
Commenters can ask the Board to quantify how much observed variation is true window dressing versus normal business seasonality.

---

### Claim

The Board's multi-source analysis is sufficient to support the calibration changes.

Underlying assumption
Outside commenters can adequately evaluate the Board's conclusions even though some impact estimates depend on confidential supervisory information and special data collection inputs.

Failure scenario
If key percentage changes or indicator adjustments cannot be reproduced from public materials, the notice may provide conclusions without enough visible analytical support for outsiders to test them.

Procedural implication
This creates a moderate transparency concern even though the Board does describe its methods.

---

### Claim

Three alternatives are enough to demonstrate reasoned consideration.

Underlying assumption
The chosen alternatives span the main credible calibration options.

Failure scenario
If narrower or phased approaches were feasible, or if the future-indexing design for alternatives 2 and 3 is underexplained, then the alternatives analysis may still be incomplete.

Procedural implication
Commenters have leverage to request side-by-side comparison of additional intermediate approaches rather than only the Board's three benchmark alternatives.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

* The Board proposes to revise the **GSIB surcharge framework** and the **FR Y-15** reporting form.
* The proposal would adjust method 2 coefficients, index them annually, redefine and reweight the short-term wholesale funding component, require averaging for some indicators, narrow method 2 score bands, and revise several systemic indicator definitions and instructions.
* The notice expressly estimates that the proposal would lower method 2 scores and reduce average GSIB surcharges.
* The proposal also identifies possible costs, including lower resilience and modest additional compliance burden from averaging requirements.

Example text:

> “The proposal would make several changes to better align surcharges with risk.”

### Inference

This is a consequential banking-capital NPRM that mixes **measurement cleanup** with a **real reduction in effective capital surcharges for the largest U.S. bank holding companies**. The Board is not hiding that tradeoff; the main question is whether the record adequately justifies the size and design of the recalibration.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

### Alternatives evaluated

* **Alternative 1:** inflation indexing using CPI-W rather than nominal GDP.
* **Alternative 2:** updating coefficients from recent global denominator data using the 2015 calibration logic.
* **Alternative 3:** a “reference bank” approach that reduces coefficients by 40 percent.

### Plausible alternatives not fully analyzed

* A **phased** recalibration that reduces surcharges gradually rather than through a one-time adjustment.
* Indicator-specific calibration changes without simultaneous narrowing of score bands.
* Public-release variants that would let outside commenters review anonymized or summarized special-collection assumptions.
* A temporary pilot or delayed implementation for the new averaging requirements.

### Evidence suggesting feasibility

* The Board already asks targeted implementation questions, including:

> “What, if any, operational challenges are associated with the proposed approach?”

* The notice also acknowledges unresolved issues for alternatives 2 and 3:

> “The economic analysis does not specify how Alternatives 2 and 3 would adjust the method 2 coefficients going forward.”

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

### Observed strengths

* The notice is internally coherent about its major design goals: reduce drift in method 2, reduce window dressing incentives, improve indicator definitions, and smooth surcharge cliff effects.
* The Board openly links the proposal's benefits and costs to the same estimated surcharge reductions, which is analytically cleaner than presenting only upside.

### Potential coherence concerns

* The proposal combines several moving parts at once, making it harder to isolate which components are necessary to solve which problem.
* The short-term wholesale funding changes, narrower score bands, and annual indexing each solve different issues; commenters can ask whether the Board has shown that the package is not overcalibrated.
* The public record does not let an outside reader fully test how sensitive the results are to assumptions embedded in the special data collection and estimation methods.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

1. **Overcorrection of capital requirements**

   * If the current framework is not as miscalibrated as the Board suggests, average surcharge reductions could weaken resilience more than intended.

2. **Averaging burden exceeds benefit**

   * Daily or monthly averaging may add operational cost without proportionate improvement for some indicators.

3. **Opaque calibration choices**

   * If outside parties cannot reproduce the most important estimates, the Board may receive comments that challenge process integrity rather than substance.

4. **Bundled proposal masks component effects**

   * Commenters may struggle to identify which proposed changes drive the largest reductions or risk shifts.

> “the proposal would reduce method 2 scores by about 29 percent and GSIB surcharges by about 40 basis points, on average across GSIBs.”

### Routine description

The notice updates the GSIB surcharge framework to change how the largest U.S. banking organizations are measured and how much capital surcharge they are likely to face.

### Regulatory effectiveness

Potentially meaningful. The proposal plausibly addresses drift, window dressing, and some measurement inconsistencies, but the package is large enough that commenters should ask for more sensitivity analysis and public reproducibility.

### Statutory compliance

Likely within the Board's authority. The more realistic challenge is whether the final rule adequately explains its calibration choices and makes enough analytical support visible.

Moderate. This is not a thin or conclusory NPRM, but some crucial quantitative support depends on data the public cannot independently inspect from the available materials.

### Real-world consequence

If adopted, the proposal could lower capital surcharges for GSIBs on average, reduce year-end reporting incentives, and change the balance between bank resilience and credit intermediation.

**High value**
