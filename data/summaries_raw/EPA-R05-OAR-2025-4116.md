## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

The package is technically coherent: the redesignation, maintenance, NOx waiver, Muskegon negative declaration, and Berrien landfill RACT conclusions all point in the same direction and rest on the same ozone and permit record. The main issue is not contradiction but fragmentation. Readers have to follow several attachments to see the complete chain from inventory screen to final SIP approval.

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

The legal grounding appears standard and specific. The notice and attachments invoke the redesignation framework, maintenance-plan authority, conformity-budget approval, serious-ozone RACT requirements, and the section 182(f) waiver mechanism. That is the right statutory architecture for a package like this. The remaining question is explanatory completeness, not basic authority fit.

## Step 8 — Procedural Normalcy

This looks like a normal EPA NPRM process: a summary, comment deadline, contact information, and a set of supporting documents that map onto the proposed approvals. The public process is not obviously irregular. The only unusual feature is the breadth of the package, which combines several analytically distinct approvals into one proposal.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Failure mode 1: borderline threshold flips.** If a candidate Muskegon source is mismeasured or a permit condition changes, the negative declaration could become a case-by-case VOC RACT matter.

**Failure mode 2: enforceability drift.** If the SIP-incorporated permit sections or supporting controls are later revised without a clean public crosswalk, the Berrien RACT approval becomes harder to audit.

**Failure mode 3: transport rationale overreach.** If local NOx contributions matter more than the notice suggests, the waiver logic narrows and EPA should explain why additional local controls would still be unhelpful.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

The proposal creates clear incentives to preserve current controls, keep permit conditions stable, and maintain clean monitoring and inventory records. It also nudges the state toward completing the later MAPCR revisions that the serious attainment plan says are expected in late 2026 to early 2027. That is practical, but it means the current notice should explain how today's SIP approvals and tomorrow's rule revisions fit together.

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

**Strong points:** The record is strong on monitoring, emissions inventory screening, permit references, field verification, and landfill-control documentation.

**Moderate point:** The actual-emissions proxy and unit-level screen are understandable, but they are still proxy choices. A public reader should be able to see why they are conservative enough for a 50 tpy serious-ozone screen.

**Weak point:** The main preamble does not reproduce the full crosswalk from candidate source to final disposition, so the evidence is easier to trust than to reconstruct.

## Step 12 — Administrative Record Gaps

**Observed gap:** The main notice does not itself reproduce the source-by-source screening chain for Muskegon or the permit-by-permit crosswalk for Berrien.

**Observed gap:** The notice also compresses the threshold logic for the 10 tpy proxy, the 2.7 tpy unit screen, and the 50 tpy major-source cutoff into references that live mostly in the attachments.

**Inference:** This is a readability and reconstructability gap more than an evidence gap. The docket appears to contain the needed material, but the public-facing notice could make the record easier to test.

## Step 13 — Litigation Vulnerabilities

The strongest litigation-facing vulnerabilities are the same ones a constructive commenter would care about: whether the threshold screens are conservative, whether the permit crosswalk is clear, and whether the landfill-control choice is sufficiently self-contained in the main notice. The authority citations themselves look ordinary. The potential problem is public auditability, not a missing statutory basis.

## Step 13A — Structural Integrity / Nonsense Detector

The proposal is structurally coherent. It is a dense, multi-track SIP package, but it does not read as a disguised reopening, an extension notice, or a contradictory record. The weakness is presentation density, not nonsense. A reader can follow the spine if they move across the attachments, but the main notice could carry more of that load itself.
