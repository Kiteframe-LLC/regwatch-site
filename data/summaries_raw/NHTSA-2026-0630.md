## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim: “without detriment to vehicle safety”

* **Underlying assumption:** Placard location is purely informational and moving it from the driver's side to the left side does not reduce usability for operators, occupants, inspectors, or emergency personnel.
* **Failure scenario:** If vehicle layout, passenger access pattern, maintenance workflow, or inspection practices are not left-oriented, the left-side substitute may make the placard harder to find or less standardized than the current rule assumes.
* **Procedural implication:** The no-safety-harm conclusion may be directionally plausible but under-explained.

### Claim: “the left side of the vehicle may be substituted”

* **Underlying assumption:** “Left side” is a sufficiently stable replacement concept across unconventional ADS vehicle designs.
* **Failure scenario:** A shuttle-style vehicle with unusual door placement, no obvious front-facing operator perspective, or symmetrical cabin access may make “left side” less intuitive than NHTSA assumes.
* **Procedural implication:** The agency may need to explain why left-side substitution is superior to other formulations, such as a side designated by manufacturer documentation or a more functional placement standard.

### Claim: “does not anticipate any new regulatory costs”

* **Underlying assumption:** Manufacturers already know where to place the placard under the amended language, and no redesign, documentation, or approval burden will follow.
* **Failure scenario:** Manufacturers may still need design review, internal compliance work, or alternate-location approval submissions for unconventional vehicles.
* **Procedural implication:** The cost discussion is light and should be treated as provisional rather than settled.

### Claim: “reduced need for vehicle exemptions”

* **Underlying assumption:** Existing or future ADS vehicles are materially constrained by the current driver's-side wording.
* **Failure scenario:** If only a small number of vehicles are affected, the exemption-reduction benefit may be real but modest.
* **Procedural implication:** A commenter could reasonably ask for examples or estimated counts of affected platforms.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

* NHTSA proposes to amend FMVSS No. 110, specifically the S4.3 placard requirement.
* The proposal would let a vehicle without a driver's side use the left side of the vehicle as the substitute placard location.
* If none of the listed locations is practicable, manufacturers could still submit alternate-location drawings or photographs to NHTSA for approval.
* The proposal applies to ADS-equipped vehicles without manually operated driving controls.
* NHTSA states the amendment is part of a broader vehicle-automation modernization effort.

### Inference

* This is a narrow deregulatory and clarifying amendment rather than a major rewrite of safety requirements.
* The agency's real policy choice is not whether to require a placard, but how much standardization to preserve when conventional “driver's side” assumptions no longer fit the vehicle design.

### Evidence Quotes

* “The proposed modification would amend a single section of the standard to enable compliance by affixing the required placard on the left side of the vehicle when there is not a ‘driver's side’...”
* “This rulemaking would allow flexibility in complying with the standard without detriment to vehicle safety.”
