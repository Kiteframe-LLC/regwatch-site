## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_consent_gap
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Consumers lack a meaningful way to consent to or decline promotional notifications.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the app flow already gives a clear, separate choice for marketing messages, the alleged deficiency is narrower than the petition suggests.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters should describe the actual consent flow and whether it is distinct from account alerts.
- **Claim Id:** claim_stop_mechanism
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Marketing alerts cannot be disabled separately from essential notices.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the platform can technically separate marketing from operational messages, the record can support a narrower control requirement.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for evidence on how notification categories are implemented in practice.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The FTC is seeking comments on whether to act on a petition about wagering-app push notifications and, if so, how to distinguish marketing notices from essential account communications.

**Affected Entities**
- wagering app users
- app operators
- mobile marketing vendors
- consumer protection groups
- responsible-gambling researchers

**Core Tradeoff:** Any future control over promotional notifications must preserve essential account, security, and fraud communications.
