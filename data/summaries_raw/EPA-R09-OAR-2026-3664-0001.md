### Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim: available monitoring data are enough to show impracticability by December 31, 2025
- Underlying assumption: the comparison between 2024 monitored concentrations and the annual-average concentration needed for an attaining 2025 design value is a reliable practical basis for concluding that 2025 attainment is infeasible.
- Failure scenario: if unusual 2025 meteorology, unexpected source reductions, or stronger-than-expected implementation of pending measures could materially change annual averages, the impracticability conclusion may look too confident from a one-year comparison.
- Procedural implication: commenters can ask EPA to summarize any sensitivity analysis or alternative scenarios showing why 2025 attainment remains impracticable even under favorable conditions.

### Claim: California has complied with all applicable Moderate-area requirements and commitments
- Underlying assumption: prior conditional approvals, incentive-program reductions, and rule revisions remain sufficient to treat the 2016 Moderate-area commitments as satisfied for extension purposes.
- Failure scenario: if any credited commitments were delayed, underperformed, or depended on measures whose reductions were not actually realized, the extension precondition could be less secure than the preamble suggests.
- Procedural implication: commenters can ask EPA to provide a concise table matching each prior commitment to the evidence EPA relied on to treat it as fulfilled.

### Claim: the identified four MSM measures and related plan elements justify the most-stringent-measures finding
- Underlying assumption: the plan's screening correctly identifies the feasible most stringent measures used or achieved elsewhere and the selected South Coast measures are both feasible and meaningful for PM2.5 attainment.
- Failure scenario: if feasible intermediate or stronger measures were screened out on thin reasoning, the MSM finding may rest on an incomplete comparison set.
- Procedural implication: commenters can ask EPA to summarize the most important rejected measures and the reasons they were deemed infeasible or unnecessary.

### Claim: 2030 is the most expeditious practicable alternative date
- Underlying assumption: the plan's modeling and implementation schedule show no credible path to attainment before 2030.
- Failure scenario: if the modeling hinges on a small number of assumed future measures, or if interim rule acceleration could produce earlier attainment, a full five-year extension might appear underexplained.
- Procedural implication: commenters can ask EPA to explain why 2028 or 2029 were not practicable alternative dates.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

1. **Modeling sensitivity failure.** If the 2030 attainment path depends heavily on optimistic emissions or implementation assumptions, the extension may rest on a brittle forecast.
2. **Implementation-slippage failure.** If any of the identified control measures are delayed, weakened, or underperform in practice, the region could fall behind the 2030 path while still operating under an extended attainment date.
3. **Insufficient interim accountability.** If quantitative milestones and contingency logic are not explained clearly enough to the public, commenters may have difficulty testing whether EPA's backstops are adequate.
4. **Representativeness failure in compliance showing.** If prior commitments were counted as satisfied on a thin record, EPA's precondition finding could be more vulnerable than the summary suggests.
5. **Public-health framing gap.** If EPA does not connect the extra five years to interim burden on highly exposed communities, the rule may look overly technical and underexplained on consequences.
