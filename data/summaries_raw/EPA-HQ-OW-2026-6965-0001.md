  **Failure Scenario:** A substantially moored or intermittently stationary processor could be classified differently from a fixed facility despite similar discharge operations.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** EPA proposes to remove the phrase limiting the vessel exclusion to a vessel being used as a means of transportation, and to replace it with a functional distinction based on whether a point source is secured to the seabed. Conforming edits would be made to 40 CFR 122.3(a).

**Affected Entities**
- offshore seafood processors and harvesters
- offshore oil and gas and aquaculture operators
- existing NPDES permittees and permitting authorities
- coastal and marine ecosystems and communities
- the public relying on NPDES monitoring and reporting

**Statutory Authority:** Federal Water Pollution Control Act, 33 U.S.C. 1362 et seq., including sections 301, 402, 501, and 502.

**Key Regulatory Change:** If finalized, additions of pollutants by an unsecured vessel or floating craft in the contiguous zone or ocean would not be a discharge requiring NPDES authorization, even when the vessel is conducting industrial or processing operations rather than transportation.

**Observed Facts**
- The rule targets 40 CFR 122.2 and 122.3(a).
- The notice estimates $1.7 million in annual compliance savings for offshore seafood processors and $50,000 in EPA administrative savings.
- The notice acknowledges possible foregone localized seafood-discharge management and loss of permit-required monitoring and reporting.
- The required Draft Economic Analysis and optional permit examples and ICR statement were ingested.

**Inference:** The strongest comment leverage is implementation and accountability: whether the seabed-securement boundary is administrable and whether the record adequately values information and environmental safeguards that would disappear.

**Evidence Quotes**
- the addition of pollutants by a vessel or other floating craft that is not secured to the seabed would not be a discharge
- Potential foregone benefits ... could include the current management of localized seafood processing discharges
- The proposed rule would also eliminate permit-required monitoring and reporting

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

EPA considers retaining the existing transportation-use limitation and adopting the proposed unsecured-vessel exclusion. Plausible alternatives not analyzed in comparable detail include a narrower exclusion limited to specified offshore activities, continued monitoring/reporting without permits, a risk-based or volume-based threshold, phased termination of existing permits, and explicit safeguards for high-risk discharges. The permit examples and ICR show that such compliance and monitoring structures exist, but the record does not compare their retained information value against savings.
