## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

**Tests**
- **Claim:** The election can be administered through Form 1062, Schedule A, and related taxpayer records.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Taxpayers and pass-through entities can identify eligible gain, allocate basis and proceeds, transmit the covenant, and meet timing rules consistently.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A multi-tier pass-through entity or mixed-use parcel cannot obtain or transmit all required information before an owner's return deadline.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final rule should explain timing coordination, correction procedures, and whether minor entity errors should eliminate an owner's election.
- **Claim:** The election is voluntary and therefore the rule's economic impact is not significant.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A voluntary tax deferral benefit offsets the paperwork, land-record, and continuing covenant burdens for affected small entities.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A small seller incurs substantial legal, title, appraisal, or accounting costs to determine eligibility even when the election is not used or later becomes unavailable.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The RFA analysis should separate costs of evaluating and preserving eligibility from the value of deferred payment.
- **Claim:** The proposed definitions implement section 1062 without expanding the statute.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Terms such as substantially all, similar circumstance, actively engaged, and run with the land have sufficiently stable meanings in the referenced law.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A taxpayer with a partial sale, inherited property, a conservation or fallow period, or a later ownership change receives inconsistent treatment.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Examples, safe harbors, and transition guidance would reduce avoidable eligibility disputes.
- **Claim:** Acceleration rules protect collection while the transferee exception preserves installment treatment in qualifying asset sales.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The transfer agreement can be filed promptly and the transferee's financial ability can be assessed without destabilizing the transaction.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The Commissioner later rejects an agreement for a material omission, retroactively accelerating the liability after the parties relied on the exception.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The rule should explain notice, cure, timing, and review protections around rejection or later determinations.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The IRS proposes section 1062 regulations for a four-year tax installment election on qualified farmland sales to qualified farmers, covering eligibility, forms, payment timing, pass-throughs, and acceleration.

**Affected Entities:** Farmland sellers and qualified farmers; pass-through entities, owners, beneficiaries, trusts, estates, corporations, transferees, and affected small entities.

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites sections 1062, 7805(a), 1062(c)(2), and 1502.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal adds a 10-year covenant, Forms 1062/Schedule A, pass-through disclosures, payment and acceleration rules, and a transferee exception.

**Observed Facts**
- The election is four equal installments limited to applicable net tax liability.
- Qualified farmland generally requires United States property, a prior 10-year farming or qualifying lease period, and a 10-year covenant.
- The first installment is due by the return due date without regard to extensions.
- The RFA estimates 1.9 million potentially qualifying small entities and $237.40/8.45 hours per affected entity.

**Inference:** This is a statutory implementation rule. Comment leverage centers on operational clarity and support for the burden and small-entity conclusions.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Regulatory Objective:** Administer the installment election while protecting tax collection and verifying eligibility.

**Alternatives Evaluated**
- The proposal includes an eligible transferee exception to avoid acceleration in qualifying sales of substantially all assets.
- The proposal allows future forms or guidance to prescribe other filing methods.

**Plausible Alternatives Not Analyzed**
- A defined cure period for a pass-through entity's late or incomplete transmission before all owners lose eligibility.
- A safe harbor or standardized schedule for mixed-use parcels and equitable basis/allocation calculations.
- An explicit correction or amended-return process for covenant, Schedule A, or K-1 errors that are not substantive fraud or ineligibility.
- A tiered burden analysis distinguishing individual sellers, small operating entities, trusts, estates, and multi-tier pass-through structures.

**Evidence Suggesting Feasibility:** The notice already proposes detailed forms, schedules, K-1 reporting, and a transfer agreement, showing that alternative administrative designs are within the rule's design space.

**Materiality:** Moderate. These alternatives could affect whether a taxpayer loses a voluntary tax benefit because of a correctable information-flow failure, and they would help test the RFA conclusion.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Failures**
- **Mode:** Eligibility loss from information-chain failure
  
  **Scenario:** A pass-through entity does not timely provide a Schedule A or covenant copy, causing owners to lose the election even though the property and gain otherwise qualify.
  
  **Consequence:** A correctable administrative error could convert a voluntary deferral into an unavailable benefit for owners who do not control the entity's reporting.
- **Mode:** Mixed-use allocation dispute
  
  **Scenario:** Only part of a parcel qualifies and the taxpayer's equitable basis or amount-realized allocation is contested.
  
  **Consequence:** Taxpayers may face inconsistent calculations and compliance costs without a safe harbor or standardized documentation method.
- **Mode:** Acceleration uncertainty
  
  **Scenario:** A sale, cessation, liquidation, or similar circumstance triggers acceleration, or a transfer agreement is later rejected for omission.
  
  **Consequence:** The remaining tax can become due before a taxpayer understands whether the exception applies or has a practical cure path.
- **Mode:** Burden underestimation
  
  **Scenario:** The RFA estimate does not capture title, appraisal, legal, accounting, or multi-tier coordination work.
  
  **Consequence:** The final rule may understate the practical cost of preserving eligibility, especially for small entities.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Incentives**
- The four-year deferral creates a legitimate incentive for eligible sellers to document qualifying farmland and use the election.
- The 10-year covenant may discourage post-sale conversion of farmland, but the notice does not analyze how covenant enforcement interacts with local land-use changes or partial property use.
- The owner-ineligibility consequence may incentivize pass-through entities to over-collect and transmit records, or may cause owners to avoid the election when they cannot control entity compliance.
- The ability to use future forms or guidance creates flexibility but may also defer important compliance choices beyond the notice-and-comment record.

**Assessment:** The principal strategic-compliance risk is not evasion of a prohibition; it is avoidance of the election or costly over-documentation because the consequences of an information error are unclear.
