## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim: The retrofit listing for `HFO-1234yf` is now adequately supported

- **Underlying assumption:** The combination of prior SNAP history, older risk literature, and the new end-use-specific risk screen is enough to justify adding the refrigerant to the supplemental proposal.
- **Failure scenario:** The `2026b` risk screen may contain assumptions about leak scenarios, ignition sources, retrofit workmanship, or servicing practices that are more contestable than the preamble suggests.
- **Procedural implication:** Commenters have a strong basis to request fuller disclosure of the new screen's scenario design and comparison logic.

### Claim: Existing and proposed retrofit conditions are likely enough

- **Underlying assumption:** Appendix D fittings, labels, retrofit procedures, and related standards sufficiently manage the new flammability risks that arise when retrofitting systems originally designed for a nonflammable refrigerant.
- **Failure scenario:** Heavy-duty retrofit contexts may create service, misuse, or field-configuration risks not fully captured by those generic retrofit conditions.
- **Procedural implication:** EPA may need to explain why no heavy-duty-specific mitigation conditions are proposed beyond the general Appendix D regime.

### Claim: The complete/incomplete heavy-duty van distinction matters for `R-444A`

- **Underlying assumption:** Incomplete heavy-duty vans present a distinct enough risk or application profile that EPA needed to correct the earlier scope statement.
- **Failure scenario:** The notice asserts the correction but does not fully explain what practical difference in risk or design justifies treating incomplete vans differently.
- **Procedural implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to make the boundary clearer to avoid confusion in the final regulatory text.

### Claim: The omission of `HFO-1234yf` in the 2025 NPRM was fixable through a supplemental notice

- **Underlying assumption:** EPA's earlier omission was an analytical incompleteness rather than evidence of unresolved safety or legal concerns.
- **Failure scenario:** If the missing listing reflects a more substantial prior uncertainty, the supplemental notice should explain that history more transparently.
- **Procedural implication:** A more explicit explanation of why the refrigerant was omitted and why EPA now changed course would strengthen the record.
