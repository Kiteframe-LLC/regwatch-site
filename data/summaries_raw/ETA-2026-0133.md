## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** C1–C2: Schedule A rationale
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Individualized testing is legally required, or categorical findings cannot be reliably maintained.
  
  **Negation Test:** If valid categorical certification is permitted and reliable shortage measures exist, the legal and methodological reasons for wholesale repeal require reconsideration.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** Persistent shortages in a specialty or rural service area can coexist with uncertainty about a national occupational list.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Employers might emphasize national shortages to avoid evidence about local availability; the rule appropriately preserves local recruitment.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request the statutory distinction between categorical certification and exemption, and a reasoned response to concrete methods submitted in the earlier RFI.
- **Claim:** C3: two-year no-layoff filing condition
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A relevant layoff up to two years earlier reliably indicates currently available workers or circumvention.
  
  **Negation Test:** If affected workers have moved, retired or declined recall, a categorical bar does not necessarily identify available labor.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** A genuine recovery after a temporary contraction can create a vacancy with no available former employee.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Related entities or changes in SOC classification could obscure layoffs; the third-party and successor provisions reduce some evasion opportunities.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Compare six-, twelve- and twenty-four-month screens with documented recall and current-availability checks.
- **Claim:** C4–C5: uniform recruitment is effective and inexpensive
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Broader, longer recruitment increases qualified responses at limited incremental cost.
  
  **Negation Test:** If additional channels attract no qualified applicants, gross newspaper savings alone do not demonstrate proportionality.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** An experienced specialist position, a household position and a rural nursing vacancy may have different search channels and hiring urgency.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Employers might choose low-visibility free websites unless equivalence, monitoring and normal-practice requirements are enforced.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request channel-by-occupation recruitment outcomes, paid-channel baselines and 30/60/90-day sensitivity analyses.
- **Claim:** C6–C8: savings and streamlined filing
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Calculations reconcile and employers can follow a consistent calendar.
  
  **Negation Test:** An unreconciled baseline or contradictory filing windows undermines predictability even if modernization remains beneficial.

  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Differing readings of completion could lead to inconsistent acceptance rather than deliberate evasion.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Correct arithmetic and provide binding worked examples of permitted filing dates before implementation.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Observed facts:** DOL proposes to end Schedule A and special sheepherder procedures, replace newspaper ads with modern recruitment, generally require at least 90 days of advertising, bar filings after relevant layoffs during the previous two years, and expand notices, documentation and oversight. Electronic filing is largely existing practice; the proposal codifies it with mail and disability accommodations. These changes are proposed, not current requirements.

**Authority:** INA § 212(a)(5)(A), 8 U.S.C. 1182(a)(5)(A), governs worker availability and adverse wage/working-condition effects. Teacher and science/arts exceptional-ability cases retain the special equally-qualified comparison.

**Affected entities:** Sponsoring employers, U.S. applicants, laid-off and sponsored workers, staffing clients, universities, attorneys and agencies; nurses and physical therapists would lose the Schedule A route.

**Inference:** Broader visibility plausibly helps workers, but the cutoffs and filing mechanics need stronger support or correction.

**Evidence:** Proposed § 656.17(c) requires advertisements to be active “for a period of no less than 90 calendar days”; § 656.13(e)(1) prohibits relevant layoffs “within 2 years prior to the filing of the application.”

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

- The 23,835 annual volume used in layoff-notification costing is the Schedule A volume used immediately earlier; notifications also concern other PERM employers. No layoff-incidence model explains that denominator.
- Proposed § 656.10(b) requires filing within 30 days of recruitment completion; § 656.17(d) requires ads to finish 30–60 days before filing. If completion means the final ad date, the windows intersect only on day 30 when inclusive. The preamble additionally describes filing 1–29 days after the last activity.

- Proposed § 656.7 hard-codes January 19 and February 16, 2027; the preamble instead describes 60 days after final publication plus 30 transition days. January 19 is also the comment deadline.

- Fixed transition dates appear to be drafting artifacts rather than a lawful adoption of final requirements; they should be replaced with dates tied to final publication.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- An employer follows the preamble’s 1–29-day example and conflicts with the operative 30-day advertising quiet period, risking rejection after substantial recruitment costs.
- A qualified former employee has moved or declined a documented offer, yet a relevant historical layoff still prevents filing for a genuinely vacant position.
- Staffing employers cannot reliably obtain client layoff data, leading to an inaccurate attestation or a decision to avoid a placement. Defined diligence and update procedures could improve compliance.
- New former-Schedule A applications and universal recruitment-document review increase OFLC workload before staffing or systems are ready; delay can affect staffing and sponsored workers. This is a scenario, not a measured forecast.
- Mandatory campus/fair/placement recruitment poorly matches an experienced or geographically specialized job, generating paperwork with limited additional qualified applicants.
- Incorrect form routing or stale transition dates cause otherwise eligible submissions to lose filing dates; align instructions, FLAG validations and rule text before launch.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Beneficial:** Parity with ordinary hiring, wage disclosure, accessible applicant channels and retention of search records reduce incentives to hide job opportunities in PERM-only processes. Upfront documents and successor liability improve auditability.

**Evasion Risks:** SOC relabeling, restructuring and undisclosed third-party assignments could avoid a layoff screen; the same-or-similar occupation, parent/subsidiary/affiliate and successor provisions partly address this.

**Unintended:** A business might reduce customary advertising channels to avoid future parity obligations, defer needed sponsorship after unrelated layoffs, or choose nominally free but low-visibility channels. These incentives are hypotheses that can be tested with administrative outcomes.

**Monitoring Request:** Track qualified U.S. applicants, actual hires, layoff-related exclusions, processing times and correction rates by occupation, employer size and former Schedule A status.

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

**Stronger Support:** Recruitment-method and duration records, enforcement examples and near-universal electronic filing support replacing print mandates, parity rules and electronic processing. They do not establish that all employers acted improperly.

**Partially Supported:** BLS job-search durations and observed short postings support longer exposure, but do not directly compare 30/60/90-day qualified-applicant yields. Layoff circumvention is plausible, but the two-year absolute screen is justified qualitatively.

**Not Reproduced:** The newspaper-savings multiplication does not follow from stated inputs. Zero incremental placement costs are not established merely by the existence of free job boards when the rule also requires customary methods and fair/campus/placement channels.

**Limits:** DOL openly discusses nonquantified costs and requests information; uncertainty alone does not invalidate a proposal. Earlier RFI submissions have not been independently re-evaluated here, so the assertion that no satisfactory methodology emerged remains an agency claim rather than a verified census of those comments.

## Step 17 — High-Leverage Comment Angles

- **Make the filing calendar workable.** A 30-day filing deadline conflicts with a 30–60-day advertising quiet period and a 1–29-day preamble example. Fixed transition dates also need correction. **Question:** Which filing dates are valid? Align the provisions, define recruitment completion, and use publication-relative transition dates.
- **Reconcile recruitment costs and savings.** 108,786 × $1,362 equals $148.17 million, not $174.47 million. Free websites do not establish zero costs for customary paid channels or recruitment staff time. **Question:** Will DOL publish corrected formulas and realistic cost ranges, including small employers and former Schedule A cases?
- **Test the two-year layoff bar against worker availability.** A past layoff can block filing even when former employees are no longer available. Post-filing rules already use documented offers and lawful rejection reasons. **Question:** Would shorter screens plus documented recall protect workers as effectively, including at staffing-client sites?
- **Explain Schedule A repeal and manage the transfer.** The proposal adds individualized testing for nurses, physical therapists and exceptional-ability cases. Uncertain national methods do not establish that local shortages disappeared. **Question:** How do categorical certification and exemption differ legally, and what staffing, capacity and reliance scenarios support the transition?
- **Measure recruitment effectiveness across occupations.** DOL provides method and duration data, but the marginal benefit of 90 days and mandatory fair/campus/placement recruitment is less clear. It considered shorter small-employer periods. **Question:** What qualified-applicant and hiring results favor this design over 30/60-day or equivalent-channel alternatives?

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

- **Key text:** § 656.10(b): “within 30 calendar days after the completion”; § 656.17(d): “no more than 60 calendar days and no less than 30 calendar days”.
  
  **Context:** Labor-market testing precedes INA certification; the proposal’s clocks must work together. **Effectiveness:** Compatible dates prevent repeated recruitment. **Statutory compliance:** Clear timing supports genuine availability determinations. **Procedural adequacy:** Define completion and publish example calendars. **Consequence:** A correction could preserve filing opportunities and reduce disputes. **Worth a comment?** High value. **Comment posture:** collaborative clarification.
