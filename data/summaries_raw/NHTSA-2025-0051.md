## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The 4-year phase-in is the right way to ease redesign burden.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Manufacturers need an extra year because mid-cycle redesigns and tooling changes are genuinely difficult to absorb on the original schedule.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the redesign burden is less severe than the notice suggests, the extra year may delay child-restraint improvements without a well-tested reason.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for the burden evidence and for a comparison with narrower timing adjustments.
- **Claim:** Small-volume manufacturers should get a separate compliance date with no phase-in.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** SVMs face distinct product-cycle and resource constraints that make the normal phase-in structure less useful.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If SVM constraints are overstated or too heterogeneous, the separate compliance date may become a category-wide carveout without a tailored record.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask how many SVM models are affected and whether a more tailored transition would work.
- **Claim:** A two-year convertible exemption extension is justified by design difficulty.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Convertible roof and packaging constraints still justify a longer exemption for tether anchorages.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If more convertible designs can already accommodate tether anchorages, the extension may postpone the rule's benefits longer than necessary.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for current fleet examples and for a narrower carveout if some convertibles are already feasible.
- **Claim:** Routing over adjustable or removable head restraints should remain denied.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The proposed sphere-based location rule better protects tether performance and usability than a routing-based workaround.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the agency has not compared routing over adjustable head restraints against the proposed location rule, the denial may read as underexplained.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for the evidence behind the denial and for any comparative test results.
- **Claim:** The technical corrections are minor and do not change the core policy.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The corrections are only clarifying and do not materially alter compliance outcomes or reporting duties.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If any correction changes a compliance trigger or test path, the rule would need a clearer explanation of its effect.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask the agency to identify which corrections are purely technical and which have practical consequences.
