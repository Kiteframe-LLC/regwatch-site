## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The proposal is needed to implement OBBBA amendments to section 36B and related Medicaid changes.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Each proposed regulatory choice is a necessary or permissible implementation of the statute rather than an independent policy expansion.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A regulation could go beyond statutory text when it adds verification items, applies cross-referenced CMS concepts, or selects an effective date that creates uncertainty about prior tax years.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final rule should map each operative provision to a specific statutory clause and explain where discretion is being exercised.
- **Claim:** Verification criteria will improve program integrity and give Exchanges administrable rules.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Exchange systems can verify the listed items accurately and within the relevant coverage-month timing without creating avoidable false negatives.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Pending or inconsistent residency, immigration-status, income, or other-coverage data could cause a month to lose coverage-month treatment even when the household is substantively eligible.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request error rates, correction paths, timing rules, and coordination with the forthcoming CMS procedures.
- **Claim:** The narrower eligible-alien reporting approach reduces implementation cost and disruption.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Limiting revised Form 1095-A reporting to APTC households captures the important reconciliation effects without shifting burden elsewhere.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Unsubsidized families or mixed-status households may still need manual calculations, issuer assistance, or later corrections, and the notice does not quantify those effects.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The agency should disclose the comparison population, implementation assumptions, and whether a broader or staged approach would reduce total taxpayer burden.
- **Claim:** A broad family-size SEP waiver will prevent delays and out-of-pocket costs for vulnerable families.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The estimated newborn population, $222 average monthly APTC, and two-month delay are representative of the relevant SEP categories.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The estimate may not transfer from newborns to marriage, adoption, foster-care, divorce, death, or other dependent-status events, and a broad waiver could permit payment before material information is verified.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final rule should show category-specific data or explain why the newborn analysis supports all covered family-size events.
- **Claim:** The community-engagement provisions produce a workable coverage-month rule.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Exchange assessments can distinguish failure to meet community engagement from other Medicaid ineligibility reasons on a timely, reliable basis.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A change in reason for Medicaid ineligibility during a plan year could create inconsistent month-by-month tax treatment or reconciliation disputes.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The agency should provide operational examples, data fields, notice timing, and a correction process for mixed or changing determinations.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Mode:** False-negative verification
  
  **Mechanism:** A late, inconsistent, or unavailable Exchange record causes a month not to qualify as a coverage month.
  
  **Consequence:** Lost or delayed PTC, later tax liability, amended returns, or coverage disruption.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Request error-rate data, notice timing, provisional treatment, and a correction process.
- **Mode:** Cross-agency timing mismatch
  
  **Mechanism:** CMS procedures and Treasury tax consequences take effect on different timelines or use different definitions.
  
  **Consequence:** Exchanges and taxpayers receive inconsistent instructions or cannot correct a determination before tax filing.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Request a crosswalk, transition plan, and examples for periods before final CMS rules.
- **Mode:** Mixed-status allocation error
  
  **Mechanism:** Premium allocation or eligible-alien classification is wrong on a shared policy.
  
  **Consequence:** Incorrect Form 1095-A or Form 8962 calculations and disputed repayment amounts.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Request issuer correction procedures and worked examples for changing status.
- **Mode:** Family-size waiver overbreadth
  
  **Mechanism:** A broad waiver applies to event categories with different data quality and timing profiles.
  
  **Consequence:** Payment may occur before necessary information is verified or later require reconciliation.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Request category-specific evidence and a narrower or conditional waiver alternative.

## Step 17 — High-Leverage Comment Angles

- **Issue:** Verification should not convert routine data latency into a tax-credit loss without measurable safeguards.
  
  **Why it matters:** The proposal makes Exchange verification central to coverage-month treatment, but does not state error rates, service standards, or a complete correction pathway.
  
  **Ask:** Publish performance evidence and add provisional treatment, notice, reason codes, and correction protections for administrative failures.
- **Issue:** Treasury should coordinate the tax consequences with the forthcoming CMS implementation record.
  
  **Why it matters:** The notice defers Exchange operational costs and effects to HHS even though Exchange procedures determine whether a month qualifies for PTC treatment.
  
  **Ask:** Publish a joint transition crosswalk, implementation milestones, and an interim rule for periods when CMS and Exchange procedures are changing.
- **Issue:** The broad family-size waiver needs category-specific support.
  
  **Why it matters:** The principal quantitative analysis concerns newborns, while the waiver covers several other events with potentially different timing and data risks.
  
  **Ask:** Provide event-specific evidence or adopt a conditional, staged, or narrower waiver with later verification.
- **Issue:** The narrower eligible-alien reporting rule may shift burden to taxpayers outside the APTC population.
  
  **Why it matters:** The notice quantifies Exchange burden for a broader alternative but not taxpayer-side burden under the selected scope.
  
  **Ask:** Compare total system and taxpayer burden and provide a standardized worksheet or issuer correction process if the narrower rule is retained.

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

- **Verification:** The proposal makes Exchange verification a condition of PTC coverage-month treatment while deferring much Exchange implementation analysis to HHS. Verification may improve program integrity, but an uncorrected data or timing failure could reduce a household's subsidy or increase tax liability. **Worth a comment:** high. **Comment posture:** evidence request.
- **Family-size SEP waiver:** The notice estimates that roughly 50,000 newborn families benefit, then extends the waiver concept to several other family events. The record should show why newborn assumptions apply across those categories or compare conditional and staged alternatives. **Worth a comment:** high. **Comment posture:** alternatives push.
- **Distribution and reporting:** The proposal affects about 15.6 million Exchange enrollees but limits some revised reporting to taxpayers for whom APTC was paid. The agency should compare system savings with taxpayer and issuer burden outside that population, especially for mixed-status households. **Worth a comment:** moderate. **Comment posture:** collaborative clarification.
