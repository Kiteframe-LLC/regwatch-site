### Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

#### Claim

One license should cover adjoining parcels or adjacent parcels sharing the same parking lot, sidewalk, or road.

Underlying assumption: These property relationships are close enough that ATF can treat them as a single business premises without undermining licensing, inspections, or tracing.

Failure scenario: If some parcels share a road or parking lot but are operationally distinct, separated by third-party tenants, or difficult to inspect as a unified site, the line may be too broad or too uncertain.

Procedural implication: Commenters can ask ATF to clarify limiting principles and provide examples of qualifying and non-qualifying layouts beyond the shopping-center and across-the-street examples in the preamble.

#### Claim

The current separate-license or variance approach imposes unnecessary burden.

Underlying assumption: Enough FFLs face this issue often enough that the administrative burden matters in practice.

Failure scenario: If qualifying fact patterns are uncommon, or if the variance process is already working adequately, the case for a rule-level change may be thinner than the notice suggests.

Procedural implication: The final rule record would be stronger if ATF disclosed how often adjoining or adjacent property situations arise and how often variances are requested or granted.

#### Claim

Dictionary definitions and cited cases adequately support the new wording.

Underlying assumption: General legal meanings of `adjoin` and `adjacent` map cleanly onto firearms-licensing administration.

Failure scenario: If those terms remain open-ended in retail, warehouse, or mixed-use settings, commenters may argue that the rule still leaves too much discretion unexplained.

Procedural implication: ATF may need more explanation for why the chosen wording and examples create predictable boundaries for regulated parties and inspectors.

#### Claim

The rule reduces burden and saves money without adding costs.

Underlying assumption: Savings from avoided duplicate licenses and labor exceed any administrative, compliance, or inspection complications.

Failure scenario: If multi-site configurations create new disputes about qualification, if businesses still need variances in many edge cases, or if ATF must spend more staff time reviewing adjacency claims, net savings may be less clear.

Procedural implication: Commenters can ask for a more transparent accounting of the affected population, expected frequency of use, and any countervailing agency burden.

#### Claim

Case-by-case evaluation will remain manageable after the rule is broadened.

Underlying assumption: The amended standard is specific enough that discretionary judgments will be limited and consistent.

Failure scenario: If terms such as `near or close to` invite inconsistent application across field offices, similarly situated FFLs may receive uneven treatment.

Procedural implication: The record would benefit from clearer criteria, examples, or guidance on distance, barriers, and shared-access arrangements.

### Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

#### Observed Facts

- ATF proposes to amend the definition of `business premises` in `27 CFR 478.11` and the licensing-location rule in `27 CFR 478.50`.
- The amendment would allow one license to cover properties that adjoin each other, or properties that are adjacent to each other and adjoin the same parking lot, sidewalk, or road.
- ATF says FFLs currently may need separate licenses for those configurations or must seek a variance.
- The notice gives examples such as two properties across the street from each other and two stores in the same shopping outlet sharing a parking lot.
- ATF says the rule would not remove the licensing requirement; it would only clarify when one license is sufficient.
- ATF states that a per-entity savings of roughly `$150` to `$300`, plus about one hour of labor worth `$74.79`, may occur from not having to obtain and maintain more than one license.
- ATF also says it did not calculate overall industry savings.

#### Inference

This is a relatively narrow deregulatory NPRM, but it is not purely ministerial. The main public-comment leverage points are not broad constitutional arguments. They are narrower questions about how clearly the new boundary is drawn, how often the problem actually occurs, whether the savings estimate is adequately supported, and whether a case-by-case standard based on `adjacent` and shared access features will produce consistent administration.

#### Evidence Quotes

- “Currently, in those situations, FFLs have been required to apply for separate licenses for each property or otherwise seek a variance.”
- “The proposed amendments to these sections would allow FFLs to apply for and maintain one license for their business”
- “These are general scenarios and each situation would need to be evaluated based on its circumstances.”
- “while an overall industry savings was not calculated”
