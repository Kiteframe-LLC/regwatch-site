## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** TSCA section 5(a)(2) supplies the legal authority for the SNUR structure.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The rule is a valid exercise of the significant-new-use authority rather than an unauthorized expansion.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the proposed uses are not actually new or significant, the rule would rest on a weak statutory fit.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to identify the specific factors that make each use significant.
- **Claim:** EPA's PMN/order record is enough to support generic SNUR restrictions across the listed substances.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The earlier order findings and the docketed analysis justify the same control logic for the final rule.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If any substance has materially different exposure pathways or hazard profile, a one-size-fits-all restriction could be overbroad.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for substance-by-substance support instead of relying on cross-reference alone.
- **Claim:** A SNUR is needed in addition to the underlying TSCA order.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The order alone would not bind all other manufacturers or processors in the same way.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the order already fully captures the relevant control universe, the SNUR could be redundant.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask why the order is not enough and what the SNUR adds operationally.
- **Claim:** The economic impacts are limited to optional SNUNs and export notifications.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Most firms will not pursue the significant new uses, so the rule creates little direct compliance cost.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If firms need to qualify products, update controls, or redesign supply chains, the burden is larger than the preamble suggests.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to show transition, validation, and small-entity effects, not only filing fees.
- **Claim:** The significant-new-use carveouts are clear enough for future users to follow.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The 90-day notice gate and the process/carveout language can be applied without ambiguity in real manufacturing settings.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If firms or permit writers struggle to map a use to the listed carveouts, the rule could be hard to implement consistently.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for examples, guidance, or a comparison table.
- **Claim:** The CBI structure is manageable without undermining notice.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** EPA can preserve confidentiality while still giving the public enough information to understand the rule.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If too much is hidden behind confidentiality claims, public review of the specific substances and their controls becomes difficult.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to disclose as much nonconfidential substance-specific rationale as possible.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** EPA proposes six substance-specific TSCA SNURs for PMN-linked chemical substances, requiring 90-day SNUN notice before listed significant new uses and applying workplace, hazard communication, import, processing, and disposal restrictions to those uses.

**Affected Entities**
- Chemical manufacturers and processors
- Importers subject to TSCA section 13 certification
- Exporters subject to TSCA section 12(b) notice
- Downstream processors and formulators that would start a designated significant new use
- Small businesses that would need to file a SNUN and pay the reduced user fee if they pursue a covered use

**Observed Facts**
- The notice is a proposed rule under TSCA section 5(a)(2) and states that the SNURs follow earlier PMN review and TSCA section 5(e) orders.
- The rule text includes a docketed economic analysis, a CBI discussion, and substance-specific SNUR sections in Unit III.
- The significant new uses include workplace controls, hazard communication, restricted import forms, non-enclosed processing limits, and disposal limits.

**Inference:** This is a compliance-control rule that extends order-based restrictions to other actors in the supply chain, not a new hazard-determination rule by itself.

**Evidence Quotes**

- hazardous waste incineration
