## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** C1: general oversight can replace targeted OB requirements
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Broad QAPI, accreditation, professional education and voluntary quality initiatives reach the same staff, facilities and disparities.
  
  **Negation Test:** If coverage differs, removing targeted duties does not merely remove duplicate paperwork.
  
  **Counterfactual:** An unaccredited rural facility or infrequently exposed emergency clinician may not receive the cited specialty training.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** A facility may treat general QAPI as permission to stop OB-specific disparity tracking.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request a duty-by-duty coverage crosswalk and outcomes monitoring; do not assume loss of all OB standards.
- **Claim:** C2: reporting capacity remains ready during a PHE
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Facilities maintain trained personnel, accounts and interoperable data despite ending routine submission.
  
  **Negation Test:** If capacity decays, reporting starts late exactly when coordinated response is needed.
  
  **Counterfactual:** High turnover or an outbreak preceding a formal declaration defeats the assumed response-ready foundation.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Facilities can stop routine reporting entirely while meeting local infection-control requirements.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request activation tests, coverage benchmarks and a low-frequency alternative.
- **Claim:** C3: existing data can approximate Medicaid compensation shares
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Payroll, resident mix, cost reports, wage estimates and claims can be linked at facility level and comparable periods.
  
  **Negation Test:** If the approximation is biased, rescission removes transparency without an adequate substitute.
  
  **Counterfactual:** Contract staffing, mixed payers, Medicaid-only facilities and managed-care encounters complicate allocation.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** No new reporting is required even if replacement feasibility work never produces a public metric.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Pilot and publish validation before relying on replacement estimates.
- **Claim:** C4: equal-or-superior safety justifies future code waivers
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** CMS can show equivalence for specified provisions and interactions with the adopted edition.
  
  **Negation Test:** If provisions depend on a broader code package, selective substitution can weaken safety.
  
  **Counterfactual:** A waiver safe in one occupancy or system configuration may fail in another.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Optional guidance could become a broad alternative standard without ordinary public scrutiny.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Define waiver limits, technical findings, election records, challenge paths and transition rules.
- **Claim:** C5: beds proxy births
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The share of all beds measures the share of delivery hospitalizations at OB facilities.
  
  **Negation Test:** If the denominator already represents deliveries in relevant facilities, multiplying by 60 percent understates affected encounters.
  
  **Counterfactual:** A maternity-heavy hospital and a hospital dominated by non-OB beds have different birth-to-bed ratios.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** An understated denominator could make added consent duties appear cheaper than they are.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Use delivery counts by eligible facility; identify existing consent practices and actual collection activity.
- **Claim:** C6: emergency transfers need no Ombudsman copy
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Appropriate initial transfer also implies no later discharge or readmission issue.
  
  **Negation Test:** A later refusal to readmit can escape early independent awareness.
  
  **Counterfactual:** An initially justified hospital visit becomes a contested permanent discharge.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** An emergency label can remove routine external visibility.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Test an exception that restores notice for readmission disputes and tracks transfers in a low-burden log.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

This proposal would remove selected training, OB quality-improvement, vaccine-education and reporting requirements; add consent and release procedures for birth tissues from live births; update incorporated fire, facility and emergency-power codes; and revise CAH location and nursing-home physical-environment rules. It retains general quality and infection-control duties, resident vaccine protections, and ICF/IID direct-care compensation reporting. Embedded RFIs ask about transplant-program entry, CRNA supervision and hospital infection-data reporting; they do not themselves change those policies.

Observed Fact: CMS proposes changes to participation and payment-transparency rules under provider-specific Social Security Act authorities. Inference: benefits depend on whether remaining safeguards deliver comparable protection and saved time actually reaches patient care.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Objective: reduce clinical training and QAPI burden.** Evaluated: retain OB QAPI outside rural facilities; reduce training frequency or narrow the staff covered (V.D.2–3). These are not missing alternatives. Request a cost-and-coverage comparison with a competency-based minimum, use of existing accreditation evidence, and low-burden OB outcome review. Existing general QAPI and cited training resources make this administratively plausible, but effectiveness remains to be shown.

**Objective: reduce routine surveillance burden.** Evaluated: reduce reporting frequency/scope and narrow PHE reporting to communicable diseases (V.D.1). CMS rejects these qualitatively. Compare automated submission or a representative sentinel system, minimum maintained accounts and periodic activation drills with complete routine repeal. These options address the stated reporting objective without assuming national visibility follows from facility-level duties.

**Objective: avoid inaccurate payment ratios.** Evaluated: estimated staff-time allocations and existing administrative datasets (V.D.7). Consider a validated pilot, staged reporting, or publication of uncertainty bands before removing the public measure. Existing data are a feasible starting point, not demonstrated equivalence.

**Objective: safety-code flexibility.** Evaluated: wait for the 2027 editions and narrower outside-window exemptions (V.D.5–6). Those alternatives are expressly considered. A time-limited, publicly documented waiver with provision-specific findings could retain flexibility while limiting future substitution; no technical feasibility finding is made here.

**Objective: resident protection with less transfer paperwork.** Compare an emergent-transfer exception with prompt notice if return is refused and an auditable transfer log. The notice already retains resident/representative notice and appeal rights; targeted external notice addresses a different function.

V.C.7 applies 59 percent hospital and 1 percent CAH all-bed shares to 3,583,224 delivery hospitalizations from 2019, estimating 2,114,102 and 35,832 patients. This is a denominator-alignment concern, not proof that every delivery entails a covered tissue activity. V.C.12 acknowledges costs for electrical maintenance, heliports, extinguishers and high-rise sprinkler/relocation requirements. The preamble estimates a 12-year compliance horizon for affected existing ASC/ESRD high-rise buildings but lacks a count of exposed facilities. ## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Maternal safety:** staff unfamiliar with uncommon emergencies may miss targeted training; general QAPI may not preserve subgroup review. Continued OB organization, staffing, clinical and emergency-readiness duties mitigate but do not resolve this coverage question.
- **Outbreak detection:** voluntary submissions could leave geographic/facility gaps before a PHE; dormant reporting workflows could reactivate slowly. Retained PHE authority is a safeguard, not evidence of immediate readiness.
- **Resident transitions:** an emergency transfer may be followed by refusal to readmit without early Ombudsman awareness. Resident notice and appeal protections remain.
- **Payment oversight:** compensation ratios may disappear before an alternative is validated, reducing public comparison of payments and staffing investment. ICF/IID direct-care reporting is retained.
- **Facility environment:** broad room/bathroom grandfathering may preserve access but also prolong crowding; construction approval dates and reconstruction triggers need consistent survey interpretation. Direct-care staff involvement remains required even though representative involvement would cease to be mandatory.
- **Safety systems:** selectively applying later code provisions may create version conflicts; high-rise retrofit/relocation cost could exceed averages. These are conditional operational risks, not findings of unsafe NFPA standards.
- **Birth tissues:** emergency clinical care, pathology needs, third-party requests and cultural release requests require clear consent timing and custody records. The proposal includes clinical priority, infection-control limits, refusal protections and safe-handling instructions.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

Removing prescriptive documentation may free useful capacity, but some facilities may reduce underlying activities rather than only duplicate paperwork. General QAPI and infection-control survey standards should identify the minimum expected conduct. An emergent-care transfer exception could make disputed non-readmission less visible; monitor that pathway without treating routine hospital care as suspect. Room-occupancy grandfathering may favor reuse of existing buildings over modernization; clarify genuine reconstruction and approval dates. Optional newer-code waivers create incentives to select favorable provisions, making compatibility review important. No evidence establishes intentional gaming, viewpoint-based targeting or partisan misuse. Birth-tissue financial-interest disclosures could improve consent incentives; clinical decisions must remain independent of donation or refusal.
