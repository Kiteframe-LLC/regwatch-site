## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Exemptions are needed to protect confidential law-enforcement information and preserve candid adjudicatory communications.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The affected records are sufficiently investigative or law-enforcement-sensitive that access, amendment, or accounting would materially compromise investigations or recommendations.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The system contains mixed records, including applicant-submitted or administrative material that could be disclosed or corrected without revealing sources or investigative methods, but the broad exemption causes all such material to be treated as restricted.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask DOJ to identify record categories and explain how it will segregate releasable or correctable material from genuinely protected material.
- **Claim:** Exempting amendment and accuracy duties protects the integrity of an adjudicatory record.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Individual correction requests would be ex parte interventions that undermine the official record rather than a controlled way to correct inaccurate information.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Third-party or non-federal source data are inaccurate, stale, or mismatched, and the applicant has no meaningful pathway to identify and challenge the error before the Attorney General acts.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request a non-ex parte correction, notice, or redress process and standards for handling disputed information.
- **Claim:** The record-specific limitation and discretionary waiver prevent overbreadth.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Officials will consistently determine when a particular record falls within 5 U.S.C. 552a(j) or (k) and will waive exemptions whenever compliance would not interfere with system purposes.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Officials apply the limitation inconsistently or rarely use the waiver because no criteria, approval record, or review mechanism is specified.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for decision criteria, documentation, supervisory review, and aggregate reporting for exemption and waiver determinations.
- **Claim:** The proposed rule has limited economic and small-entity effects.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The rule affects only personal Privacy Act rights and does not materially alter burdens on applicants, data sources, representatives, or organizations interacting with PARDON.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Applicants and representatives incur additional delay, duplication, or dispute costs because they cannot access, amend, or obtain disclosure-accounting information, while non-federal data contributors face unclear accuracy responsibilities.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask DOJ to distinguish direct regulatory costs from rights and process effects that may not be captured by small-entity or economic-significance screens.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** DOJ proposes to amend 28 CFR 16.79 to exempt records in JUSTICE/OPA-002, the Firearms Rights Restoration Electronic Records Database, from specified Privacy Act accounting, access, amendment, relevance, direct-collection, and accuracy provisions when the statutory law-enforcement exemption predicates apply.

**Affected Entities:** Applicants seeking restoration of federal firearms rights; individuals whose information is collected or supplied during investigations; PARDON and DOJ officials; other federal, state, tribal, and local agencies contributing information; and representatives or third parties seeking to understand or challenge records used in adjudication.

**Statutory Authority:** The notice relies on 5 U.S.C. 552a(j)(2), 5 U.S.C. 552a(k)(2), 5 U.S.C. 553, and the Attorney General's authority under 5 U.S.C. 552a and related delegations.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal would list JUSTICE/OPA-002 in § 16.79(c), specify nine categories of Privacy Act protections from which qualifying information may be exempt, and state that DOJ may waive an exemption in its sole discretion when compliance would not interfere with the system's purpose or law-enforcement operations.

**Observed Facts**
- The covered system will receive, track, and adjudicate applications and prepare recommendations for the Attorney General under 18 U.S.C. 925(c).
- The proposed exemptions cover disclosure accounting, notice of amendment to recipients, individual access and amendment, maintaining relevant and necessary information, collecting directly from the subject, and maintaining accurate, relevant, timely, and complete records.
- The notice gives subsection-specific rationales involving confidential sources, ongoing investigations, third-party privacy, the official adjudicatory record, and multi-source data.

**Inference:** The strongest comment leverage concerns tailoring, accuracy safeguards, and accountable waiver administration.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Overclassification of mixed records
  
  **Mechanism:** Officials treat the presence of any sensitive information as sufficient to restrict an entire record or file.
  
  **Consequence:** Applicants cannot see or correct non-sensitive information that affects adjudication.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Record-category rules, segregation standards, redaction guidance, and periodic supervisory review.
- **Failure Mode:** Propagation of inaccurate source data
  
  **Mechanism:** Non-federal or third-party information enters the system while e(5) accuracy duties are exempted.
  
  **Consequence:** An error can influence a recommendation without a timely correction path.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Provenance fields, confidence or verification markers, dispute flags, correction timelines, and decision-maker notice.
- **Failure Mode:** Waiver becomes nominal
  
  **Mechanism:** Sole-discretion language is used without criteria or reporting.
  
  **Consequence:** The formal waiver does not constrain unnecessary withholding.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Written standards, approval levels, reasons, appeal or supervisory review, and aggregate waiver reporting.
- **Failure Mode:** Disclosure accounting becomes unavailable even when harmless
  
  **Mechanism:** The c(3) exemption is applied broadly because accounting could reveal that a record exists.
  
  **Consequence:** Affected individuals cannot understand downstream disclosures or identify a need to challenge misuse.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Delayed, redacted, or aggregate accounting alternatives after investigative sensitivity declines.
