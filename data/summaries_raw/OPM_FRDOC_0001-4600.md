# Phase 2 — Claim-Centered Analysis

## Step 0 — Extract Agency Reasoning Claims

1. **The statute requires implementing regulations.** OPM says the First Responder Fair RETIRE Act amended CSRS and FERS statutes and directed corresponding regulations.
2. **A qualifying duty injury should not erase enhanced retirement protection.** The proposal would preserve special retirement treatment for covered first responders who can no longer perform the original covered job but return under a qualifying appointment.
3. **Covered placement should remain the preferred outcome.** OPM proposes that agencies seek related supervisory or administrative covered positions before relying on deemed coverage in a non-covered position.
4. **Agency certification is the gatekeeper.** The employing agency must certify the duty connection, permanent inability to perform the covered job, and continued ability to serve in Federal employment.
5. **The rule should operationalize timing and eligibility conditions.** OPM proposes definitions and rules addressing qualifying injuries, reappointment, breaks in service, deemed coverage, and recordkeeping.

**Bottom line:** The statutory implementation theory is coherent, but important eligibility and agency-certification judgments remain largely in agency hands and deserve operational detail.

## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim: enhanced retirement protection should follow a qualifying injury
**Assumption:** the statutory criteria can be applied consistently across agencies and occupations.
**Failure scenario:** similarly situated first responders receive different outcomes because agency certification practices differ.
**Procedural implication:** OPM should explain review, documentation, and quality-control standards.

### Claim: covered supervisory or administrative placement should be attempted first
**Assumption:** agencies can identify genuinely related covered positions and make them available.
**Failure scenario:** an aspirational placement preference becomes a delay or an inconsistent screening device.
**Procedural implication:** the final rule should distinguish a good-faith search from a mandatory placement obligation.

### Claim: a non-covered reappointment can preserve enhanced treatment
**Assumption:** the qualifying reappointment and break-in-service rules are administrable from personnel records.
**Failure scenario:** leave, OWCP status, agency changes, or pay-system differences produce disputes over eligibility.
**Procedural implication:** OPM should provide examples and a clear record trail.

### Claim: the proposal can cover CSRS and FERS together
**Assumption:** CSRS applicability is meaningful despite the age and workforce facts OPM identifies.
**Failure scenario:** part 831 amendments create confusion or unused procedures.
**Procedural implication:** commenters should ask OPM to justify retaining or omitting the CSRS provisions.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** Implement the First Responder Fair RETIRE Act through amendments to 5 CFR parts 353, 831, and 842.

**Affected Entities**
- Federal law enforcement officers, firefighters, customs and border protection officers, air traffic controllers, nuclear materials couriers, Capitol Police, and Supreme Court Police personnel
- Federal agencies and human-resources offices
- Employees with qualifying duty-related injuries or illnesses
- Retirement and workers' compensation administrators

**Statutory Authority:** 5 U.S.C. 353, 8151, 8336, 8347, 8412, and 8461, as amended by the First Responder Fair RETIRE Act.

**Key Regulatory Change:** Create definitions and procedures for qualifying conditions, qualifying reappointments, agency certification, breaks in service, deemed covered positions, and retirement treatment after reappointment.

**Observed Facts**
- The Act applies to qualifying injuries or illnesses incurred on or after December 9, 2024, according to the notice.
- The proposal requires a covered position at the time of injury, a direct duty connection, permanent inability to provide useful and efficient service in that position, and continued ability to serve in Federal employment.
- A qualifying reappointment must satisfy position, agency, coverage, and timing conditions.

**Inference:** The practical shift is to preserve enhanced retirement treatment during a medically necessary transition to another Federal position, while leaving agencies responsible for the key factual certification.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Alternatives that deserve more analysis:**
- A uniform OPM certification form and evidentiary checklist.
- A centralized OPM review or appeal process for disputed agency certifications.
- Explicit safe harbors for leave, OWCP, agency transfer, and records gaps.
- A narrower or delayed CSRS implementation if OPM cannot identify practical applicability.

**What the notice does instead:** it assigns the principal factual determination to the employing agency and provides broad definitions, without fully comparing centralized review, staged implementation, or standardized documentation.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

1. **Uneven certification.** Agencies may apply medical and causation criteria differently.
2. **Opaque denial.** Employees may lack a clear way to understand or challenge a rejected certification.
3. **Placement delay.** The preference for covered supervisory or administrative placement could delay reemployment if responsibilities are unclear.
4. **Record discontinuity.** Transfers, leave, OWCP periods, and agency changes could produce disputes over the three-day break rule or deemed coverage.
5. **Privacy risk.** Medical evidence may be shared or retained without a sufficiently clear minimum-necessary protocol.
