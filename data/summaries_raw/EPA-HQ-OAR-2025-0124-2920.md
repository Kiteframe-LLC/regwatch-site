## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Section 111 does not authorize regulation of power-plant GHG emissions in response to global climate change concerns.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The statutory terms ‘air pollution,’ ‘welfare,’ ‘cause or contribute,’ and ‘significantly’ exclude a global causal chain even when the regulated source category contributes to the relevant atmospheric concentration.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A commenter could show that the proposed local/regional reading treats the geographic scale of the effect as dispositive without fully resolving the statutory text, structure, prior agency interpretation, or the distinction between the pollution and the source category’s contribution.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** EPA should identify the complete textual and structural bridge for the proposed interpretation and address contrary readings rather than relying mainly on history and policy significance.
- **Claim:** The major questions doctrine independently prevents the proposed standards.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The standards represent a new economically and politically significant assertion of authority rather than an application of a delegation Congress already made in section 111.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If section 111’s text and historical implementation provide a sufficiently specific delegation, the major-questions rationale may not independently support repeal without a more precise account of what makes this application qualitatively different.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to distinguish the statutory delegation, the prior 2015 and 2024 rules, and the Supreme Court precedents on which the proposal relies.
- **Claim:** Complete elimination of U.S. power-sector GHG emissions is a conservative proxy for section 111 controls and proves futility.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The relationship between the complete-elimination scenario and the narrower, source-specific reductions achievable under section 111 is sufficiently monotonic and policy-relevant for legal purposes.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A result that is de minimis under a global-temperature or sea-level metric may still coexist with material domestic emissions, co-pollutant, health, welfare, or technology-development effects; the proxy may therefore answer a different question from whether the source category contributes significantly.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** EPA should disclose the model runs, uncertainty intervals, plausible section 111 reduction cases, and the reasons global indicators are the decisive statutory measure.
- **Claim:** Repeal will produce substantial cost savings and the same modeling can represent both the broad and narrower alternatives.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The 2024 CPS scenarios remain an adequate proxy despite later market, regulatory, demand, tax-credit, and technology changes, and the scope difference between alternatives would not materially change modeled outcomes.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Updated conditions or a separate model run could change dispatch, retirements, compliance choices, local air-quality effects, or the relative cost of the alternatives.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request an updated baseline and a scope-specific comparison before EPA selects a final action.
- **Claim:** The proposed repeal can be finalized while preserving criteria-pollutant standards and other CAA requirements.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The regulatory and preemption relationships are sufficiently separable that removing GHG standards will not create gaps or implementation confusion in related programs.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** States, sources, and permitting authorities could face uncertainty about overlapping source categories, existing plans, reliance interests, or how repeal changes compliance pathways and co-pollutant outcomes.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** EPA should provide program-by-program implementation examples and explain how transition, reliance, and enforcement questions will be handled.
- **Claim:** The proposal adequately accounts for costs, benefits, distributional effects, and uncertainty.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The RIA’s stated limitations and planned future analysis are sufficient at proposal stage even though current estimates use an older baseline and do not monetize CO2 effects.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If future analysis materially changes the estimated costs, emissions, or affected communities, the current proposal may not give commenters a stable basis for evaluating the choice among repeal options.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** EPA should identify which future analyses are decision-critical and commit to completing them before final action, with an opportunity for meaningful response.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Outdated baseline drives final scope selection
  
  **Mechanism:** The final decision uses 2024 CPS scenarios despite later market, regulatory, demand, tax-credit, and technology changes.
  
  **Consequence:** Projected cost savings, emissions changes, dispatch, and affected communities may differ materially from current conditions.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Request updated baseline runs or a bounded sensitivity analysis before finalizing the repeal scope.
- **Failure Mode:** Global futility metric displaces domestic consequences
  
  **Mechanism:** GMST/GSLR results are treated as decisive while local/regional co-pollutant and welfare consequences receive less weight or remain unmonetized.
  
  **Consequence:** The final rationale may not explain why the chosen global indicators answer the full statutory and policy question.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Ask EPA to address domestic health, welfare, co-pollutant, and technology effects separately.
- **Failure Mode:** Full and narrower alternatives are treated as equivalent
  
  **Mechanism:** One modeled impact set is used as the best estimate for both alternatives.
  
  **Consequence:** The record may not reveal whether a narrower action could capture the asserted benefits with fewer emissions or transition effects.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Request separate scenario results or explain why the difference is immaterial.
- **Failure Mode:** Transition and reliance uncertainty
  
  **Mechanism:** States, sources, and investors have already expended resources under prior standards while EPA proposes rescission and repeal.
  
  **Consequence:** Permitting, compliance, investment, and state-plan decisions may face avoidable uncertainty.
  
  **Comment Leverage:** Provide concrete transition examples and explain how reliance interests will be considered.
