## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The labels are incompatible with updated processing equipment.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The relevant equipment cannot reliably scan, apply, or process the legacy labels and the incompatibility is material to service performance.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If incompatibility occurs only at particular facilities or for particular services, a universal discontinuation could impose unnecessary burdens on unaffected users.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask USPS to identify the affected equipment, services, facilities, and failure rates and explain why a targeted approach would not work.
- **Claim:** Eliminating the labels will improve visibility, tracking, service performance, and customer experience.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The replacement IMpb and provider-generated options will be available, accurate, and operationally superior for all affected users.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Small, occasional, metered, or non-RSS-office customers may experience gaps or added costs during the transition, especially where replacement printing is not yet available.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request baseline and projected performance data, including service-specific and office-specific transition impacts.
- **Claim:** Existing stock may be used until January 31, 2028.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The transition period is long enough to use inventory without creating confusion or incompatible processing after implementation begins.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The proposed January 17, 2027 implementation date and later stock-use date could create uncertainty about when USPS will stop distributing or applying labels and which uses remain accepted.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask USPS to publish a clear implementation matrix for distribution, application, and customer use of each label.
- **Claim:** Customers will continue to have many options to meet shipping needs.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The alternatives are comparably available, affordable, and functional for each service and customer type.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The alternatives may work for package labels but not for every Marketing Mail, Heavy Printed Matter, metered, or insurance use described in the existing labels.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request service-by-service comparison and cost information for replacement methods.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Rule Summary:** The Postal Service proposes to revise the Domestic Mail Manual to discontinue Label 888 for Tracking, Label 889 for Signature Confirmation, Label 891 for insurance up to $500, and Label 892 for insurance over $500. The proposal deletes the legacy label option for some services, directs retail employees to affix a USPS Tracking label, adjusts the text for electronic tracking, and allows existing stock through January 31, 2028.

**Policy Problem:** USPS says the legacy labels are incompatible with updated processing equipment and no longer support operational needs. It proposes IMpb-compliant 4-by-6-inch shipping labels and other provider or retail alternatives.

**Affected Entities**
- Retail customers using USPS-provided tracking, signature, or insurance labels
- Metered customers and postage-meter providers
- Commercial mailers and mailers using Marketing Mail or Heavy Printed Matter parcels
- Post Office employees and facilities being upgraded to RSS or new printing devices
- Recipients who rely on tracking, signature confirmation, or insurance processing

**Key Mechanisms**
- Discontinue USPS printing and distribution of Labels 888, 889, 891, and 892
- Remove Label 888-based tracking and Label 889-based signature-confirmation options from specified DMM provisions
- Shift customers toward IMpb-compliant 4-by-6-inch labels and meter-provider solutions
- Permit existing label stock through January 31, 2028
- Implement the proposed change effective January 17, 2027

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Customers lose practical access to tracking or insurance options during the transition
  
  **Trigger:** A non-RSS office, meter provider, or commercial workflow cannot produce the replacement format when legacy stock is no longer supplied.
  
  **Consequence:** Mailers may face delay, extra trips, additional vendor costs, or reduced service choices.
  
  **Mitigation:** Publish office-by-office readiness information, transition support, and an exception process.
- **Failure Mode:** Conflicting effective and stock-use dates create inconsistent acceptance practices
  
  **Trigger:** USPS stops printing or applying labels on a different schedule from the date customers may use existing stock.
  
  **Consequence:** Customers and employees may receive inconsistent guidance or reject otherwise permitted mailpieces.
  
  **Mitigation:** Define the permitted use of each label by date, service, facility, and customer type.
- **Failure Mode:** Replacement methods shift costs to small or occasional mailers
  
  **Trigger:** IMpb-capable equipment, commercial software, or meter-provider changes are unavailable or expensive.
  
  **Consequence:** Reduced access to services or disproportionate burden on smaller mailers.
  
  **Mitigation:** Provide cost comparisons, low-volume options, and longer transition support.
