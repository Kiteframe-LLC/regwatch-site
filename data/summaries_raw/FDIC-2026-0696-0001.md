## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

Underlying assumption: Principles-based standards will be specific enough to supervise novel stablecoin activities consistently.
Failure scenario: Institutions receive uneven supervisory expectations because the rule leaves too much operational detail to later examiner judgment.
Procedural implication: Commenters can ask for examples, baseline control expectations, and clearer links between principles and supervisory outcomes.

Claim: Deposits backing a payment stablecoin should be insured to the PPSI, not to the stablecoin holders on a pass-through basis.
Underlying assumption: The corporate-deposit framework is the correct legal fit for reserve arrangements and can be administered predictably in a failure.

Procedural implication: Commenters can ask the FDIC to provide clearer examples of reserve-account structures and claim treatment in failure scenarios.

Procedural implication: Commenters can press for more granular burden examples and institution-type distinctions.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

Observed Facts:
- The proposal would amend `12 CFR parts 324, 330, and 350`.
- Subpart A of proposed part 350 would govern FDIC-supervised permitted payment stablecoin issuers.
- Subpart B of proposed part 350 would govern FDIC-supervised custodians providing stablecoin-related custody or safekeeping services.
- The proposal would clarify that reserve deposits backing a payment stablecoin would be insured to the issuer under corporate-deposit rules, not passed through to holders.
- The proposal would clarify that tokenized deposits are deposits, not payment stablecoins.
- The notice expressly links the proposal to GENIUS Act implementation and to related rulemakings by other federal payment stablecoin regulators.

Inference:
- The rule is not just a narrow technical cleanup. It is a foundational banking-law package for how FDIC-supervised institutions may issue, custody, and describe payment stablecoins.
- The notice is also trying to reduce legal ambiguity around a politically salient question: whether reserve accounts or tokenized bank liabilities could be mistaken for insured claims of stablecoin holders.

Evidence Quotes:
- "The proposed rule would implement requirements that would apply to FDIC-supervised permitted payment stablecoins issuers (PPSIs), including requirements related to reserve assets, capital, liquidity, and risk management requirements."
- "The proposed rule would also implement requirements that would apply to FDIC-supervised PPSIs and insured depository institutions (IDIs) that provide payment stablecoin-related custodial and safekeeping services."
- "deposits held as reserves backing a payment stablecoin would be insured to the PPSI under the FDIC's coverage rules for corporate deposits, but would not be insured to payment stablecoin holders on a pass-through basis."
- "The proposed rule aims to establish a tailored, principles-based regulatory regime for FDIC-supervised PPSIs and FDIC-supervised custodians."

## Step 3 — Agency Argument Tree

Regulatory objective:
- Implement the FDIC's portion of the GENIUS Act for supervised issuers and custodians while clarifying deposit-insurance and tokenized-deposit treatment.

Problem definition:
- Stablecoin issuance and custody need banking-agency standards for reserves, liquidity, risk management, customer protections, and disclosure.
- Existing deposit-insurance rules could be misunderstood when applied to stablecoin reserve structures.
- Tokenized deposits need to be distinguished from payment stablecoins.

Data used:
- Statutory directives in the GENIUS Act.
- Existing FDIC deposit-insurance law and regulations.
- Labor-allocation and burden assumptions for implementation, reporting, recordkeeping, and disclosure.

Analytical method:
- Legal implementation of statutory directives combined with a qualitative burden analysis and limited quantitative burden estimates.

Alternatives considered:
- The notice asks for comments on further alignment with other regulators.
- The available text does not show a detailed alternatives analysis for narrower deposit-insurance treatment, different custody-control tests, or more prescriptive disclosure templates.

Agency conclusion:
- A principles-based regime, plus clarifications on insurance and tokenized deposits, will support responsible digital-asset growth while protecting customers and preserving banking-law boundaries.

Missing links:
- The notice does not provide much evidence for why a principles-based structure is preferable to a more prescriptive one for novel stablecoin operations.
- The burden discussion appears thin relative to the operational complexity of reserve segregation, custody controls, disclosures, and audit obligations.
- The notice points to interagency alignment as important but does not fully explain where divergence remains material.

Unsupported premises:
- That the burden of compliance for custodians and related institutions is minimal.
- That the proposed clarifications will be widely understood without additional examples or consumer-facing explanatory framing.

Logical jumps:
- From statutory implementation need to confidence that the selected structure is appropriately calibrated without more comparative analysis.
