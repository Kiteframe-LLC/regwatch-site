## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Removing full-board review and rotating-signatory requirements will reduce burden without compromising safety and soundness.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Annual board review, non-employee authorization, and existing supervisory oversight will preserve enough control after the prescriptive 2019 procedures are removed.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A smaller FICU may treat a broadly authorized committee or individual as sufficient without maintaining a documented, risk-based review of coverage, leaving the institution and bond carrier with less traceable oversight.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final record would be stronger if NCUA explained what records, approval evidence, or supervisory checks will demonstrate that the remaining controls work.
- **Claim:** The annual risk-based review is sufficiently effective for determining coverage amounts.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Boards can identify relevant fidelity risks without the removed cash-on-hand and money-in-transit guidance and can translate those risks into coverage decisions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** FICUs with materially different technology, insider-fraud, cash-handling, or operational risks may apply the flexible standard inconsistently or retain coverage below their actual exposure.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask NCUA to identify minimum documentation, risk factors, or supervisory expectations for the annual review.
- **Claim:** Removing the ten-year expiration for approved forms reduces unnecessary reapproval while NCUA retains the ability to review and rescind approval.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** NCUA will detect outdated or altered forms through ordinary supervision or on-demand review before a material coverage problem occurs.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A form can remain in use for an extended period after relevant statutory, regulatory, or insurance-market changes without a scheduled recheck, particularly where no event prompts NCUA review.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The record should address how NCUA will monitor approved forms and communicate changes to FICUs and carriers.
- **Claim:** Removing the § 713.5(b) and (c) guidance will simplify coverage requirements without diminishing fiduciary responsibilities.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** General fiduciary duties and annual review are sufficiently specific to guide coverage decisions across FICUs.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The word 'adequate' may leave boards uncertain about when additional coverage is expected, producing underinsurance, inconsistent examination outcomes, or reliance on insurer advice that does not fully reflect supervisory risk.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** A commenter can request examples or nonbinding supervisory factors while preserving flexibility.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

- **Regulatory Objective:** Reduce burden from board and signatory procedures while preserving fraud controls
  
  **Alternatives Evaluated:** Full removal of full-board review and rotation, with authorization for an eligible individual or committee and retention of a non-employee signatory.
  
  **Plausible Alternatives Not Analyzed**
  - Permit delegation but require a documented annual board or committee certification.
  - Retain rotation only for the highest-risk or largest FICUs, or require a documented reason when the same signatory serves consecutively.
  - Specify minimum records showing who reviewed the application, what risks were considered, and why coverage is adequate.
  
  **Evidence Suggesting Feasibility:** The notice already relies on annual review and expressly permits board discretion, suggesting a documentation-based intermediate option could fit the proposed design.
  
  **Materiality:** Material for comment because it could preserve the burden reduction while addressing the notice's stated fraud and traceability concerns.
- **Regulatory Objective:** Avoid recurring reapproval of suitable bond forms without allowing stale or altered forms to persist
  
  **Alternatives Evaluated:** Remove the automatic ten-year expiration while retaining NCUA authority to review and rescind approval.
  
  **Plausible Alternatives Not Analyzed**
  - Replace automatic expiration with periodic confirmation or a scheduled review based on statutory or regulatory changes.
  - Require carriers or FICUs to notify NCUA of material changes or to certify continued conformity.
  - Publish a current approved-form register with revision dates and transition instructions.
  
  **Evidence Suggesting Feasibility:** The notice identifies a known problem with outdated provisions and says NCUA can review forms at any time, but does not explain the operational trigger or monitoring system.
  
  **Materiality:** Material for comment because form approval is a retained safety control and the proposal removes the only described periodic trigger.
- **Regulatory Objective:** Simplify coverage amounts while preserving adequate protection
  
  **Alternatives Evaluated:** Remove advisory additional-coverage language and the cash/money-in-transit formula, relying on annual risk review and fiduciary duty.
  
  **Plausible Alternatives Not Analyzed**
  - Retain the formula as a safe harbor or illustrative factor rather than a rigid requirement.
  - Replace it with a short list of nonexclusive risk factors covering technology, insider fraud, cash, money in transit, and operational complexity.
  - Provide examples of when coverage above the minimum would ordinarily be expected.
  
  **Evidence Suggesting Feasibility:** The notice itself says a rigid formula may not fit every FICU, which supports considering a flexible factor-based alternative.
  
  **Materiality:** Material for comment because the proposed text leaves the meaning of adequate coverage largely to individual boards and examiners.
