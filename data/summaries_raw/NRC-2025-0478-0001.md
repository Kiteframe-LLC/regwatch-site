## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- ### Claim 1 - Scope narrowing
  
  **Underlying assumption**
  The NRC can identify every relevant environmental effect by limiting review to effects within its substantive statutory authority.
  
  **Failure scenario**
  If a close-causal but non-radiological effect still matters to a licensing decision, the scope narrowing could omit a consequential issue from the record.
  
  **Procedural implication**
  The final rule may need a clearer boundary rule and examples.
- ### Claim 2 - Guidance substitution
  
  **Underlying assumption**
  Moving regulatory text into guidance will preserve clarity while reducing burden.
  
  **Failure scenario**
  If staff apply guidance unevenly or if guidance changes without adequate record support, the regulated public may lose traceability and notice.
  
  **Procedural implication**
  The final rule may need explicit limits on which procedures can safely live outside the CFR.
- ### Claim 3 - Regulatory analysis
  
  **Underlying assumption**
  The draft regulatory analysis is enough to support the burden-savings claim in the notice.
  
  **Failure scenario**
  If the underlying assumptions or Appendix A sensitivities are not persuasive, the claimed savings may be overstated or unstable.
  
  **Procedural implication**
  Commenters can ask for the draft analysis and uncertainty tables to be published in full.
- ### Claim 4 - Categorical exclusions
  
  **Underlying assumption**
  Public comment alone can surface the right set of new categorical exclusions.
  
  **Failure scenario**
  If the proposal finalizes exclusions before testing edge cases, the agency may codify a category that is too broad or too narrow.
  
  **Procedural implication**
  The notice should compare intermediate exclusion options and limiting criteria.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Observed Facts**

- The notice proposes to revise 10 CFR part 51 across NRC licensing and related regulatory functions.
- The rule would narrow NEPA review to effects within the NRC's substantive statutory authority, create new categorical exclusions, and give licensees, applicants, and petitioners more flexibility to submit environmental information.
- The NRC also issues draft guidance NUREG-2270 and a draft regulatory analysis and invites comment on both.

**Inference**

- This is a broad procedural rewrite, not a narrow cleanup. Its main effect is to relocate part of the environmental-review architecture from regulation into guidance and application-specific documentation.

**Statutory authority**

- NEPA as amended by the Fiscal Responsibility Act and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, plus the AEA, Energy Reorganization Act, Uranium Mill Tailings Radiation Control Act, and the cases and executive directives cited in the notice.

**Affected entities**

- NRC licensees, applicants, petitioners for rulemaking, Tribal governments, state agencies, and the public that comments on NRC licensing actions.

- Problem definition: the NRC says some existing procedures are outdated, overly detailed, or duplicative of NEPA/case law.
- Method: narrow the effects definition, add categorical exclusions, move some details to guidance, and expand applicant/petitioner flexibility.
- Expected result: faster and less burdensome reviews that still satisfy NEPA.

**Node / Edge Map**
