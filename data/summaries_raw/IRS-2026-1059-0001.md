## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Mixed service-provider payments can be separated through itemization, with a $5,000 threshold for the itemization rule.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Service providers can reliably identify investment-management fees and other investment-related expenses, and plan administrators can apply the threshold consistently.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A bundled contract includes investment, custodial, recordkeeping, or trustee services but does not itemize them in a way that maps cleanly to the proposed categories.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final rule should explain examples and documentation expectations, including how estimates and partial-year arrangements are handled.
- **Claim:** Late-adopted plans and amendments can be included in prior-year calculations when statutory elections and effective-date conditions are met.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Plan administrators can determine the relevant adoption, effective, valuation, tax-return, and contribution deadlines without conflicting records.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** An amendment is adopted after the valuation date but before a tax-return deadline, while operations or contributions have already been administered under a different assumption.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The agency should provide a worked timeline and identify how actuarial reports, corrected calculations, and prior filings interact.
- **Claim:** The more-than-twice comparison is an appropriate way to identify disproportionate increases in target normal cost.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The ratio is stable across plan designs and does not create anomalous results when the baseline funding target or target normal cost is small.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A plan amendment produces a large percentage change because of a small denominator, or an alternative comparison would classify the same amendment differently.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for examples, sensitivity testing, and a response to the alternative measurement methods expressly solicited by the notice.
- **Claim:** The proposal has no economically meaningful behavioral effects and no significant impact on a substantial number of small entities.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The amendments mostly codify existing practice and any changes in funding or deductible-contribution calculations are immaterial across relevant plan populations.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Small sponsors, service providers, or administrators face material transition, data, or professional-service costs even if aggregate national effects are modest.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The agency should disclose the basis for the certification and invite plan-level or service-provider evidence before finalization.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Observed Facts**
- The proposal updates 26 CFR 1.430(d)-1 under Code sections 430 and 7805.
- It addresses investment-related expenses, late plans and amendments, remedial operations, a two-times anti-abuse test, pending actuarial approvals, and mortality-related conforming edits.
- The proposed applicability date is six months after final-rule publication.

**Inference:** This is a technical implementation rule, but its classifications and timing tests can affect actuarial calculations and deductible contribution timing.

**Regulatory Action:** Update minimum-funding regulations for single-employer defined benefit plans.

**Affected Entities:** Employers, plan administrators, enrolled actuaries, participants and beneficiaries, service providers, small sponsors, and professional advisers.

**Statutory Authority:** Code sections 430(g)(3)(B), 430(h)(3), and 7805, with related sections 401(b), 404(o), 412, and 436.

**Key Changes:** Define expense categories and a $5,000 itemization rule; specify late-adoption and remedial-amendment treatment; add the disproportionate-increase test; and update actuarial-assumption and mortality rules.

**Evidence Quotes:** The summary says the proposal updates target normal cost and funding target rules; the authority section identifies sections 430 and 7805; and the applicability section provides a six-month-after-finalization date.
