## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_scope_extension
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** FDA can reliably identify and register the universe of foreign establishments that actually manufacture, prepare, compound, or process tobacco products for the U.S. market.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the baseline foreign-establishment count is incomplete or quickly changes, the rule may miss relevant firms or impose costs on the wrong set of firms.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The notice should explain how the foreign baseline was built and how often it will be refreshed.
- **Claim Id:** claim_egov
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Most registrants can use TRLM-NG or a waiver process without material delay or data loss.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If firms lack reliable internet access, need translation help, or cannot navigate the workflow, the electronic-default design may slow compliance or reduce completeness.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** FDA should spell out fallback processing, translation handling, and support expectations.
- **Claim Id:** claim_public_health
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** More complete registration and listing data will materially improve enforcement and import-screening outcomes.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the data are not timely, accurate, or operationally useful, the health rationale may not translate into real-world enforcement gains.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask FDA for operational metrics, not just broad public-health framing.
- **Claim Id:** claim_costs
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The PRIA’s time and entity estimates are stable enough to support a representative cost range.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If foreign wage assumptions, filing times, or inspection time assumptions are off, the cost range may understate or overstate burden materially.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** FDA should provide sensitivity analysis and burden distributions for foreign and small firms.
- **Claim Id:** claim_adequate_means_condition
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The foreign-country adequacy condition can be applied consistently and without arbitrary delay.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the standard is vague, foreign registration could become a moving gate for import eligibility.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The agency should explain the criteria, process, and review path for that determination.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** FDA proposes to require domestic and foreign tobacco-product establishments to register, list products, maintain historical files, submit information electronically unless waived, and follow foreign-establishment procedures tied to import screening and misbranding consequences.

**Affected Entities**
- Domestic tobacco-product manufacturers, owners, and operators
- Foreign tobacco-product manufacturers, owners, and operators
- Importers and consignees that rely on foreign manufacturing chains
- FDA staff responsible for registration, listing, inspection, and enforcement
- Consumers and downstream market participants who depend on product traceability

**Observed Facts**
- The notice says foreign establishments are currently outside the registration and listing regime.
- The notice proposes electronic submission as the default and a waiver path for registrants who cannot reasonably use the internet.
- The PRIA quantifies costs and uses TRLM-NG and import-compliance data to estimate burdens and benefits.

**Inference**
- The rule is mainly an information, traceability, and enforcement rule rather than a new product-standard rule.
- The main operational burden falls on registration quality, electronic workflow design, and foreign-establishment onboarding.
- Commenters can focus on whether the proposed information system will work at the scale and diversity the notice assumes.

**Evidence Quotes**
- This proposed rule would extend registration and listing requirements to foreign establishments.
- Proposed 21 CFR 1108 generally would require the electronic submission of the registration and listing information.
- The 20-year annualized total costs ... range from $6.80 million to $26.55 million.
