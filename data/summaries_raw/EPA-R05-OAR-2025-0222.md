## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Attainment and redesignation

**Claim:** The area has attained the 2008 Pb NAAQS and should be redesignated.

**Underlying assumption:** The monitoring record is complete and representative, and the design value calculation correctly reflects the 2022-2024 period.

**Failure scenario:** If monitor siting, data completeness, or treatment of shutdown-period data is not adequately explained, commenters could question whether the record fully demonstrates sustained attainment.

**Procedural implication:** The final rule should clearly point to the monitoring tables, completeness checks, and design-value calculation in the TSD or state submittal.

### Permanent and enforceable emission reductions

**Claim:** Ambient improvement is caused by permanent and enforceable shutdown of Republic Steel permitted emission units.

**Underlying assumption:** Permit termination and court/state orders prevent resumption of relevant Pb-emitting operations unless new-source requirements are met.

**Failure scenario:** A later owner undertakes demolition, deconstruction, remediation, or site reuse that creates fugitive Pb-bearing dust while remaining outside the ordinary operating-emissions frame.

**Procedural implication:** The final rule should explain how the consent order, Ohio findings/orders, NSR requirements, and contingency measures interact for future owners and non-production activities.

### AERMOD demonstration

**Claim:** Modeling of future cleanup/remediation emissions predicts a maximum three-month average of 0.099440 micrograms per cubic meter, below the 0.15 standard.

**Underlying assumption:** The modeled cleanup/remediation scenario, emissions inputs, meteorology, receptors, background concentrations, and fugitive dust assumptions are conservative enough.

**Failure scenario:** Actual demolition or remediation activity differs from modeled assumptions in intensity, duration, location, haul-road use, or control practices.

**Procedural implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to summarize the controlling assumptions and sensitivity checks in the final preamble, especially because the preamble only gives the headline modeled value.

### RACM conclusion

**Claim:** No additional RACM would appreciably reduce fugitive dust or expedite attainment.

**Underlying assumption:** Because permitted production has permanently ceased and monitored Pb levels are at or near zero, additional routine controls would not materially improve attainment.

**Failure scenario:** Remaining contaminated structures, dust, soil, or stored material could become relevant during site work even without production.

**Procedural implication:** The final rule should distinguish between routine operating RACM, which may no longer be meaningful, and activity-triggered fugitive-dust controls for demolition or remediation.

### Contingency triggers

**Claim:** Monitor-triggered contingency measures are sufficient.

**Underlying assumption:** Daily and monthly concentration triggers provide timely warning before a rolling three-month Pb NAAQS problem develops.

**Failure scenario:** Short, high-emission demolition or material-handling episodes could occur before monitor-based triggers cause enforceable response actions.

**Procedural implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to explain whether pre-activity dust-control plans and monitor-triggered responses are both enforceable and timely enough.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Observed Facts**

EPA proposes to approve Ohio's September 19, 2024 Canton Pb SIP revision and April 25, 2025 maintenance plan and redesignation request. The area was redesignated to nonattainment effective April 10, 2023 based on 2019-2021 monitoring at the Republic Steel monitor showing a 0.40 micrograms per cubic meter design value, above the 0.15 micrograms per cubic meter 2008 Pb NAAQS. Republic Steel's Canton plant was later idled and then permanently closed, and associated air permits were terminated effective July 26, 2024.

EPA proposes to approve the emissions inventory, determine that the area attained the 2008 Pb NAAQS, approve a first 10-year maintenance plan, determine that redesignation criteria are met, and redesignate the area to attainment. EPA states that Ohio's attainment demonstration used AERMOD and predicted a maximum three-month rolling average Pb concentration of 0.099440 micrograms per cubic meter for potential future cleanup/remediation activity.

**Inference**

The rule is a cleanup and redesignation action for a localized Pb nonattainment area where the principal source has shut down. The affected entities are primarily Ohio EPA, Canton APC, Republic Steel or a future site owner/operator, and people living or working near the former Republic Steel-Canton plant.

**Evidence quotes**

> "The Canton Nonattainment Area for the 2008 Pb NAAQS surrounds the Republic Steel-Canton Plant located at 2633 Eighth Street NE in Canton, Ohio."

> "Ohio EPA performed an atmospheric dispersion modeling analysis using AERMOD which accounted for potential cleanup and/or remediation efforts at the site in the future."

> "These contingency measures would be triggered if monitored Pb concentrations at either the Republic Steel or Republic Community monitoring sites exceed certain levels."

This is a SIP approval and redesignation action, not a technology standard. Even so, the record would be stronger if EPA more clearly explained why additional pre-activity controls, enhanced monitoring, or lower action levels were unnecessary or already embedded in Ohio's orders.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

EPA's technical reasoning is coherent at a high level. The principal Pb source shut down, permits were terminated, reported emissions fell sharply, monitored concentrations fell to 0.00 micrograms per cubic meter, and modeling of future cleanup/remediation predicted a concentration below the NAAQS. The result fits the structure of a localized source-oriented Pb nonattainment area.

The main technical questions are about margins and future activity. The modeled maximum of 0.099440 micrograms per cubic meter is below 0.15 but not zero. If actual demolition or remediation differs from the modeled case, the final rule should make clear how the maintenance plan detects and controls that difference. EPA's own Pb implementation Q&A excerpts emphasize that fugitive emissions, haul roads, background concentrations, receptor placement, and light-wind meteorology can matter in Pb modeling.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

1. **Demolition/remediation dust exceeds modeled assumptions.** Cleanup, demolition, deconstruction, haul-road use, or material handling could generate Pb-bearing dust at a rate or duration greater than assumed.

2. **Future-owner implementation gap.** A subsequent owner or contractor may need clear notice of applicable orders, dust-control plan requirements, trigger levels, and response deadlines.

3. **Monitor-trigger delay.** If contingency measures depend heavily on observed daily or monthly Pb values, some activities may occur before a trigger is detected and acted upon.

4. **Limited public transparency.** Nearby residents may have difficulty tracking monitoring results, dust-control plans, or trigger responses unless EPA or Ohio commits to accessible reporting.

5. **Residual contamination pathway.** The source has shut down, but deposited Pb in dust, soil, structures, or roads can be re-entrained if disturbed.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

The proposal does not appear to create a major incentive for strategic noncompliance. The facility has ceased permitted operations, and new or reopened Pb-emitting operations would need to meet applicable NSR requirements.

The main incentive issue is practical rather than strategic: if obligations are triggered only after monitored concentrations cross action levels, contractors or future site operators may underinvest in pre-activity dust controls. A final-rule explanation that emphasizes pre-activity plans and enforceability would reduce this risk.

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

**Strong evidence**

* Ambient monitoring showed a decline from earlier exceedances to reported 0.00 micrograms per cubic meter levels by late 2023-2024.
* Republic Steel's permitted emitting units have been permanently shut down and permits terminated.
* Ohio submitted an emissions inventory and maintenance plan.
* EPA prepared a TSD and reviewed AERMOD modeling.

**Moderate evidence / needs clearer public explanation**

* The preamble states the AERMOD result but not the controlling assumptions.
* The preamble lists contingency measures but does not fully explain trigger selection and response timing.
* The preamble's RACM conclusion is plausible after shutdown, but final-rule clarity would help distinguish no additional routine RACM from activity-specific dust controls.

**Weak evidence**

Unknown from available text for distributional impacts, community-facing monitoring communication, and the practical enforceability of obligations as applied to future contractors.

## Step 16 — Evidence Requests

Commenters could request that EPA include or better summarize:

* the design-value calculation and monitor data-completeness checks for 2022-2024;

## Step 17 — High-Leverage Comment Angles

1. **Ask EPA to summarize the modeling assumptions.** A constructive comment can request a final-rule table showing the cleanup/remediation emissions scenario, source parameters, meteorology, receptor grid, background concentration, and modeled maximum.

2. **Ask EPA to explain trigger protectiveness.** A commenter can ask how the daily and monthly trigger levels provide timely protection for a rolling three-month Pb standard.

3. **Ask EPA to clarify future-owner enforceability.** A useful comment can request a plain-language explanation of how the consent order, Ohio findings/orders, NSR requirements, and maintenance-plan obligations attach to subsequent owners and contractors.

4. **Ask EPA to improve public transparency.** A local public-health comment can ask EPA or Ohio to commit to accessible posting of monitoring data, dust-control plans, and trigger-response actions during demolition or remediation.

5. **Ask EPA to distinguish routine RACM from activity-specific controls.** The final rule should make clear that the "no additional RACM" conclusion is based on permanent shutdown and does not weaken dust-control duties during future site activity.
