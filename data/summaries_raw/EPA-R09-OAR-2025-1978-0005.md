## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The adjusted 2017 inventory is a reliable maintenance baseline.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The 2017 inventory and the wildfire normalization represent ordinary attainment conditions and use stable source-category methods.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If wildfire normalization, source growth factors, or direct-PM-only treatment materially understates ordinary emissions, future projections could remain below an understated baseline while actual risk is higher.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for the underlying inventory, normalization rationale, precursor screening, and sensitivity cases.
- **Claim:** Projected emissions below the 2017 baseline demonstrate maintenance through 2036.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The growth and control factors remain reasonable across changing population, traffic, fleet, construction, and meteorological conditions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A larger-than-projected increase in on-road activity or a slower control-program turnover could erase the projected margin.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request the projection inputs, uncertainty range, and a sensitivity case for higher growth or weaker controls.
- **Claim:** Three monitors and annual network review are adequate to verify attainment.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The three SLAMS locations capture the highest-risk portions of the maintenance area and remain representative despite growth and changing source patterns.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A localized hotspot, monitor relocation, or incomplete data could leave a meaningful portion of the area under-observed.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask EPA and NNPH to identify representativeness criteria, data-completeness safeguards, and responses to a monitor outage.
- **Claim:** The 1.05 trigger and screening process will promptly identify controllable violations.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** NNPH and EPA can consistently distinguish exceptional or uncontrollable events from violations requiring new controls without delaying action.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A disputed exclusion or late exceptional-event determination could postpone selection or implementation of contingency measures.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for worked examples, the evidence standard for exclusions, and the fallback timeline when EPA and NNPH disagree.
- **Claim:** The proposed budgets are consistent with maintenance and the 2040 budget is appropriate.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The safety margins and 2040 transportation assumptions do not create a practical emissions increase that undermines the approved maintenance period.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A budget that uses most of the available margin, or a 2040 budget beyond the statutory maintenance horizon, could reduce future flexibility to respond to new data.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for the safety-margin calculation, the basis for the 2040 year, and how later monitoring will constrain conformity decisions.
