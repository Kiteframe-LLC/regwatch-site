## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

Claim: Higher thresholds are an efficient way to cut burdens.
Underlying assumption: Smaller filers and smaller hedge fund advisers are less important to systemic-risk monitoring than the added compliance burden suggests.
Failure scenario: If smaller advisers or funds can still generate important risk signals, the thresholds could remove useful data before problems appear.
Procedural implication: Commenters can ask for item-by-item evidence that the chosen thresholds preserve the information the agencies actually need.

Claim: Reasonable-estimate reporting can replace prescriptive look-through instructions.
Underlying assumption: Different internal methodologies and service-provider conventions produce comparable enough exposure figures.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- Threshold gaming: advisers may reorganize or report differently to stay below the new lines.
- Comparability loss: reasonable-estimate reporting can drift across firms if methodologies differ.
- Event blindness: deleting current-reporting items can make episodic risk developments harder to spot.
- Reconstruction problems: fewer fields can make later audits or after-the-fact reconstruction harder.
- Mixed-channel confusion: if related reporting channels are treated differently, advisers may struggle to classify hybrid interactions consistently.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

- The request for comments on private credit could draw in outside data contributors, which is useful if the agencies specify how the input will be used.
