## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim: Portola attained the annual PM2.5 standard by December 31, 2025

* **Underlying assumption:**
  The single Portola monitor produced complete, certified, and representative data for the relevant 2023-2025 period.

* **Procedural implication:**
  Commenters can press EPA to explain not only that the area attained, but how durable that attainment appears in light of the local source profile and weather sensitivity.

### Claim: The monitoring record is reliable enough for a regulatory determination

* **Underlying assumption:**
  The certification letters, network-plan reviews, and TSAs together are enough to support confidence in the AQS record.
* **Failure scenario:**
  If the supporting monitoring documents contain caveats that the notice does not summarize, the public record in the preamble may understate uncertainty.
* **Procedural implication:**
  A useful comment can ask EPA to summarize the concrete design-value and certification evidence more directly in the final rule.

### Claim: A clean data determination should suspend serious-area planning obligations

* **Underlying assumption:**
  The current attainment signal is strong enough that temporary suspension of additional planning submissions is appropriate.
* **Failure scenario:**
  If continued attainment is fragile because winter residential wood smoke remains the dominant source, suspension of certain planning elements may reduce pressure to keep contingency-oriented planning current.
* **Procedural implication:**
  The clean data determination is legally familiar, but commenters can ask EPA to explain the practical consequences of suspension and the triggers for resumed obligations if monitored conditions worsen.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

* EPA proposes two related actions for the Portola nonattainment area: a determination that the area attained the 2012 annual PM2.5 standard by the December 31, 2025 Serious-area attainment date, and a clean data determination based on 2023-2025 monitoring data.
* The proposal would suspend certain Serious-area planning obligations if EPA finalizes the clean data determination, while leaving the area designated nonattainment unless and until California obtains EPA approval of a redesignation request and maintenance plan.
