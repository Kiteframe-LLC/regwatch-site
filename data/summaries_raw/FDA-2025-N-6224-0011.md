## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

**Tests**
- **Claim:** General controls alone are sufficient for each listed accessory.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The listed accessory's intended use and risk profile are sufficiently separable from the parent device and do not require design controls.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A product described by the same category varies in materials, pressure, electrical characteristics, sterilization, compatibility, or use environment in a way that makes parent-device-specific controls important.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** FDA should show the risk and compatibility basis for each entry or explain how the general-control framework handles the variation.
- **Claim:** Device-specific accessories are generally not eligible through this process.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The proposed distinction between generic and device-specific accessories can be applied consistently from product descriptions and classification regulations.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** An accessory has some standardized features but also parent-device-specific interfaces, leaving sponsors uncertain whether it belongs on the list or needs a different pathway.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** A decision framework and examples would reduce inconsistent classification and improve the final record.
- **Claim:** The proposed classifications can be implemented through new regulations and exemption limitations in parts 870, 876, and 878.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The proposed device-type descriptions and existing classification regulations map cleanly onto actual products and future submissions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A product fits multiple categories, has a hybrid parent-device relationship, or falls within a limitation of exemption that is not apparent from the table.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** FDA should explain overlap, boundary, and transition treatment before finalizing the list.
- **Claim:** The public can propose additional accessories by supplying product codes or classification regulations.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Potential commenters can identify the relevant product code and have enough information to evaluate general-controls sufficiency.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The product-code database or classification regulation does not make the accessory-parent relationship or applicable controls clear to smaller manufacturers, clinicians, or patients.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** FDA could provide a structured template or more examples for evidence-based additions and objections.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

FDA proposes to move seven categories of medical-device accessories into Class I, distinct from their parent devices, generally relying on general controls and applicable premarket-notification exemption limits. The notice is tentative and requests comments before any final classification action. The central question is whether FDA's item-specific risk, compatibility, and control analysis is sufficiently visible to support the proposed burden reduction.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Administrative Failure Modes**
- A broad category description could be applied inconsistently across sponsors or classification regulations.
- Sponsors could be uncertain whether a hybrid or parent-device-specific accessory is covered by the final Class I regulation or remains subject to a parent submission.

**Safety Failure Modes**
- A compatibility, sterilization, electrical, pressure, or materials issue could be under-addressed if a product is treated as generic when its intended use is actually parent-device-specific.
- A mistaken exemption boundary could delay recognition of a product that needs additional premarket controls.

**Severity:** Moderate but bounded at proposal stage; the notice retains a comment and final-action step.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Positive Incentives**
- Distinct classification could reduce duplicative review and encourage clearer accessory identification by product code.
- The public invitation to propose additional categories may encourage commenters to provide structured classification information.

**Negative Incentives**
- Manufacturers may favor broad generic descriptions if doing so reduces premarket burden, even where compatibility evidence would be useful.
- Sponsors may avoid proposing useful additions if the product-code and classification-regulation evidence requested is difficult to assemble.

**Comment Angle:** Ask FDA to define category boundaries and maintain a clear path for additional controls when an accessory's intended use or parent-device interface makes general controls insufficient.

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

- **Support the burden-reduction objective with conditions.** The pathway is coherent, but FDA should publish an item-by-item risk and control crosswalk before finalizing the list. Key question: what evidence shows that general controls alone provide reasonable assurance across each category's intended-use and compatibility range? **Comment posture:** evidence request.
- **Request a staged or conditional option where variation is material.** FDA should explain why a narrower product-code list, staged review, or reassessment trigger is unnecessary for categories with different interfaces or clinical environments. **Comment posture:** alternatives push.
