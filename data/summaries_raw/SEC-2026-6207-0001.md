## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Self-custody can safely substitute for a permitted third-party custodian when the adviser documents unavailability, expertise, systems, cybersecurity controls, joint authorization, statements, and internal-control reports.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Advisers can make reliable asset-specific determinations and maintain controls that are as dependable as independent custody across changing networks and key-management arrangements.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** An adviser concludes that no custodian is available, but a viable custodian existed or the adviser’s safeguards fail during a key compromise, protocol change, insider event, or insolvency.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request comparative evidence, objective minimum factors, independent testing, and a remediation/reporting path when the determination or safeguards fail.
- **Claim:** State trust companies can provide a practical additional custodial option while protecting client and fund crypto assets through segregation, audits, and internal-control reports.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** State supervision and the proposed annual determinations are sufficiently comparable across jurisdictions and adequately address bankruptcy, operational, and network-specific risks.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A state trust company has weak or non-comparable controls, an unavailable audit, or an asset-segregation regime that does not protect customers when the entity fails.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for a state-by-state comparison, objective eligibility criteria, insolvency treatment, and specific consequences when annual review materials are late or adverse.
- **Claim:** Crypto-network records can support adviser and fund recordkeeping and improve transparency.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** On-chain records are complete, durable, attributable, accessible, and sufficiently readable for the relevant legal record even when privacy features, forks, smart contracts, or off-chain records are involved.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The network record is immutable but incomplete or difficult to interpret, while critical authorization, valuation, client identity, or off-chain control records are unavailable.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request retention, readability, reconciliation, access, and reconstruction standards for records maintained partly or wholly on a crypto network.
- **Claim:** Modernizing traditional custody rules and permitting discretionary trading under conditions will facilitate investment and reduce unnecessary compliance friction without reducing investor protection.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The new conditions prevent transfers to adviser-controlled accounts, preserve account-level records, and fit the varied operational arrangements used by advisers, funds, broker-dealers, and custodians.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Trading authority, settlement, account-control, or related-person arrangements effectively give an adviser custody without triggering the proposed safeguards.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request worked examples for non-DVP trading, related persons, omnibus accounts, and transfers involving crypto-network addresses.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Adviser self-custody determination error
  
  **Scenario:** A custodian is practically available but the adviser’s quarterly determination is incomplete, stale, or based on an overly narrow search.
  
  **Consequence:** Clients lose the separation and independent records associated with third-party custody.
  
  **Monitoring Or Mitigation:** Require documented market search factors, independent review, prompt reconsideration, and reporting after a viable custodian becomes available.
- **Failure Mode:** Private-key or authorization compromise
  
  **Scenario:** A key, shard, signer, device, or joint-authorization process is compromised, or a protocol change invalidates a control assumption.
  
  **Consequence:** Assets may be transferred irreversibly before the client or Commission can correct the event.
  
  **Monitoring Or Mitigation:** Request tested recovery, incident response, signer separation, immutable audit logs, and client-notice requirements.
- **Failure Mode:** State trust company insolvency or control failure
  
  **Scenario:** Segregation, audit, or internal-control evidence does not translate into priority and recovery protection under the relevant state-law regime.
  
  **Consequence:** Clients or funds face delays, loss, or competing claims during a failure.
  
  **Monitoring Or Mitigation:** Require jurisdiction-specific insolvency analysis, asset-level reconciliation, and a clear response when annual financial or internal-control reports are adverse.
- **Failure Mode:** Network-record incompleteness
  
  **Scenario:** A fork, privacy feature, outage, smart contract, off-chain transaction, or discontinued network makes the network record insufficient to reconstruct the legal account.
  
  **Consequence:** Auditors, clients, and examiners cannot reliably determine ownership, authorization, valuation, or transaction history.
  
  **Monitoring Or Mitigation:** Require human-readable mirrored records and periodic reconciliation to addresses, keys, accounts, and off-chain documentation.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Evasion:** Advisers may have incentives to characterize custody as trading authority, related-person custody, or a temporary airdrop or network event to avoid a more demanding custody pathway.

**Threshold Gaming:** The proposed tests for custodian availability, safeguarding expertise, reasonable care, significant deviation from value, and asset classification may invite favorable internal characterizations if not accompanied by objective factors.

**Strategic Compliance:** Entities may select jurisdictions, networks, control arrangements, or disclosure formats that minimize review burden rather than maximize client protection.

**Mitigations:** Require documented reasons, comparable reporting fields, independent review, cross-referencing of related-person arrangements, and adverse-event escalation.
