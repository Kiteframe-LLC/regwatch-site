## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The removed items no longer provide a critical military or intelligence advantage.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The agency's category-level and characteristic-level judgments capture current technology, foreign availability, military utility, and national-security consequences across affected variants.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A model or class of item has military utility that is not visible from general market availability, or a broad narrowing of a catch-all control reaches a sensitive variant that the preamble does not separately identify.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The Department should identify the criteria, evidence, and edge cases used for each principal removal and explain how commenters can distinguish controlled from non-controlled variants.
- **Claim:** The revisions improve transparency and clarity.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The new definitions, cross-references, thresholds, and category subparagraphs will be easier to apply than the existing text without creating new classification uncertainty.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Practitioners cannot determine how mixed hardware/software systems, custom builds, continuous integration, post-production modifications, or the new § 120.35 exclusions interact with § 120.41 and the EAR.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The Department should use concrete examples and respond to the expressly solicited lifecycle, software, and scope questions.

  **Failure Scenario:** A repair, upgrade, replacement, or reconditioning transaction falls near the exemption boundary, or an authorized provider's role is unclear, producing inconsistent licensing decisions or an unintended control gap.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final rule should define documentation, return, recordkeeping, and mixed-purpose safeguards or explain why they are unnecessary.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Boundary uncertainty in mixed ITAR/EAR systems
  
  **Mechanism:** A component or software package may appear to qualify for a proposed exclusion while related technical data, see-through rules, or a different system context preserves ITAR treatment.
  
  **Consequence:** Inconsistent classifications, delayed transactions, or conservative over-compliance.
  
  **Mitigation Comment:** Request worked examples and a transition safe harbor for good-faith classifications.
- **Failure Mode:** Repair exemption scope creep
  
  **Mechanism:** Upgrading, reconditioning, or testing may change function or performance or may be combined with a non-exempt purpose.
  
  **Consequence:** An unintended licensing gap or inconsistent treatment of similar servicing transactions.
  
  **Mitigation Comment:** Ask for documentation, return, mixed-purpose, and authorized-provider conditions.
- **Failure Mode:** Underinclusive or overinclusive category thresholds
  
  **Mechanism:** Optical-density, foreign-availability, catch-all, or submersible-equipment criteria may not track real-world variants.
  
  **Consequence:** Sensitive items may be excluded or ordinary items may remain subject to avoidable controls.
  
  **Mitigation Comment:** Provide representative specifications and request threshold sensitivity analysis.
- **Failure Mode:** Transition confusion for previously licensed items
  
  **Mechanism:** Classification changes are paired with guidance said to be available on DDTC's website rather than fully described in the rule.
  
  **Consequence:** Contract, license, agreement, and technical-data obligations may be applied inconsistently during transition.
  
  **Mitigation Comment:** Request a binding transition crosswalk, effective-date examples, and treatment of existing authorizations.
