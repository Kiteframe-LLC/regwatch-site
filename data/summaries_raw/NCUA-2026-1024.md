## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Redemption timing can be operationalized in two business days.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Liquidity management, settlement, and reserve liquidation can reliably support that deadline.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A stressed or less liquid reserve mix cannot meet the deadline without fire sales or delayed customer access.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The NCUA should explain why two business days is the right anchor and whether the deadline should vary with reserve liquidity.
- **Claim:** Reserve diversification and concentration thresholds will improve resilience.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The chosen concentration rules actually reduce run and credit risk without forcing impractical reserve structures.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A threshold is too tight for smaller or niche issuers, or too loose to matter in stress.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for threshold calibration data, safe harbors, and sensitivity analysis.
- **Claim:** White-label or multi-brand stablecoins can be allowed or limited in a way that preserves clarity.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Branding and issuer identity remain clear enough for customers, custodians, and supervisors to trace liabilities.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Multiple brands under one issuer blur reserve identity and increase confusion or run risk.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The record should explain whether one-brand limits or stronger branding disclosures are needed.
- **Claim:** Tokenized share recordkeeping can be made compatible with share-insurance and supervision rules.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Blockchain records, account titles, and legal ownership can be reconciled cleanly at failure time.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A tokenized share product makes it hard to identify owners or aggregate accounts for insurance purposes.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The NCUA should map the recordkeeping path from normal operations to a FICU failure scenario.
- **Claim:** Monthly and quarterly reporting will let the NCUA monitor PPSIs effectively.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The reports will be accurate, auditable, and useful without becoming unmanageable or stale.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The reports become rote compliance documents that do not reveal actual operational risk or fraud.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The NCUA should specify validation, update, and exception-handling rules for the reports.
- **Claim:** The rule will not materially harm small credit unions or credit creation.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Current CUSO and market patterns are a good proxy for how smaller institutions will actually participate.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The rule makes entry or investment meaningfully harder for smaller credit unions even if existing data look benign.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The NCUA should quantify the effect on smaller institutions and on credit availability.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The proposal would create part 706 to implement the GENIUS Act for FICU-affiliated payment stablecoin issuers, including application procedures, reserve and redemption standards, custody rules, reporting, share-insurance clarifications, and tokenized-share amendments.

**Affected Entities**
- FICU subsidiaries that seek to issue payment stablecoins
- federally insured credit unions that may invest in or control those subsidiaries
- custodians and sub-custodians handling reserves, collateral, or private keys
- customers and holders who rely on redemption, recordkeeping, and share-insurance treatment
- smaller credit unions that may face the same framework with fewer compliance resources

**Statutory Authority**
- GENIUS Act sections 4, 5, 6, 10, and 12
- Federal Credit Union Act sections 120, 204, 206, and 209
- NCUA share-insurance authority for member accounts and related regulatory definitions

**Observed Facts**

- The export metadata reports no supporting related materials or ingested supporting materials.

**Inference**
- This is a front-end institutional-design rule: it sets the licensing and operating architecture before the full substantive standards are finalized.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Alternatives that matter but are not fully compared**
- A tiered filing model based on control or ownership percentage instead of a more unitary application process.
- A staged or conditional licensing path that lets early entrants operate while the later standards rule is finalized.
- A liquidity-based redemption timetable instead of a single fixed deadline for all reserve mixes.
- More concrete safe harbors for tokenized-share recordkeeping and account aggregation.
- A narrower or more explicit one-brand / white-label rule with anti-confusion controls.

**Why this matters**
- The agency asks about many of these ideas, but the notice does not yet compare them with enough operational detail for a public record that needs to support a final rule.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Likely failure modes**
- Redemption delays if the two-business-day standard does not match the liquidity of the reserve mix.
- Confusion and run risk if white-label or multi-brand arrangements blur the identity of the issuer or the reserve pool.
- Insurance and ownership errors if tokenized-share records do not map cleanly to account records at failure time.
- Oversight gaps if reporting and examination rules are too generic for blockchain-based operations.
- False comfort if monthly or quarterly reports do not actually reveal the conditions that matter in stress.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Regulatory incentives**
- Issuers will likely prefer flexibility in reserve assets, branding, and reporting frictions.
- Smaller credit unions will likely prefer clear thresholds, low-friction compliance, and explicit treatment of tokenized-share products.
- Regulators have an incentive to standardize across agencies, but that incentive can obscure local operational differences in credit-union systems.
- The rule should be tested against those incentives, not just against legal symmetry.
