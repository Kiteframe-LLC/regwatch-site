## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim: the package achieves at least 15% RFP

- **Underlying assumption:** Wisconsin's 2017-to-2023 reductions are properly attributed, permanent, enforceable, and sufficiently explained for each approved area.
- **Failure scenario:** Sheboygan's numerical showing is visible in the support, but the combined three-area action still leaves commenters with uneven access to Milwaukee- and Kenosha-specific derivations in the supplied packet.
- **Procedural implication:** EPA may need to provide a clearer area-by-area derivation table so the public can test the triplet approval on equal footing.

### Claim: the budgets are clearly identified and precisely quantified

- **Underlying assumption:** Precision of the final budget numbers is matched by sufficient transparency about assumptions, especially the larger `15%` VMT cushion for Sheboygan.
- **Failure scenario:** A budget can be numerically precise while still leaving commenters unable to evaluate whether the chosen VMT margin is conservative, justified, or outcome-sensitive.
- **Procedural implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to explain the practical basis for the Sheboygan cushion and whether alternative sensitivity cases were tested.

### Claim: segmentation of the package is acceptable

- **Underlying assumption:** EPA can approve inventory, RFP, budgets, I/M, and NNSR now without materially obscuring later decisions on attainment, RACM, and contingency measures.
- **Failure scenario:** The Sheboygan supporting plan openly shows a simple contingency table ending in `No No`, while EPA says contingency measures will be handled later, creating a natural public question about how the deferred action interacts with the present approvals.
- **Procedural implication:** EPA should explain the relationship between the current approvals and the later action more directly.

### Claim: existing approved NNSR rules satisfy Moderate-area needs

- **Underlying assumption:** Wisconsin's existing SIP-approved NNSR structure maps cleanly onto the revised Moderate-area geography and current ozone requirements.
- **Failure scenario:** The legal sufficiency may be real, but the public explanation is compressed and does not give a clear crosswalk from the older approvals to the current three-area setting.
- **Procedural implication:** EPA should make the legacy-to-current mapping more legible in the record.

### Claim: local planning elements are meaningful in a transport-driven ozone setting

- **Underlying assumption:** Even though shoreline ozone is heavily shaped by Lake Michigan transport and lake-breeze meteorology, the approved local planning tools still form a rational and legally sufficient part of the response.
- **Failure scenario:** If transport and mesoscale weather dominate exceedances, commenters may question whether EPA has sufficiently explained both the limits and the value of local measures.
- **Procedural implication:** EPA could strengthen the record by linking the transport discussion more directly to the chosen local planning elements and their expected contribution.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

- EPA proposes to approve portions of Wisconsin's April 2, 2025 Moderate-area SIP submission for three Wisconsin ozone areas: Kenosha, Milwaukee, and Sheboygan.
- The proposed approvals cover the `2017` base-year emissions inventory, the `15%` RFP demonstration, the `2023` motor-vehicle emissions budgets, the inspection and maintenance certification, and the NNSR certification.
- EPA is also initiating the transportation-conformity adequacy process for the `2023` budgets.
- The NPRM explicitly says the attainment demonstration, RACM demonstration, and contingency measures will be handled in a separate action.
- The Sheboygan supporting plan says NOx emissions fall from `10.05` to `6.94` tons per ozone-season day between `2017` and `2023`, while VOC emissions fall from `6.55` to `5.68`.
- The same Sheboygan plan's contingency table shows only `0.08` combined NOx-plus-VOC reductions from `2023` to `2024`, against a `0.20` contingency target, and marks the simple comparison as `No No`.
- The Sheboygan plan emphasizes that Lake Michigan transport and lake-breeze circulation are major drivers of persistent shoreline ozone.

### Inference

- This is a partial but consequential SIP approval. Even though EPA is postponing some of the most outcome-oriented pieces, the present notice would still lock in the planning foundation and conformity budgets that shape later transportation and permitting decisions.
- The best comment leverage is not a broad challenge to EPA's authority. It is a targeted request for fuller explanation of the split action, the Sheboygan budget assumptions, the treatment of contingency issues in a later action, and the relationship between transport-heavy ozone dynamics and the local planning elements being approved now.
