## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Using general Federal income-tax principles will produce a clear sale-versus-license boundary.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Existing classification principles are sufficiently determinate for section 250 transactions with mixed rights, software, cloud delivery, and contingent consideration.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Economically similar arrangements may receive different FDDEI treatment because their legal form or rights package is characterized differently under fact-intensive principles.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request additional examples and explain recurring transaction forms that need a predictable classification result.
- **Claim:** The seller-side character test appropriately separates inventory from excluded business property.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Historical use and tax character in the seller's hands can be documented consistently when assets are mixed-use, refurbished, remanufactured, or transferred within a group.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A previously depreciated asset later held for sale may remain excluded even when substantial new value is created, while similar newly produced inventory remains eligible.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can provide transaction records and propose a remanufacturing or depreciation-recapture rule with anti-abuse safeguards.
- **Claim:** The proposal's economic effects are small.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The materially affected population is a narrow subset of FDII/FDDEI claimants and the proposed clarifications mostly reduce uncertainty rather than change tax outcomes.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The unavailable transaction-level data conceal concentrated effects in information, manufacturing, transportation-equipment, or other asset-intensive industries.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can submit affected-transaction counts, tax-base estimates, compliance hours, and examples of changed treatment.
- **Claim:** The statutory effective date and interim reliance approach are administrable.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Taxpayers can reconstruct and consistently apply the proposed rules to dispositions after June 16, 2025 before final regulations are published.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Taxpayers may have already filed, priced, or structured transactions without knowing which proposed interpretations Treasury and the IRS would adopt.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for transition relief, prospective application, or explicit treatment of reliance and amended-return situations.
- **Claim:** The related-party anti-abuse rule protects the inventory clarification without overreaching.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A principal-purpose standard and modified affiliated-group definition will identify avoidance transactions without creating uncertainty for ordinary internal reorganizations.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Routine basis-carryover transfers may be swept into the rule because purpose and property character are difficult to document years after the transaction.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request examples, a clearer purpose standard, and a safe harbor for ordinary-course reorganizations.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** Implement section 250(b)(3)(A)(i)(VII) by defining excluded property sales income, distinguishing sales or other dispositions from leases and licenses, excluding certain seller-side depreciable, amortizable, or depletable property and intangible property from DEI, preserving ordinary-course inventory treatment, and adding a related-party anti-abuse rule.

**Affected Entities**
- Domestic corporations claiming or potentially claiming the section 250 FDDEI deduction
- Software, information, digital-content, manufacturing, transportation-equipment, industrial, pharmaceutical, and natural-resource businesses with foreign sales or dispositions
- Tax departments, advisers, and recordkeepers evaluating property character, sale-versus-license status, related-party transfers, and section 250 eligibility

**Statutory Authority:** Section 250(b)(3)(A)(i)(VII) authorizes the exclusion and permits exceptions as provided by the Secretary; section 250(c) and section 7805(a) supply additional authority to carry out section 250.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposed rules would apply the exclusion to dispositions occurring after June 16, 2025, allow pre-final reliance only if the proposed rules are followed entirely and consistently, and clarify that FDDEI remains a subset of DEI.

**Observed Facts**
- The statute excludes income and gain from sales or other dispositions of specified intangible and depreciable, amortizable, or depletable property.
- The proposal treats ordinary-course inventory as outside the other-excluded-property definition when it has always been held as inventory.
- The economic analysis reports 5,500 FDII claimants in 2021, $912 billion in aggregate gross FDDEI, and an estimated 7,000 to 7,500 FDDEI claimants in 2026, but says transaction-level effects cannot be precisely estimated.

**Inference:** This is principally an interpretive and boundary-setting rule implementing a recent statutory exclusion, but its proposed retroactive applicability and reliance conditions could affect already completed or reported transactions.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Hybrid transaction misclassification
  
  **Mechanism:** A software, cloud, or intellectual-property arrangement may be characterized differently under general tax principles and section 250 practice.
  
  **Consequence:** Similar foreign-market revenue may receive different FDDEI treatment and increase disputes.
- **Failure Mode:** Historical-character documentation failure
  
  **Mechanism:** Taxpayers may lack records showing whether property was previously depreciated, remanufactured, or held as inventory.
  
  **Consequence:** Compliance costs and audit disputes may be concentrated in asset-intensive businesses.
- **Failure Mode:** Related-party anti-abuse overbreadth
  
  **Mechanism:** A principal-purpose inquiry may reach ordinary basis-carryover reorganizations with mixed business reasons.
  
  **Consequence:** Taxpayers may avoid efficient internal transfers or face uncertain treatment.
- **Failure Mode:** Transition mismatch
  
  **Mechanism:** The proposal applies after June 16, 2025 while finalization is expected later and interim reliance requires complete consistency.
  
  **Consequence:** Earlier returns and transactions may need reconstruction or changed treatment.
