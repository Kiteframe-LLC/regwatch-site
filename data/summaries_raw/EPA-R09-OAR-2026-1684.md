## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim: Rule 205 satisfies offset integrity requirements

* **Underlying assumption:**
  The application review, permit revision, certification, and post-certification controls collectively prevent non-surplus or non-permanent credits from being used.
* **Failure scenario:**
  If fleet baselines, historic vehicle miles traveled, or replacement assumptions are overstated, credits could be certified for reductions already embedded in ordinary fleet turnover.
* **Procedural implication:**
  Commenters have a strong basis to ask for clearer documentation of baseline-setting and verification practice.

### Claim: MOVES + MAG coordination prevents double counting

* **Underlying assumption:**
  Interagency coordination and inventory adjustments will happen consistently across future conformity cycles and future MERC projects.
* **Failure scenario:**
  If notice timing, data sharing, or inventory adjustments lag, a mobile-source reduction could be credited both in planning inventories and as an offset.
* **Procedural implication:**
  The program's legality may depend as much on administrative execution as on the text of Rule 205 itself.

### Claim: Monitoring and permit conditions make credits enforceable

* **Underlying assumption:**
  Permit conditions under Rules 210 or 220 will be specific enough to verify long-term implementation and to revise or reduce credits when necessary.
* **Failure scenario:**
  If permit conditions are uneven across projects or if follow-up review is weak, credit integrity could deteriorate after issuance.
* **Procedural implication:**
  The record would benefit from clearer examples of how permit revisions and certificate adjustments are expected to operate.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

* EPA proposes to approve Maricopa County Rule 205 into the Arizona SIP.
* Rule 205 would allow fleet owners or operators to generate mobile emission reduction credits by replacing or retrofitting on-road vehicles and then transfer those credits to stationary sources that need NNSR offsets.
* EPA says the relevant legal standards are CAA sections 110(a)(2), 110(l), 173, and 193, plus 40 CFR 51.165.
* The record reflects concern about whether credits remain real, surplus, quantifiable, permanent, and federally enforceable, and the TSD describes program features intended to address those issues.

### Inference

* The proposal is not just a ministerial SIP filing. It is an attempt to adapt a market-style crediting structure to mobile-source reductions in a nonattainment permitting program.
* Because the proposal depends on future modeling inputs, permit conditions, and county-regional coordination, practical implementation details matter almost as much as the rule text itself.

### Evidence Quotes

* "These ERCs are intended for use as offsets for major stationary sources under the Department's Nonattainment New Source Review (NNSR) program."
* "Rule 205 creates a regulatory structure for the generation and use of nontraditional ERCs from on-road mobile sources."
* "The MCAQD and MAG will work together to ensure the emissions reductions used as MERCs are not 'double counted' in the SIP modeling and transportation conformity analysis."
