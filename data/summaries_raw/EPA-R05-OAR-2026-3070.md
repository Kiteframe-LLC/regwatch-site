## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

**Attainment conclusion.** If the excluded wildfire-affected days should not have been removed from the design-value calculation, the proposal's attainment conclusion may not hold. The record therefore needs a clear public-facing before-and-after comparison of monitor values and design values.

**Exceptional-events concurrence.** If the causal showing is weaker than the notice implies, the proposal still may have a technical basis in the docket, but commenters would need a more transparent explanation of why alternative meteorological or local-emissions explanations are insufficient.

**Weight-of-evidence method.** If the GAM, met-adjusted ozone, EMBER modeling, and AQS event reports point in different directions on any event day, the public should be able to see how EPA resolved that conflict rather than only the bottom-line concurrence.

**Procedural review posture.** Because the concurrence letter states that judicial review occurs through the later regulatory action, any missing explanation in the NPRM matters more than usual: the proposal is not just a routine notice, it is the public's live review vehicle for the exclusion-backed attainment outcome.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**What It Does:** Proposes to determine that the Detroit moderate ozone nonattainment area attained the 2015 ozone NAAQS by the August 3, 2024 attainment date, while also proposing final agency action on Michigan's exceptional-events request to exclude specified 2023 wildfire-influenced ozone data from regulatory use.

**Why It Matters:** If finalized, the action affects whether Detroit avoids the legal and planning consequences that can follow from failing to attain by the applicable deadline.

**Affected Entities**
- residents and local governments in the Detroit area
- Michigan environmental regulators
- EPA Region 5 and national ozone-program staff
- regulated sources whose planning obligations can be shaped by nonattainment status
- public commenters focused on exceptional-events methodology, ozone attainment, and transparency of record support

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

**Observed Fact**

The proposal rests on familiar ozone-attainment and exceptional-events authorities rather than on an aggressive new assertion of EPA power. The notice quotes the Clean Air Act attainment-determination duty and the Exceptional Events Rule framework.

**Inference**

The main legal vulnerability is therefore reasoned explanation and record transparency, not lack of statutory authority. If commenters challenge this proposal successfully, the argument is more likely to be that the notice does not sufficiently expose why the excluded wildfire days lawfully and persuasively support the attainment conclusion.

## Step 8 — Procedural Normalcy

This looks procedurally normal for an NPRM that follows an earlier concurrence package. EPA identifies the comment deadline, the docket, and the supporting materials. The unusual feature is that the concurrence letter says final agency action and judicial review occur through the later regulatory action. That increases the importance of making the technical basis legible during this proposal stage rather than leaving the core arithmetic mostly buried in attachments.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Failure mode 1: Thin public crosswalk.** Commenters may struggle to test the attainment conclusion because the notice lacks a simple included-versus-excluded comparison table.

**Failure mode 2: Outcome sensitivity near the threshold.** Several reported design values are close to 0.070 ppm, so small disagreements about event treatment can matter materially.

**Failure mode 3: Understated consequences.** The statutory review discussion may lead casual readers to treat the action as low-impact even though it affects whether the area avoids failure-to-attain consequences tied to ozone status.

**Failure mode 4: Procedural bottleneck.** Because the concurrence itself is not the final agency action, any ambiguity left unresolved here becomes harder for the public to challenge later.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

The proposal creates an incentive for commenters to focus on the reliability and transparency of exceptional-events exclusions rather than on broad anti-EPA themes. EPA, Michigan, and regulated actors all benefit from a clean explanation of how wildfire-affected data should be handled when attainment status sits near the threshold. A final rule that relies heavily on technical attachments without a simple public crosswalk could weaken confidence in future exceptional-events use even when the underlying science is sound.

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

**Strong evidence:** The docket contains the actual concurrence TSD, Michigan's demonstration, cover letters, and AQS reports rather than bare assertions.

**Moderate evidence:** The notice's attainment table provides the post-exclusion results and the TSD describes modeling and causal analysis.

**Weaker evidence presentation:** The main preamble does not itself display the included-versus-excluded arithmetic or a compact day-by-day summary of which pieces of evidence were decisive for each excluded date.

## Step 12 — Administrative Record Gaps

**Gaps**
- A simple public table comparing included and excluded design values at each monitor.
- A concise explanation of whether the proposal's attainment conclusion would change if one excluded date were restored.
- A tighter cross-reference from each excluded monitor-day to the specific model outputs and AQS reports EPA found most persuasive.
- A more candid explanation in the NPRM of the practical consequences of the attainment finding for Detroit's regulatory posture.

## Step 13 — Litigation Vulnerabilities

Potential vulnerabilities are modest but real. A challenger could argue that the notice relies on support in the docket without clearly presenting the decisive quantitative steps in the proposal itself, especially where reported design values sit close to the attainment threshold. EPA has stronger defenses on statutory authority and existence of a real technical record. The more durable defense would be a final rule that clearly shows the before-and-after arithmetic, the causal basis for each excluded day, and the practical consequences of the exclusion decision.

## Step 13A — Structural Integrity / Nonsense Detector

The proposal is structurally coherent. Its weakness is compression, not contradiction: a consequential attainment outcome is made to depend on a technically rich exceptional-events record that the notice only summarizes at a high level. The most constructive comment posture is to ask EPA to expose the decisive calculations and event-day evidence more directly in the final rule.
