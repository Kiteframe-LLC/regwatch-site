## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Rule Summary:** FDA proposes to reclassify prescription digital breast tomosynthesis systems used for breast-cancer screening and diagnosis from class III/PMA regulation to class II/special-controls regulation, requiring 510(k) clearance and a new device classification regulation.

**Affected Entities**
- DBT device manufacturers and sponsors
- FDA reviewers
- mammography facilities and radiologists
- patients receiving breast imaging
- third-party testing and quality-control providers

**Main Tradeoff:** The proposal would lower the marketing and modification burden for manufacturers while relying on standardized performance evidence and labeling to manage risks that previously received PMA-level review.

**Decision Point:** Whether the record demonstrates that the special controls are sufficiently concrete, validated, and adaptable to provide reasonable assurance for the full range of DBT systems.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Observed Facts**
- The proposed controls cover image quality, diagnostic accuracy, software, radiation, EMC, biocompatibility, infection, training, and labeling.
- The notice permits objective task-based assessment using human subjects, physical phantoms, in silico methods, or combinations as appropriate.

**Inference:** The framework is technically coherent at a high level, but its flexibility may produce inconsistent evidence unless FDA supplies common endpoints, validation expectations, and decision thresholds.

**Unknowns**
- How diagnostic accuracy endpoints should be selected across screening and diagnostic uses.
- How in silico evidence will be validated and compared with clinical evidence.
- How software changes and adaptive reconstruction features will be handled over time.

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

**Observed Fact:** FDA cites sections 513(f)(3), 513(a)(1)(B), 513(a)(3), 520(h)(4), 510(k), and 21 CFR part 860.

**Assessment:** The proposed legal pathway is facially grounded in the cited device-classification authorities. The principal legal-record question is reasoned explanation: whether the evidence and controls sufficiently connect the statutory standard to the broad reclassification.

**Comment Leverage:** Request a clear explanation of how each proposed special control meets the statutory reasonable-assurance standard and how FDA considered limitations in the underlying evidence.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Administrative Failure Modes**
- Under-specification of performance endpoints may shift important policy choices to case-by-case review.
- A broad class II pathway may create inconsistent evidence requirements across sponsors if guidance is not clear.

**Safety Failure Modes**
- False positives, false negatives, incomplete coverage, excessive radiation, and software or hardware malfunction could harm patients if testing does not represent actual clinical use.

**Severity:** Material but remediable through clearer minimum criteria, transition rules, and monitoring.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Positive Incentives**
- Lower PMA burden may encourage innovation, upgrades, and wider availability of DBT systems.
- Performance-based controls may allow technology-neutral evaluation.

**Negative Incentives**
- Sponsors may favor test strategies that satisfy flexible review expectations without demonstrating performance across difficult cases or diverse populations.
- Reduced pathway burden may reduce incentives to generate long-term comparative evidence unless required by controls or postmarket mechanisms.

**Comment Angle:** Ask FDA how the framework preserves incentives for robust evidence while avoiding unnecessary duplication.

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

**Strengths**
- The notice draws on multiple evidence types and prior approved devices.
- The supporting packet includes the reference list and FDA PMA summaries for early DBT systems.
- The proposal identifies specific clinical and technical risks rather than relying only on a general safety assertion.

**Limitations**
- The central evidence is summarized rather than presented in a unified exposure-adjusted analysis.
- The notice does not quantify postmarket reporting rates, recall search sensitivity, or subgroup performance.
- The optional reference set is broader than the four records ingested in the packet.

**Overall:** Moderate to strong for the proposition that DBT is an established technology; less complete for the proposition that the proposed flexible controls will be adequate for all future systems.

## Step 12 — Administrative Record Gaps

- Quantitative baseline and projected effects of moving from PMA to 510(k) are not presented.
- The record does not show a comparison of alternative classification scopes or staged implementation.
- The record does not specify minimum diagnostic-performance endpoints or acceptance thresholds for the proposed task-based assessments.
- The record does not explain a monitoring and feedback plan for emerging software, artificial-intelligence, or reconstruction risks.
- The record does not provide detailed subgroup analysis for breast density, age, race, or other clinically relevant populations.

## Step 13 — Litigation Vulnerabilities

**Potential Issues**
- A challenger could argue that FDA did not adequately explain why the broad reclassification, rather than a narrower or staged option, satisfies the reasonable-assurance standard.
- A challenger could argue that flexible performance-based controls are insufficiently definite if the final rule does not provide clear minimum expectations.
- A challenger could question whether the economic and transition consequences were adequately considered.

**Strength Of Concerns:** These are proposal-stage record-building concerns, not conclusions that the rule is unlawful.

**Requested Record Building:** FDA should explain the evidence-to-control mapping, compare alternatives, and specify how it will address transition and evolving software risks.

## Step 13A — Structural Integrity / Nonsense Detector

**Assessment:** Coherent proposal with material evidence and implementation gaps; no structural nonsense identified.

**Observed Fact:** The notice presents a recognizable chain from statutory authority to risks, controls, and a proposed classification outcome.

**Inference:** The main weakness is underdeveloped specification and monitoring, not a contradiction in the proposal's basic architecture.
