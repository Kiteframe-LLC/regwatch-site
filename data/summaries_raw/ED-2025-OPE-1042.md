## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** Revise federal recognition criteria and review procedures for accrediting agencies, reduce prescriptive process requirements, add outcome, legal-compliance, transfer-credit, transparency, conflict-of-interest, and institutional-flexibility provisions, and amend related institutional disclosure rules.

**Affected Entities**
- students and borrowers
- institutions participating in title IV programs
- institutional and programmatic accrediting agencies
- new or expanding accrediting organizations
- faculty and administrators
- state and local public institutions
- small accrediting agencies
- the Department and NACIQI

**Statutory Authority:** 20 U.S.C. 1099b and related Higher Education Act authorities cited in the proposed amendments; the notice also relies on Executive Order 14279 as a policy directive.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal shifts part 602 toward outcome-focused and less prescriptive recognition criteria while giving accreditors more flexibility and requiring neutrality, legal compliance, burden minimization, and transfer-credit disclosures.

**Observed Facts**
- The proposal covers 34 CFR parts 600, 602, and 668.
- The RIA reports approximately $417.9 million in annualized costs at a 7 percent discount rate over a perpetual time horizon.
- The notice estimates a one-time 470-hour burden for institutional accreditors and substantial student transcript-disclosure burden.

**Inference:** The central tradeoff is reduced prescriptive burden and more accreditation competition in exchange for greater reliance on agency discretion, outcome metrics, and Department oversight.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The proposed regulations would eliminate requirements not required by statute ... and reduce regulatory burden.
- The Department does not anticipate a significant budget impact of the proposed regulations.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- Outcome metrics may penalize institutions serving students or programs for whom completion, licensure, or earnings measures are not directly comparable.
- New accrediting agencies may enter before their oversight capacity and data systems are sufficiently tested.
- Institutions may receive inconsistent demands from multiple accreditors despite burden-minimization language.
- Transfer-credit disclosures may be difficult to provide before enrollment when transcript timing and student location are uncertain.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

- Accreditors may favor easily measured outcomes over harder-to-measure educational quality.
- Institutions may strategically select or change accreditors if recognition competition produces materially different compliance expectations.
- Broad flexibility may encourage minimum-compliance interpretations unless Department review and public reporting are sufficiently specific.
