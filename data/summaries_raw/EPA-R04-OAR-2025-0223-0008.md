## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_maintenance_emissions
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Projected source activity, vehicle mix, model inputs, and the 2032 proxy used for 2035 remain sufficiently accurate to keep total emissions below the 2018 attainment inventory.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Growth, vehicle electrification, industrial activity, or model-policy changes differ from the planning assumptions, especially in a sector whose VOC estimate increases between 2018 and 2035.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final record should identify sensitivity tests, update triggers, and how the state would revisit the budgets if assumptions materially change.
- **Claim Id:** claim_mov es_reasonableness
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Using MOVES4 remains a reasonable planning choice because significant SIP work occurred before the February 2026 rescission rule and EPA guidance allowed earlier model versions in that circumstance.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The later policy change materially changes the vehicle or emissions assumptions that drive future NOx reductions, or a commenter identifies a local input that differs from the model defaults.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** EPA should explain the effect of the later policy change and identify whether a sensitivity run with updated assumptions would alter the 2035 budgets.
- **Claim Id:** claim_monitoring_continuity
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The five ozone monitors will remain operational and representative through 2035 and any future network changes will not reduce the ability to detect a violation.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Funding, site changes, or data-completeness problems interrupt a monitor or reduce spatial coverage before a three-year design value can be calculated.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The plan should make the fallback monitoring, data-completeness, and public-notification path explicit.
- **Claim Id:** claim_budget_adequacy
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Allocating 50 percent of the NOx safety margin and 100 percent of the VOC safety margin to 2035 budgets leaves enough protection for unanticipated growth and modeling uncertainty.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A smaller-than-expected remaining margin, changed travel demand, or a sector-specific increase makes the aggregate budget ceiling less protective than the statewide inventory comparison suggests.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The record should show the five-step allocation calculation, sensitivity to alternative allocations, and why the zero remaining VOC margin is acceptable.
- **Claim Id:** claim_contingency_sufficiency
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The tertiary, secondary, and primary triggers and the 24-month implementation commitment will produce timely corrective action.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Interstate consultation, legislative review, funding, or selection among several possible NOx measures delays the measure after a trigger, or a monitoring anomaly is detected too late.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** EPA should clarify which actions are mandatory, how trigger determinations are documented, and how implementation timing is enforced if legislative review is required.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** Approve and incorporate North Carolina's second 10-year maintenance plan for the 2008 ozone NAAQS into the North Carolina SIP through August 27, 2035; replace the prior 2014 and 2026 transportation-conformity budgets with proposed 2018 and 2035 NOx and VOC sub-area budgets; and notify the public of the separate adequacy-review status.

**Affected Entities**
- North Carolina, South Carolina, and local air agencies responsible for monitoring and SIP implementation.
- Metropolitan planning organizations and transportation partners subject to future conformity demonstrations.
- Sources and residents in the Charlotte-Rock Hill area affected by continued ozone maintenance and monitoring.

**Statutory Authority:** Clean Air Act sections 110, 175A, and 176(c), with transportation-conformity criteria in 40 CFR part 93 and ambient-monitoring requirements in 40 CFR part 58.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposed SIP approval would establish 2018 and 2035 NOx/VOC sub-area conformity budgets and retire the prior 2014 and 2026 budgets once the new budgets are approved or found adequate, whichever occurs first.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice reports 2018 total emissions of 92.76 tons/summer day of NOx and 104.96 tons/summer day of VOC, compared with 2035 projections of 42.71 and 100.92.
- It reports five active ozone monitors below the 2008 standard for 2023-2025 and three contingency trigger levels.

**Inference:** The proposal is routine and legally bounded, but depends on future-year modeling, safety-margin allocation, and durable monitoring and contingency commitments.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The EPA is proposing to approve the second 10-year Charlotte Area maintenance plan.
- The State has decided to allocate a portion of the available safety margin to the 2035 budgets.
