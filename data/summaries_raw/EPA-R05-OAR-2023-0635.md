## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim A

**Claim:** The area is attaining because monitored values stayed below the standard and the modeled maximum is `62.8 ppb`.

**Underlying assumption:** The post-2022 control configuration at Belle River and the St. Clair shutdown are stable enough that the 2020-2022 monitoring period and the modeling inputs remain representative.

**Failure scenario:** If Belle River's operating profile changes materially, if its fuel-conversion timeline slips, or if other significant SO2 sources return, the maintenance logic becomes less durable than the notice suggests.

**Procedural implication:** A constructive comment can ask EPA to explain how continued representativeness will be checked now that the two DTE near-source monitors are gone.

### Claim B

**Claim:** The Mills monitor provides a conservative background concentration.

**Underlying assumption:** The straight-line alignment and plume-overlap logic actually makes Mills a worst-case proxy for remaining background in the modeled domain.

**Failure scenario:** If the background field changes after St. Clair's closure or after Belle River fuel changes, the historic monitor-derived background may become less informative than EPA implies.

**Procedural implication:** A commenter could request a short explanation of how EPA will know when background assumptions should be refreshed.

### Claim C

**Claim:** Port Huron exceedances do not undermine redesignation because the air mass moved away from the nonattainment area.

**Underlying assumption:** The trajectory analysis and the invalid-wind-direction correction for the November 2024 event are enough to separate Port Huron from the St. Clair nonattainment area's attainment status.

**Failure scenario:** If outside readers cannot follow the trajectory logic or the instrument-error correction, Port Huron can still look like unresolved contradictory evidence.

**Procedural implication:** EPA would strengthen the record by summarizing the Port Huron reasoning more clearly in the final rule rather than leaving most of that work in the TSD.

### Claim D

**Claim:** The maintenance plan is adequate because future emissions will stay low or drop to zero.

**Underlying assumption:** Belle River's natural-gas conversion proceeds roughly as projected and no large SO2 source resumes operation in the area.

**Failure scenario:** If the conversion is delayed or a source resumes, the projected `2033` zero-emissions table becomes more aspirational than demonstrative.

**Procedural implication:** EPA could improve the record by clarifying what specific event would trigger a refreshed modeling review before the eight-year SIP revision.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

* EPA proposes three related actions: redesignating the St. Clair area to attainment for the `2010 1-hour SO2 NAAQS`, approving Michigan's maintenance plan, and incorporating Belle River `Permit to Install 51-22` into the SIP.
* The attainment case relies on two pillars: monitored attainment at the DTE Mills and Remer monitors through `2022`, and dispersion modeling showing a maximum concentration of `62.8 ppb`, below the `75 ppb` standard.
* The key permanent reductions are the `2022` shutdown of the St. Clair Power Plant and Belle River's revised `0.680 lbs/MMBtu` 30-day and `1.2 lbs/MMBtu` 3-hour SO2 limits.
* EPA's TSD directly addresses Port Huron exceedances outside the nonattainment area and concludes the relevant air masses moved away from the St. Clair nonattainment area.
* Michigan's maintenance plan projects `0` tons per year from both major plants by `2033`, with St. Clair already closed and Belle River expected to convert from coal to natural gas around `2027`.

### Inference

This is a technical redesignation rule with a plausible and fairly well-documented attainment theory. The main comment opportunities are not about overturning the basic attainment case. They are about making the maintenance story more durable and more transparent after the on-site DTE monitoring ended, and about pressing EPA and Michigan to define more concrete verification and contingency expectations if circumstances change.

### Evidence Quotes

* “Michigan's modeling analysis demonstrates... a maximum SO2 concentration of 62.8 ppb, which is below the 2010 SO2 NAAQS of 75 ppb.”
* “The trajectories demonstrate that the air mass containing the higher SO2 concentrations did not disperse into the St. Clair nonattainment area.”
* “Because there are no large sources of SO2 projected to be constructed or resume operations in the NAA, projected emissions to 2033 for the Belle River facility will be zero...”
* “To track the progress of the maintenance plan, EGLE commits to periodically reevaluate the modeling assumptions and input data...”

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Alternatives evaluated:** The record evaluates monitored attainment, modeled attainment, and EPA's explanation for why Port Huron exceedances do not count against the nonattainment area. It does not meaningfully compare alternative maintenance-plan architectures.

**Plausible alternatives not analyzed:** EPA and Michigan do not appear to analyze whether a modest continuing ambient-monitoring commitment, a scheduled re-modeling checkpoint before the eight-year revision, or a more specific contingency trigger would materially strengthen the maintenance plan.

**Evidence suggesting feasibility:** The state already commits to periodically reevaluate modeling assumptions and to provide annual Belle River emissions summaries. That suggests a more concrete verification protocol would have been feasible without changing the redesignation outcome.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

The technical story is coherent overall.

**Observed Fact:** Table `2-1` in the state package shows both DTE monitors below the standard for `2020-2022`, with design values of `42 ppb` at Mills and `35 ppb` at Remer.

**Observed Fact:** The modeling analysis uses EPA's standard regulatory tools, a five-year meteorological data set, a dense receptor grid, and a conservative variable background derived from the Mills monitor.

**Observed Fact:** The TSD explains that Port Huron's three-year design value reached `78` for `2021-2023` and `73` for `2022-2024`, but EPA's trajectory and meteorology review concluded those exceedance events did not disperse into the St. Clair nonattainment area. It also documents that wind-direction data from `06/18/2024` through `11/27/2024` were invalid because of an installation error.

**Inference:** The technical case is strongest on present attainment and weaker on future verification. The modeling is not obviously broken. The weaker point is whether the maintenance plan leaves enough durable monitoring or review discipline in place after redesignation.

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

**Observed Fact:** EPA grounds the redesignation criteria in CAA section `107(d)(3)(E)`, relies on the Clean Data Determination and related approvals for section `110` and part `D` requirements, and proposes to make Belle River `Permit to Install 51-22` federally enforceable through SIP incorporation.

**Observed Fact:** The notice explains that the Clean Data Policy suspended some traditional attainment-planning requirements while the area continued to attain, but redesignation still requires EPA to find permanent and enforceable reductions and to approve a maintenance plan.

**Inference:** The statutory footing appears solid from the available materials. The more realistic legal risk is not lack of authority but adequacy of explanation regarding the maintenance plan and the specificity of future safeguards.

**Unknown:** The available text does not show whether EPA received any preproposal concerns from outside parties about the loss of near-source monitoring, because there are currently no docket comments.

## Step 8 — Procedural Normalcy

**Classification:** **Complex but normal**

**Observed Fact:** EPA is following a familiar SO2 redesignation path: prior nonattainment designation, later Clean Data Determination, approval of emissions inventory and NNSR elements, later attainment-by-deadline finding, then redesignation and maintenance-plan review.

**Observed Fact:** The state held public-comment periods in both `2023` and `2025`, and the record states that no comments were received and no hearings were requested.

**Observed Fact:** EPA prepared a TSD specifically to address the Port Huron monitor issue rather than leaving that contradiction unexplained.

**Inference:** The process looks normal for a technical EPA SIP action, but the public-facing explanation is somewhat compressed for readers who are not already steeped in SO2 redesignation practice.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

* Belle River could remain on coal longer than projected, making the `2033` zero-emissions projection less informative than the maintenance table suggests.
* The absence of continued near-source monitoring could make it slower to detect a mismatch between modeled assumptions and actual operations.
* If new or resumed sources seek permits in the area, EPA and Michigan will rely heavily on NSR and updated modeling; the current notice does not explain that handoff in much detail.
* The Port Huron issue could continue to confuse commenters if EPA does not summarize the TSD's trajectory and instrument-error findings more directly in the final rule.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

* The redesignation does not create a major new loophole by itself because Belle River's SIP-incorporated permit limits become federally enforceable.
* It does create an incentive for agencies to rely on permit-based and modeling-based maintenance demonstrations without keeping localized monitoring in place once attainment is achieved.
* The contingency structure is open-ended enough that future agencies retain substantial discretion about what measure to choose and when to choose it.
* The zero-emissions projection for `2033` creates some incentive to treat expected future operational changes as though they are already baked into maintenance, even when they are not yet complete.

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

The evidentiary strength is moderate to strong for the immediate attainment question and moderate for the longer-horizon maintenance question.

EPA has a defensible monitored-and-modeled attainment case, a concrete permit, a documented shutdown, and a targeted TSD on Port Huron. That is a substantial record. The thinner part is prospective: the maintenance plan leans on assumptions about future operations and on general commitments to reevaluate modeling assumptions, without much specificity about timing or triggers.

## Step 13 — Litigation Vulnerabilities

The most plausible APA-style weakness is not that EPA lacks support for redesignation. It is that the notice could be criticized as underexplaining the durability of the maintenance plan after monitoring ended and as leaving too much of the Port Huron answer and contingency design in supporting materials rather than the notice itself. Those are fixable record-quality issues, not obvious fatal defects.

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

**Key sentences from the NPRM/supporting record:** “Because there are no large sources of SO2 projected to be constructed or resume operations in the NAA, projected emissions to 2033 for the Belle River facility will be zero...” “To track the progress of the maintenance plan, EGLE commits to periodically reevaluate the modeling assumptions and input data used in the redesignation SIP development...”

**Routine description situated in context:** This is a conventional EPA SO2 redesignation action built around improved source conditions at two coal plants: one closed and one tightened by permit. That is a familiar and generally legitimate basis for redesignation. The maintenance plan, however, shifts from a detailed retrospective showing to a more generalized future-looking assurance structure once redesignation occurs.

**Regulatory effectiveness, statutory compliance, procedural adequacy:** The proposal likely satisfies the basic legal path to redesignation, but the final record would be stronger if EPA made the maintenance-verification framework more specific. The current “periodic reevaluation” language is workable but underspecified given that near-source monitoring ceased after `2022` and the `2033` inventory assumes future conversion at Belle River.

**Real-world consequence of invoking the leverage:** A targeted comment here is unlikely to reverse the redesignation, but it could press EPA to commit to clearer triggers for renewed review, making the long-term maintenance record more durable if operations change.
