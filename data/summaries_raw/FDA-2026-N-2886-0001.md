## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_eo_compliance
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Executive Order compliance supplies an adequate explanation for each technical change in FDA regulations.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A provision may use terminology for a distinct clinical, research, device, adverse-event, or tobacco-reporting purpose that is not resolved merely by replacing a word.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** FDA should identify the purpose of each change and explain why the selected replacement or deletion preserves the provision's operation.
- **Claim Id:** claim_editorial_character
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** All changes are editorial and do not alter regulated parties' duties or the meaning of data requirements.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Changing a demographic term in clinical validation, clinical-trial, safety-reporting, or tobacco provisions could affect what information is collected, compared, or disclosed.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request a provision-by-provision crosswalk and examples showing no substantive change.
- **Claim Id:** claim_no_industry_impact
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Regulated entities can implement the terminology changes without revising forms, systems, protocols, databases, or compliance training.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** FDA, sponsors, device applicants, investigators, and tobacco firms may need to update controlled documents and information systems even if legal obligations are intended to remain constant.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** FDA should explain its implementation and transition assumptions and identify any affected information collections.
- **Claim Id:** claim_no_public_health_loss
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Removing or replacing the term does not reduce the usefulness of sex-related or other demographic information for clinical interpretation and health surveillance.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A deletion could make a required data field less precise, while a substitution could conflate concepts that a regulated program previously distinguished.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** FDA should show how data comparability and clinical interpretation will be preserved.
- **Claim Id:** claim_case_by_case_replacement
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The notice's phrase as applicable provides a sufficiently determinate standard for choosing replacement versus deletion.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Different programs could apply the phrase inconsistently, producing gaps or inconsistent reporting expectations.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** FDA should publish criteria and a table explaining every replacement and deletion.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** FDA proposes to revise terminology in 21 CFR Parts 10, 56, 106, 201, 251, 310, 312, 314, 329, 600, 803, 862, 866, 870, 882, and 1114 by removing the word gender, replacing it with sex, or deleting the reference, with related editorial changes.

**Affected Entities**
- FDA program offices and reviewers
- Drug, biologic, device, and tobacco-product sponsors and manufacturers
- Institutional review boards, investigators, and clinical researchers
- Entities responsible for adverse-event, clinical-validation, and product-reporting systems
- Patients, study participants, and members of populations represented in regulated data

**Observed Facts**
- The notice lists 26 CFR provisions for revision.
- The stated authority includes the FD&C Act, PHS Act, Administrative Procedure Act, and other listed statutes.
- The notice characterizes the changes as editorial, predicts no measurable industry change, and estimates zero quantified costs, savings, transfers, and public-health benefit loss.
- The rule text changes examples and data descriptors in clinical and product-reporting provisions, including references to age, sex, race, or ethnicity.

**Inference**
- The proposal is primarily a terminology and regulatory-text alignment rule, but its provisions touch data collection and reporting contexts where terminology can affect meaning.
- The central comment leverage is not whether editorial correction is permissible in the abstract, but whether FDA has demonstrated semantic continuity and workable implementation for every affected provision.

**Evidence Quotes**
- remove the term gender wherever it appears
- does not impact industry practices
- We do not expect any loss of public health benefits as a result of this rule.
