## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** Approve Indiana's partial CCR permit program so approved Indiana requirements operate in lieu of corresponding federal CCR requirements after state permits are issued; listed excluded federal provisions remain directly applicable.

**Why It Matters:** The action changes the regulatory pathway for CCR landfills and surface impoundments in Indiana. The practical protection of groundwater, surface water, closure, monitoring, and public participation will depend on both the state-federal crosswalk and how the partial boundary is implemented in permits and public records.

**Affected Entities**
- Indiana CCR landfill and surface-impoundment owners and operators
- Indiana Department of Environmental Management and its permit, compliance, enforcement, and legal staff
- EPA Region 5 and EPA offices responsible for CCR oversight and program review
- Tribes, communities, property owners, and water users near Indiana CCR units
- Commenters and future permit participants relying on the IDEM Virtual File Cabinet

**Statutory Authority:** RCRA sections 4005(d) and 7004(b)(1), 42 U.S.C. 6945(d) and 6974(b)(1), as amended by section 2301 of the WIIN Act.

**Key Regulatory Change:** After final approval and issuance of state permits, Indiana's approved provisions become the operative requirements for covered matters, while the four excluded federal categories and the non-approved variance provision do not receive state-program substitution.

**Observed Facts**
- IDEM submitted its initial application on June 26, 2026, and a revised application on September 9, 2026.
- Indiana's Article 14 incorporates the July 1, 2025 edition of the federal CCR rule and uses Indiana permitting, monitoring, enforcement, and public-participation authorities.
- The application reports 14 RWS landfills needing CCR-landfill permits and estimates 64 CCR surface impoundments needing permits; the application also presents a broader 101-unit inventory by category.
- Indiana's requested approval excludes slope-stability vegetative-cover provisions, groundwater-monitoring suspension, Appendix IV no-MCL groundwater protection standards, and the February 2026 deadline-extension amendments.

**Inference:** The highest-value comments should test whether EPA's protectiveness determination is reproducible by topic and whether the partial-program boundary is operationally visible, while recognizing that the notice supplies a substantial analytical spine.

**Evidence Quotes**
- EPA has preliminarily determined that Indiana's partial CCR permit program meets the statutory standard for approval.
- For the State provisions that are not approved upon finalization, the corresponding Federal requirements will continue to apply directly to facilities.
- IDEM's CCR permit program follows the established solid waste permitting framework in Indiana code and 329 IAC 10, with adjustments for CCR units under 329 IAC 14.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Failure Modes**
- A textual crosswalk is treated as outcome equivalence without explaining a material difference in a standard, trigger, deadline, monitoring decision, or remedy.
- A facility or community cannot tell whether a requirement is governed by Indiana Article 14, an excluded federal provision, or both.
- Indiana's future incorporation of federal amendments lags the federal rule, creating a version mismatch that is difficult to identify from a permit or public record.
- A variance request under the broad text of 329 IAC 14-1-6 is misunderstood as available for federal-equivalent CCR criteria even though EPA is not approving that provision.
- Public participation exists formally but becomes difficult to use because application materials, responses, VFC records, and state/federal applicability information are not organized around the unit-level question a commenter must answer.
- The estimated unit inventory and stated personnel are insufficient for timely review, inspection, enforcement, or record maintenance without an observable implementation plan.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

- EPA has an incentive to recognize an eligible state program while retaining inspection, information-gathering, enforcement, and program-review authorities.
- Facilities have an incentive to obtain a single state permit for approved provisions, but also need clear identification of federal requirements that remain directly applicable.
- Nearby communities, Tribes, and water users benefit when technical differences, monitoring information, permit conditions, and enforcement records remain reconstructable.
