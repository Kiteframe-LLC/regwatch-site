## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim

The CLEO notice no longer meaningfully aids criminal investigations or local law enforcement.

Underlying assumption: Local agencies either do not use the notices or do not use them in a way that materially improves screening, tracing, or enforcement outcomes.

Failure scenario: If some CLEOs do use the notices to flag local prohibitions, identify suspicious activity, or coordinate with ATF in rare but important cases, the rule's `no meaningful benefit` premise may be overstated.

Procedural implication: Commenters can ask ATF to disclose whether it has any record of CLEO objections, referrals, or local investigative use since 2016, and whether the agency contacted CLEOs before concluding the system failed.

### Claim

Federal background checks and ATF's own legal review are adequate substitutes for local notice.

Underlying assumption: The functions that mattered under the old system are now fully covered by federal review and state/local law analysis done by ATF.

Failure scenario: If local agencies possess practical information not captured in federal databases or if state/local legal constraints are harder to identify centrally than ATF suggests, then the substitution story may be incomplete.

Procedural implication: Commenters can ask ATF to explain what types of local information, if any, remain outside the federal review process and why those gaps do not matter.

### Claim

The proposal will save about `$11.4 million` annually.

Underlying assumption: The projected notification volume, the `15 minute` burden estimate, and the leisure-wage assumptions are all reasonable approximations of real-world applicant behavior.

Failure scenario: If copying and mailing burdens vary widely, if many applicants submit electronically with low marginal cost, or if the projection of future notifications is too aggressive, the estimate may overstate savings.

Procedural implication: This is a strong opening for comments with actual applicant costs, mailing practices, and evidence on how often the burden is meaningfully more or less than the agency assumes.

### Claim

Removing CLEO notice is legally straightforward because the requirement is regulatory, not statutory.

Underlying assumption: The implied authority that once supported adoption of the notice requirement also supports its repeal, and no other statutory purpose implicitly requires retaining local notice.

Failure scenario: If local notice is intertwined with statutory approval criteria more than ATF acknowledges, opponents could argue that the repeal is not adequately reasoned even if the rule is not expressly barred by statute.

Procedural implication: Commenters can ask ATF to explain more directly why the same broad application-form authority that supported adding the notice requirement now supports removing it without undermining statutory objectives.

### Claim

The rule does not significantly affect small entities.

Underlying assumption: Any effects on gunsmiths, dealers, or other service providers are too indirect or too small to undermine ATF's RFA certification.

Failure scenario: If a material number of businesses derive meaningful revenue from CLEO-copying assistance, paper-processing support, or related NFA filing services, the small-entity discussion may be too cursory.

Procedural implication: Small businesses can improve the record by quantifying how much revenue or staff time is tied to the current notice process and whether repeal would have noticeable downstream effects.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

- ATF proposes to amend `27 CFR 479.62` and `27 CFR 479.84` by removing paragraph `(c)` in each section, which currently requires applicants and responsible persons to forward copies of specified NFA forms to a local chief law enforcement officer.
- The proposal applies to both applications to make an NFA firearm (`Form 1`) and applications to transfer an NFA firearm (`Form 4`), plus the related responsible-person questionnaire (`Form 23`).
- ATF explains that before `2016` the regime required CLEO certification, but the `2016 final rule` changed that system to notice only.
- ATF now says the notice requirement no longer serves a significant investigative or screening purpose because federal background checks are thorough and ATF independently evaluates state and local legality.
- The NPRM estimates annual savings of about `$11.4 million` from eliminating the notice burden and presents PRA burden reductions for Forms `1`, `4`, and `23`.
- The notice expressly invites comments from CLEOs on whether the notifications provide any actual benefit to them.

### Inference

This is a narrow deregulatory proposal that removes a procedural step from NFA application processing rather than changing who may lawfully possess regulated firearms. The highest-leverage comment opportunities are therefore not broad policy arguments for or against firearm regulation in general. They are focused questions about whether ATF has shown enough evidence for three specific conclusions: that CLEO notice is operationally useless, that the savings estimate is well grounded, and that removing the notice does not create underexplained local-information or small-entity tradeoffs.

### Evidence Quotes

- “ATF has no information that the CLEO notification meaningfully aids criminal investigations or serves a significant purpose for local law enforcement.”
- “During this time period, ATF is not aware of CLEOs performing independent background checks or filing objections to making or transferring a firearm.”
- “ATF estimates the savings from removing this requirement to be approximately $11.4 million annually.”
- “The CLEO notice is not in the statutory requirements for applying to make or transfer NFA firearms.”
- “ATF encourages public comments from CLEOs on whether these notices provide a benefit to them.”

## Step 3 — Agency Argument Tree

Regulatory objective  
↓  
Remove a procedural requirement that ATF says imposes cost without providing meaningful investigative or screening value  
↓  
Problem definition  
Applicants and responsible persons must currently copy and forward NFA application materials to a local CLEO even though ATF no longer requires local certification and says local agencies generally do not use the notice  
↓  
Data used  
Historical notification counts from 2015 through 2024, a forecast through 2034, a `15 minute` time estimate, a leisure-wage methodology, and ATF's own asserted experience that CLEOs do not perform background checks or file objections  
↓  
Analytical method  
Project future notification volume, multiply burden assumptions by wage assumptions, compare that burden against ATF's view of negligible law-enforcement utility, and frame the rule as consistent with statutory authority because the notice requirement is regulatory rather than statutory  
↓  
Alternatives considered  
Maintain the notice system; keep the 2016 structure; rely on public comments from CLEOs and other stakeholders rather than retaining the current burden by default  
↓  
Agency conclusion  
Repealing the notice requirement would save money, reduce burden, and remain legally permissible without materially impairing safety or law-enforcement interests

Missing links:
- The NPRM does not visibly show the evidentiary basis for the claim that CLEOs do not use the notices in practice.
- The notice does not quantify how many applications already avoid significant copying or mailing burdens through modern workflows.
- The local-law-enforcement side of the record is thin enough that ATF explicitly asks CLEOs to fill it in during the comment period.

Unsupported premises:
- That `no information` of benefit is equivalent to a reliable conclusion that the benefit is negligible.
- That the forecasted application growth rate is a stable basis for long-run savings estimates.

Logical jumps:
- From `ATF is not aware of objections` to `the system has not worked as intended`.
- From a procedural burden estimate to a confident conclusion that the rule will be net beneficial without a fuller discussion of possible informational loss.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

### Missing alternatives

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

### Leverage Point 1

Target issue: The factual basis for repealing notice is thinner than the confidence of the NPRM's conclusion.

Why it matters:
- The proposal's central justification is empirical, not purely legal.
- If ATF cannot show that local notice is actually unused or unhelpful, the final record may look more conclusory than reasoned.

Constructive comment ask:
- Request publication of any data, tracking records, or agency outreach showing how often CLEOs used the notice process, filed objections, or provided information relevant to NFA application review after the `2016` shift to notice.

### Leverage Point 2

Target issue: The savings estimate is clear enough to critique but not robust enough to treat as settled.

Why it matters:
- The `$11.4 million` figure is likely to be repeated as the rule's main practical benefit.
- It depends on assumptions about notification volume, applicant behavior, and time burden that may vary materially in practice.

Constructive comment ask:
- Submit real-world evidence on copying, mailing, or electronic-filing burdens and ask ATF to present a sensitivity analysis rather than a single central estimate.

### Leverage Point 3

Target issue: The agency's explanation for changing course from the `2016 final rule` could be stronger.

Why it matters:
- Agencies may change policy, but they need a reasoned explanation for why the earlier rationale no longer persuades them.
- The NPRM acknowledges the earlier rationale but does not yet show much evidence that the promised local benefit failed in practice.

Constructive comment ask:
- Ask ATF to compare the 2016 justification for notice with current evidence and explain specifically what changed in law, operations, or observed outcomes.
