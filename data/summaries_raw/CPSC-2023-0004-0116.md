## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

The notice does not identify alternatives to an all-or-nothing public inspection model. Useful alternatives include a CPSC change summary, a publicly accessible comparison table, staged review, an extension or supplemental comment period if material changes are complex, and acceptance of comments on discrete provisions. The agency should also ask about implementation timing, testing capacity, small-business compliance costs, accessibility for consumers with disabilities, and whether any revision creates new gaps or conflicts with the additional requirements in 16 CFR part 1263.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

The principal failure modes are an underinformed decision caused by inaccessible technical details; a record dominated by standards participants and regulated entities; inability to distinguish safety improvements from compliance costs; and a transition decision that creates uncertainty because the interaction with the existing additional requirements is not shown.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

Incorporation by reference can make a private standard highly consequential while concentrating technical control in the standards developer and participating industry. Transparent change summaries and provision-level evidence would give public commenters a meaningful way to test the safety rationale and reduce incentives to submit only conclusory support or opposition.
