## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The rule can remove manual brake controls for ADS-only vehicles.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Passengers will still have a practical way to request a stop and the ADS will translate that request safely.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If stop-request interfaces are inconsistent or poorly designed, the absence of a manual brake pedal could complicate emergency behavior or passenger expectations.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask how NHTSA expects stop requests to function in real vehicles and whether the final rule should require more detail.
- **Claim:** Stopping distance requirements can remain unchanged.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The primary safety function of FMVSS No. 135 is still captured by stopping performance rather than by the presence of a manual control.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the actuation path for ADS-only vehicles materially changes brake feel, latency, or fail-safe behavior, the unchanged standard may not fully capture the design risk.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The agency may need to explain why retained stopping-distance tests remain sufficient across both control architectures.
- **Claim:** On-board actuation is the right constraint.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** An ADS-operated vehicle should not depend on an external command source to actuate braking.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the on-board control chain is underspecified, manufacturers may differ in how they translate commands into brake application.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for a clearer boundary between on-board braking logic and any external fleet-management interface.
- **Claim:** The proposed definition of service brake control is non-restrictive.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A definition tied to the vehicle component that activates the service brake system will work across hydraulics, electronics, and brake-by-wire systems.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Some architectures may be distributed enough that the defined control is not a single component in the usual sense.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final rule may need a broader explanation or a more flexible definition if commenters identify edge-case architectures.
- **Claim:** The proposal will remove unnecessary burdens and costs.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Current manual-control wording is a design barrier rather than a safety-critical feature for ADS-only vehicles.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The amendment may still leave manufacturers with design-review and alternate-location compliance work for atypical layouts.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The cost discussion should distinguish direct requirement changes from residual certification work.

- Manufacturers of ADS-equipped light vehicles without manually operated driving controls
- Manufacturers that convert conventional vehicles into ADS-equipped platforms
