## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** A broad definition of being located in a State will increase access to scholarship funds.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Multistate SGOs authorized to do business in a State can deliver compliant scholarships without a physical headquarters or in-State staff.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A multistate SGO has limited local capacity or different operating practices across States, making State verification and enforcement less reliable than the access estimate assumes.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask the agency to show how cross-State operations, local accountability, and State-level verification will be measured.
- **Claim:** Annual audits and reporting will protect the credit against waste, fraud, abuse, and improper use of funds.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The required audits, certifications, and portal reports will detect material noncompliance early enough to prevent or correct improper payments.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Audits occur after scholarships are paid, use inconsistent standards, or identify errors without a timely correction and recovery pathway.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request audit standards, correction timelines, public reporting expectations, and examples of how findings affect future contributions.
- **Claim:** The proposed income-verification safe harbors reduce burdens while preserving eligibility integrity.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The listed public-benefit, qualified-census-tract, tutoring, special-needs, and foster-care indicators reliably identify eligible students without material overinclusion or exclusion.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Families with unstable housing, mixed households, or nonstandard school arrangements cannot use the safe harbors and face more intrusive direct verification, while other students receive categorical eligibility without comparable review.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for subgroup validation and a consistent fallback process for applicants who do not fit a safe harbor.
- **Claim:** The proposed rules do not have federalism implications because State participation is voluntary.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A State can decline the Federal credit without material practical pressure and can participate without losing meaningful authority over SGO administration.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** States that participate must administer a federally specified program while being barred from imposing additional requirements that reflect local accountability or student-protection priorities.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask the agency to distinguish voluntary program entry from the extent of administrative discretion after entry.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The IRS proposes regulations implementing new Internal Revenue Code section 25F, a nonrefundable Federal tax credit of up to $1,700 for qualified cash contributions to scholarship granting organizations, including rules for taxpayers, SGOs, States, eligible students, reporting, audits, State lists, and removal from the IRS SGO list.

**Affected Entities:** Individual taxpayers and donors; eligible students and their households; scholarship granting organizations, including multistate organizations; participating States and designated officials; schools and service providers receiving scholarship payments; tax advisers and return preparers.

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites section 25F, including section 25F(h), and section 7805(a), as well as the OBBBA provisions adding sections 25F and 139K.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal converts the statutory credit into an operating system: it defines qualified contributions, eligible students, household income, family size, SGOs, qualified digital wallets, State elections and lists, donor acknowledgements, Form 8525 substantiation, annual SGO certifications, audits, and Federal and State removal procedures.

**Observed Facts**
- The credit is capped at $1,700 and reduced by State tax credits, with carryforwards for up to five years subject to statutory limitations.
- SGOs must maintain segregated accounts, verify scholarship use and student eligibility, report to donors and the IRS, and undergo annual audits.
- The notice estimates 600 to 700 SGOs, $26 billion in annual contributions, and up to 2 million annual full-time scholarships by 2030.
- It estimates $12 million to $15 million in annual audit costs and includes an IRFA because small-entity effects remain uncertain.

**Inference:** The strongest comment leverage concerns whether the evidence and implementation design justify the projected scale, audit costs, student verification methods, and limits on State oversight.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Portal and data-interface failure
  
  **Mechanism:** State lists, SGO reports, donor numbers, and taxpayer forms do not match or cannot be submitted on time.
  
  **Consequence:** Valid contributions may be denied or require manual correction, while improper claims may not be detected promptly.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Publish data specifications, outage relief, correction procedures, and a non-digital fallback.
- **Failure Mode:** Audit threshold behavior
  
  **Mechanism:** SGOs near $500,000 redirect contributions or restructure receipts to avoid the higher audit cost.
  
  **Consequence:** Funds and students may be shifted for compliance reasons unrelated to scholarship effectiveness.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Model a sliding scale or temporary safe harbor and monitor organizations near the threshold.
- **Failure Mode:** Uneven student verification
  
  **Mechanism:** Safe-harbor categories are easy for some households but unavailable to families with mixed, unstable, or nonstandard circumstances.
  
  **Consequence:** Access and privacy burdens vary across similarly situated students.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Create a documented fallback and appeal process with data-minimization rules.
- **Failure Mode:** State implementation divergence
  
  **Mechanism:** States use different certification, review, and enforcement processes within Federal limits.
  
  **Consequence:** SGOs and families face different administrative burdens and correction experiences across States.
  
  **Mitigation To Request:** Set common minimum process standards and publish State-level performance measures.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Evasion:** The $500,000 audit threshold and the ability to operate as a multistate SGO may create incentives to redirect contributions, separate operations, or choose organizational structures based on regulatory thresholds.

**Threshold Gaming:** The 85-percent activity safe harbor, 90-percent spending requirement, and audit tier may encourage accounting or timing choices near thresholds.

**Strategic Compliance:** SGOs may direct donors or scholarship activity toward arrangements that simplify verification or avoid a higher-cost audit, while States may reduce oversight to remain within the Federal participation framework.

**Constructive Response:** Ask the agency to monitor near-threshold behavior and explain how it will distinguish legitimate planning from circumvention.
