## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The IRS proposes to amend 26 CFR part 300 to raise the user fee for issuing an estate tax closing letter from $56 to $76.

**Affected Entities**
- Authorized persons who request estate tax closing letters
- Estates and fiduciaries that use closing letters to document estate-tax administration status
- Tax practitioners and service providers who may request letters on behalf of estates
- IRS staff who process and quality-review the requests

**Statutory Authority:** 31 U.S.C. 9701, OMB Circular A-25, and the IRS's cited user-fee rulemaking authority in 26 CFR part 300.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal increases the fee amount in 26 CFR 300.12(b) and would apply the updated fee to requests received 30 days after final-rule publication.

**Observed Facts**
- The preamble says the current fee is $56 under final regulations adopted in December 2025.
- The preamble says the new 2025 cost model calculates a full cost of $76 per request.
- The notice gives an arithmetic breakdown from labor and benefits plus overhead to a total program cost of $615,593 and a per-request fee of $76.

**Inference:** This is a narrow fee rule, but it still depends on whether the agency has adequately explained the cost model inputs and the real-world burden of the increase.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The proposed regulations would increase the amount of the user fee imposed on authorized persons requesting the issuance of an estate tax closing letter.
- The $76 cost per request was determined by dividing the full cost by the average annual volume of processed requests.

  **Why It Matters:** Clarifying the actual payer population can sharpen the RFA and practical-impact discussion.
  
  **Supporting Evidence**
  - The notice refers both to estates and to authorized persons requesting letters.

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

- **Option:** Support with transparency requests
  
  **When It Fits:** Useful for commenters who accept cost-recovery authority but want fuller public support for the amount.
  
  **Upside:** Keeps the comment constructive and focused on improving the record.
  
  **Risk:** May not change the fee amount if the agency already has strong internal support.
- **Option:** Request phased or alternative calibration
  
  **When It Fits:** Useful if the commenter is concerned about abrupt increases or volatility in the underlying assumptions.
  
  **Upside:** Targets the weakest part of the notice: missing alternatives analysis.
  
  **Risk:** Requires the commenter to offer a plausible alternative rather than only objecting.
- **Option:** Focus on burden allocation and RFA fit
  
  **When It Fits:** Useful for practitioners or small firms with direct experience requesting closing letters.
  
  **Upside:** Can add factual evidence the agency may not currently have in the record.
  
  **Risk:** Will be stronger with concrete examples or cost data.
