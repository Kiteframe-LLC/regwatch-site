## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim: the package achieves at least 15% RFP

- **Underlying assumption:** Wisconsin's 2017-to-2023 reductions are properly attributed, permanent, enforceable, and sufficiently explained for each approved area.
- **Failure scenario:** Milwaukee's numbers are visible in the supporting plan, but the public-facing packet does not surface equally detailed Kenosha and Sheboygan support for side-by-side scrutiny.
- **Procedural implication:** EPA may need to provide clearer area-by-area derivations so commenters can test the triplet approval on the same footing.

### Claim: the budgets are clearly identified and precisely quantified

- **Underlying assumption:** Precision of the final numbers is matched by sufficient transparency about assumptions, especially VMT cushions and model inputs.
- **Failure scenario:** A budget can be numerically precise while still leaving commenters unable to evaluate whether the 7.5% VMT increase for Milwaukee is conservative, justified, or outcome-sensitive.
- **Procedural implication:** Commenters can ask EPA to explain the practical basis for the VMT margin and whether alternative sensitivity cases were tested.

### Claim: segmentation of the package is acceptable

- **Underlying assumption:** EPA can approve inventory, RFP, budgets, I/M, and NNSR now without materially prejudging or obscuring later decisions on attainment, RACM, and contingency measures.
- **Failure scenario:** The public may struggle to judge whether the approved pieces are adequate without seeing how EPA will treat the omitted elements that speak most directly to attainment and backup measures.
- **Procedural implication:** EPA should explain the interaction between the present approvals and the later action more plainly.

### Claim: existing approved NNSR rules satisfy Moderate-area needs

- **Underlying assumption:** Wisconsin's existing SIP-approved NNSR structure maps cleanly onto the revised Moderate-area geography and current ozone requirements.
- **Failure scenario:** The rules may be legally sufficient in general while still leaving underexplained questions about how they apply to revised boundaries and reclassified areas.
- **Procedural implication:** EPA should make the crosswalk between the older approvals and the current Moderate obligations more legible in the docket narrative.

### Claim: local planning elements are meaningful in a transport-driven ozone setting

- **Underlying assumption:** Even though Milwaukee's ozone problem is heavily shaped by Lake Michigan transport and mesoscale meteorology, the approved local planning tools still form a rational part of the statutory response.
- **Failure scenario:** If transport and lake-breeze dynamics dominate exceedances, commenters may question whether EPA has sufficiently explained the limits as well as the value of local measures.
- **Procedural implication:** EPA could strengthen the record by linking the transport discussion more directly to the chosen local planning elements and their expected practical contribution.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

- EPA proposes to approve portions of Wisconsin's April 2, 2025 Moderate-area SIP submission for three Wisconsin ozone areas: Kenosha, Milwaukee, and Sheboygan.
- The proposed approvals cover the 2017 base-year emissions inventory, the 15% RFP showing, the 2023 motor-vehicle emissions budgets, the I/M program certification, and the NNSR certification.
- EPA is also initiating the transportation-conformity adequacy process for the 2023 budgets.
- The NPRM explicitly says the attainment demonstration, RACM demonstration, and contingency measures will be addressed in a separate action.
- The Milwaukee supporting plan says the Milwaukee area's NOx emissions fall from `93.84` to `73.41` tons per ozone-season day between 2017 and 2023, and VOC emissions fall from `109.93` to `92.16`.
- The Milwaukee supporting plan emphasizes that Lake Michigan transport and lake-breeze circulation are major drivers of persistent shoreline ozone.

### Inference

- This is a partial but consequential SIP approval. Even though EPA is postponing some of the most outcome-oriented pieces, the current proposal would still lock in the planning foundation and conformity budgets that shape later transportation and permitting decisions.
- The best comment leverage is not a broad attack on SIP authority. It is a targeted request for fuller explanation of the split action, area-specific support, and the relationship between transport-heavy ozone dynamics and the local planning elements EPA is approving now.
