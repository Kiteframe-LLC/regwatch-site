## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** FY 2026 action counts through June show that current policy changes have not materially increased performance-based and adverse actions.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Six months of FY 2026 data are comparable with full prior fiscal years and are not materially distorted by seasonality, delayed reporting, deferred resignations, reductions in force, or other workforce changes.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If reporting is incomplete or the first half of FY 2026 is not representative, the comparison may overstate or understate the need for structural changes.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request a completion-rate analysis, monthly trend data, and an explanation of how confounding workforce events were handled.
- **Claim:** A maximum 30-day opportunity period will reduce delay while remaining a sufficient opportunity to improve.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Thirty calendar days is suitable across occupations, performance plans, work cycles, accommodations, and cases requiring evidence over longer periods.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The limit may be too short for complex work or too rigid where performance cannot be observed fairly within one month, even though agencies retain discretion to extend it.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for criteria governing extensions and examples of work for which 30 days is or is not adequate.
- **Claim:** Replacing the Douglas factors with a totality-of-the-circumstances standard will improve penalty review.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Decision-makers and reviewing judges can apply the less structured standard consistently without a checklist or calibration guidance.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Different agencies or adjudicators may weigh similar circumstances differently, reducing predictability or increasing litigation.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can request sample applications, minimum explanation requirements, and data on how consistency will be monitored.
- **Claim:** The newly released data support structural changes to incentivize supervisors.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The observed action counts diagnose a procedural incentive problem rather than changes in workforce composition, reporting, or agency conditions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The data may be consistent with several explanations, so the proposed remedy may be broader than the evidence supports.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The reopened comment should ask OPM to distinguish the data-supported inference from the policy inference.
