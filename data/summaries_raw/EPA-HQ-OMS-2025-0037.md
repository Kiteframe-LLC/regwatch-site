# Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim

The new clause will allow cleanup work to continue when EPA personnel are absent and will prevent work stoppages.

**Underlying assumption**
Manifest-signature availability is a meaningful cause of work delays at EPA cleanup sites, and delegating signature authority to contractors addresses that problem across the relevant site categories.

**Failure scenario**
Delays are actually caused by transporter scheduling, waste characterization, or contractor coordination rather than by the absence of EPA personnel authorized to sign.

**Procedural implication**
If the problem statement is overstated, commenters can ask EPA to show how often manifest-signature availability has actually delayed shipments or cleanup operations.

### Claim

The change will produce cost savings.

**Underlying assumption**
EPA has some baseline experience showing avoided downtime, fewer EPA site visits, or lower contractor standby costs.

**Failure scenario**
EPA has not quantified the savings or distinguished between rare edge cases and common operations.

**Procedural implication**
A commenter can request the baseline, methodology, and range of expected savings before EPA finalizes a benefit claim.

### Claim

Conflict-of-interest and fraud risks can be managed through management controls and COI review.

**Underlying assumption**
EPA's procurement review process is specific enough to identify when a contractor has incentives to misdescribe waste, shipment status, or compliance conditions while signing manifests on EPA's behalf.

**Failure scenario**
The COI process is generic procurement screening and does not address site-level chain-of-custody, record integrity, or incentives created during field operations.

**Procedural implication**
The administrative record may be vulnerable if EPA adopts the delegation without describing the controls that make the delegation safe in practice.

### Claim

The contractor can sign manifests without becoming the generator or entering a broader agency relationship.

**Underlying assumption**
The legal disclaimer in the clause is sufficient to preserve EPA's intended allocation of responsibility under the hazardous-waste manifest regime.

**Failure scenario**
Real-world disputes arise over responsibility for errors in the manifest, land-ban records, or shipment information because the contract text does not fully resolve those questions.

**Procedural implication**
Commenters can ask EPA to explain how liability, record-correction duties, and enforcement exposure work when EPA delegates signature authority but retains generator status.

# Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

- EPA proposes a new clause at 48 CFR 1552.223-73 and a prescription at 48 CFR 1523.303-73.
- The clause would authorize contractors, when directed in writing by the EPA COR, to sign EPA Form 8700-22 Uniform Hazardous Waste Manifest and related land-ban records for EPA Superfund and non-Superfund worksites.
- Alternate II would also authorize contractors to sign non-hazardous-waste shipping manifests and related records for EPA worksites.
- The notice says the current local clause, `EPA-H-11-104`, applies to Superfund sites, and the new clause would extend signature authority more broadly.
- EPA says the change is intended to avoid work stoppages when EPA personnel are not present at the worksite.

### Inference

This is a procurement-rule change with environmental-compliance consequences. It does not alter the substantive hazardous-waste manifest regulations in 40 CFR parts 262 and 268, but it does alter who may execute the paperwork on EPA's behalf in the field and therefore changes the control structure around waste shipment documentation.

### Evidence Quotes

> "The EPA currently has a local clause, EPA-H-11-104, which involves the signing of Uniform Hazardous Waste Manifests for Superfund sites."

> "The new clause will enable contractors to sign the Waste Manifest at EPA worksites for the removal of hazardous and non-hazardous materials at both Superfund and non-Superfund sites."
