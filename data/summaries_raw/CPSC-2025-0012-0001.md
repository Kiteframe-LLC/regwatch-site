## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** Require covered micromobility electrical systems, replacement batteries, aftermarket chargers, and eBike conversion components to meet modified UL requirements; add covered children's products to third-party testing rules.

**Problem Definition:** Thermal runaway, fires, explosions, gas release, burns, smoke inhalation, overheating, and possible electric shock.

**Affected Entities:** Consumers, manufacturers, importers, battery and charger suppliers, conversion-kit sellers, rental fleets, small entities, labs, and children's-product firms.

**Mechanism:** Mandatory performance and labeling requirements, including tamper-resistant enclosures, post-discharge charging tests, reverse-polarity tests, and revised warnings.

**Decision Stage:** Proposal stage; CPSC requests data on hazards, alternatives, compliance, costs, benefits, and scope.

**Key Tradeoff:** Potential injury reduction versus testing, redesign, certification, supply, and availability costs.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Primary:** Broad scope and incorporated standards may impose uneven burdens if categories, aftermarket configurations, testing pathways, transition, and version control are unclear.

**Secondary**

- Labels may be less effective for modified or informal aftermarket products.

**Mitigations To Request:** Clarify definitions, transition rules, lab capacity, version control, and enforcement.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Risk:** A single compliance route may favor larger suppliers without addressing informal aftermarket channels.

**Question:** How will CPSC measure incident reduction and unintended market exit?
