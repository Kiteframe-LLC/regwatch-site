## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** The December 18, 2026 deadline could cause significant implementation problems for off-site processing of byproduct TCE and wastewater disposal.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Byproduct-containing streams are routinely transferred between facilities and cannot be handled within the existing site-limited exclusion without material disruption.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Transfer volumes, facilities, permit limits, or wastewater concentrations differ materially across sites, so a uniform one-year extension either over-relieves some operations or does not solve the constrained operations' problem.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** EPA should identify the affected operations, quantify the range of impacts, and explain why a narrower or conditional extension would not work.
- **Claim:** The HFC-134a feedstock reduction should move from June 7, 2027 to December 31, 2027.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** AIM Act allowances and demand from critical infrastructure and national-security users justify continued higher production through the revised date.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Demand or allowance conditions change, or alternative refrigerant supply is available, making the extension longer than needed or shifting risk to TCE exposure without a corresponding necessity showing.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final record should disclose the demand and allowance basis, identify feasible alternatives, and explain a review or sunset mechanism.
- **Claim:** The extension mainly delays costs and benefits already estimated in the 2024 rule.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The original cost and benefit estimates remain valid when timing and affected operations change.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The new delay changes exposure duration, investment timing, market conditions, or the set of entities that can continue using TCE, so prior estimates do not capture the incremental distributional or health effects.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** EPA should show the incremental calculation, sensitivity to alternative dates, and any changed assumptions rather than relying only on the prior analysis.
- **Claim:** Thirty days after publication is a practicable period to update SDS text and downstream notifications.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Affected manufacturers, processors, and distributors can revise documents and communicate the changes across existing supply chains within that window.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Multi-tier distribution, inventory already in commerce, or document-control processes cause inconsistent SDS language or notice failures.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** EPA should explain the timing basis and clarify how prior and revised SDS language will coexist during the transition.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** The extension is too broad
  
  **Mechanism:** Entities outside the documented byproduct-transfer or HFC-134a constraints retain an additional year to process TCE.
  
  **Consequence:** Additional exposure or delayed risk reduction without a demonstrated operational need.
  
  **Mitigation:** Use use-specific conditions, reporting, or a shorter review date.
- **Failure Mode:** The extension is too short
  
  **Mechanism:** Facilities cannot complete engineering, logistics, or document changes by December 31, 2027.
  
  **Consequence:** Noncompliance, forced incineration, permit stress, or supply disruption after the extension expires.
  
  **Mitigation:** Require transition milestones and explain what evidence would support a further targeted adjustment.
- **Failure Mode:** SDS and notification inconsistency
  
  **Mechanism:** A 30-day update window interacts with existing inventory and multi-tier distribution systems.
  
  **Consequence:** Downstream users receive conflicting instructions about allowable uses and dates.
  
  **Mitigation:** Provide model language, transition instructions, and an effective-date rule for existing stock.
- **Failure Mode:** Demand forecast changes
  
  **Mechanism:** HFC-134a demand or AIM Act allowances do not remain at the projected level.
  
  **Consequence:** The continued feedstock allowance is longer than necessary or causes avoidable TCE exposure.
  
  **Mitigation:** Add periodic reporting or a condition tied to documented demand and allowance conditions.
