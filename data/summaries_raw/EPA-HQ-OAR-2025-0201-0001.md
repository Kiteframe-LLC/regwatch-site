### Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

**Claim:** EPA's current-classification-only interpretation is the best reading of CAA section 182.
**Underlying assumption:** Section 182's references to requirements "as may be applicable to the area as reclassified" make the current classification dispositive and do not preserve separate prior-classification deadlines.
**Failure scenario:** A commenter shows that section 182's cross-references to lower-classification submissions were intended to preserve both substantive obligations and pre-existing deadlines, or that Congress expected pending sanctions/FIP consequences to survive reclassification.
**Procedural implication:** EPA would need to address why its new reading does not undercut statutory timing, anti-backsliding, or enforcement structures.

**Claim:** The proposal does not relieve States of substantive obligations because higher classifications incorporate lower-classification elements.
**Underlying assumption:** Reclassified-area SIP submissions will include the same substantive planning elements on the higher-classification schedule.
**Failure scenario:** A State uses the rule to delay or avoid controls that would have mattered for timely emissions reductions before the higher-classification SIP deadline.
**Procedural implication:** EPA may need to clarify which lower-classification elements remain substantively required, how already-missed deadlines are treated, and whether transition provisions are needed.

**Claim:** Reliance interests are minimal because the rule relieves State obligations.
**Underlying assumption:** States, regulated entities, local governments, and the public did not materially rely on the January 2025 leftover SIP elements policy.
**Failure scenario:** Communities or regulated entities identify planning, enforcement, permitting, or sanctions-related reliance on pending Moderate-area SIP obligations or failure-to-submit consequences.
**Procedural implication:** EPA would need to respond to reliance evidence and explain any transition treatment.

**Claim:** The action does not impose new burdens or major distributional impacts.
**Underlying assumption:** Shifting obligations to the current-classification plan mainly reduces duplicative submissions without materially changing emissions-control timing or public-health outcomes.
**Failure scenario:** A commenter shows area-specific delay in RACT, RFP, inspection and maintenance, or contingency-measure implementation.
**Procedural implication:** EPA may need a clearer implementation-impact record for affected nonattainment areas.

### Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Observed Facts**

EPA proposes to revise 40 CFR 51.1403, which was codified in the January 2025 final rule on SIP submittal deadlines and implementation requirements for reclassified ozone nonattainment areas.

The proposed rule would provide that, after reclassification, an ozone nonattainment area's current classification governs the area's applicable CAA section 182 SIP requirements. EPA states that the proposal would apply nationwide to past and future reclassifications associated with the 2008, 2015, and future ozone NAAQS.

EPA is not reopening other parts of the January 2025 final rule, including default deadlines in 40 CFR 51.1402.

EPA identifies Clean Air Act sections 172, 181, and 182 as relevant statutory authority.

**Inference**

The practical effect is to move unresolved prior-classification SIP obligations into the current-classification planning framework and to moot some prior failure-to-submit consequences where the old classification has been extinguished.

**Evidence quotes**

"In this proposed action, the EPA is proposing a new interpretation that, upon reclassification, an area is subject only to those requirements in Clean Air Act (CAA) section 182 that are specific to that area's current classification."

"The EPA is specifically soliciting comments on whether any reliance interest exists that the Agency did not consider here."

### Step 3 — Agency Argument Tree

Regulatory objective:
Clarify which SIP requirements apply after ozone nonattainment areas are reclassified.

Problem definition:
The January 2025 rule adopted a "leftover SIP elements policy" under which some prior-classification SIP obligations survived reclassification. EPA now concludes that this interpretation is not the best reading of CAA section 182.

Data used:
EPA relies mainly on statutory text, prior ozone NAAQS implementation rules, prior reclassification actions, the October 2023 failure-to-submit findings, and the universe of areas reclassified under the 2008 and 2015 ozone standards.

Analytical method:
EPA interprets CAA section 182's classification structure and applies the resulting rule to each classification level: Marginal, Moderate, Serious, Severe, and Extreme.

Alternatives considered:
The preamble compares the January 2025 leftover SIP elements policy with the proposed reclassified-area SIPs policy. It does not present a detailed intermediate alternative, such as preserving some prior deadlines during a transition period while eliminating duplicative submissions.

Agency conclusion:
Upon reclassification, the current classification governs, previously satisfied elements need not be resubmitted, and outstanding prior-classification findings may become moot.

Missing links:
EPA could more clearly show how the policy affects implementation timing in each of the 18 areas reclassified from Moderate to Serious in 2024 and 2025, and how the proposal avoids delay in emission-reduction planning.

Unsupported premises:
The minimal-reliance-interest conclusion is stated plausibly but with little concrete analysis beyond the deregulatory nature of the change.

Logical jumps:
EPA moves from "single classification at a time" to "prior-classification deadlines and sanctions consequences no longer apply" without fully separating substantive SIP elements from timing and enforcement consequences.

### Step 7 — Legal Grounding

EPA cites Clean Air Act sections 172, 181, and 182. The central legal question is whether CAA section 182 requires prior-classification SIP obligations and deadlines to survive after an ozone nonattainment area is reclassified.

EPA's proposal is legally grounded in the text and structure of CAA sections 181 and 182, especially the premise that an area has one classification at a time and that higher classifications incorporate lower-classification substantive elements. The record also cites general administrative-law principles allowing an agency to revise a prior interpretation if it acknowledges the change, gives a reasoned explanation, and considers reliance interests.

Uncertain: Whether courts or commenters would accept EPA's conclusion that prior failure-to-submit findings and related sanctions/FIP obligations become moot when the prior classification is extinguished.

### Step 8 — Procedural Normalcy

**Classification:** Complex but normal.

EPA is proceeding by notice-and-comment rulemaking, explains the change from the January 2025 final rule, identifies statutory authority, discusses implementation impacts, and notes OMB review under Executive Order 12866. The process is normal for a significant EPA rule revising a nationwide SIP implementation policy. The complexity arises from the short interval between the January 2025 final rule and this reconsideration, plus the existing sanctions/FIP consequences for some affected areas.

### Step 9 — Failure Modes

1. A State with an area reclassified to Serious may delay completing elements that had been due under the Moderate classification, creating a planning gap before the Serious-area SIP deadline.

2. EPA Regions may apply mootness and sanctions-clock consequences inconsistently if the final rule does not specify transition procedures.

3. Affected communities may lose near-term procedural leverage tied to existing findings of failure to submit, while the replacement Serious-area SIP process may occur later.

4. Regulated entities and States may disagree about whether a prior submission fully satisfies a current-classification element.

5. The final rule could generate litigation over whether EPA adequately considered reliance interests and implementation impacts.

### Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

The proposal could reduce incentives for States to complete lower-classification SIP submissions before reclassification if missed obligations can be reset into a later current-classification schedule. That risk is partly limited because higher classifications still incorporate many lower-classification substantive elements, but timing may matter for emissions reductions and enforceability.

The proposal may also encourage commenters to focus on whether specific SIP elements are genuinely duplicative or whether they remain necessary to maintain timely progress toward attainment.

### Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

EPA's legal reasoning is clearly stated and tied to statutory text. The strongest evidence is the statutory structure of CAA sections 181 and 182 and the incorporation of lower-classification elements into higher-classification plans.

The weaker evidentiary areas are practical impacts: burden reduction is asserted but not quantified; public-health or emissions-timing effects are not quantified; reliance interests are treated as minimal largely because the rule relieves State obligations; and the area-specific impact of mooting the October 2023 failure-to-submit findings is not laid out in a table.

### Step 17 — High-Leverage Comment Angles

1. **Ask for an area-by-area transition table.** A commenter can request a table identifying each affected area, current classification, prior missed SIP elements, current deadline, and expected treatment under the final rule.

2. **Request a clearer mootness procedure.** A commenter can ask EPA to explain whether prior findings and sanctions clocks become moot automatically, by final rule operation, or through later area-specific actions.

3. **Propose a narrow transition alternative.** A commenter can suggest preserving prior enforcement consequences until a complete current-classification SIP is submitted, while still avoiding duplicative lower-classification submissions.

4. **Submit reliance evidence.** A commenter can explain whether local planning, public participation, enforcement expectations, or permitting decisions relied on existing SIP-submission obligations.

5. **Ask EPA to separate substance from timing.** A commenter can ask EPA to show that the proposal does not merely preserve substantive requirements while materially delaying their enforceable submission and implementation.

### Step 18 — Decision Brief

**Strongest leverage point:** Implementation impacts and transition mechanics for pending failure-to-submit findings.

**Key sentences from the NPRM:** "If this rule is finalized as proposed, the October 2023 FFS for the Moderate area SIP submissions would be moot since the Moderate elements would no longer be required SIP elements upon reclassification to Serious." "A revised SIP submission addressing all relevant elements would be evaluated anew by the EPA as part of the Serious or higher classification plan."

**Routine description:** EPA is proposing a nationwide legal interpretation that would treat the current ozone classification as controlling after reclassification. That may simplify duplicative SIP submissions, but it also changes how missed prior-classification obligations, sanctions clocks, and FIP duties are handled.

**Regulatory effectiveness:** The final rule would be stronger if it showed that affected areas will still submit and implement necessary planning elements on a schedule that supports timely attainment.

**Statutory compliance:** EPA should explain why CAA section 182 permits not only shifting substantive obligations to the current classification, but also mooting prior deadlines and enforcement consequences.

**Procedural adequacy:** The record would benefit from an area-specific transition table and a more explicit reliance-interest analysis.

**Real-world consequence of invoking the leverage:** Commenters can push EPA to clarify which obligations remain enforceable, when current-classification SIPs are due, and how the Agency will prevent planning delays in affected ozone nonattainment areas.

**Worth a comment?** High value.

**Comment posture:** evidence request
