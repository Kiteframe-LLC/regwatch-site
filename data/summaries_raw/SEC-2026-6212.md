## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Observed Facts**
- The proposal would expand rule 205-3 so advisers to registered management investment companies and BDCs may use performance-based compensation subject to a 20% ceiling, fund-governance standards, and board findings.
- The proposal would allow performance fees based on net realized and net unrealized capital gains or appreciation over a specified period or definite dates.
- The proposal would add accredited investors to the qualified-client definition, remove the separate net-worth and assets-under-management tests, and make conforming changes to related Advisers Act provisions.
- Forms N-1A and N-2 would receive distinct performance-fee fee-table disclosure and detailed narrative and graphical disclosure; Form N-CSR would receive more particularized disclosure of board approval of qualifying performance fees.
- A 12-month compliance period is proposed for the form amendments, but a fund relying on the new rule provision would have to comply with the amended rule and forms when it first relies on that provision.

**Inference**
- The proposal is a deregulatory access-and-disclosure package: it relaxes eligibility restrictions while attempting to shift protection toward board oversight, fee limits, and disclosure.
- Its practical effect will depend less on the formal 20% ceiling than on how funds define measurement periods, value unrealized assets, manage liquidity, and document board judgments.

**Regulatory Action:** Expand the exemption from the Advisers Act performance-fee prohibition, broaden qualified-client eligibility, and add fund and board disclosures.

**Affected Entities**
- Registered investment advisers
- Registered open-end and closed-end management investment companies
- Business development companies
- Section 3(c)(1) private funds and separately managed accounts
- Accredited investors and other fund shareholders
- Fund boards, independent directors, service providers, and small advisers or funds

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites Advisers Act sections 203A, 204, 205(e), 206A, 206(4), and 211(a); Investment Company Act sections 6, 8, 24, 30, 31, 37, 38, 59, and 63; Securities Act sections 5, 7, 10, 19(a), and 28; and Exchange Act sections 12, 13(a), 14, 15(d), 23(a), 35A, and 36.

**Key Regulatory Change:** Performance fees on capital gains or appreciation would become available to a broader set of fund and individual clients, including regulated funds using the board channel and accredited investors using the qualified-client channel.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The proposal would expand the ability of investment advisers to receive performance-based compensation from regulated funds, subject to certain conditions.
- The proposal would require separate disclosure of all performance-based compensation paid by regulated funds to their investment adviser.
- The proposed amendments would allow an investment adviser to calculate performance fees on net realized and net unrealized capital appreciation.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Fee crystallization after unrealized gains reverse
  
  **Mechanism:** A fund accrues or pays performance compensation on appreciation that is later lost.
  
  **Consequence:** Shareholders may bear fees that do not correspond to durable investor returns.
  
  **Mitigation In Record:** The notice discusses high-water marks and loss carryforwards but leaves their use to the arrangement and board findings.
  
  **Comment Priority:** High
- **Failure Mode:** Short measurement windows or valuation timing
  
  **Mechanism:** A manager's incentives become concentrated around measurement dates or valuation judgments.
  
  **Consequence:** Risk-taking, portfolio concentration, turnover, or valuation choices may increase near fee crystallization.
  
  **Mitigation In Record:** The board must consider strategy, valuation, and measurement period, but no minimum period is prescribed.
  
  **Comment Priority:** High
- **Failure Mode:** Formal eligibility without practical access
  
  **Mechanism:** Advisers retain high investment minimums or do not launch products for newly eligible investors.
  
  **Consequence:** The proposal's access and competition benefits may be smaller than projected.
  
  **Mitigation In Record:** The economic analysis discusses minimum investments but does not quantify adoption responses.
  
  **Comment Priority:** Moderate
- **Failure Mode:** Investor misunderstanding of variable fees
  
  **Mechanism:** A fee table example based on a five-percent return shows zero or limited performance fees while other scenarios produce materially higher costs.
  
  **Consequence:** Investors may understate the fee's contribution to expenses when comparing funds.
  
  **Mitigation In Record:** The proposal adds a cross-reference, narrative explanation, and graphical scenarios.
  
  **Comment Priority:** Moderate

  **Real World Consequence:** A poorly controlled arrangement could charge fees on gains that later reverse or encourage behavior around valuation and measurement dates.
