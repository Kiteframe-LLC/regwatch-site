## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** A one-to-one reserve rule with specified liquid assets will protect holders in stress.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Permitted assets can be identified, monetized, and segregated quickly enough under severe conditions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Custody, tokenization, concentration, or market-stress frictions delay monetization or reduce available value.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for stress-test assumptions, reserve reconciliation examples, and evidence supporting the principles-based diversification approach.
- **Claim:** A two-business-day redemption outer limit balances responsiveness and liquidity management.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Issuers can meet ordinary and surge redemptions within two business days without destabilizing reserve management.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A high-volume or operationally complex redemption event exposes delays, fees, or discretionary extension risk.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for surge scenarios, service-level data, and clear public criteria for extensions and safe-harbor use.
- **Claim:** Standardized capital requirements are appropriately tailored to PPSI risks.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The selected two-percent charges, issuance thresholds, revenue measure, and loss scalar are reasonable proxies for unexpected loss.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The market's risk profile differs from banking analogues, causing undercapitalization or unnecessary barriers to entry.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request calibration data, sensitivity analysis, and comparison with internal-model and simpler alternatives.
- **Claim:** Principles-based requirements will preserve flexibility without sacrificing consistency.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Issuers and examiners will apply terms such as sufficient diversification and public interest consistently.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Similar issuers receive materially different treatment or spend heavily on defensive documentation.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for worked examples, supervisory metrics, and published interpretation or review practices.
- **Claim:** The framework will increase competition and market resilience.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Regulatory clarity will overcome entry costs and will not concentrate activity in only the largest firms.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Compliance, custody, and capital costs increase concentration or pass through to users.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for market-structure scenarios and distributional analysis by issuer size and user type.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The Board proposes Regulation UU in 12 CFR part 247 and related amendments to implement its GENIUS Act responsibilities for Board-supervised permitted payment stablecoin issuers, covered custodians, State-qualified PPSIs, and the tying prohibition applicable to all PPSIs.

**Affected Entities**
- Board-supervised PPSIs, including subsidiaries of insured State member banks
- State-qualified PPSIs subject to the Board's backup or transition authority
- Banks, holding companies, and other Board-supervised covered custodians
- Payment stablecoin holders, digital-asset service providers, and other market participants
- State member banks considering a PPSI subsidiary and small entities that may provide related services

**Statutory Authority:** The GENIUS Act, 12 U.S.C. 5901 et seq., including sections 4, 5, 6, 7, 10, and 16, with related amendments under the Federal Reserve Act, the Bank Holding Company Act, HOLA, and the Board's existing capital and procedural rules.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal would define the Board's stablecoin framework; restrict activities and remuneration; require one-to-one segregated reserves, monthly disclosure, examination, and certification; establish redemption, risk-management, reporting, capital, custody, tying, backup-enforcement, and transition rules; and align related banking regulations.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice is labeled a notice of proposed rulemaking and requests comments on all aspects of the proposal.
- The rule covers reserve assets, redemption, risk management, audits and supervision, capital, custody, tying, State-qualified PPSIs, and related amendments.
- The economic analysis states that the market is nascent, that no PPSIs currently operate under a uniform Federal framework, and that the analysis is mostly qualitative.
- The regulatory analysis includes PRA and IRFA discussions and identifies estimated reporting costs per Board-supervised PPSI.

**Inference:** This is a broad implementing NPRM rather than a narrow technical rule because it creates an integrated compliance architecture across issuance, reserves, custody, capital, and supervision.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The proposal implements the Board's responsibilities under the GENIUS Act.
- A Board-supervised PPSI must maintain reserve assets that at all times have a total fair value that equals or exceeds the outstanding issuance value.
- The subsequent discussion is mostly qualitative in nature.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Mode:** Under-calibrated capital
  
  **Trigger:** The selected operational-risk and reserve-asset capital parameters do not capture emerging loss patterns.
  
  **Why It Matters:** Issuers could appear compliant while retaining insufficient loss-absorbing capacity.
  
  **Comment Hook:** Request sensitivity analysis and a recalibration commitment tied to actual loss data.
- **Mode:** Over-concentration or monetization friction
  
  **Trigger:** Principles-based reserve diversification is applied inconsistently or fails under stress.
  
  **Why It Matters:** A one-to-one balance may not translate into timely par redemption if reserves cannot be monetized or are operationally trapped.
  
  **Comment Hook:** Ask for concentration examples, stress scenarios, and minimum liquidity-management evidence.
- **Mode:** Inconsistent supervisory discretion
  
  **Trigger:** Terms such as public interest, unusual and exigent circumstances, sufficient diversification, or reasonably prudent methods are interpreted differently.
  
  **Why It Matters:** Comparable issuers may face different burdens and users may receive less predictable protection.
  
  **Comment Hook:** Request published criteria, examples, and review timelines.
- **Mode:** Entry-cost concentration
  
  **Trigger:** Audit, custody, reporting, capital, technology, and compliance costs are larger than the benefits for smaller entrants.
  
  **Why It Matters:** The final rule could strengthen incumbent concentration despite the proposal's competition rationale.
  
  **Comment Hook:** Ask for market-structure and pass-through analysis by issuer and bank size.
- **Mode:** Interagency overlap
  
  **Trigger:** Board, State, Treasury, OCC, FDIC, NCUA, SEC, and CFTC requirements overlap or diverge.
  
  **Why It Matters:** Conflicting requirements could create unnecessary burden or gaps in responsibility.
  
  **Comment Hook:** Request an interagency crosswalk and conflict-resolution process.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Positive Incentives**

- Monthly disclosures, audits, certifications, and capital requirements incentivize reliable records and loss absorption.
- Regulatory clarity may encourage entry and standardize user expectations.
