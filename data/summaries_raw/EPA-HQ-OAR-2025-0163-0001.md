## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Rescinding the Super Emitter Program is justified by policy direction and new information about expected effectiveness.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The new information materially changes the expected net value of the program and is sufficiently reliable to support rescission.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the evidence shows that the program remains capable of identifying large emissions events or that less costly design changes would preserve its benefits, full rescission may be broader than necessary.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask the EPA to identify the evidence, quantify the expected lost reductions, and compare rescission with narrower revisions.
- **Claim:** Site-level profitability is an appropriate measure of cost reasonableness for marginal wells.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The modeled site-level revenue and operating-cost data represent actual operator decisions and do not obscure costs or benefits borne elsewhere.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Small changes in production decline, severance taxes, commodity prices, plugging obligations, or cost allocation could change whether a modeled well shuts in.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request sensitivity analysis and a transparent explanation of how the model treats uncertainty and multi-well operators.
- **Claim:** The marginal and conventional well-site options provide a better fit than the March 2024 requirements.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The proposed category definitions reliably track compliance burden and emissions differences across the national source population.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A production-based or well-type threshold may group facilities with different equipment, emissions, or access to controls and may create incentives to change classification rather than emissions.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for classification-error analysis, boundary examples, and alternatives that use actual equipment or emissions characteristics.
- **Claim:** The selected control and monitoring options are cost effective for representative facilities.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Representative model plants capture regional, operational, and equipment variation well enough to support national standards.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** High-pressure systems, remote sites, temporary equipment, or facilities without nearby infrastructure may face materially different costs and performance.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request model-plant ranges, regional cases, and evidence from operators and states showing how the options work in practice.
- **Claim:** The RIA's modeled effects provide a useful picture of the proposal's economic and emissions consequences.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Equipment assignment, annual operating costs, production declines, and baseline assumptions are stable enough for the 2027-2050 projection.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If those inputs are correlated with facility size or region, the national totals may mask concentrated impacts or overstate foregone reductions.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask the agency to publish sensitivity tables and distributional results rather than only national aggregates.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The EPA proposes to revise several oil-and-natural-gas NSPS and EG provisions. The package would rescind the Super Emitter Program, revise well-site categories and emissions controls, offer an Appendix K OGI option for certain gas plants, and make technical corrections.

**Affected Entities:** Oil and gas producers, marginal and conventional well operators, gas plants, compressor stations, states and Tribes implementing section 111(d), and communities affected by methane and VOC-control changes.

**Statutory Authority:** The notice relies primarily on Clean Air Act sections 111(b) and 111(d), with related authority for NSPS and emissions guidelines and associated implementation requirements.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal would replace parts of the March 2024 framework with more source-specific requirements and two co-proposed approaches for well-site subcategorization.

**Inference:** This is a broad reconsideration and partial redesign, not a narrow correction, so the final rule needs traceable explanations for each change and for the co-proposal choice.
