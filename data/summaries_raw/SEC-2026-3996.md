  **Underlying Assumption:** Separate account treatment is still necessary for some security and derivative combinations.
  
  **Negation Test:** If the same positions can be safely combined, the burden justification for separate account treatment weakens.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** The claim may not hold across every product pair, especially where the underlying risk and settlement mechanics are closely aligned.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Market participants could read the current separation as a reason to keep the most favorable account form rather than the most risk-appropriate one.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for product-by-product and account-by-account evidence instead of a broad assertion.
- **Claim Id:** claim_4
  
  **Claim:** portfolio margining can provide benefits
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Offset recognition will reduce collateral use without eroding customer protection.
  
  **Negation Test:** If offsets are overestimated, the same mechanism could hide concentrated risk instead of reducing it.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** The benefit may be smaller where positions move on different trading schedules or respond differently to stress.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Firms could treat margin relief as the main objective and underweight the risk of later liquidity spikes.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The notice needs sharper stress-testing and safeguard questions before any expansion choice becomes concrete.

  **Adversarial Interpretation:** A participant could exploit the seams between regimes to choose the least protective treatment for the same economic exposure.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for a regime-by-regime mapping before the agencies widen the program.
