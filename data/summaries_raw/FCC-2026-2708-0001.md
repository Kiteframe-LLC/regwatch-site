## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** claim_2
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A three-year deadline and a minimum of two states plus three providers will reveal whether NG911 interoperability is real rather than nominal.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The test becomes a compliance checkbox that misses the hardest cross-jurisdictional failures or gives a false sense of readiness.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask whether the regime should be phased, risk-tiered, or seeded with a pilot before a national certification expectation is imposed.
- **Claim:** claim_3
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The proposed definition of a successful transfer can capture the technically meaningful elements of interoperability without leaving out edge cases.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the definition is too narrow, providers may optimize for the checklist instead of actual call handling and metadata continuity.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for examples showing how the definition works across different traffic types, network architectures, and emergency conditions.
- **Claim:** claim_4
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A local 911 Authority can identify the right partner states and facilities and can do so in a way that improves, rather than fragments, interoperability.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If jurisdictions choose too few or too easy partners, the testing regime may miss the hardest interoperability scenarios.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask whether the FCC should set a minimum baseline while allowing local customization above that floor.
- **Claim:** claim_6
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Jurisdictions can signal DVC readiness once routing information and network support are available, even though no 911 Authority is currently using DVC to answer calls.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The readiness framework could authorize a feature before staffing, routing, or device-handling workflows are proven.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask what minimum deployment conditions must exist before a jurisdiction can legitimately signal readiness.
- **Claim:** claim_8
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The needed operational standards for DVC-to-911 can be identified through comment and later implemented without major delay or mismatch.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The proposal may outpace the telecommunicator workforce, training pipeline, or PSAP infrastructure needed to handle ASL-based video calls.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for staffing, training, and fallback-answering data before the rule finalizes any DVC workflow.
- **Claim:** claim_9
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The notice can impose or expand information collection without unduly burdening small providers if the burden is further reduced through comment.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Small entities may face disproportionate compliance and recordkeeping costs if the final design is not scaled to provider size.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for a size-stratified burden analysis and a phase-in schedule for the smallest providers.
