## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** Annual consolidated notices are more useful than monthly notices for transparency.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A once-yearly authoritative snapshot is sufficient for applicants, employees, agencies, and the public to identify changes in agency-specific authorities.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** An authority is established, modified, limited, or revoked between annual notices and affected hiring or oversight decisions occur before the next consolidated publication.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** OPM should explain the timing, update, and archival controls that would preserve timely notice and permit reconstruction of the authority's status between annual publications.
- **Claim:** Case-by-case priority consideration can replace priority reemployment lists without diminishing statutory rights.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Priority candidates will know when relevant vacancies exist, be able to identify themselves, and receive consistent evaluation before non-priority selection.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A qualified priority candidate does not learn of a vacancy, cannot keep placement information current, or is evaluated under an agency procedure that is not comparable to another agency's procedure.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The final rule should specify minimum notice, record, evaluation, and review practices, or the record should explain why existing requirements are sufficient.
- **Claim:** The rule will streamline administration and impose only modest implementation costs.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Agencies can translate a ten-part regulatory package into policy, HR-system, training, and documentation changes within an average of 100 hours per agency.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Small or specialized agencies must build new procedures, code new personnel actions, or reconcile legacy lists and pending recruitments in ways not captured by the average.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** OPM should disclose the basis for the average and provide transition guidance for agencies with no existing excepted-service policy.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts
OPM proposes a ten-part conforming and modernization package. It would align schedules E, Policy/Career, and G with recent Executive orders; replace legacy part 302 lists and orders with agency procedures for identifying and considering priority candidates; authorize specified Pathways conversions; and update ALJ appointment and RIF rules. The notice identifies Federal agencies, applicants, employees, preference-eligible veterans, restoration-rights holders, Pathways participants, and ALJs as affected.

### Inference
The practical shift is from formalized, centralized procedural landmarks toward agency-level implementation judgment. The proposal is legally and administratively coherent on its face, but its benefits depend on common documentation, transition, and oversight controls.

### Evidence Quotes
- The proposed rule would replace legacy employment-list and order-of-consideration procedures in part 302 with a more direct framework.
- The annual consolidated notice is a more complete and useful transparency mechanism because it provides a single authoritative listing of agency-specific authorities.
