## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_1
  
  **Claim:** Increased access can coexist with resource protection and local decision-making.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Local officials will have adequate information, staff, funding, and enforceable standards to identify justified restrictions.
  
  **Negation Test:** If local capacity or baseline data are uneven, the presumption may produce inconsistent protection and access outcomes.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** A national access presumption may be unnecessary where existing planning already provides timely, site-specific decisions.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** The access framing could make closures appear exceptional even where environmental or safety conditions are ordinary and recurring.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The EIS should compare access presumptions with alternatives retaining stronger default protections and specify measurable decision criteria.
- **Claim Id:** claim_2
  
  **Claim:** Current procedures create disproportionate delay and burden.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The delay and cost are material, documented, and not offset by environmental or public-participation benefits.
  
  **Negation Test:** If the notice lacks unit-level evidence, the burden claim may be anecdotal or overstated.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** Streamlining only minor revisions might address delay without changing national minimization criteria.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Administrative convenience could become a proxy for reducing scrutiny of consequential route changes.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters should request implementation-time, cost, and outcome data by decision type.
- **Claim Id:** claim_3
  
  **Claim:** Existing routes can be presumed open when restrictions are not justified.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The terms existing, appropriate public use, route, jurisdiction, legal access, and justified restriction will be consistently defined and verified.
  
  **Negation Test:** If physical evidence is mistaken for a legal route or access right, the presumption could generate disputes and unauthorized use.
  
  **Counterfactual Test:** A presumption limited to mapped or legally verified features would reduce uncertainty but provide less access expansion.
  
  **Adversarial Interpretation:** Aerial imagery and other public data could be used to press for access before ownership, jurisdiction, or resource conditions are resolved.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The agency should publish a verification protocol and explain how conflicting evidence is resolved before a route is treated as open.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes
