## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** claim_operational_envelopes_improve_flexibility
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The FCC can define broad operational envelopes that preserve interference, orbital-debris, safety, and enforcement constraints without repeated case-by-case review.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A licensee changes operations within a broad envelope in a way that creates an unanticipated interference, collision-risk, or accountability problem before the Commission can review it.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should request concrete envelope boundaries, trigger conditions, reporting duties, and stop-or-correct mechanisms.
- **Claim Id:** claim_unified_experimental_license_reduces_delay
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A single process and Space Bureau intake will reduce delay without lowering technical review quality or obscuring the distinction between experimental and commercial operations.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** An experimental authorization becomes a de facto commercial pathway, or centralized processing creates a single queue that lacks expertise for unusual systems.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The record should compare processing times, staffing, escalation, public notice, and conversion safeguards under part 5 and part 100 alternatives.
- **Claim Id:** claim_ephemeris_certification_advances_safety
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Private tracking systems can be certified against stable, auditable criteria and will provide data of sufficient quality, availability, and independence for collision-risk reduction.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A certified provider misses an object, suffers an outage, or supplies data that operators cannot reconcile, while responsibility for the error is unclear.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should specify performance standards, redundancy, audit access, incident reporting, and operator responsibility when certified systems fail.
- **Claim Id:** claim_orbital_resources_should_be_reassigned
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The Commission can distinguish abandonment from temporary outage, replacement activity, in-fleet relocation, or a commercially justified transition period.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** An automatic one-year or similar trigger releases resources while a licensee is maintaining continuity, resolving a failure, or completing a replacement that does not fit the proposed bright-line rule.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The FCC should publish a fact-specific transition, notice, objection, and cure process before changing replacement expectancy rules.
- **Claim Id:** claim_us_control_point_improves_coordination
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** A U.S.-based control point can provide meaningful operational coordination and security assurance without duplicating export-control, national-security, or foreign-ownership reviews.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Third-party control arrangements create unclear command authority, technical-data access, or liability when a foreign operator and U.S. control provider disagree or when urgent action is needed.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The record should define control, access, audit, emergency command, export-control, and accountability requirements.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Failure Mode:** Operational envelope overreach
  
  **Mechanism:** Broad authority permits material operational changes without a new application.
  
  **Consequence:** Interference, safety, or accountability problems may be detected only after deployment.
  
  **Mitigation:** Define envelope limits, notification triggers, audit logs, and immediate correction authority.
- **Failure Mode:** Experimental-to-commercial slippage
  
  **Mechanism:** A broad experimental license and conversion pathway can become a low-friction commercial route.
  
  **Consequence:** Public review and technical scrutiny may be weaker than for an ordinary commercial authorization.
  
  **Mitigation:** Use explicit noncommercial limits, conversion criteria, public notice, and independent review.
- **Failure Mode:** Private-provider dependency
  
  **Mechanism:** Operators rely on FCC-certified ephemeris or control providers.
  
  **Consequence:** Outage, error, or provider concentration can create systemic safety and accountability risk.
  
  **Mitigation:** Require redundancy, provider audits, incident reporting, fallback data, and clear operator responsibility.
- **Failure Mode:** Premature resource reassignment
  
  **Mechanism:** Bright-line renewal or replacement expectancy rules release orbital or spectrum resources.
  
  **Consequence:** A temporary failure or replacement transition may be treated as abandonment.
  
  **Mitigation:** Provide notice, cure, objections, temporary protection, and documented case review.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

- Operational envelopes and notification pathways create incentives to request broad authority and may reward applicants that can best document internal controls.
- Processing-round status, secondary markets, and reassignment rules create incentives to hold, transfer, or accelerate use of orbital and spectrum resources.
- Private ephemeris certification creates incentives for tracking providers to meet FCC criteria, but may also create provider concentration and dependence.
- A U.S.-based control point and export-control certifications may shift organizational structures and technical-data access toward U.S.-based providers.
- Small-entity burden reduction may improve entry, but broad simplification can also favor established operators with resources to use flexible authority safely.
