## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim Id:** C2
  
  **Claim:** EPA is creating a new site-specific permitting pathway for several CCR decisions.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Permitting authorities can apply flexible case-by-case judgments without eroding baseline protection.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The pathway becomes a rolling exception rule, and similar facilities receive materially different protections with little public traceability.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for floor criteria, reporting requirements, and a crosswalk showing how each permit decision stays reviewable.
- **Claim Id:** C3
  
  **Claim:** EPA is narrowing the beneficial-use definition and carving out specific uses from federal CCR regulation.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The excluded uses are reliably low-risk across settings and applications.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The same material or end use can present different exposure pathways in different facilities or markets, so a categorical exclusion overreaches.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can press EPA to justify the 12,400-ton threshold and explain why each excluded use belongs outside the federal rule.
- **Claim Id:** C4
  
  **Claim:** EPA is summarizing RIA-based cost savings and benefit changes for the rule.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The RIA captures the important compliance, implementation, and residual-risk effects of the proposal.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Savings look large in the summary but shrink once state workload, monitoring, and distributional effects are fully included.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for a clearer sensitivity analysis and a better mapping between the summary numbers and each proposed amendment.
- **Claim Id:** C7
  
  **Claim:** EPA is keeping some related permit-program issues for a separate later action and is not reopening unrelated issues here.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The split record will still allow the public to evaluate linked decisions coherently.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** EPA proposes targeted CCR amendments, including dewatering-structure exemptions, legacy-unit and CCRMU changes, a site-specific permitting pathway, beneficial-use revisions, and a separate future reopening for the federal CCR permit program.

**Why It Matters:** The proposal could loosen or reroute some existing CCR protections while preserving the overall Subtitle D framework.

**Affected Entities**
- electric utilities and independent power producers
- state permit authorities and environmental agencies
- communities near CCR units and groundwater monitoring networks
- beneficial-use processors and downstream users of CCR-derived materials
- local governments and property owners affected by closure and reuse decisions

**Statutory Authority:** Solid Waste Disposal Act / RCRA / HSWA / WIIN, as cited in Section II.C.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The biggest change is not a single exemption; it is the move from a mostly self-implementing CCR rule toward more permit-driven, site-specific judgments for several core decisions.

**Observed Facts**
- The summary identifies the proposed amendments and the comment deadline.
- The preamble separately discusses costs, benefits, small-entity impacts, federalism, tribal implications, and children’s health.

**Inference:** The proposal is a substantial recasting of the CCR rule, not a narrow technical cleanup.

**Evidence Quotes**

- **Mode:** A categorical beneficial-use exclusion could become too broad for mixed or changing uses.
  
  **Why It Matters:** A use that is low risk in one setting can be higher risk in another.
- **Mode:** Cost savings could be overstated if implementation and monitoring burdens are shifted rather than removed.
  
  **Why It Matters:** The summary numbers may not capture the full compliance picture.
- **Mode:** The separated permit-program action could fragment public review of related CCR issues.
  
  **Why It Matters:** Commenters may need to track multiple dockets to understand one integrated program choice.
- **Mode:** The public record may not show enough of the minimum guardrails for each flexibility choice.
  
  **Why It Matters:** A flexible rule can still be under-justified if the boundaries are opaque.
