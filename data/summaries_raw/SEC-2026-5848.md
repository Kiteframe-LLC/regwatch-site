## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** EDGAR and electronic delivery have substantially displaced the original rationale for paper-era delivery requirements.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Investors can reliably find and use the relevant information electronically, with no material loss from removing delivery or timing safeguards.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Retail, dispersed, or less digitally engaged shareholders may receive less usable information or less time to review it even if the documents remain technically available online.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request evidence on actual investor access and comprehension, not only the existence of EDGAR, and ask whether a narrower transition or delivery safeguard would retain the stated cost savings.
- **Claim:** Removing the 20-business-day incorporated-information deadline will not create a meaningful timing concern because other factors drive proxy delivery.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** State law, transaction timelines, and solicitation needs will continue to keep delivery sufficiently early.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A registrant uses the removed federal minimum in a contested or fast-moving transaction, leaving beneficial owners less time to locate and evaluate incorporated information.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask for transaction-level evidence, a backstop minimum, or a defined disclosure/access requirement where the incorporated material is decision-relevant.
- **Claim:** Five business days is sufficient for brokers, dealers, and banks to conduct proxy-related searches.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Intermediary systems and ownership records can identify and transmit beneficial-owner information accurately within the shortened period across ordinary and complex cases.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Investment-company series, multiple classes, securities lending, or operational exceptions produce incomplete or late searches, reducing voting participation or increasing correction costs.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request operational performance data, error rates, and a separate period or phase-in for populations the notice itself identifies as more complex.
- **Claim:** Eliminating required Notices of Exempt Solicitation will reduce burden without material loss of useful information.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Press releases, public announcements, and other channels adequately substitute for a centralized EDGAR filing record.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Investors and registrants lose a searchable, standardized way to identify campaigns, compare claims, or reconstruct what was publicly communicated before a vote.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Ask the SEC to quantify reliance on PX14A6G filings and compare full repeal with voluntary filing, delayed filing, or an index/notice-only alternative.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The SEC proposes to modernize proxy rules by changing delivery and filing requirements, shortening the broker search period from 20 to five business days, and adding filer contact information.

**Affected Entities:** Public companies, investment companies and BDCs, shareholder groups, investors, brokers, banks, clearing firms, and securities-lending participants.

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites the Securities Act, Exchange Act, Investment Company Act, and Trust Indenture Act, with investor protection, efficiency, competition, and capital formation as relevant factors.

**Key Regulatory Changes**
- Use Form 10-K or EDGAR annual reports instead of mandatory delivery in specified cases, and remove most non-fund performance-graph requirements.
- Remove the 20-business-day incorporated-information delivery deadline.
- Rescind mandatory Notices of Exempt Solicitation.
- Shorten the Rule 14a-13 broker search period to five business days.
- Add representative contact information to Schedule 14A and 14C cover pages.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice estimates $7.7 million in annual benefits and $450,000 in annual costs.
- It identifies 5,357 section 12 companies, 2,720 potentially registered investment companies, and 3,376 historical PX14A6G notices by 311 filers.
- It discusses differentiated periods for investment companies.

**Inference:** The central tradeoff is lower compliance burden versus less investor time, traceability, and reliable beneficial-owner communication.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- Beneficial-owner searches completed in five days may be incomplete in complex investment-company, multi-class, or securities-lending cases.
- Proxy materials incorporating information by reference may reach shareholders too close to a meeting for meaningful review.
- Removal of mandatory exempt-solicitation notices may make campaigns harder to discover, compare, or reconstruct through a centralized source.
- Replacing a readable annual-report delivery practice with Form 10-K availability may reduce practical comprehension for some retail investors.
- New cover-page contact information may improve communication with staff but does not itself create an investor-facing correction or monitoring mechanism.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Evasion:** The proposal could incentivize issuers or intermediaries to use the shortest permissible timing even where additional time would improve investor access.

**Threshold Gaming:** A fixed five-day period may encourage workflows optimized for nominal deadline compliance rather than complete and accurate beneficial-owner identification.

**Strategic Compliance:** Without a searchable notice substitute, campaign participants may shift communications to less standardized channels, increasing information asymmetry between well-resourced participants and dispersed investors.

**Mitigations To Request:** Ask for measured error-rate reporting, safe harbors for documented complexity, and a low-burden traceability mechanism for exempt solicitations.
