## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The FCC seeks comment on revising Rural Health Care Program rate-comparability and cost-study methods, supporting lower-cost backup services, creating an eligible-services list, adopting USAC processing metrics, increasing or reallocating the funding cap if demand exceeds it, and eliminating evergreen-contract approval and HCF annual-report requirements.

**Affected Entities**
- rural health care providers and provider consortia
- telecommunications and broadband service providers
- USAC and FCC program administrators
- small telecommunications businesses
- patients and communities relying on rural telehealth
- Universal Service Fund contributors and beneficiaries

**Statutory Authority:** Sections 1, 4(j), 214, 254, and 303(r) of the Communications Act, plus 47 CFR 1.3, as stated in the notice.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The principal proposed rule text would make services on a future eligible-services list eligible for support, require secondary-service cost and bandwidth to reflect secondary use and be cost-effective, authorize list revision by Public Notice, reserve § 54.618, and remove the HCF annual-report requirement.

**Core Tradeoff:** The proposal trades some prescriptive review and reporting for lower administrative burden and more flexible program access, while relying on future listmaking, cost-comparison methods, and performance metrics to protect program funds.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice expressly asks for comments and alternatives on nearly every major policy choice.
- The notice identifies the 30-percent speed comparison rule, cost studies, the 65-percent HCF program share in funding year 2024, and the proposed § 54.634 eligible-services-list mechanism.

**Inference:** The proposal is primarily a process and methodology refresh, not a single technical mandate; its final effects will depend heavily on future FCC and USAC implementation decisions.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- A broad comparable-service universe could produce upward-biased rural rates if providers select favorable observations or adjustments are poorly calibrated.
- An eligible-services list could become outdated or exclude innovative services during a filing window.
- Removing annual reports or evergreen approval could reduce visibility into program outcomes, contract changes, or service use.
- Performance metrics could prioritize speed over accuracy or equitable review of complex rural applications.
- The funding-cap prioritization choices could shift support toward administratively easy applications rather than highest-need providers.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Incentives**
- Providers may have incentives to submit comparable rates that maximize the derived rural rate unless the data universe and selection rules are comprehensive.
- Applicants may favor services likely to appear on the eligible-services list, while service innovation outside the list may be delayed.
- Reduced reporting may lower administrative burden but also reduce incentives to document outcomes and changing needs.
- USAC may have incentives to meet processing metrics by simplifying difficult cases or deferring quality review unless metrics include accuracy and equity.

**Inference:** The proposal's cost-effectiveness and anti-fraud goals are compatible with these risks, but the safeguards need operational detail.

## Step 13 — Litigation Vulnerabilities

**Issues**
- A final rate methodology could face reasoned-explanation concerns if the Commission selects a new speed or geographic rule without explaining the supporting data and adjustment mechanics.
- A final removal of reporting or approval safeguards could be challenged as inadequately explained if the record does not address oversight consequences and alternatives.
- Future Public Notices may create implementation uncertainty if the eligible-services list materially determines eligibility without clear standards and review procedures.

**Caution:** These are comment-useful administrative-record concerns, not predictions of litigation outcome.

## Step 13A — Structural Integrity / Nonsense Detector

**Overall Assessment:** The proposal is structurally coherent but has a governance feedback-loop weakness: it reduces front-end burdens while deferring several substantive controls to future listmaking, rate adjustments, and performance metrics. The most constructive improvement is to specify data, auditability, correction, and outcome safeguards alongside simplification.

**Observed Fact:** The proposed § 54.634 gives the Wireline Competition Bureau authority to issue and revise an eligible-services list by Public Notice, while the proposed text also removes § 54.618 from the rules.

**Inference:** The final design could remain workable if future administrative mechanisms are transparent and reviewable; the present notice does not fully specify those mechanisms.
