## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** A three-year delay is the right amount of lead time for heavy vehicles.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Heavy-vehicle validation and re-certification really need until September 1, 2030 across the full product mix.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Some vehicle families or manufacturers could comply by 2027 or 2028, which would make the blanket three-year delay overbroad.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for the intermediate reasoning that links the burden narrative to the exact date chosen.
- **Claim:** Heavy vehicles are meaningfully different from light vehicles for purposes of this compliance schedule.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Longer-lived platforms and more variable production runs make the heavy-vehicle transition harder to compress.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the affected heavy-vehicle set is internally diverse, a one-size-fits-all date may be too blunt.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The agency may need class- or configuration-specific analysis rather than a broad category-level delay.
- **Claim:** Excluding heavy vehicles would reduce safety.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The updated test procedures materially improve anchorage performance and should not be deferred indefinitely.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the safety gain is modest or already achievable through voluntary early compliance, exclusion may be less harmful than the notice suggests.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The safety tradeoff should be quantified, not just stated in conclusory form.
- **Claim:** The figure corrections are technical only.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Readers will rely on the incorporated drawings rather than the mistaken illustration dimensions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the figure labels affect interpretation, the correction may matter more than the notice implies.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask whether the correction has any downstream compliance effect.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory action:** NHTSA proposes to extend the compliance date for vehicles with a GVWR greater than 4,536 kg from September 1, 2027 to September 1, 2030, while leaving the lighter-vehicle dates unchanged and preserving optional early compliance.

**Affected entities:** Heavy-vehicle manufacturers, multistage manufacturers, alterers, and other entities that certify to FMVSS No. 210; commenters focused on vehicle safety, manufacturing lead times, and test procedure implementation.

**Statutory and procedural authority:** The notice cites FMVSS authority and NHTSA's reconsideration process under 49 CFR 553.35.

**Key regulatory change:** The proposal keeps the updated body-block/FAD test procedures but gives heavy vehicles three more years before the new date becomes mandatory.

**Observed facts:** The 2024 final rule had already clarified body-block positioning, added an optional FAD, and set the original September 1, 2027 compliance date.

**Inference:** The agency is trying to preserve the safety revision while softening near-term compliance pressure on heavy-vehicle manufacturers.
