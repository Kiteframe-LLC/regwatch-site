## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim A

Claim: Unpublished studies on these 16 chemicals are needed to inform TSCA section 6 prioritization, risk evaluation, and risk management.

Underlying assumption: The unpublished studies likely contain materially useful information not already available to EPA through existing submissions or public literature.

Failure scenario: If many responsive studies were already submitted under other TSCA authorities or are of limited relevance to current section 6 decisions, the burden may exceed the information gain.

Procedural implication: EPA's rationale is strongest if it can show the likely marginal value of the information for each chemical group.

### Claim B

Claim: Reporting should cover impurities, articles, mixtures, and any grade or purity level when the listed chemical is specifically identified.

Underlying assumption: Risk-relevant information can arise from these pathways often enough that broad coverage is justified.

Failure scenario: Firms may face broad search obligations for trace-presence contexts that yield little useful data, especially where the chemical is incidental.

Procedural implication: Comments can test whether narrower carveouts or materiality thresholds would preserve useful data while reducing burden.

### Claim C

Claim: Requiring OHT formatting only for CBI-claimed health and environmental effects information is the right balance.

Underlying assumption: Limiting mandatory structured templates to CBI submissions meaningfully reduces burden without materially harming EPA's ability to use non-CBI studies.

Failure scenario: EPA may receive non-CBI studies in inconsistent formats that are harder to compare, summarize, or publish.

Procedural implication: Commenters can ask whether broader structured reporting would improve data usability enough to justify the extra burden.

### Claim D

Claim: The rule will not significantly harm a substantial number of small entities.

Underlying assumption: The economic analysis reasonably estimates the affected universe, reporting rates, and firm revenues.

Failure scenario: If the affected population or study counts are understated, burden could be concentrated more heavily on smaller firms than EPA assumes.

Procedural implication: The sensitivity analysis matters, and commenters can ask for more transparency on low-end and high-end burden cases.

### Claim E

Claim: Including 6PPD-quinone is justified by the section 21 petition and potential aquatic harm evidence.

Underlying assumption: The petition and supporting materials are sufficient to show serious enough information need despite the chemical not appearing in standard CDR data the same way as other listed substances.

Failure scenario: If production pathways or responsive study inventories are highly uncertain, burden estimates and selection logic may be thinner for this chemical than for the other 15.

Procedural implication: EPA should explain the evidence basis and expected respondent universe for 6PPD-quinone clearly.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

- EPA finalizes a TSCA section 8(d) reporting rule for 16 chemical substances.
- Regulated parties are manufacturers, including importers, of the listed chemicals, including firms that manufactured or proposed to manufacture them within the prior 10 years and firms that manufacture after listing.
- The rule requires copies and lists of specified unpublished health and safety studies, including some ongoing and later-completed studies.
- The general reporting deadline in the preamble is March 13, 2025, with additional deadlines for studies initiated or completed after listing.
- EPA explicitly includes reporting where the chemical appears as an impurity and requires reporting for any chemical grade or purity level, so long as the listed chemical is specifically identified in the study.
- EPA requires OHT format for certain submissions with CBI claims where OECD templates exist, but not for non-CBI studies.
- EPA says the first-year cost is about `$5.884 million` with `70,630` burden hours, and that `35` small businesses are affected, with `2` estimated above the `1% of revenues` threshold.

### Inference

This is an information-forcing rule. EPA is not directly restricting manufacture or use here; it is compelling the submission of unpublished studies so the agency can improve later section 6 decisions. The main practical stakes are the breadth of the study-search obligation, the treatment of impurities and mixtures, the burden of CBI-compliant submissions, and how much useful risk information the broad reporting sweep is likely to uncover.

- `EPA is requiring reporting on any chemical grade/purity level`

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Observed Fact:** The rule consistently ties requested studies to health effects, environmental effects, exposure, degradation, and physical-chemical properties relevant to TSCA section 6 work.

**Observed Fact:** EPA limits reporting to studies in which the listed chemical is specifically identified, which avoids an otherwise broader universe of loosely related mixture studies.

**Observed Fact:** EPA simultaneously broadens coverage through impurity, article, mixture, and any-purity reporting.

**Inference:** The technical design is coherent in the sense that EPA is trying to maximize capture of risk-relevant unpublished studies while avoiding some false positives. The tension is calibration, not internal contradiction.

**Potential inconsistency:** The visible record explains why EPA wants broad study access more clearly than why every covered manufacturing context is likely to generate proportionally useful information.

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

**Observed Fact:** EPA grounds the rule in TSCA section 8(d), codified at `15 U.S.C. 2607(d)`, and in the existing part 716 Health and Safety Data Reporting framework.

**Observed Fact:** EPA relies on section 8(d) to compel both copies of existing studies and lists of studies not yet complete or not in the submitter's possession.

**Observed Fact:** The rule also incorporates CBI handling through part 703 and the OHT requirement for certain CBI-claimed submissions.

**Inference:** The central legal footing is straightforward. The more contestable issues are not whether EPA can use section 8(d), but how broadly it can frame the reporting universe and whether its explanation is sufficient for the breadth it chose.

**Comment leverage:** Future commenters can press EPA on explanatory sufficiency and tailoring rather than on bare authority.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

### Failure Mode 1

If firms interpret impurity and any-purity coverage very broadly, the rule may generate expensive searches with limited useful yield.

Procedural implication: EPA may need clarifying guidance or later narrowing if implementation proves noisy.

### Failure Mode 2

If non-CBI submissions arrive in inconsistent formats, EPA may face data-management and comparability problems even while lowering submitter burden.

Procedural implication: The chosen compromise on OHT scope may shift cost from submitters to EPA.

### Failure Mode 3

If the expected number of reporting firms or studies is understated, small-entity and burden findings may be overly optimistic.

Procedural implication: Sensitivity analysis helps, but real-world monitoring would matter.

### Failure Mode 4

If 6PPD-quinone inventories are less predictable than for the other chemicals, respondent identification and burden forecasts may be less reliable for that chemical.

Procedural implication: EPA should keep the record clear on why that one chemical was added and how respondents were estimated.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Observed Fact:** The rule pushes firms to inventory unpublished studies, determine whether listed chemicals are specifically identified, and decide whether to assert CBI claims.

**Observed Fact:** OHT formatting is mandatory for covered CBI studies where templates exist, but optional for non-CBI studies.

**Inference:** Firms have incentives to minimize burden by narrowing what they consider responsive, avoiding unnecessary robust summaries, and possibly avoiding CBI claims where possible to escape extra formatting work.

**Inference:** EPA's design also creates an incentive for firms to keep better internal study records if they manufacture listed chemicals in multiple contexts.
