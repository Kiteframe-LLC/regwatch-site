## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** A two-year initial deferral will let funds build portfolios and operations without materially undermining investor expectations.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Prospectus and fundamental-policy disclosures will make the longer wait sufficiently clear, and investors who need earlier liquidity will select other products.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Retail investors enter a fund without appreciating that a rules-based repurchase opportunity may not begin for two years, or an unexpected liquidity need arises before the first offer.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request evidence on investor understanding and consider a disclosure, transition, or maximum-deferral backstop tied to the fund's strategy.
- **Claim:** Monthly intervals can expand investor choice while remaining operationally manageable.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Funds can process frequent notices, NAV calculations, tenders, pro rata allocations, and liquidity decisions without increasing error or cross-class inequity.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Monthly requests cluster during market stress and the fund's operational or liquidity systems cannot meet the promised schedule without distressed sales or inconsistent treatment.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request error-rate, staffing, notification, and stress-period evidence, and ask whether monthly funds need distinct minimums or safeguards.
- **Claim:** A principles-based liquidity requirement is more effective than holding liquid assets equal to 100 percent of the repurchase offer amount.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Boards, advisers, and compliance systems can identify and manage liquidity risk consistently even when asset values, cash flows, and repurchase demand change rapidly.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** A fund holds less immediately liquid capacity, repurchase demand exceeds expectations, and the fund must sell illiquid assets at a significant deviation from value or defer practical liquidity.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request measurable liquidity metrics, stress scenarios, escalation triggers, board reporting, and a correction path for failure to satisfy repurchase obligations.
- **Claim:** Standardized multiple-share-class rules can replace case-by-case exemptive orders without reducing investor protection.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Board plans, disinterested-director approval, equal repurchase treatment, Form N-2 disclosures, and rule 12b-1-style conditions address conflicts across classes.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Different fee, distribution, liquidity, or NAV experiences create conflicts that a generic plan and disclosure do not surface before investors commit capital.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Request comparative evidence from existing exemptive orders and ask how the Commission will monitor class-level expense allocation and equal treatment after rescission.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** The SEC proposes more flexible interval-fund repurchase timing and liquidity management, including a two-year initial deferral, monthly intervals, annual discretionary repurchases, deferred sales-load deductions, and a principles-based liquidity standard. It would also permit qualifying regulated closed-end funds and BDCs to use multiple share classes under board plans, equal-treatment rules, and enhanced Form N-2, Form N-CEN, and shareholder-report disclosures.

**Affected Entities:** Interval and other regulated closed-end funds, BDCs, advisers, boards, distributors, current and prospective shareholders, private-market portfolio companies, and fund service providers.

**Core Tradeoff:** The proposal may reduce cash drag and exemptive-order costs, but the record needs more evidence that delayed startup liquidity and a less prescriptive liquidity floor will protect investors during oversubscription and market stress.

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

- **Liquidity standard:** The proposal replaces a 100-percent liquid-asset floor with a principles-based duty to meet repurchase requests without selling at a significant deviation from value. This could reduce cash drag, but commenters can request objective metrics, stress tests, board escalation, and a correction path. **Worth a comment:** high. **Comment posture:** evidence request.
- **Two-year startup deferral:** The proposal gives new interval funds more time to build longer-duration portfolios before the first repurchase offer. Commenters can request staged alternatives, stronger front-cover disclosure, and transition protections because investors may be unable to tender at NAV during the startup period. **Worth a comment:** high. **Comment posture:** alternatives push.
