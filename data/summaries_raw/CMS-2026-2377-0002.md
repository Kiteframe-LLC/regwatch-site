## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

  **Underlying Assumption:** A uniform indirect-PE allocation rule will better reflect resource use across services than the current triplet/non-triplet split.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If the shift redistributes costs in ways that do not track actual practice patterns, some specialties could see payment changes that are formulaically cleaner but operationally less accurate.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask CMS to show code-level or specialty-level impact estimates and to explain why narrower fixes would not work.
- **Claim Id:** claim_low_volume_specialties
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Claims data and commenter-submitted crosswalks are sufficient to assign low-volume specialties without introducing large distortions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** If small-sample claims patterns are noisy, a crosswalk may overreact to sparse data and misstate resource inputs for some codes.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask for the underlying claims distributions and a sensitivity analysis for the proposed specialty overrides.

  **Underlying Assumption:** The reporting repository and the 2027 start date are operationally feasible for covered entities.

  **Procedural Implication:** Commenters can ask CMS to provide implementation milestones, testing, and fallback procedures.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

**Regulatory Action:** CMS proposes the CY 2027 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule update and several related Medicare payment, reporting, and interoperability changes.

**Affected Entities**
- physicians and other Medicare PFS billers
- specialties affected by low-volume code crosswalks and PE valuation changes
- 340B covered entities and other providers subject to Part D repository reporting
- ACO participants and Shared Savings Program organizations
- clinicians and practices subject to QPP and Medicare Promoting Interoperability reporting changes

**Statutory Authority**
- Social Security Act section 1848 for PFS payments
- Social Security Act sections 1847A(i) and 1860D-14B for drug inflation rebate programs
- Social Security Act section 1899 for the Shared Savings Program
- related Medicare payment and reporting authorities cited throughout the preamble

**Key Regulatory Changes**
- A technical change to indirect practice-expense allocation would apply the same methodology across services, with exceptions for 010- and 090-day global codes.
- CMS proposes additional expected specialty assignments for low-volume HCPCS/CPT services based on claims data and commenter input.
- The rule proposes new Part D 340B repository reporting for covered entities beginning in 2027 and related inflation-rebate adjustments.
- CMS proposes Shared Savings Program CEHRT-use options and public-reporting changes, plus other QPP reporting and data-submission changes.
- The rule also includes RFIs on CPT-related issues and duplicate laboratory testing, imaging, result sharing, and interoperability.
