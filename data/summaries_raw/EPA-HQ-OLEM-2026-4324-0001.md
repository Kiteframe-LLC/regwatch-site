## Step 4 — Analysis Obligations Review

**Adequate data:** Largely satisfied by the application, TSD, and regulatory-text appendix. **Transparent methodology:** Partly satisfied; the crosswalk is extensive, but material differences should be easy to trace to an outcome-level conclusion. **Alternatives:** The governing statute frames a program-approval determination rather than a broad policy menu, so the central alternative is approval with defined conditions or nonapproval of inadequately supported elements. **Impacts:** The notice addresses landfill protections and oversight; commenters can request clearer treatment of implementation and community-facing accountability.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

**Alternatives**
- Approve only provisions for which EPA publishes an outcome-focused equivalence explanation.
- Condition approval on standardized public permit findings that identify applicable state and remaining federal requirements.
- Require a public crosswalk update when Wisconsin changes a relied-on provision or EPA revises the comparable federal criterion.
- Use targeted partial approval rather than treating a broad group of provisions as equivalent without separate explanation.

**Comment Use:** These options preserve the state-program pathway while making the approved scope and protectiveness determination easier to audit.

## Step 6 — Technical Coherence

**Observed Fact:** The record compares federal CCR landfill criteria with Wisconsin statutes and administrative-code provisions, including locational criteria, floodplains, endangered species, surface water, permitting, monitoring, closure, and enforcement. **Inference:** The program-level theory is coherent: a state permit system can substitute for corresponding federal requirements when it remains at least as protective. The main technical-review question is whether the record explains outcome equivalence where wording, terminology, or process differs.

## Step 7 — Legal Grounding

**Observed Fact:** EPA cites RCRA sections 4005(d) and 7004(b)(1). The notice explains that a state program may operate in lieu of federal requirements after EPA determines it is at least as protective, and that public participation is required. **Inference:** The visible issue is not a clear absence of statutory authority; it is whether the administrative record sufficiently explains the protectiveness determination and the scope of substitution.

## Step 8 — Procedural Normalcy

The notice provides a 60-day comment period and an online hearing, identifies the supporting technical materials, and describes continuing EPA oversight. This appears procedurally ordinary for a proposed state-program approval. The practical concern is record usability: the application and appendix are detailed enough that commenters may need clear navigation to test individual equivalence conclusions.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

**Failure Modes**
- A state-federal difference is treated as equivalent without showing how the same protective outcome follows.
- The partial-program boundary is unclear in a permit or compliance context, leading to mistaken reliance on state requirements where federal requirements remain effective.
- Future amendments erode a provision relied on for approval without prompt public explanation or EPA review.
- Public participation rights are technically present but hard to use because material comparisons are difficult to locate.

## Step 10 — Regulatory Incentives

**Incentives**
- Wisconsin has an incentive to administer landfill permitting through its existing solid-waste system.
- EPA has an incentive to recognize an eligible state program while retaining oversight authority.
- Regulated facilities benefit from a clear single permitting pathway; communities benefit when that pathway preserves visible enforceable safeguards.

## Step 11 — Evidentiary Strength

The TSD and regulatory-text appendix provide a substantial analytical spine. Strength is highest where the record quotes or maps a specific Wisconsin provision to a federal criterion. Strength is lower for a public reader where the explanation depends on dispersed cross-references or on future permit implementation.

## Step 12 — Administrative Record Gaps

**Gaps**
- A short, topic-based index of material differences and EPA's outcome-level rationale would improve reviewability.
- The record should identify how the public can determine, for a CCR landfill, which federal provisions remain outside the approved partial program.
- The available record does not establish how changes to Wisconsin rules will be tracked against EPA's approval basis between periodic reviews.

## Step 13 — Litigation Vulnerabilities

**Issues**
- A challenger could focus on a material state-federal difference if EPA's at-least-as-protective explanation is conclusory rather than outcome-based.
- Ambiguity about the partial-program boundary could invite disputes over enforceable requirements.
- The public-participation record is stronger if EPA makes the key crosswalk conclusions readily traceable rather than relying only on volume of underlying material.

## Step 13A — Structural Integrity / Nonsense Detector

**Why:** The proposal uses the statutory state-program mechanism and a substantial docketed comparison. Its structural vulnerability is modest but real: a technically large crosswalk is not itself a clear explanation of which differences matter, why they remain protective, and how the partial boundary will operate.
