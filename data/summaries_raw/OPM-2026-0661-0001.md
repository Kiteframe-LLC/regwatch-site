## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

- **Claim:** More, fewer, or different plan options could help manage premium growth.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Plan-option design materially affects carrier competition, enrollment choices, utilization, or government contributions.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** The main drivers of premium growth may be medical prices, provider concentration, benefit design, or risk selection that option counts do not address.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The record should distinguish an option-count effect from broader premium drivers and identify measurable outcomes.
- **Claim:** More choice could improve value for enrollees.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Enrollees can compare premiums, deductibles, networks, and expected out-of-pocket costs well enough to make additional choice useful.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** More options could increase confusion, cause plan switching based on premium alone, or steer people toward high-deductible coverage without adequate understanding of cost sharing.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** OPM should seek evidence about decision quality, not only the number of plans selected.
- **Claim:** Additional HDHPs with HSAs could reduce costs for enrollees or the government.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** Tax treatment and lower premiums would outweigh higher deductibles and would be usable by the affected population.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** People with lower liquidity, predictable treatment needs, or limited HSA access could face higher near-term burdens even if nominal premiums fall.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** The agency should seek subgroup-specific evidence on deductibles, HSA use, care timing, and government contributions.
- **Claim:** Existing comparison, payroll, and human-resources systems can support a changed option portfolio.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The systems can represent, explain, enroll, and administer additional or differently structured options without material errors.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** More plan variation could produce enrollment mistakes, comparison-tool bias, payroll defects, or increased support burdens.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** Operational evidence should include system testing, training needs, implementation cost, and error rates.
- **Claim:** The 2023 FEBS value result identifies a tractable plan-option problem.
  
  **Underlying Assumption:** The value rating is linked to the number or type of plan options rather than premiums, networks, claims experience, or other program features.
  
  **Failure Scenario:** Changing option counts may not improve the value gap and could worsen it if comparison burdens rise.
  
  **Procedural Implication:** OPM should disclose the survey instrument, subgroup results, and alternative explanations for the value rating.

## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- **Mode:** Choice expansion without usable decision support
  
  **Mechanism:** More plans increase comparison burden and lead enrollees to select on premium alone.
  
  **Affected Groups:** Employees, annuitants, and family members with limited time, health literacy, or liquidity.
  
  **Mitigation Question:** What standardized comparison, counseling, and cost-sharing displays would be required before adding options?
- **Mode:** Premium reduction through cost shifting
  
  **Mechanism:** Lower premiums or additional HDHPs shift expenses to deductibles, coinsurance, HSA funding, or delayed care.
  
  **Affected Groups:** People with chronic conditions, lower savings, predictable treatment needs, or limited HSA access.
  
  **Mitigation Question:** What subgroup affordability and care-use measures would OPM monitor?
- **Mode:** Carrier selection and adverse-selection effects
  
  **Mechanism:** Additional options change risk pools or encourage selection into plan types in ways that raise premiums or destabilize offerings.
  
  **Affected Groups:** Carriers and enrollees who remain in affected plans.
  
  **Mitigation Question:** What enrollment and risk-adjustment evidence would show whether additional options improve or worsen pool stability?
- **Mode:** Operational error and fragmented administration
  
  **Mechanism:** More options create errors in comparison tools, enrollment, payroll, training, or carrier support.
  
  **Affected Groups:** Agency HR offices, payroll staff, carriers, and enrollees during open season.
  
  **Mitigation Question:** What system testing, error thresholds, and correction processes would apply?

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

- **Id:** brief_general
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Worth A Comment:** yes
  
  **Why:** The inquiry affects a large federal and postal health-benefits population, and the central causal and distributional questions remain open.
  
  **Minimum Useful Comment Package:** Submit a short comment asking for the baseline data, FEBS methods, subgroup affordability analysis, and comparison of option expansion with disclosure or pilot alternatives.
- **Id:** brief_enrollee
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Worth A Comment:** yes
  
  **Why:** Enrollees can provide practical evidence about comparison burden, premium-versus-deductible tradeoffs, HSA access, care delays, and regional plan availability.
  
  **Minimum Useful Comment Package:** Describe a concrete enrollment or care-use experience, identify the relevant plan features, and ask OPM to measure the same effect across affected groups.

  **Priority:** medium
  
  **Worth A Comment:** yes
  
  **Why:** Carriers and agency benefits staff can supply the operational and market evidence that is missing from the visible record.
