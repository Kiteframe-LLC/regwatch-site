## Step 9 — Failure Modes

- Proprietary-data skew: large issuers, consultants, and State agencies may be better positioned than smaller commenters to answer the questions.
- Method drift: without a decision framework, CMS could end up mixing benchmark, AV, and survey logic without explaining why.
- Transition uncertainty: if CMS later changes EHB rules quickly, issuers and States may face compliance churn.
- State variation ambiguity: the record may identify differences among States without clarifying which differences matter most.

- The absence of explicit evidence thresholds may reward the best-resourced commenters rather than the most representative ones.
