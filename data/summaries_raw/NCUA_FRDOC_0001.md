## Step 1 — Claim Stress Test

### Claim

The proposal simplifies the regulations "without altering the compliance obligations" of federally insured credit unions.

**Underlying assumption**

The repealed provisions are truly redundant cross-references, except for the limited text being relocated into parts 701 and 713.

**Failure scenario**

If some regulated readers currently rely on part 741 as the main map for determining which off-part requirements apply, deleting those provisions could reduce clarity even if the underlying substantive duties technically remain elsewhere.

**Procedural implication**

The "no substantive change" claim may still leave a meaningful navigation and notice question for commenters to probe.

---

### Claim

The benefit of removal outweighs the benefit of the existing cross-referencing function.

**Underlying assumption**

The cross-reference map imposes more burden through length and redundancy than it provides value through centralized readability.

**Failure scenario**

If FISCUs and FCUs actually use part 741 as a compliance checklist, removal could save little regulatory text while making compliance research more fragmented.

**Procedural implication**

The record would benefit from concrete examples, user feedback, or examiner experience supporting the agency's burden-reduction premise.

---

### Claim

Selective transfer of the fidelity-bond termination notice and the state-supervisor consultation text preserves what matters most.

**Underlying assumption**

The Board correctly identified which provisions in part 741 carry independent operative effect and which are safe to repeal outright.

**Failure scenario**

If the agency overlooked similar embedded guidance elsewhere in part 741, repeal could accidentally remove useful interpretive direction or create avoidable uncertainty.

**Procedural implication**

Commenters can ask for a section-by-section crosswalk showing why each deleted section is either purely referential or fully accounted for elsewhere.

---

### Claim

Removal of §§ 741.213 and 741.218 can proceed even though the Board is still asking whether parts 747 and 709 should be clarified.

**Underlying assumption**

The existing provisions are not necessary to preserve clear notice of which part 747 and part 709 provisions apply to FISCUs.

**Failure scenario**

If the cited parts are genuinely ambiguous without these cross-reference provisions, repeal without simultaneous clarification could weaken compliance clarity.

**Procedural implication**

This looks less like an ordinary defect and more like a deliberate solicitation point, but it remains a practical leverage point for comments.

---

### Claim

The rule would not significantly affect a substantial number of small credit unions.

**Underlying assumption**

Removing cross-reference provisions does not materially change compliance cost, search cost, or training burden for smaller institutions.

**Failure scenario**

If small institutions disproportionately rely on centralized regulatory signposts because they have less in-house legal capacity, the net effect on navigation costs could be larger than the RFA certification suggests.

**Procedural implication**

Commenters can ask the Board to explain the factual basis for the certification with more specificity.

## Step 2 — Essence of the Rule

### Observed Facts

* The NPRM would remove and reserve numerous sections in 12 C.F.R. part 741 that currently function mainly as references to substantive requirements codified elsewhere.
* The rule would add a new 12 C.F.R. § 713.3(c) requiring a federally insured credit union whose fidelity bond coverage is terminated to notify the appropriate NCUA regional office in writing at least 35 days before the termination takes effect.
* The rule would add a new 12 C.F.R. § 701.14(c)(4) preserving consultation language for FISCUs, their state supervisors, and the NCUA in change-in-officer reviews.
* The Board explicitly requests feedback on whether parts 747 and 709 should be amended if related cross-reference provisions in part 741 are removed.
* The comment deadline in the notice text is July 6, 2026.

**Evidence quote**

> "The following provisions are straight cross-references to other provisions and do not contain any substantive information that would be helpful to retain. The Board proposes eliminating these outright."

### Inference

This is a deregulatory cleanup rule rather than a programmatic rewrite of share-insurance standards. Its most important practical question is not whether it imposes new obligations, but whether it removes too much navigational structure for credit unions, especially where applicability to FISCUs is not obvious from the destination parts themselves.

## Step 5 — Missing Alternatives Detector

### Alternatives evaluated

* Retain the current cross-reference structure.
* Remove the provisions and potentially place a reference list outside the regulations.
* Remove §§ 741.213 and 741.218 while asking whether clarifying amendments to parts 747 and 709 are still needed.

### Plausible alternatives not analyzed in depth

1. Retain a short appendix or centralized crosswalk in part 741 instead of repealing the provisions entirely.
2. Remove only those sections that are unquestionably redundant, while keeping provisions that help identify FISCU applicability.
3. Adopt simultaneous conforming amendments to parts 709 and 747 rather than deferring that question to commenters.
4. Publish a formal section-by-section crosswalk in the docket explaining where each removed obligation now appears.

### Evidence suggesting feasibility

The preamble itself contemplates a non-regulatory reference list and explicitly recognizes that clarifying amendments to other parts may be useful. That suggests narrower or hybrid cleanup options were available.

## Step 17 — High-Leverage Comment Angles

### 1. Preserve navigability, not duplication

Ask the Board to distinguish between unnecessary duplication and useful compliance mapping. A concise appendix or crosswalk could preserve notice value while still shortening the CFR.

### 3. Strengthen the RFA record

Ask the Board to explain why search and training costs are immaterial for small credit unions and whether smaller institutions rely more heavily on centralized cross-reference provisions.

If the Board plans a non-regulatory reference list, commenters can ask where it will live, how it will be updated, and whether it will be authoritative enough to prevent confusion.

## Step 18 — Decision Brief

### Key NPRM Sentence

> "The intended effect is to simplify the regulatory text and make it easier to navigate without altering the compliance obligations of federally insured credit unions."

### Routine Description

The NPRM is a cleanup proposal that removes mostly cross-reference-heavy insurance provisions from part 741, relocates a small amount of operative text, and asks whether some destination parts need clarifying edits.

### Key Leverage Point

The best comment leverage is not opposition to simplification in the abstract. It is demanding a clearer public showing that repeal will preserve navigability and will not leave FISCUs guessing about applicability in parts 709 and 747.

### Real-World Consequence

If finalized without a strong crosswalk or conforming clarifications, the rule could reduce regulatory clutter while also making compliance research more fragmented for credit unions that currently use part 741 as a roadmap.

### Worth a Comment?

**Moderate value**

Reason:

* the rule is modest
* but it affects how regulated institutions find and interpret their obligations
* targeted comments could realistically improve clarity without resisting the overall cleanup goal
