# Public Summary: EPA-R09-OAR-2026-2608-0001

_Source file: `summaries/EPA-R09-OAR-2026-2608-0001.md`_
_Detected type: `NPRM`_

## What This Document Does
- EPA proposes two linked findings for the Portola nonattainment area in Plumas County, California.
- First, EPA proposes to determine that the area attained the 2012 annual PM2.5 standard by the December 31, 2025 Serious-area attainment date.
- Second, EPA proposes a clean data determination based on 2023-2025 monitoring data.
- If EPA finalizes the clean data determination, some Serious-area planning obligations would be suspended while the area continues to attain the standard.
- The area would still remain formally designated nonattainment unless California later obtains EPA approval of a redesignation request and maintenance plan.
- EPA says the clean data determination would not suspend the emissions-inventory requirement, nonattainment new source review, or BACM/BACT obligations.

## Why It Matters
- This is not a major new control rule. It is a status determination with practical consequences for which planning requirements stay active.
- That matters because clean data determinations can reduce near-term planning burdens even while an area remains officially nonattainment.
- The notice appears legally orthodox. EPA is using a familiar Clean Air Act framework for an attainment-by-deadline finding and a clean data determination.
- The main public-interest question is not whether EPA has authority to do this. The better question is whether EPA has explained the current attainment showing clearly enough, and whether the public can judge how durable that improvement is.
- The support materials describe Portola as a winter PM2.5 problem heavily driven by residential wood burning and inversion conditions in a mountain valley. That history does not defeat the proposal, but it does make durability an important question.
- EPA says the 2023-2025 monitor data were complete and certified, and that audit findings did not invalidate the monitoring record. But the public notice, at least in the available text, does not present much concrete current-period numerical detail beyond those conclusions.
- A useful public comment here would likely aim to strengthen the explanation, not to make an overstated claim that the record is fatally defective.

## Who Is Affected
- Portola and nearby residents who are affected by winter PM2.5 conditions.
- California Air Resources Board and the Northern Sierra Air Quality Management District, which remain responsible for monitoring and other remaining nonattainment obligations.
- EPA Region IX, which will decide whether the attainment and clean data findings should be finalized.
- Local stakeholders who may care whether serious-area planning requirements are paused while the area is still formally nonattainment.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. The notice should present the current attainment result more directly
- Why it matters: EPA says the proposal rests on 2023-2025 monitoring data, but the available notice text does not clearly surface the resulting design value or much comparable current-period context.
- Practical question to ask: Please state the actual 2023-2025 annual design value and briefly summarize the key monitoring facts that support the attainment finding.

2. Continued attainment may deserve a clearer durability explanation
- Why it matters: The support materials describe a recurring winter wood-smoke problem shaped by local geography and inversion conditions, so commenters may reasonably ask how stable the recent improvement appears.
- Practical question to ask: Please explain why EPA believes the recent attainment showing is durable enough to support a clean data determination rather than only a bare attainment finding.

3. The practical effect of the clean data determination could be clearer
- Why it matters: The notice explains doctrinally which Serious-area obligations are suspended, but many readers will still want a practical explanation of what remains required and what would restart suspended obligations.
- Practical question to ask: Please provide a plain-language explanation of which obligations remain active after the clean data determination and what monitoring or factual changes would reactivate suspended planning requirements.
