# Public Summary: PHMSA-2025-0109-0008

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** PHMSA proposes to revise the property-damage threshold used to decide when pipeline releases must be reported as incidents or accidents, exclude certain indirect costs from that threshold calculation, align gas and hazardous-liquid/carbon-dioxide threshold treatment, and clarify the relationship between accident reporting and immediate NRC notice.

**Affected Entities**
- Gas pipeline operators
- Hazardous liquid pipeline operators
- Carbon dioxide pipeline operators
- The National Response Center and PHMSA reporting systems
- Members of the public and oversight users who rely on incident and accident data

**Observed Facts**
- The notice retains reportability for fire, explosion, releases of five gallons or more in specified cases, and injuries resulting in death or hospitalization.
- The notice says the proposed changes would apply both to reporting thresholds and to the immediate-notice design for hazardous liquid and carbon dioxide pipelines.

**Inference**
- This is mainly a reporting-structure rule, not a wholesale rewrite of pipeline safety obligations, so the strongest comments should focus on data quality, threshold design, and reporting alignment rather than the general existence of incident reporting.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The other criteria for fire or explosion, a release of five gallons or more ... and injury resulting in death or hospitalization remain reportable regardless of a change in the property damage definition.
- This proposal is consistent with historical practice and avoids unnecessarily complicated reporting procedures.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The rule is coherent, but it depends on trendable incident data, notice alignment, and clear boundary examples to deliver the claimed benefits.
