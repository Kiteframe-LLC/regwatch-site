# Public Summary: USPS-2026-2179-0001

## What This Document Does

**Rule Summary:** The Postal Service proposes to revise the Domestic Mail Manual to discontinue Label 888 for Tracking, Label 889 for Signature Confirmation, Label 891 for insurance up to $500, and Label 892 for insurance over $500. The proposal deletes the legacy label option for some services, directs retail employees to affix a USPS Tracking label, adjusts the text for electronic tracking, and allows existing stock through January 31, 2028.

**Policy Problem:** USPS says the legacy labels are incompatible with updated processing equipment and no longer support operational needs. It proposes IMpb-compliant 4-by-6-inch shipping labels and other provider or retail alternatives.

**Affected Entities**
- Retail customers using USPS-provided tracking, signature, or insurance labels
- Metered customers and postage-meter providers
- Commercial mailers and mailers using Marketing Mail or Heavy Printed Matter parcels
- Post Office employees and facilities being upgraded to RSS or new printing devices
- Recipients who rely on tracking, signature confirmation, or insurance processing

**Key Mechanisms**
- Discontinue USPS printing and distribution of Labels 888, 889, 891, and 892
- Remove Label 888-based tracking and Label 889-based signature-confirmation options from specified DMM provisions
- Shift customers toward IMpb-compliant 4-by-6-inch labels and meter-provider solutions
- Permit existing label stock through January 31, 2028
- Implement the proposed change effective January 17, 2027

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal has a coherent modernization objective, with its main weakness being thin evidence and transition detail for the breadth of the label discontinuation.
