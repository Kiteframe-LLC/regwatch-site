# Public Summary: FINCEN-2026-0298-0001

## What This Document Does

- FinCEN proposes to codify relief from repeating beneficial-owner identification at every new account. Checks would occur at the first account, when facts call prior information into question, and when risk-based ongoing diligence requires them.
- It would specify identifying information in the rule, exclude ITINs as qualifying numbers, permit third-party sources subject to customer certification, and allow oral or written confirmation of previously certified information.
- Institutions could request additional information, including immigration or employment status, when justified by risk indicators or supervisory concerns. This is not a universal immigration-document requirement or a blanket account ban.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: Repeat-account relief has a clear rationale. Broader operative language for status inquiries needs limits, and the no-significant-access-effects prediction lacks subgroup evidence.

## Who Is Affected

Covered banks (including covered credit unions), broker-dealers, mutual funds, futures commission merchants and introducing brokers; their legal entity customers, beneficial owners, and compliance vendors. The notice estimates 14,554 institutions, not 14,554 affected individuals. Stablecoin issuers are addressed in separate proposals.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Bound status-information requests.** The operative text permits useful information for broad supervisory concerns; the preamble describes necessary information and material concerns. That difference could turn status requests into a practical access condition. **Question:** What independent risk nexus, limits on proxy-based screening, and correction process will govern these requests?
- **Measure access and costs separately.** Repeat-check savings do not measure the extra work or delays caused by identifier restrictions and status inquiries. FinCEN requests cost data but predicts no significant access effects. **Question:** What component-level cost ranges and subgroup access scenarios support that prediction under both stated baselines?
- **Compare corroborated ITIN use.** FinCEN explicitly asks about alternatives and lawful owners lacking other documents. Commenters can submit anonymized document-availability cases and measured verification costs. **Question:** How does exclusion compare with ITIN plus independent corroboration on fraud detection, false positives, and customer delay?
- **Make the transition workable.** Differing CIP/CDD identifiers, vendor changes, and oral confirmations require clear instructions. Recordkeeping remains; the question is how to make it reconstructable. **Question:** Will FinCEN specify existing-file treatment, minimum confirmation records, and a phased transition where vendor schedules show it is needed?
