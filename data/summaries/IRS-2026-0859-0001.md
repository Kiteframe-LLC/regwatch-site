# Public Summary: IRS-2026-0859-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The IRS proposes regulations under sections 898(c) and 960(d)(4) that allocate certain foreign taxes between a first required year and a succeeding taxable year, define affected corporations and specified foreign income taxes, create election procedures for edge cases, and implement the 10 percent foreign tax credit disallowance for certain PTEP distributions.

**Affected Entities**
- U.S. shareholders of foreign corporations using the one-month deferral election
- Affected corporations that accrue foreign income taxes
- Taxpayers claiming the foreign tax credit on post-06/28/25 PTEP distributions
- Advisers and controlled domestic shareholders that must make the related elections and notices

**Statutory Authority:** Section 70352(c) of Public Law 119-21, section 960(f), section 7805(a), and the related foreign tax credit provisions.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal turns the statutory transition rule into a detailed allocation regime, adds procedures for CFTEs and relevant succeeding-year taxes, and applies the section 960(d)(4) disallowance rules to post-06/28/25 section 951A PTEP.

**Observed Facts**
- The one-month deferral election was repealed for taxable years beginning after November 30, 2025.
- Section 960(d)(4) disallows 10 percent of certain foreign income taxes connected to post-06/28/25 section 951A PTEP distributions.
- The proposal includes examples showing how allocation percentages and income-group rules work in practice.

**Inference:** The rule is technically coherent, but the most consequential line-drawing choices still depend on discretionary implementation choices that the public record does not fully stress-test.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The proposed regulations would allocate specified foreign income taxes between an affected corporation's first required year and its succeeding taxable year.
- The proposed regulations would provide election procedures for specified distributive shares of CFTEs, relevant succeeding year taxes, income group specific allocation percentages, and an election to not allocate.
