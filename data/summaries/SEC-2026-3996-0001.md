# Public Summary: SEC-2026-3996-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** Joint SEC/CFTC request for comment on possible further implementation of portfolio margining and cross-margining for securities and derivatives.

**Affected Entities**
- customers and market participants using related securities and derivatives positions
- broker-dealers and futures commission merchants
- swap dealers and security-based swap dealers
- clearing agencies and derivatives clearing organizations
- foreign financial institutions that compete with U.S. market intermediaries
- intermediaries that already run limited portfolio margin or cross-margin programs

**Statutory Authority:** The notice discusses existing SEC and CFTC authority over margin, clearing, segregation, insolvency, and related account structures, along with the regime differences that arise under the Exchange Act, the CEA, SIPA, and bankruptcy law.

**Key Regulatory Change:** No immediate rule text changes are proposed; the notice asks what additional products, account types, margin methodologies, safeguards, and competitive effects should matter if the agencies expand current programs.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice inventories current portfolio margin and cross-margin programs that already exist in limited form.
- The notice asks about account types, new asset categories, bankruptcy treatment, liquidity effects, and competition.
- The notice requests empirical data and quantitative analysis from commenters.

**Inference:** This is a solicitation-heavy NPRM-stage record: the agencies are trying to identify the design space before narrowing the policy choice.

**Evidence Quotes**
- further implement portfolio and cross-margining
- distinct approaches to segregation of customer funds, insolvency, and margin
- What challenges do market participants face
- supporting data and analysis

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The notice is coherent as a request for comment, but it leaves the decision rule open for which product and account combinations should qualify for broader portfolio margining.
