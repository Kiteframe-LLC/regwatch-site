# Public Summary: SEC-2026-5190-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The SEC proposes Regulation Crypto Assets: a startup exemption capped at $5 million over four years, a fundraising exemption capped at $75 million per 12 months with enhanced disclosure and reporting, a conditional safe harbor from investment-contract status, and related state-registration preemption.

**Affected Entities**
- Crypto-asset issuers and project sponsors
- Investors, including retail investors and qualified purchasers
- Trading platforms, transfer agents, broker-dealers, and other intermediaries
- State securities administrators
- Auditors, counsel, and service providers supporting covered offerings

**Statutory Authority:** The notice invokes the Securities Act of 1933 and Exchange Act of 1934, including registration and security definitions, and proposes rules under the Commission's exemptive and rulemaking authority.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal would replace case-by-case reliance on existing securities-law frameworks with tailored exemptions and a conditional status safe harbor.

**Observed Facts**
- The proposal keeps antifraud and antimanipulation provisions applicable to exempt offerings.
- The second exemption requires financial statements and ongoing reporting.
- The proposal includes inflation adjustment, bad-actor disqualification, forms, recordkeeping, and a state-preemption provision.

**Inference:** The proposal is a substantial framework change whose practical effects depend on the boundary between covered investment contracts and assets that qualify for the safe harbor.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The first exemption would permit offerings of up to $5 million during a four-year period.
- Issuers that rely on these exemptions would remain subject to the antifraud and antimanipulation provisions of the Federal securities laws.
- If the conditions of that proposed safe harbor are satisfied, then a crypto asset would be deemed not to be subject to an investment contract.
