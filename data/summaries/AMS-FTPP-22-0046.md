## What This Document Does
- Proposes to delay the effective date of USDA AMS's 2025 poultry grower payment systems final rule from July 1, 2026 to December 31, 2027.
- Keeps four already-issued provisions from taking effect on schedule: the rules on broiler grower compensation design, fair operation of ranking systems, disclosures tied to capital-improvement demands, and severability.
- Says the delay is needed so AMS can take more time to consider possible next steps for the underlying rule, including policy and legal issues.
- Uses the same basic analytical framework as the 2025 rule's economic discussion and focuses mainly on recurring administrative costs that would be postponed during the delay.

## Why It Matters
- It would postpone protections that AMS previously adopted after several years of rulemaking about poultry tournament systems and grower complaints.
- The notice strongly emphasizes compliance-cost savings for live poultry dealers (LPDs). It says LPDs would otherwise face about $4.9 million in ongoing administrative costs per year for the first four years after the rule becomes effective, with growers facing about $249,000 in annual quantified administrative costs.
- AMS also admits growers were expected to benefit from the underlying 2025 rule, even though the agency still says it cannot predict those benefits with certainty. That creates an uneven record: quantified savings from delay on one side, but underexplained foregone protections on the other.
- Earlier docket materials say the tournament system can operate "unfairly and deceptively," that growers may have limited control over inputs that affect their pay, and that many growers complained the system was inherently unfair. The delay proposal does not really rebut that fairness case. It mainly says the agency needs more time.
- The biggest record weakness is not a total lack of analysis. It is that the notice does not clearly explain what changed after the 2025 final rule was issued. If the same uncertainty already existed when AMS finalized the rule, commenters can reasonably ask why it now justifies postponement.

## Who Is Affected
- Broiler growers whose expected protections against tournament-based compensation practices and capital-investment pressure would be postponed.
- Live poultry dealers that would receive more time before complying with the 2025 rule.
- Small LPDs in particular, because AMS says 24 broiler-processing LPDs met the SBA small-business standard in fiscal year 2023.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. The notice does not clearly identify what is new since the 2025 final rule.
- Why it matters: AMS previously finalized the rule, but this delay NPRM mostly relies on uncertainty and cost concerns that appear to have already been known at that time.
- Practical question: What new studies, legal analyses, market developments, or operational data emerged after January 16, 2025 that now justify delaying implementation?

2. The record is lopsided on costs versus delayed protections.
- Why it matters: AMS quantifies savings from delay but gives much less detail about the grower protections that would be postponed.
- Practical question: How did AMS compare the value of delayed LPD compliance savings against the expected, but unquantified, benefits to growers from the underlying rule?

3. The 18-month delay is only thinly explained.
- Why it matters: The notice says 12 months may not be enough, but does not tie the extra 6 months to concrete review tasks or milestones.
- Practical question: What specific work will AMS complete during months 13 through 18 that it could not complete within a 12-month delay?

4. The agency discusses alternatives, but not many narrower ones.
- Why it matters: AMS compares no delay, 12 months, and 18 months, but does not seriously examine partial implementation or staged compliance.
- Practical question: Did AMS evaluate narrower options that would preserve some grower protections while still allowing additional review, and if not, why not?

5. The notice leaves the "policy and legal issues" mostly undefined.
- Why it matters: Vague references to unresolved issues make it harder for the public to test the agency's reasoning or respond with useful evidence.
- Practical question: What specific legal and policy concerns is AMS referring to, and will it place any memoranda or analyses about those concerns in the docket?

## Evidence Quotes
- “AMS is proposing to delay the effective date of the Poultry Grower Payment Systems and Capital Improvement Systems final rule ... to allow time for further consideration of possible actions that may be taken regarding the disposition of the rule.”
- “At the time of publication, AMS estimated the final rule would result in significant costs to both LPDs and poultry growers with no quantifiable benefits.”
- “AMS acknowledged it could not rule out the possibility of increased compliance costs, fewer growers participating in the market, and/or reduced production efficiencies, all of which could lead to higher consumer prices.”
- “AMS expects that LPDs would incur $4.9 million in ongoing administrative costs for each of the first four years after the Payment Systems rule becomes effective...”
- “AMS expects broiler growers would benefit from the Payment Systems rule, though AMS is unable to predict the size of these benefits with certainty.”
- “Because twelve months may not provide adequate time for the thorough consideration needed, AMS chose the preferred alternative of proposing to delay the effective date by 18 months.”
- From the attached 2025 final rule: “The tournament system can operate unfairly and deceptively.”
