## What This Document Does

- Proposes a broad set of amendments to EPA's coal combustion residuals rules rather than one isolated fix.
- Would exempt some `CCR dewatering structures` from the federal CCR rules.
- Would revise the rules for `legacy CCR surface impoundments` and `CCR management units`.
- Would create a new permitting pathway for `site-specific considerations` on groundwater monitoring points of compliance, corrective-action cleanup levels, closure requirements, closure timeframes, and extraction of CCR for beneficial use during post-closure care.
- Would revise the `beneficial use` framework by removing the environmental-demonstration requirement for certain large non-roadway uses of unencapsulated CCR, defining `CCR storage pile`, and excluding some beneficial uses from federal CCR regulation.
- Separately signals that EPA plans to reopen the comment period for the older federal CCR permitting proposal in another action.

## Why It Matters

- This proposal affects the outer boundary of what counts as regulated CCR disposal activity versus exempt handling or beneficial use.
- That matters because the rule is not just changing paperwork. It could change where monitoring occurs, what cleanup targets apply, how closure is handled, and when material can be extracted after closure.
- EPA appears to be responding to long-running concerns that the current rule can be too rigid or poorly fitted to site conditions. The new proposal tries to add flexibility and carve out some activities from direct federal control.
- The benefit of that approach is obvious: some CCR sites and structures may not present the same risks, and some cleanup or closure decisions may need more site-specific tailoring than a one-size-fits-all rule can provide.
- The risk is also obvious: flexibility and categorical carveouts can weaken consistency if EPA does not explain exactly where the new boundaries are and what minimum protections still apply.
- The available record shows EPA has some analytical support behind the proposal. The export packet includes appended extracts from a Regulatory Impact Analysis and related risk/economic materials, so this is not a bare assertion-only notice.
- But the visible packet is still only a partial window into a very large docket. Commenters can see that EPA has a cost/benefit and risk-analysis spine, yet the proposal-specific logic for several key line-drawing decisions is not fully visible from the materials loaded here.

## Who Is Affected

- Electric utilities and other owners or operators with CCR units, legacy impoundments, or CCR management units.
- State agencies or permit writers that would have to apply site-specific criteria if EPA finalizes the new permitting pathway.
- Communities near CCR disposal or storage areas, especially where monitoring points, cleanup levels, closure design, or closure timing could vary by site.
- Companies involved in handling, storing, processing, or reusing CCR in non-roadway applications.
- Commenters focused on groundwater protection, beneficial-use oversight, long-term closure integrity, environmental justice, and administrative consistency.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- The dewatering-structure exemption may need a sharper boundary.  
Why it matters: if EPA exempts structures that function in practice like longer-term CCR management units, the exemption could become a loophole rather than a clarification.  
Question to ask: What objective criteria distinguish temporary dewatering from regulated CCR disposal or storage?

- Site-specific permitting discretion may be too open-ended.  
Why it matters: the proposal would allow case-by-case decisions on monitoring points, cleanup levels, closure requirements, and closure timeframes, but the available packet only partially shows the limiting principles for those decisions.  
Question to ask: What minimum findings, data showings, and protective floor standards must be met before a permit can depart from default requirements?

- The large-use beneficial-use safeguard may be getting removed too broadly.  
Why it matters: EPA proposes to drop the environmental-demonstration requirement for certain large non-roadway uses of unencapsulated CCR, which could blur the line between beneficial use and disposal-like placement.  
Question to ask: What evidence shows that these very large uses remain protective without a case-specific demonstration?

- The proposal may rely heavily on older legacy-rule analysis without fully showing the fit.  
Why it matters: the loaded support documents include legacy-impoundment RIA and risk materials, but commenters still need to know how that earlier work supports the 2026 amendment package specifically.  
Question to ask: Which prior analyses support each new amendment, and what proposal-specific updates did EPA perform?

- Post-closure extraction needs clearer guardrails.  
Why it matters: extracting CCR for beneficial use during post-closure care could affect the assumptions behind closure and long-term protectiveness.  
Question to ask: What monitoring, engineering, and reclosure conditions would apply before, during, and after post-closure extraction?
