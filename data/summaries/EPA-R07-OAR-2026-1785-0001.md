## What This Document Does

- EPA proposes to approve Missouri revisions to a state implementation plan rule on sulfur dioxide emissions from stationary boilers in the St. Louis area.
- The rule being updated is `10 CSR 10-5.570`, which applies to larger industrial, commercial, and institutional boilers and process heaters in several St. Louis-area counties and the City of St. Louis.
- EPA says the revisions are mostly administrative. The docketed materials describe them as citation cleanup, terminology cleanup, punctuation edits, and removal of duplicate federal references.
- The one change EPA highlights as most significant is replacing a reference to rescinded `10 CSR 10-6.260` with a reference to `10 CSR 10-6.261` in the rule's excess-emissions reporting provision.
- EPA says the revisions are consistent with the Clean Air Act and will not affect the area's ability to attain or maintain air-quality standards because they are administrative in nature.

## Why It Matters

- This looks like a low-drama SIP maintenance action, not a broad policy rewrite. The main sulfur dioxide control structure remains in place.
- The supporting documents back that up. The redline keeps the core emission limit of `1.0 lb SO2 per mmBtu` over a `30-day` period, and the TSD says the edits mostly clean up wording and citations.
- The practical question is narrower: whether EPA has explained clearly enough that the switch from `10 CSR 10-6.260` to `10 CSR 10-6.261` does not quietly change reporting or enforceability.
- EPA's TSD says Missouri rescinded `10 CSR 10-6.260` and replaced it with `10 CSR 10-6.261`, and says the revised boiler rule now points to the replacement sulfur dioxide rule for excess-emissions reporting.
- That may well be correct. But because EPA itself calls this the “most significant revision,” commenters could reasonably ask for a clearer final explanation of why the new cross-reference is functionally equivalent or otherwise preserves adequate enforcement.
- This matters less because the proposal appears dangerous and more because routine administrative approvals still benefit from a record that is easy for outside readers to audit.

## Who Is Affected

- Owners and operators of covered stationary boilers and process heaters in Franklin, Jefferson, St. Charles, St. Louis, and the City of St. Louis.
- Missouri air regulators responsible for administering and submitting SIP revisions.
- EPA Region 7, which is deciding whether the updated state text can be incorporated into the federally enforceable SIP.
- Community groups, local governments, and technical commenters who track whether SIP cleanup actions preserve clear compliance and reporting obligations.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

1. EPA should explain the `10 CSR 10-6.260` to `10 CSR 10-6.261` switch more clearly.
Why it matters: EPA identifies this as the most significant change, yet the public-facing explanation is brief. Commenters should be able to confirm that the reporting obligation still works the same way after the old rule was rescinded.
Practical question a commenter can ask: What is the practical compliance and enforcement difference, if any, between the old cross-reference and the new one used in `10 CSR 10-5.570(4)(A)4.B`?

2. EPA should confirm that removing duplicate `40 CFR part 60` references does not create ambiguity.
Why it matters: EPA says the deleted citations were duplicative, but the NPRM gives only a short explanation of why the remaining text still clearly identifies the applicable monitoring or testing methods.
Practical question a commenter can ask: Which remaining provisions now supply the operative federal method references, and why does EPA conclude that regulated entities and enforcement staff will still have unambiguous direction?
