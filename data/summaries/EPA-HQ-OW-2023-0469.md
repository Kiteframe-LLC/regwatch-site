# Public Summary: EPA-HQ-OW-2023-0469-0097

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** EPA proposes the sixth Unregulated Contaminant Monitoring Rule (UCMR 6), a national monitoring program for 30 unregulated contaminants in drinking water.

**Affected Entities**
- Large community water systems and non-transient non-community water systems serving more than 10,000 people
- Smaller community water systems and non-transient non-community water systems in the representative sample
- Commercial laboratories approved to analyze UCMR samples
- States, territories, and tribes that participate through voluntary partnership agreements

**Observed Facts**
- The proposal says the data will be publicly available and will inform EPA decisions.
- EPA ties the scope to SDWA section 1445(a)(2) and AWIA-based population thresholds.

**Inference**
- This is a monitoring-and-data rule with downstream policy consequences, not a direct treatment standard.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The UCMR program gathers data about unregulated contaminant occurrence in drinking water.
- The data collected will be publicly available and will inform decisions by the EPA.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- Ask EPA to publish the lab-capacity assumptions and fallback scenarios.
  Why it matters: if the capacity assumption fails, the rule may shrink or stall before it produces the intended national data.
  Ask agency to show the throughput math, contingency options, and any trigger for reducing the small-system sample.
- Ask EPA to justify the final contaminant list against the alternatives it considered.
  Why it matters: the value of the national monitoring data depends on whether the selected list is the right one.
  Ask agency to publish the screening criteria and comparative ranking that led to the 30-contaminant package.
- Ask EPA to disclose the unit-cost assumptions behind the burden estimate.
  Why it matters: lab and shipping costs are the biggest pieces of the national cost picture, so they should be easy to audit.
  Ask agency to publish the pricing inputs, shipping assumptions, and any sensitivity analysis.
- Ask EPA to show how the smaller-system sample changes under budget pressure.
  Why it matters: representative coverage only works if the fallback scenario is transparent.
  Ask agency to explain the smallest defensible sample size and what data-quality tradeoff that creates.
