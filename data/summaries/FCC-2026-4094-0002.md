# Public Summary: FCC-2026-4094-0002

## What This Document Does

The FCC seeks comment on four possible TCPA consent-revocation changes: a shorter deadline, reply-text capability, a revoke-all method, and clearer affiliate treatment.

Observed Fact: The notice supplies the current ten-business-day and one-way-texting baselines, cites prior FCC action, and includes PRA and IRFA discussion.

Inference: The central record-building question is whether these options improve consumer control enough to justify their technical, scope, and small-entity burdens.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal coherently targets consumer control over robocalls, but its possible technical and scope mandates are more developed than the evidence and safeguards needed to implement them reliably.

## Who Is Affected

Consumers receiving robocalls or texts; callers and text senders; telecommunications, financial, health, marketing, collection, and other businesses; schools and public institutions using one-way notifications; affiliates and service providers that share consent-management systems; and small entities subject to TCPA obligations.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Id:** angle_measure_deadline
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Posture:** evidence request
  
  **Issue:** A shorter revocation deadline needs a measurable operational basis.
  
  **Why It Matters:** Without baseline and exception data, the deadline may be either too slow for consumers or too rigid for small and distributed systems.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** Publish processing-time evidence and compare seven days with phased alternatives.
- **Id:** angle_message_type_alternative
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Posture:** alternatives push
  
  **Issue:** A universal two-way-texting requirement may be broader than necessary.
  
  **Why It Matters:** Informational and public-institution messages may have different technical needs and lower consumer-protection risks than advertising messages.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** Compare message-type, equivalent-functionality, transition, and safe-harbor alternatives.
- **Id:** angle_revoke_all_scope
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Posture:** collaborative clarification
  
  **Issue:** A revoke-all method needs a precise, consumer-understandable scope and reversal design.
  
  **Why It Matters:** A broad control could stop wanted account, fraud, health, or public-service messages, while an unclear control could generate repeated clarification contacts.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** Define message categories, confirmation, reversal, and ambiguous-request defaults.
