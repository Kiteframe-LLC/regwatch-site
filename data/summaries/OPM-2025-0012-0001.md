# Public Summary: OPM-2025-0012-0001

## What This Document Does

**Observed Facts**
- Joint NPRM revising OPM performance-based action procedures, MSPB penalty review, and settlement-record rules.
- The notice proposes changes to both OPM's agency procedures and MSPB's review standard.
- The notice cites older GAO, MSPB, and court materials as support for the accountability rationale.
- The notice says settlement terms that remove or withhold official performance or conduct records should be restricted.

**Inference**
- The proposal is best read as a management-forward redesign that narrows the practical role of Douglas-factor review and reduces the room for clean-record settlements.
- The document is a policy reversal from the Biden-era framework rather than a wholly new program built around fresh empirical findings.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Angle:** Ask for current baseline data before the rule is broadened.
  **Why it matters:** The historical citations show there was a problem, but not how the problem looks today.
  **Specific ask to agency:** Publish current data on removals, appeals, and settlement use.
  **Evidence to attach or request:** Agency case-processing statistics, MSPB appeal statistics, and settlement frequency data.
  **Expected value:** high
- **Angle:** Challenge the breadth of the remedy.
  **Why it matters:** A Douglas clarification or phased approach might preserve accountability while reducing collateral burden.
  **Specific ask to agency:** Explain why the final rule needs this full package instead of a narrower alternative.
  **Evidence to attach or request:** Side-by-side comparison of the current rule, the proposal, and narrower options.
  **Expected value:** high
- **Angle:** Require guardrails for the new totality standard.
  **Why it matters:** Greater deference can become arbitrary if the calibration rule is left vague.
  **Specific ask to agency:** Give examples, factors, and appeal-safe guardrails that keep the standard predictable.
  **Evidence to attach or request:** MSPB examples, proposed guidance, or pilot data on how the standard will work.
  **Expected value:** medium
- **Angle:** Narrow the settlement-record restriction.
  **Why it matters:** The rule may improve transparency, but it also can reduce dispute-resolution flexibility and create downstream burden.
  **Specific ask to agency:** Show why a narrower record-retention rule or explicit carve-outs would not work.
  **Evidence to attach or request:** Examples of settlement terms, record-retention practices, and downstream hiring impacts.
  **Expected value:** medium
- **Angle:** Ask for an implementation plan.
  **Why it matters:** The rule assumes agencies can absorb the change without a detailed transition map.
  **Specific ask to agency:** Explain training, consistency, and monitoring plans.
  **Evidence to attach or request:** Internal rollout plans, training materials, or transition milestones.
  **Expected value:** low
