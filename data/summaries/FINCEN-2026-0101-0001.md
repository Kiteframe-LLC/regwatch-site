# Public Summary: FINCEN-2026-0101-0001

## What This Document Does

**What It Does:** The joint proposal would require permitted payment stablecoin issuers to establish written, risk-based customer identification programs under proposed 31 CFR part 1033. It would govern collection and verification of customer information, records, government-list checks, customer notice, limited reliance on another financial institution's CIP, and possible exemptions.

**Why It Matters:** The proposal extends bank-like CIP obligations to a new stablecoin issuer population while preserving flexibility for digital account opening and issuer-specific risk. The central comment question is whether that flexibility remains consistent, auditable, accessible, and protective across different technologies and customer groups.

**Affected Entities**
- Permitted payment stablecoin issuers, including federal and state-supervised issuers
- Customers and account holders using payment stablecoins
- Financial institutions whose CIPs may be relied upon
- FinCEN, OCC, Federal Reserve, FDIC, NCUA, and state supervisors
- Compliance vendors, digital identity providers, and public-interest stakeholders

**Statutory Authority:** The notice relies principally on the GENIUS Act, 31 U.S.C. 5318(l), the BSA, and joint rulemaking authority with the primary federal payment stablecoin regulators.

**Core Tradeoff:** The proposal trades prescriptive uniformity for risk-based flexibility, with potential benefits for innovation and proportionality but greater dependence on issuer judgment and supervisory consistency.

**Observed Facts**
- The rule requires a written CIP and identity information before account opening, with verification within a reasonable period after opening.
- Records must include identifying information, relied-on documents, verification methods and results, and resolution of substantive discrepancies.
- The notice proposes five-year retention periods and allows reliance on another regulated financial institution under specified contractual conditions.

**Inference:** The practical effect will depend less on the existence of the CIP obligation than on how regulators and issuers interpret reasonable, risk-based, and adequate procedures.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is structurally coherent, but its risk-based flexibility depends on implementation judgments that may vary across issuers and affect customers unevenly.
