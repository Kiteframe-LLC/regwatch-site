## What This Document Does
- Updates the FY 2027 Medicare hospice wage index, payment rates, and aggregate cap amount.
- Estimates a net payment increase of about `$785 million` tied to a proposed `2.4 percent` payment update.
- Proposes several additional policy changes beyond the annual rate update:
- A mandatory hospice election statement addendum for all elections.
- A claims-based hospice spending variation index (SSVI) that CMS says could support beneficiary decisions and program integrity.
- Technical rule changes on who may discharge a patient from hospice care and on telehealth face-to-face recertification rules.
- A new Care Compare icon for hospices that miss the HOPE reporting-submission threshold.
- Includes three requests for information on community palliative care, a hospice-specific wage index, and overlap between hospice and medical aid in dying.

## Why It Matters
- The annual payment update is the routine core of the rule, but the more comment-worthy parts are the newer transparency and monitoring tools attached to it.
- CMS says non-hospice spending during hospice elections is rising and cites figures such as nearly `167 percent` higher non-hospice spending per day for beneficiaries in for-profit hospices than in nonprofit hospices, along with sharp growth in certain claim categories. That trend is being used to justify stronger disclosure and monitoring policies.
- The agency also says the SSVI would use nine claims-based metrics to rank hospice services in a way that could help beneficiaries and support program integrity. That is a significant claim because claims-based rankings can be useful for oversight while still being imperfect as consumer guidance if they do not fully account for case mix, geography, or coding/payment distortions.
- The mandatory addendum proposal matters because it would shift the current rule from "available on request" to a universal disclosure obligation. CMS would require hospices to furnish the addendum within the first 5 days of election and provide updates within 3 days of plan-of-care changes affecting the determination.
- The Care Compare icon matters because consumers may read it as a quality signal even though it is framed as a reporting-compliance signal tied to whether a hospice submits `90 percent` of HOPE assessments within 30 days of admission or discharge.

## Who Is Affected
- Medicare-certified hospices, especially those with smaller administrative staffs or more clinically complex patients.
- Beneficiaries and representatives who need to understand what hospice will and will not cover.
- Non-hospice providers and Medicare contractors that rely on clearer documentation about unrelated services and drugs.
- Consumers using Medicare's comparison tools, who may be influenced by new icons or future ranking signals.
- Commenters focused on access, rural care, small-provider operations, program integrity, or patient-information quality.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. Claims-based ranking may need more validation before it is treated as consumer guidance
   - Why it matters: CMS says the SSVI can help beneficiaries make more informed choices, but claims-based rankings can reflect case mix, billing incentives, and geography as much as provider quality.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: What validation, risk adjustment, and confounding analysis support using SSVI for beneficiary-facing comparisons rather than only for internal monitoring or program integrity?

2. The record should connect the spending problem more directly to the chosen disclosure remedy
   - Why it matters: Rising non-hospice spending may reflect beneficiary confusion, but it may also reflect clinician ordering, reimbursement changes, or patient complexity. A disclosure mandate is easier to defend if the agency explains why it addresses the diagnosed cause.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: What evidence shows that the observed growth in non-hospice spending is materially driven by beneficiary misunderstanding of hospice coverage responsibility, rather than by other market or clinical factors?

3. Mandatory addendum timing may be harder for some hospices than the rule suggests
   - Why it matters: Requiring the addendum within 5 days of election and updates within 3 days of certain plan-of-care changes may be manageable in standard cases but harder in fast-changing or medically complex cases.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Should CMS provide safe harbors, examples, phased implementation, or more flexible update rules for complex cases and smaller hospices?

4. The HOPE icon may communicate more than CMS intends
   - Why it matters: A negative icon tied to reporting completeness can be read by consumers as a broad quality warning, even if the agency means only to flag insufficient data submission.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: What evidence supports the `90 percent` threshold, and how will CMS ensure consumers understand that the icon measures reporting completeness rather than overall care quality?

5. Payment gains and administrative burdens should be separated more clearly
   - Why it matters: The rule highlights higher hospice payments, but commenters may want a cleaner explanation of which effects are transfer increases and which are new compliance or workflow burdens from the addendum and quality-reporting changes.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Can CMS provide a clearer breakdown separating transfer payments from provider compliance costs and workflow burden for the new disclosure and reporting elements?
