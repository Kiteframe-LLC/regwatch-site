# Public Summary: SEC-2026-6187-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The SEC proposes more flexible interval-fund repurchase timing and liquidity management, including a two-year initial deferral, monthly intervals, annual discretionary repurchases, deferred sales-load deductions, and a principles-based liquidity standard. It would also permit qualifying regulated closed-end funds and BDCs to use multiple share classes under board plans, equal-treatment rules, and enhanced Form N-2, Form N-CEN, and shareholder-report disclosures.

**Affected Entities:** Interval and other regulated closed-end funds, BDCs, advisers, boards, distributors, current and prospective shareholders, private-market portfolio companies, and fund service providers.

**Core Tradeoff:** The proposal may reduce cash drag and exemptive-order costs, but the record needs more evidence that delayed startup liquidity and a less prescriptive liquidity floor will protect investors during oversubscription and market stress.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is generally coherent, but the principles-based liquidity standard and two-year startup deferral need measurable safeguards and transition evidence before the record can show that flexibility will preserve reliable investor liquidity.

- **Liquidity standard:** The proposal replaces a 100-percent liquid-asset floor with a principles-based duty to meet repurchase requests without selling at a significant deviation from value. This could reduce cash drag, but commenters can request objective metrics, stress tests, board escalation, and a correction path. **Worth a comment:** high. **Comment posture:** evidence request.
- **Two-year startup deferral:** The proposal gives new interval funds more time to build longer-duration portfolios before the first repurchase offer. Commenters can request staged alternatives, stronger front-cover disclosure, and transition protections because investors may be unable to tender at NAV during the startup period. **Worth a comment:** high. **Comment posture:** alternatives push.

## Who Is Affected

Interval funds, other regulated closed-end funds, business development companies, advisers, fund boards and independent directors, distributors, current and prospective shareholders, private-market portfolio companies, and service providers handling NAV, notices, tenders, and records.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Issue:** Make the principles-based liquidity duty measurable. **Why it matters:** Flexibility may reduce cash drag, but investors need comparable assurance of repurchase capacity. **Ask:** Add minimum factors, stress scenarios, board reporting, escalation triggers, and correction steps, or retain a partial floor. **Evidence:** II.B; III.C.2; III.E.4.
- **Issue:** Limit or condition the two-year startup deferral. **Why it matters:** It may improve portfolio construction while delaying the first NAV-based liquidity opportunity. **Ask:** Compare a shorter default, staged offers, a documented board finding, and stronger front-cover disclosure. **Evidence:** II.A.1; III.E.1; request 76.
- **Issue:** Protect reliance interests when exemptive orders are rescinded. **Why it matters:** Existing funds may have built systems and disclosures around order-specific conditions. **Ask:** Inventory affected orders, preserve protective conditions during transition, and explain the one-year period for smaller and exempted funds. **Evidence:** II.E-II.F; requests 71, 74, 75, 77.
- **Issue:** Demonstrate equal treatment across share classes. **Why it matters:** Fees, distribution arrangements, and repurchase access can create conflicts. **Ask:** Add class-level reporting and monitoring fields and explain how investors can compare net outcomes. **Evidence:** II.D.1-3; Forms N-2 and N-CEN.
