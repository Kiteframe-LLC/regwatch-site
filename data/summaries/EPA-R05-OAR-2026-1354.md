## What This Document Does

- EPA proposes to treat the Berrien and Muskegon, Michigan ozone nonattainment areas as currently attaining the `2015` ozone standard for the `2023-2025` design-value period.
- The proposal is a `clean data determination`, not a redesignation. EPA says the areas would remain nonattainment areas unless and until EPA separately approves redesignation and maintenance-plan requirements.
- EPA's conclusion depends on excluding several wildfire-influenced ozone readings under the Exceptional Events Rule. The notice says Michigan submitted exceptional-events demonstrations on December 26, 2025, EPA concurred on January 12, 2026, and EPA will rely on the adjusted data set for this rulemaking.
- After those exclusions, EPA says both areas have design values of exactly `0.070 ppm`, which is the ozone attainment threshold.
- If EPA finalizes the proposal, it would suspend several attainment-planning obligations for these areas, including attainment demonstrations, RACM, RFP plans, contingency measures, and related attainment-planning SIP elements, for as long as the areas continue to attain.

## Why It Matters

- This is a narrow but important EPA action. It does not itself redesignate the areas to attainment, but it would pause major planning duties that normally apply to Serious ozone nonattainment areas.
- The strongest issue is whether the record explains clearly enough why the exceptional-events exclusions are justified and why they support the planning relief EPA wants to grant.
- That matters because the attainment result is knife-edge. EPA says Berrien is at `0.070 ppm` and Muskegon is also at `0.070 ppm`. When both areas sit exactly at the threshold, a small change in the included data set or in the treatment of the wildfire days could matter.
- The supporting materials included in the export do help. EPA's concurrence TSD excerpts say the agency used a weight-of-evidence approach, and the cover letters say that, with the data adjustments, the two areas show attainment for `2023-2025`.
- Even so, the notice is compressed. It does not spend much time showing how decisive each excluded day was, what the unadjusted outcome would look like, or how the planning suspension fits with the separate redesignation and maintenance-plan actions.

## Who Is Affected

- Residents of Berrien County and the western portion of Muskegon County, because the proposal affects the regulatory posture of those ozone nonattainment areas.
- Michigan air regulators, because the action would suspend several attainment-planning submissions while attainment continues.
- Local governments, transportation planners, and businesses that track ozone planning obligations and permitting consequences in these areas.
- Public-health, environmental, and technical commenters focused on the wildfire exclusions and the resulting attainment finding.
- Anyone concerned with how EPA uses exceptional-events determinations when wildfire smoke affects ozone compliance outcomes.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

1. EPA should show the adjusted and unadjusted design values more clearly.
Why it matters: The proposal depends on excluded wildfire days, and both areas end up exactly at the standard. Commenters need to see how sensitive the attainment result is to those exclusions.
Practical question a commenter can ask: What would the `2023-2025` design values be in Berrien and Muskegon if each excluded day were restored individually, or if all excluded days remained in the regulatory data set?

2. EPA should explain the exceptional-events causal showing in more plain language.
Why it matters: The NPRM says Michigan's demonstrations met the rule criteria, but the public-facing explanation is brief relative to the importance of those exclusions.
Practical question a commenter can ask: What are the strongest monitor-level facts showing that Canadian wildfire smoke, rather than ordinary local or regional ozone formation alone, caused each excluded exceedance?

3. EPA should explain the planning consequences more concretely.
Why it matters: The proposal would suspend several Serious-area planning duties, yet the notice also says this action is not redesignation and not maintenance-plan approval.
Practical question a commenter can ask: Which planning obligations would be suspended immediately if this proposal is finalized, which obligations remain pending, and how does the separate redesignation rulemaking affect that answer?

4. EPA should explain how it will handle worsening data before final action.
Why it matters: EPA says it will not finalize the determination if a monitor violates before final approval, but the notice gives limited detail on how that safeguard would work in practice.
Practical question a commenter can ask: What updated monitoring period or decision rule will EPA use to decide whether a pre-final-action violation defeats the clean-data determination?
