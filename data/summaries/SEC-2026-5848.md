# Public Summary: SEC-2026-5848-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The SEC proposes to modernize proxy rules by changing delivery and filing requirements, shortening the broker search period from 20 to five business days, and adding filer contact information.

**Affected Entities:** Public companies, investment companies and BDCs, shareholder groups, investors, brokers, banks, clearing firms, and securities-lending participants.

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites the Securities Act, Exchange Act, Investment Company Act, and Trust Indenture Act, with investor protection, efficiency, competition, and capital formation as relevant factors.

**Key Regulatory Changes**
- Use Form 10-K or EDGAR annual reports instead of mandatory delivery in specified cases, and remove most non-fund performance-graph requirements.
- Remove the 20-business-day incorporated-information delivery deadline.
- Rescind mandatory Notices of Exempt Solicitation.
- Shorten the Rule 14a-13 broker search period to five business days.
- Add representative contact information to Schedule 14A and 14C cover pages.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice estimates $7.7 million in annual benefits and $450,000 in annual costs.
- It identifies 5,357 section 12 companies, 2,720 potentially registered investment companies, and 3,376 historical PX14A6G notices by 311 filers.
- It discusses differentiated periods for investment companies.

**Inference:** The central tradeoff is lower compliance burden versus less investor time, traceability, and reliable beneficial-owner communication.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is generally coherent, but two mechanisms warrant focused record development: the five-day broker-search gateway and removal of centralized exempt-solicitation notices.
