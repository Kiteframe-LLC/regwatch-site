# Public Summary: EPA-R05-OAR-2026-3070-0001

## What This Document Does

**What It Does:** Proposes to determine that the Detroit moderate ozone nonattainment area attained the 2015 ozone NAAQS by the August 3, 2024 attainment date, while also proposing final agency action on Michigan's exceptional-events request to exclude specified 2023 wildfire-influenced ozone data from regulatory use.

**Why It Matters:** If finalized, the action affects whether Detroit avoids the legal and planning consequences that can follow from failing to attain by the applicable deadline.

**Affected Entities**
- residents and local governments in the Detroit area
- Michigan environmental regulators
- EPA Region 5 and national ozone-program staff
- regulated sources whose planning obligations can be shaped by nonattainment status
- public commenters focused on exceptional-events methodology, ozone attainment, and transparency of record support

**Observed Facts**
- The notice is styled as a proposed rule under 40 CFR part 52 and identifies June 29, 2026 as the comment deadline in the Federal Register text.
- EPA states that the proposal relies on excluding event-influenced 2023 ozone data after concurring with Michigan's exceptional-events demonstration.
- Table 1 in the notice reports post-exclusion 2021-2023 design values at or below 0.070 ppm for all listed Detroit-area monitors.

**Inference:** The highest-value comments are likely to focus on the transparency and reproducibility of the exclusion-driven attainment calculation rather than on broad statutory-authority objections.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The EPA is proposing to determine that the Detroit area attained the 2015 ozone NAAQS by the Moderate attainment date.
- The EPA found that Michigan's demonstration met the Exceptional Events Rule criteria and determined that these wildfire events had regulatory significance.
- As discussed in section II above, in making this proposed determination, the EPA is relying on calculated values that exclude the event-influenced data.
