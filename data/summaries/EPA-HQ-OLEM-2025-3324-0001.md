## What This Document Does

- EPA proposes to approve Louisiana's **partial** coal combustion residuals (CCR, or coal ash) permit program under RCRA.
- If finalized, Louisiana would administer the approved portion of the CCR program through LDEQ permits instead of the federal self-implementing CCR program.
- The approval would not cover every federal CCR requirement. EPA says omitted federal provisions would continue to apply directly.
- The record includes EPA's technical support document, Louisiana regulations, a 40 CFR part 257 checklist, EPA's 2017 state-program guidance, and a state-versus-federal comparison.
- EPA also identifies one Louisiana exclusion it is **not** approving: the state exclusion for CCR surface impoundments that no longer contain water or can no longer impound liquids.

## Why It Matters

- This is mainly a governance and accountability proposal. It affects who regulates CCR units, which requirements are enforced by Louisiana permits, and which federal rules still apply directly.
- EPA says LDEQ identified 9 CCR disposal units at 4 facilities in Louisiana. Those include 3 landfills and 6 surface impoundments.
- Louisiana has not yet issued CCR permits under LAC 33:VII.Chapter 10. Existing CCR landfills and surface impoundments would need to submit a new permit application or permit-modification application within 365 days after approval.
- Until a permit is issued under an approved state program, the owner or operator must continue to comply with federal CCR regulations.
- The proposal also matters for Tribal and community notification. EPA says the Jena Band of Choctaw Indians requested notification of major incidents and/or plant failure because Tribal boundaries are close to the Cleco Brame Power Plant.

## Who Is Affected

- Owners and operators of CCR landfills and surface impoundments in Louisiana.
- Communities near the 4 facilities covered by the Louisiana CCR program.
- The Jena Band of Choctaw Indians and other Tribes EPA consulted during the approval process.
- LDEQ, which would administer the approved state program.
- EPA, which would retain inspection and enforcement authority and would continue to enforce federal provisions that remain outside the approved state program.
- Public commenters trying to understand whether a specific requirement is controlled by Louisiana permits, direct federal CCR rules, or both.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- Issue: The split federal/state regime may be hard to follow in practice.
  - Why it matters: EPA proposes to approve only part of Louisiana's program. Requirements for legacy CCR surface impoundments, CCR management units, and other omitted provisions would remain federal.
  - Practical question a commenter can ask: Can EPA add a facility-level table showing which requirements will be governed by Louisiana permits and which will remain direct federal requirements?

- Issue: No Chapter 10 CCR permits are currently part of the record.
  - Why it matters: EPA is approving the program framework, not existing CCR permits under that chapter. The public needs to know how the transition from federal self-implementation to state permits will occur.
  - Practical question a commenter can ask: What is the expected timeline for permit applications, draft permits, public comment, and final permits after the 365-day application deadline?

- Issue: Groundwater terminology deserves clearer explanation.
  - Why it matters: EPA says it discussed the difference between federal "uppermost aquifer" requirements and Louisiana "uppermost permeable zone" requirements. Groundwater monitoring is central to CCR protection.
  - Practical question a commenter can ask: Please explain in plain language how Louisiana's "uppermost permeable zone" approach is at least as protective as the federal "uppermost aquifer" standard at affected facilities.

- Issue: The Jena Band notification request should be operationalized.
  - Why it matters: A consultation statement is useful, but nearby Tribes and communities need a practical mechanism for receiving notice of major incidents or plant failures.
  - Practical question a commenter can ask: Will EPA or LDEQ require permit terms, procedures, or public tracking that ensure timely notification of major incidents and plant failures?

- Issue: The unapproved Louisiana exclusion could create confusion.
  - Why it matters: EPA says the state exclusion for certain surface impoundments is not at least as protective, so federal rules will continue to apply. Facilities and the public need to know when that federal backstop matters.
  - Practical question a commenter can ask: Can EPA identify whether any covered Louisiana units may rely on, or be affected by, the excluded state provision?
