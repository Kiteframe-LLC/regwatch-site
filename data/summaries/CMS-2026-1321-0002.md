## What This Document Does

* CMS proposes the annual Skilled Nursing Facility Prospective Payment System update for federal fiscal year 2027.
* The payment side of the rule is mostly conventional. CMS proposes a 3.2 percent SNF market basket increase, subtracts a 0.8 percentage-point productivity adjustment, and arrives at a proposed 2.4 percent market basket update.
* CMS says the overall payment effect is favorable to the industry: “an estimated increase of $888 million in aggregate payments to SNFs during FY 2027.”
* The rule also changes the Skilled Nursing Facility Quality Reporting Program in more operational ways than the annual payment update alone suggests.
* CMS proposes to remove two COVID-related quality measures beginning with FY 2028.
* CMS proposes to move QRP submission deadlines from 4.5 months after quarter end to the 15th day of the second month after quarter end beginning with FY 2029.
* CMS also proposes a larger scope change beginning with FY 2031: facilities would have to submit MDS data for each resident receiving covered skilled care “regardless of payer.”
* The rule includes value-based purchasing updates and an RFI on how CMS should quantify and address case-mix creep under the Patient-Driven Payment Model.

## Why It Matters

* This is the kind of rule that can look routine because it is an annual payment update, but it contains several policy and reporting changes that matter beyond the yearly rate adjustment.
* The strongest public-comment opportunities are not broad attacks on CMS authority. They are narrower questions about whether CMS has shown enough work on reporting burden, implementation practicality, and the reasons for replacing or retiring existing quality measures.
* The record is not thin. The proposal has the expected CMS structure, statutory citations, economic effects, PRA discussion, and Regulatory Flexibility Act discussion. That means useful comments should focus on where the agency's explanation is incomplete, not on claiming the docket is missing basics.
* The biggest practical issue is the all-payer MDS expansion. CMS estimates that change alone would create “an estimated increase of $88 million annually to SNFs beginning with the FY 2031 SNF QRP.” That is large enough to justify close scrutiny of whether the added data collection is really necessary, how it will be used, and whether narrower options were considered.
* The shorter submission deadline is also important. Earlier deadlines can improve timeliness, but they can also increase error risk or staffing pressure if the public record does not show that the new window is operationally realistic.
* The COVID-measure removals are less dramatic, but still worth reviewing. If CMS removes measures without clearly explaining what replaces their monitoring function, commenters can ask for a more explicit transition rationale.

## Who Is Affected

* Skilled nursing facilities subject to the annual PPS update.
* SNFs that must comply with QRP reporting deadlines and data-submission requirements.
* Facilities that would need to submit MDS data for covered skilled-care residents regardless of payer.
* Small entities in particular. CMS's own RFA discussion says: “Most SNFs and most other providers and suppliers ... are small entities.”
* Small rural hospitals with swing-bed agreements or hospital-based SNFs. The proposal states that it will affect “small rural hospitals” that furnish SNF services in those ways.
* Organizations, trade groups, and advocates focused on quality measurement, patient safety, and payment incentives in post-acute care.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

* Issue: CMS proposes a much earlier QRP submission deadline without making the implementation case especially detailed in the public-facing summary.
Why it matters: Shorter timelines can improve timeliness, but they can also reduce data quality or increase compliance strain if facilities do not have enough operational runway.
Question to ask: What workflow testing, burden analysis, or error-rate assumptions support moving from 4.5 months after quarter end to the 15th day of the second month after quarter end?

* Issue: CMS proposes to require MDS reporting for each resident receiving covered skilled care regardless of payer.
Why it matters: This is a major scope expansion, and CMS estimates an added annual impact of $88 million. That creates a strong case for asking why narrower or phased alternatives were not chosen.
Question to ask: What specific policy uses require all-payer MDS collection, and why would a narrower or phased approach fail to meet CMS's objectives?

* Issue: The proposal removes two COVID-related QRP measures, but the available summary text does not fully spell out what monitoring function replaces them.
Why it matters: If the measures still convey useful quality or public-health information, their removal could leave a gap unless CMS explains the substitute data source or rationale more clearly.
Question to ask: What surveillance value is being retired with these measures, and what alternative data source or policy logic justifies removing them now?

* Issue: The rule uses large aggregate payment and burden figures, but the public-facing explanation is thinner on distribution across facility types and smaller operators.
Why it matters: Aggregate gains can mask uneven burdens. Smaller SNFs and rural providers may experience reporting changes differently than the national averages suggest.
Question to ask: Can CMS provide a clearer facility-type and small-entity breakdown of the administrative burden associated with the QRP deadline and all-payer MDS changes?

* Issue: CMS includes an RFI on case-mix creep under PDPM, which is normal for an NPRM, but the future methodology is still undefined.
Why it matters: Later action on case-mix creep could affect payment policy materially. Good comments now can shape the metrics, evidence thresholds, and safeguards CMS uses later.
Question to ask: What empirical indicators, thresholds, and protections against overcorrection is CMS considering for any future case-mix-creep methodology?
