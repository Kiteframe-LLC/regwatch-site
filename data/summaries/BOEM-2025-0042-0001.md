## What This Document Does

- Proposes to relax parts of BOEM’s 2024 offshore financial-assurance rule for oil, gas, sulfur, right-of-use-and-easement, and pipeline right-of-way obligations on the Outer Continental Shelf.
- Lowers the credit-rating threshold used to decide when supplemental financial assurance is required, from investment-grade `BBB-/Baa3` to `BB-/Ba3`.
- Lets a current lessee rely more heavily on the financial strength of jointly liable predecessors when BOEM decides whether extra bonding is needed.
- Lowers the probabilistic decommissioning-cost value used to size supplemental assurance from `P70` to `P50`.
- Expands or clarifies acceptable instruments, including dual-obligee instruments and a short-term decommissioning alternative path.
- Removes the requirement to post an appeal bond equal to the demand in order to stay a supplemental-financial-assurance demand during an IBLA appeal.

## Why It Matters

- This is a real deregulatory proposal, not a housekeeping edit. BOEM says the proposal would reduce regulated-party burden by about `$6.2 billion`.
- The agency frames the proposal as a way to free capital for offshore production and reduce what it sees as excessive supplemental assurance under the 2024 rule.
- The tradeoff is straightforward: less upfront security can mean more flexibility for operators, but it can also increase the chance that decommissioning liabilities are undersecured if a weaker operator fails.
- The main public-comment opportunity is not “BOEM showed nothing.” It is “BOEM used a contestable set of assumptions, and those assumptions may understate downside risk to the public.”

## Who Is Affected

- Offshore oil, gas, and sulfur lessees on the Outer Continental Shelf.
- Holders of related right-of-use-and-easement and pipeline right-of-way grants.
- Smaller or lower-rated operators that may benefit from reduced supplemental-bonding requirements.
- Taxpayers and the public, to the extent weaker bonding increases the risk that cleanup or decommissioning costs are not fully covered when operators fail.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

1. Whether one-year default rates are the right risk measure
- Why it matters: BOEM uses one-year corporate default rates to estimate expected risk, but offshore decommissioning liabilities can persist for much longer. A short time horizon may understate real exposure.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: Why are one-year default rates the right proxy for long-duration decommissioning liabilities, and how would the results change under multi-year or stressed-default scenarios?

2. Why `BB-/Ba3` is the right new threshold
- Why it matters: Moving from `BBB-/Baa3` to `BB-/Ba3` is a direct line-drawing choice. It materially expands the set of operators who can avoid supplemental assurance.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: What evidence shows that `BB-/Ba3`, rather than an intermediate threshold, still provides adequate protection against decommissioning default?

3. How much BOEM should rely on predecessor financial strength
- Why it matters: Joint-and-several liability may exist legally, but that does not automatically show that predecessor liability is timely or easy to collect.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: What historical evidence shows that predecessor liability has provided a reliable and timely backstop when current operators became unable to perform?

4. The shift from `P70` to `P50`
- Why it matters: This lowers the probability-based decommissioning-cost estimate used to set security amounts, reducing both costs and the protection margin if actual costs run high.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: Why did BOEM choose `P50` instead of a more protective middle option, and how sensitive are the results to cost-overrun scenarios?

5. Removal of the appeal-bond stay requirement
- Why it matters: BOEM would no longer require a bond equal to the demand to stay the demand during IBLA appeals, which may weaken BOEM’s leverage if a party’s finances worsen while an appeal is pending.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: What safeguards will prevent strategic delay or increased collection risk if a supplemental-assurance demand can be stayed without equivalent security?

6. Whether the RIA adequately addresses public-loss scenarios
- Why it matters: The RIA is detailed on reduced premiums and released capital, but thinner on how taxpayer exposure is bounded if defaults, distressed transfers, or decommissioning costs turn out worse than expected.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: Will BOEM provide scenario analysis showing how the proposal performs if defaults rise, assets move to weaker operators, or actual decommissioning costs exceed the `P50` estimate?

## Evidence Quotes

- “The major proposed amendments in this rule include returning to the previous BOEM practice of considering the financial strength of jointly liable predecessor lessees… revising the credit rating threshold… revising the decommissioning estimate used to determine the amount of supplemental financial assurance required, and revising the appeals bond provision….”
- “BOEM estimates that a total of approximately $6.2 billion of financial burden to the regulated community will be reduced.”
- “With this rule, the Department is proposing to revise this credit rating threshold to BB− (S&P), or Ba3 from Moody's… and to consider the credit rating of predecessors….”
- “BOEM uses the default rates to estimate the expected consequence of provisions analyzed in this RIA.”
- “For this analysis, BOEM finds that 1-year default rates are an appropriate measure of risk….”
- “The Department is proposing to revise the probabilistic value used for determining the amount of supplemental financial assurance required from P70 to P50.”
- The proposal would remove “the requirement that a lessee provide an appeal bond in the amount of the demand as a condition of staying the demand during the IBLA appeal process.”
