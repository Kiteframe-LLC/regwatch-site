# Public Summary: FCC-2026-3404-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The FCC proposes and solicits comment on reforms to USAC administration of the USF, including audit and recovery authority, operational efficiency, costs, oversight, and Board governance. The proposed rule text would make Board meetings public, expressly include beneficiaries in audit authority, and permit recovery calculations using a statistically representative sample.

**Affected Entities**
- USAC and FCC administrators
- USF contributors and telecommunications carriers
- participating schools, libraries, and rural health care providers
- small service providers and nonprofit beneficiaries
- rate-paying telecommunications customers
- communities dependent on universal-service support

**Statutory Authority:** Sections 1, 2, 4(i)-(j), 201(b), 254, 303(r), and 403 of the Communications Act, as cited by the notice.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The most concrete changes are proposed revisions to 47 CFR §§ 54.703 and 54.707: open Board meetings, beneficiary audits, possible suspension or delay of support for inadequate verification, and extrapolated audit recoveries.

**Core Tradeoff:** The proposal seeks faster, less costly administration and stronger anti-fraud controls while increasing the administrator's reach and reducing or restructuring procedural and governance constraints.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice identifies four review areas: processes and oversight, USAC structure, operating costs, and Board impact.
- The proposed text expressly authorizes extrapolation from a statistically representative sample and expands audit language to beneficiaries.
- The notice contains an IRFA and states that small entities may incur costs from audits, collections, evaluation, and appeals.

**Inference:** The proceeding is a broad administrative redesign whose practical effects will depend on implementation details that are only partly specified in the notice.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal has a plausible stewardship objective, but its most consequential audit and recovery mechanisms are not operationally specified.
