# Public Summary: DOE-HQ-2026-0496-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** DOE proposes to revise 10 CFR part 708, the Contractor Employee Protection Program, by changing definitions, filing and exhaustion rules, ADR tolling, notice, investigation timing, hearing administration, appeals, remedies, and notice-posting requirements.

**Problem Addressed:** The agency identifies confusion, changed office titles and practices, and processing delays under the rule last updated in 2019.

**Mechanism:** The proposal uses clarified text, new deadlines and tolling rules, expanded ADR procedures, revised filing and appeal mechanics, and greater discretion over some extensions and hearing logistics.

**Affected Entities:** DOE contractor employees who bring retaliation complaints, DOE contractors and subcontractors, DOE field elements, the Employee Concerns Program, the Office of Hearings and Appeals, investigators, and Administrative Judges.

**Observed Facts**
- The proposal is an NPRM and requests comments.
- The rule covers retaliation for protected disclosures, participation, and refusal to participate in certain activities.
- The proposal extends the investigation report deadline from 60 to 90 days and permits ADR tolling up to 90 days.

**Inference:** The proposal is primarily procedural, but procedural gateways can affect whether and when an employee receives merits review and relief.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is a coherent procedural update, but it leaves material questions about the evidentiary basis for minimized burden claims and the safeguards around discretionary procedural gateways.
