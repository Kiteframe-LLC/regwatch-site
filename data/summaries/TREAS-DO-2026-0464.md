# Public Summary: TREAS-DO-2026-0464-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** Treasury proposes to amend 31 CFR part 1 to exempt the Treasury .032 Federal Program Waste, Fraud, and Abuse Tip Intake and Referral Records system from selected Privacy Act provisions.

**Affected Entities**
- Members of the public who submit tips, complaints, or supporting information
- People who may be identified in those tips or referrals
- Treasury personnel who review, triage, and route the records
- Other federal agencies and law-enforcement entities receiving referrals

**Statutory Authority:** 5 U.S.C. 552a(k)(2) and the Secretary's authority under 31 CFR 1.23(c).

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal would exempt covered records from access, amendment, accounting-of-disclosures, source-identification, and related Privacy Act procedures to the extent the records are investigatory material compiled for law-enforcement purposes.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice is labeled a proposed rule and invites written comments.
- The system is described as collecting tips, complaints, allegations, leads, supporting information, and related correspondence.
- Treasury says the exemption is intended to protect investigatory material compiled for law-enforcement purposes.

**Inference:** This is a conventional Privacy Act exemption notice, but the mixed intake design makes the record-segregation question more important than the text itself makes explicit.

**Evidence Quotes**

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- Issue: Ask Treasury to define the mixed-record boundary. Why it matters: the system mixes intake material with investigatory records. Ask agency to publish objective criteria for when the exemption applies.
- Issue: Ask for a narrower-alternative analysis. Why it matters: the notice does not compare segregation, delayed release, or partial redaction. Ask agency to explain why those approaches would not protect the same law-enforcement interests.
- Issue: Ask for a correction path after the investigation ends. Why it matters: access and amendment are limited during the inquiry. Ask agency to add a post-closure review or correction process for mistaken intake records.
- Issue: Ask Treasury to explain how it will preserve auditability. Why it matters: an exemption can protect an investigation without erasing the record needed to reconstruct disclosure decisions later. Ask agency to retain a nonpublic audit trail or equivalent oversight log.
