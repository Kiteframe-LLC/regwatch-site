## What This Document Does
- EPA proposes to conditionally approve San Diego County Air Pollution Control District Rule 69.7 into the California SIP.
- The rule covers NOx emissions from landfill gas flares at municipal solid waste landfills that meet the federal major-source threshold.
- For enclosed flares, the rule uses a NOx limit of `0.06 lb/MMBtu` and a CO limit of `0.20 lb/MMBtu`, along with monitoring, recordkeeping, and source-test requirements.
- EPA is not proposing full approval because the rule lets operators use the named ASTM methods or the "most current version" of those methods.
- EPA says that future-version option can allow use of test methods EPA has not reviewed and approved for SIP purposes, so the rule is not fully enforceable as submitted.
- EPA instead proposes a conditional approval based on commitment letters from SDCAPCD and CARB promising a revision by `June 30, 2027` or within one year of final approval, whichever comes first.

## Why It Matters
- This is a classic SIP enforceability issue, not a fight over whether landfill flares should be regulated at all. EPA agrees the rule is largely usable, but says one test-method feature blocks full approval.
- The public record available for this run is better than the short notice alone. The export bundle includes EPA's TSD and attachment-derived text showing the district's comparison and cost rationale.
- That added context matters because the district frames Rule 69.7 as part of San Diego's response to Severe-area ozone obligations, while EPA says this action only decides SIP approvability under CAA section 110 and does **not** decide whether the rule satisfies future RACT obligations.
- That split is the main place where commenters can add value. A final rule should make very clear what this approval resolves and what it does not.
- The bundled support text also says existing flares already emit at or below the adopted NOx and CO limits. That means the immediate practical value of the rule is less about forcing new controls today and more about locking in a federally enforceable baseline and preventing backsliding.
- When a rule works mostly as a baseline-preservation tool, details about test methods, monitoring, and applicability matter more than usual.

## Who Is Affected
- Municipal solid waste landfills in San Diego County with landfill gas flares that meet the relevant major-source threshold.
- SDCAPCD and CARB, because they would need to submit a revision curing the ASTM-version problem to keep the conditional approval from turning into a failure.
- Nearby communities concerned about ozone-forming NOx emissions and the strength of enforceable monitoring requirements.
- Future commenters on San Diego's RACT-related SIP actions, because this notice may be read as a preview of how EPA treats Rule 69.7 even though EPA says a true RACT decision is still coming later.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. EPA should define the required fix more precisely
   - Why it matters: The notice says commitment letters will cure the defect, but does not quote the exact rule text EPA expects to change.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Will EPA specify the precise revision it expects so the public can tell whether the cure is limited to deleting "most current version" language or includes any related enforceability cleanup?

2. EPA should explain more clearly why this action is not also a RACT determination
   - Why it matters: The district support materials treat Rule 69.7 as part of San Diego's RACT response, but EPA says this action only addresses SIP approvability.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Please explain what conclusions should not be drawn about RACT adequacy from this conditional approval and what issues EPA plans to revisit in the later RACT action.

3. EPA should say why the ASTM-version issue is the only material enforceability defect
   - Why it matters: If existing flares already comply and the rule's value is mainly enforceability and anti-backsliding, readers need confidence that EPA examined the rest of the rule closely too.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Did EPA review other possible enforceability issues in Rule 69.7, such as open-flare treatment or APCO-driven monitoring provisions, and why did those not prevent approval?

4. EPA should explain practical coverage better
   - Why it matters: The record would be easier to evaluate if it showed how many facilities or flares are actually covered and how enclosed flares differ from existing open flares under the rule.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Please provide a simple explanation of which landfill flares are covered in practice and how the rule treats existing open flares compared with enclosed flares.

5. EPA should surface the alternative-limit comparison more directly
   - Why it matters: The attachment-derived district material says San Diego considered a lower `0.025 lb/MMBtu` NOx limit but rejected it as too costly, while describing the adopted `0.06 lb/MMBtu` limit as much more cost-effective.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Will EPA summarize in the final rule the basis for rejecting the lower alternative and explain how that local analysis relates to the later RACT review?

## Evidence Quotes

- "By providing the option to use a version of a test method other than the specific versions listed in the rule, the rule allows for the use of alternative test methods not reviewed and approved by the EPA."
- "This action focuses on the approvability of Rule 69.7 for inclusion into the SIP under CAA section 110. If relied upon to meet RACT, we will evaluate whether Rule 69.7 meets the applicable CAA requirements for RACT when acting on the District's RACT SIP submittal."
- "The District evaluated existing facility data for MSW landfills in San Diego County with flares subject to proposed new Rule 69.7 and determined that all existing flares currently emit at or below the NOx and CO emission limits in the proposed rule."
- "Staff considered a lower NOx standard for proposed new Rule 69.7 ... but found it was cost prohibitive for sources in San Diego County at an estimated $21 per pound of NOx emissions reduced."
