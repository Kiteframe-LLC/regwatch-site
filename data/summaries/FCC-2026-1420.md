## What This Document Does
- This is a Public Notice that asks commenters to refresh the record on pending petitions for reconsideration tied to the Lower C-band proceeding.
- It connects those petitions to technical proposals in the Upper C-band NPRM and asks for a more harmonized approach across the C-band.
- The main topics are technical: emissions limits into 4.2-4.4 GHz, a possible 4 Watt EIRP limit for mobile devices, and any other rule changes needed to align Lower and Upper C-band operations.
- It also asks commenters to address the costs and benefits of changing current Lower C-band requirements.

## Why It Matters
- The C-band is nationally important spectrum, so even a technical adjustment can affect wireless deployment, coexistence with nearby services, and protection of adjacent users such as radio altimeters.
- The current packet is a thin record by itself: it does not include the underlying petitions, the Upper C-band NPRM, or any supporting engineering studies.
- That means public comments can matter a lot. Commenters can help define the baseline problem, the right coexistence metrics, and whether harmonization is actually the best policy frame.
- A weak or underdefined technical record can leave the agency relying on broad assertions instead of measurable standards.

## Who Is Affected
- Wireless carriers and spectrum licensees in the Lower and Upper C-band.
- Equipment vendors, RF engineers, and test labs that can measure emissions and coexistence performance.
- Aviation stakeholders and radio altimeter interests that care about out-of-band emissions and interference protection.
- Smaller carriers and rural deployers that may face transition costs if technical limits change.
- Public-interest and consumer advocates that want a record grounded in clear metrics rather than assumption.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. Issue: The notice does not show the actual baseline problem behind the proposed technical changes.
   - Why it matters: Without a measured baseline, it is hard to tell whether the proposed changes solve a real coexistence problem or just shift the policy default.
   - Practical question to ask: What specific interference measurements or incident data justify revisiting the current Lower C-band technical limits now?

2. Issue: The record does not yet show why harmonization should be preferred over narrower alternatives.
   - Why it matters: A full harmonization model may create unnecessary transition costs if a partial or phased approach would work.
   - Practical question to ask: What intermediate options were evaluated, such as partial harmonization, phased compliance, device-class-specific limits, or geographic carve-outs?

3. Issue: The notice asks for technical details but does not define the coexistence test standard.
   - Why it matters: Commenters need to know what evidence will count as persuasive or sufficient.
   - Practical question to ask: What test methods, assumptions, and pass/fail thresholds should be used to evaluate coexistence with radio altimeters?

4. Issue: The notice invites cost and benefit comments but does not identify the baseline for that analysis.
   - Why it matters: Costs and benefits cannot be compared meaningfully without a clear starting point and a defined implementation scenario.
   - Practical question to ask: What baseline assumptions should commenters use when estimating costs, deployment delays, and equipment replacement needs?

5. Issue: Smaller and rural stakeholders may face uneven compliance burdens.
   - Why it matters: A rule that looks workable for large operators may be harder for smaller licensees or less flexible deployments.
   - Practical question to ask: How would the proposed technical changes affect rural networks, niche vendors, and small operators with limited upgrade budgets?
