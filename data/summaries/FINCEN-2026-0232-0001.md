# Public Summary: FINCEN-2026-0232-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** FinCEN proposes a section 311 special measure five finding and prohibition concerning Banque Misr UAE. Covered financial institutions would be barred from opening or maintaining correspondent accounts for or on behalf of the covered institution, required to take reasonable steps not to process transactions involving it through foreign correspondent accounts, and required to apply risk-based due diligence and document specified notices.

**Affected Entities**
- U.S. banks and other covered financial institutions that maintain foreign correspondent accounts
- foreign correspondent account holders and foreign banking institutions
- Banque Misr UAE and potentially its UAE offices, branches, affiliates, and subsidiaries
- U.S. regulators and self-regulatory organizations that may review compliance records
- legitimate businesses and customers using affected correspondent-banking channels
- Iranian illicit-finance networks and other actors seeking access to U.S. dollar clearing

**Statutory Authority:** Section 311 of the USA PATRIOT Act, codified at 31 U.S.C. 5318A, with BSA authority delegated to FinCEN and implementing authority in 31 CFR part 1010.

**Core Tradeoff:** The proposal seeks to reduce alleged Iranian shadow-banking access to the U.S. financial system while accepting correspondent-account disruption, screening costs, possible de-risking, and uncertainty for legitimate activity involving the defined institution.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice states that Banque Misr UAE has three direct U.S. correspondent relationships and approximately $6 billion in assets.
- The proposed rule includes a prohibition, indirect-transaction prevention, notification, risk-based screening, investigation, possible termination, and documentation requirements.
- The notice says FinCEN consulted with multiple agencies and considered special measures one through four and conditional access under special measure five.
- The notice estimates 128 expected respondents, 1,024 first-year burden hours, and $130,079 in first-year cost.

**Inference:** The proposal is legally and operationally coherent at a high level, but the available record does not let commenters fully test whether the breadth of the defined target and indirect-transaction controls is proportionate to the disclosed evidence.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is structurally plausible but its broad access restriction and indirect-screening duties are more detailed than the public evidence, scope crosswalk, and review safeguards supporting them.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- Issue: Explain the methodology for the 103-company and $1.8 billion estimates. Why: The central finding is consequential, partly non-public, and described as potential. Question: What validation checks and uncertainty ranges support it?
- Issue: Compare full prohibition with targeted or phased controls. Why: The notice does not show why narrower controls would fail. Question: Could periodic review reduce disruption?
- Issue: Align the summary and regulatory definition. Why: The text reaches beyond the five branches highlighted in the summary. Question: Which entities are covered and how will updates be communicated?
- Issue: Add indirect-transaction and false-positive safeguards. Why: Ordinary screening may not resolve hidden relationships. Question: What are reasonable steps for an unresolved indirect match?
