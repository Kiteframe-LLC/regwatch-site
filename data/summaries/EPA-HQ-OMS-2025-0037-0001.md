## What This Document Does
- EPA proposes a new EPA Acquisition Regulation clause that would let contractors sign hazardous-waste manifests, land-ban records, and some non-hazardous-waste shipping manifests on EPA's behalf when the Contracting Officer Representative gives written direction.
- The proposal would replace EPA's narrower existing local clause for Superfund hazardous-waste manifests.
- EPA would use one main clause plus alternates to cover three situations: hazardous waste from Superfund worksites, hazardous waste from non-Superfund worksites, and non-hazardous waste from EPA worksites.
- EPA says the point is to keep waste-removal work moving when EPA personnel are not physically present to sign manifests.

## Why It Matters
- Manifest signatures are not trivial paperwork. They sit at the point where waste classification, shipment records, and accountability meet actual off-site transport.
- This rule does not appear to change the underlying hazardous-waste rules in 40 CFR parts 262 and 268. It changes who may execute key shipment documents on EPA's behalf.
- The strongest reason EPA gives is operational continuity: it says the new clause will avoid work stoppages and save money. But the available record reviewed here does not show how often absent EPA signers actually delay shipments or how EPA calculated the expected savings.
- EPA also recognizes the obvious integrity concern. The notice says EPA will use management controls and conflict-of-interest review to address fraud and conflicts, but it does not describe those controls in enough detail for the public to evaluate them.
- The record used here is thin. The export metadata reports no supporting docket materials, so the proposal stands or falls largely on the notice text itself unless EPA later points commenters to additional materials.

## Who Is Affected
- EPA contractors performing cleanup, removal, and transport work at Superfund and non-Superfund sites.
- EPA procurement staff, especially Contracting Officers and Contracting Officer Representatives who will decide when delegated signature authority is used.
- Transporters and disposal facilities that rely on accurate manifest paperwork.
- Communities near cleanup sites, because paperwork integrity affects whether wastes are tracked and removed as intended.
- Small contractors that may receive delegated signature authority and may face added documentation or liability questions if responsibilities are not clearly defined.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. The claimed cost savings and work-stoppage benefits are not shown.
   - Why it matters: EPA says the clause will save money and prevent interrupted cleanup work, but the available text does not show how often this problem actually happens or how the savings were calculated.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: What data, incident history, or cost analysis supports the statement that lack of an on-site EPA signer has caused delays significant enough to justify this new clause?

2. The fraud and conflict-of-interest safeguards are too vague.
   - Why it matters: EPA says it will use management controls and conflict-of-interest review, but it does not explain what those controls are or how EPA will audit contractor-signed manifests.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: What specific management controls, audit procedures, and COI criteria will EPA require before and after a contractor is authorized to sign manifests on EPA's behalf?

3. Accountability for manifest errors is not fully explained.
   - Why it matters: The clause says the contractor is not the generator solely because it signed, and it limits the agency relationship to the signature authorization. Practical responsibility may still be unclear if paperwork is wrong.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: How will EPA allocate responsibility for inaccurate manifests, land-ban records, or shipment documentation when a contractor signs on EPA's behalf?

4. The proposal does not show that EPA considered narrower alternatives.
   - Why it matters: EPA might have options short of a broad new clause, such as site-specific delegations, pilots, dual review, or limiting delegation to certain shipment types.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Did EPA evaluate narrower alternatives to broad delegated signature authority, and why were they rejected?

5. The expansion to non-Superfund and non-hazardous contexts is underexplained.
   - Why it matters: The proposal starts from a local clause used for Superfund hazardous-waste work and extends the approach to additional settings without much explanation of why the same solution fits all of them.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: What differences among Superfund, non-Superfund, hazardous-waste, and non-hazardous-waste settings did EPA analyze before deciding the same delegation model should apply across them?

## Evidence Quotes
- "The new clause will enable contractors to sign the Waste Manifest at EPA worksites for the removal of hazardous and non-hazardous materials at both Superfund and non-Superfund sites."
- "The addition of the new clause will allow work to continue when EPA personnel are not present at the worksite."
- "This will result in cost savings and prevent work stoppages at cleanup sites, enabling the cleanup to continue uninterrupted."
- "To prevent conflicts of interest and fraud, the EPA will implement management controls and address these concerns during the conflict of interest (COI) review for worksite cleanup procurements."
- "The Contractor shall not be considered a generator of hazardous wastes solely as a result of having signed the manifests or land ban records on behalf of the EPA."
- "Nothing contained in this paragraph shall be construed to create an agency relationship between the Contractor and EPA except with respect to the authorization to sign the manifests and land ban records."
