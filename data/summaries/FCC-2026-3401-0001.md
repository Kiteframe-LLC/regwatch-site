# Public Summary: FCC-2026-3401-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The FCC seeks comment on revising Rural Health Care Program rate-comparability and cost-study methods, supporting lower-cost backup services, creating an eligible-services list, adopting USAC processing metrics, increasing or reallocating the funding cap if demand exceeds it, and eliminating evergreen-contract approval and HCF annual-report requirements.

**Affected Entities**
- rural health care providers and provider consortia
- telecommunications and broadband service providers
- USAC and FCC program administrators
- small telecommunications businesses
- patients and communities relying on rural telehealth
- Universal Service Fund contributors and beneficiaries

**Statutory Authority:** Sections 1, 4(j), 214, 254, and 303(r) of the Communications Act, plus 47 CFR 1.3, as stated in the notice.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The principal proposed rule text would make services on a future eligible-services list eligible for support, require secondary-service cost and bandwidth to reflect secondary use and be cost-effective, authorize list revision by Public Notice, reserve § 54.618, and remove the HCF annual-report requirement.

**Core Tradeoff:** The proposal trades some prescriptive review and reporting for lower administrative burden and more flexible program access, while relying on future listmaking, cost-comparison methods, and performance metrics to protect program funds.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice expressly asks for comments and alternatives on nearly every major policy choice.
- The notice identifies the 30-percent speed comparison rule, cost studies, the 65-percent HCF program share in funding year 2024, and the proposed § 54.634 eligible-services-list mechanism.

**Inference:** The proposal is primarily a process and methodology refresh, not a single technical mandate; its final effects will depend heavily on future FCC and USAC implementation decisions.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is coherent, but its simplification measures depend on future administrative mechanisms that are not fully specified.
