# Public Summary: NPS-2026-0133-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The National Park Service proposes a special regulation that would designate a bicycle-access trail network in the Brandywine Valley unit, publish that network through maps and park notices, and preserve superintendent discretion to limit or close trail use later.

**Affected Entities**
- Park visitors who bike, hike, or otherwise use the Brandywine Valley trail system
- Park staff responsible for trail mapping, monitoring, maintenance, and enforcement
- Nearby recreation users who depend on clear connector-trail routing and route postings
- Commenters interested in park-resource protection, user conflict, and long-term trail management

**Statutory Authority:** The rule relies on NPS special-regulation authority for park management, along with the bicycle-use criteria in 36 CFR 4.30 and the broader park-authority provisions cited in the notice.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The rule would authorize bicycle use on approximately 25 miles of trails, identify 12 named trails plus 51 connector trails, and allow the Superintendent to later limit, restrict, or close bicycle use as conditions require.

**Observed Facts**
- The National Park Service proposes a special regulation for First State National Historical Park in 36 CFR part 7.
- The proposed text would allow the Superintendent to designate bicycle use on approximately 25 miles of trails in the Brandywine Valley unit.
- The rule lists 12 named trails and states that 51 connector trails will link those named trails.
- The notice ties the rule to a trail inventory, a Trail Plan/Environmental Assessment, a revised EA and PA, and a FONSI.
- The rule states that trail maps will be available at visitor centers and on the park website, and that the Superintendent will provide notice under § 1.7.

**Inference:** This is a site-specific park access rule that turns a prior planning record into enforceable trail-designation authority. The main public questions are how the trail network was selected, how the connector trails will be kept legible to the public, and what monitoring or closure standards will govern future changes.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The proposed rule would authorize the Superintendent to designate approximately 25 miles of trails for bicycle use.
- Connector Trails C1 through C51 9.0
- A map showing trails open to bicycle use will be available at park visitor centers and posted on the park website.
- The EA, informed by the comprehensive trail inventory, evaluates the suitability of each trail surface and soil conditions for accommodating bicycle use.
