# Public Summary: FTC-2026-1849-0001

## What This Document Does

The FTC is inviting comments on an individual’s petition before deciding whether to pursue rulemaking. It has not adopted the petition’s claims or proposed its own device ban. Comments may support or oppose the request, in whole or in part.

The petitioner requests three measures: informed opt-in for preinstalled residential-proxy software; a prohibition on deceptive device identity or certificate spoofing; and verifiable boot plus restrictions on built-in, undocumented DRM bypasses. The notice’s broad description of “uncertified” devices should be read alongside those specific requests.

The notice gives December 21, 2026 as the filing deadline. The export’s December 22 UTC timestamp represents the same local closing date; it should not be read as an extra day.

## Who Is Affected

- Consumers using low-cost streaming boxes and home networks could benefit if covert proxy activity is reduced. The supplied materials do not establish how many devices or households are affected.
- Manufacturers, firmware developers, importers, distributors, and commercial sellers could face future design, verification, or support duties if a rulemaking follows. No new obligations arise from this receipt notice.
- Independent operating-system developers, repair and modification communities, small suppliers, and licensed streaming platforms could be affected by how certification and DRM requirements are eventually defined. These effects are plausible, not measured in the petition.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Id:** baseline
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Posture:** evidence request
  
  **Issue:** Establish the scale and source of device compromise.
  
  **Why It Matters:** The petition alleges widespread infection but supplies no sample, prevalence estimate, testing method, or incident citations. That leaves the size of the problem and the appropriate reach of a response uncertain.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** What reproducible testing and representative market evidence distinguish preinstalled malware from later user-installed software?
  
  **Supporting Evidence:** Petition III describes covert proxy software and data harvesting without supporting datasets.
- **Id:** definitions
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Posture:** collaborative clarification
  
  **Issue:** Separate certification status from demonstrated security defects.
  
  **Why It Matters:** The petition discusses uncertified devices alongside infected devices; its operative provisions target proxy software, deceptive identity spoofing, boot mechanisms, and DRM bypasses. A general certification requirement is not clearly defined.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** Which certification, technical test, and decision maker would identify noncompliance, and could independent devices satisfy an equivalent security standard?
  
  **Supporting Evidence:** Petition II lists three requested requirements; the conclusion uses broader uncertified-device language.
- **Id:** fit
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Posture:** alternatives push
  
  **Issue:** Test whether DRM and boot requirements fit the demonstrated harm.
  
  **Why It Matters:** The petition links proxy abuse to security harms but also requests DRM integrity and verifiable boot across all streaming hardware. It does not show the incremental security benefit or address lawful modification and independent operating systems.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** Would narrower rules against undisclosed proxy operation and deceptive attestation achieve the security objective with fewer restrictions on lawful use?
  
  **Supporting Evidence:** Petition II.1 requires informed opt-in; II.3 addresses all consumer streaming hardware.
- **Id:** implementation
  
  **Priority:** moderate
  
  **Posture:** evidence request
  
  **Issue:** Describe enforceable duties and practical compliance costs.
  
  **Why It Matters:** Importers, distributors, and sellers have different visibility into firmware. The petition does not allocate testing duties, provide update or correction procedures, or estimate effects on prices and smaller suppliers.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** What testing, update support, transition periods, and correction procedures would each supply-chain actor need, and what would they cost?
  
  **Supporting Evidence:** Petition II names multiple commercial actors but provides no implementation schedule or cost analysis.
