# Public Summary: EPA-HQ-OPP-2026-1818-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** EPA is seeking comment on a pesticide petition notice for sulfoxaflor that would establish a tolerance on pistachio at 0.5 ppm.

**Affected Entities**
- Corteva Agriscience and other petitioners or registrants seeking food-use tolerances
- Pistachio growers, handlers, and processors
- Laboratories and enforcement staff that would use method 091116
- Consumers, including sensitive subpopulations EPA must consider
- Commenters focused on pesticide residues, exposure modeling, and international tolerance alignment

**Statutory Authority:** FFDCA section 408(d)(3), with the petition completeness framework in 40 CFR 180.7 and EPA's ability to vary the final tolerance under section 408(d)(4)(A)(i).

**Observed Facts**
- The notice is framed as a petition notice and request for comment rather than a final tolerance action.
- The petition summary is detailed enough to identify residue chemistry, analytical method, toxicology, aggregate exposure, and international tolerances.

**Inference:** The document is useful for comment because the petition summary gives a technical scaffold, but EPA has not yet shown its own final evaluation of that scaffold.

**Evidence Quotes**
- EPA has not fully evaluated the sufficiency of the submitted data at this time or whether the data supports granting the pesticide petitions.
- requests to establish a tolerance ... for residues of the insecticide sulfoxaflor in or on pistachio at 0.5 parts per million (ppm).
- Nine residue field trials for sulfoxaflor on pistachio were conducted in the United States during the 2022 growing season.
- Acute and chronic dietary exposures are below the aPAD and cPAD, respectively, for all subpopulations.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Issue:** Ask EPA to publish the math that connects the 2022 residue trials to 0.5 ppm.
  **Why it matters:** Commenters need the actual bridge from trial data to the proposed tolerance, not just the petition summary.
  **Ask agency to:** Show the residue table, processing factors, and the calculation that supports 0.5 ppm.
  **Evidence:** Nine residue field trials for sulfoxaflor on pistachio were conducted in the United States during the 2022 growing season.
- **Issue:** Ask EPA to explain the acute and chronic exposure inputs behind the no-harm conclusion.
  **Why it matters:** A below-aPAD/cPAD conclusion is only as strong as the residue and subgroup assumptions behind it.
  **Ask agency to:** Identify the model inputs, drinking-water assumptions, and population subgroup estimates used in the assessment.
  **Evidence:** Acute and chronic dietary exposures are below the aPAD and cPAD, respectively, for all subpopulations.
- **Issue:** Ask EPA to clarify method 091116 and the residue definition it enforces.
  **Why it matters:** A method can be technically valid yet still leave matrix or metabolite questions unresolved.
  **Ask agency to:** State whether the method covers parent sulfoxaflor, X11719474, or both, and provide the pistachio validation package.
  **Evidence:** The Offline Solid-Phase Extraction and Liquid Chromatography with Tandem Mass Spectrometry Detection method is used to measure and evaluate the chemical sulfoxaflor.
- **Issue:** Ask EPA to explain how processed pistachio products and related commodities were handled.
  **Why it matters:** Processing can change the residue picture even when the raw commodity result looks acceptable.
  **Ask agency to:** Describe whether processed products or related commodities were considered and why they were included or excluded.
  **Evidence:** Residue data following processing of pistachio nutmeat show that residues of sulfoxaflor and X11719474 may transfer to processed washed dried nutmeat and unwashed dried nutmeat.
- **Issue:** Ask EPA to distinguish petitioner assertions from EPA findings in any later action.
  **Why it matters:** The visible docket is still petitioner-driven, so commenters need to know what EPA independently found.
  **Ask agency to:** Label the agency's own conclusions separately from the petitioner summary in any final decision document.
  **Evidence:** EPA has not fully evaluated the sufficiency of the submitted data at this time or whether the data supports granting the pesticide petitions.
- **Issue:** Ask EPA how harmonization references affect the U.S. decision, if at all.
  **Why it matters:** International MRL references can help explain the request, but they should not replace U.S.-specific analysis.
  **Ask agency to:** Separate harmonization reasoning from the statutory safety review and show whether it changes the final level.
  **Evidence:** Codex MRLs have been established on ... Pistachio nuts.
