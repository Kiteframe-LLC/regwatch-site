# Public Summary: FNA-2026-0034-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** FNA proposes to revise SNAP regulations so the Federal share of most State administrative costs changes from 50 percent to 25 percent beginning in FY 2027, while retaining specified 50 percent E&T reimbursement and 75 percent treatment for SNAP administration by States or ITOs on reservations.

**Affected Entities**
- State SNAP agencies
- Indian Tribal Organizations administering SNAP on reservations
- FNA and USDA program administrators
- SNAP applicants and participants who depend on timely administration
- State contractors and technology or service providers
- State and local governments funding or operating SNAP administration

**Statutory Authority:** Section 10106 of Public Law 119-21, amending section 16(a) of the Food and Nutrition Act of 2008; the notice also cites 7 U.S.C. 2011-2036.

**Key Change:** The Federal reimbursement rate for covered State administrative expenses is reduced from 50 percent to 25 percent starting in FY 2027, with conforming regulatory edits in parts 272, 274, and 277.

**Core Tradeoff:** The proposal implements an enacted cost-allocation change and preserves the statutory program structure, but shifts a large share of financing responsibility to States and may alter their capacity and incentives to administer SNAP.

**Observed Facts**
- The RIA estimates a $16.876 billion reduction in Federal costs and corresponding increase in State costs over FY 2027-2031.
- The notice states that benefit spending and participation are expected to be negligibly affected, but also acknowledges possible longer wait times, reduced customer service, increased error rates, and lower fraud detection.
- The notice says no alternatives were considered because the rate change is required by statute.

**Inference:** The strongest comment leverage concerns whether the record adequately analyzes the consequences of shifting administrative financing when the agency itself identifies plausible service and accuracy effects.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The rule's statutory mechanism is coherent, but its financing shift may create service harms that are acknowledged without a monitoring or mitigation design.
