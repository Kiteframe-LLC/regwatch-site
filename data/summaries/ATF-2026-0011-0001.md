## What This Document Does

- ATF proposes a narrow change to firearms-licensing rules for federal firearms licensees (`FFLs`).
- The rule would amend `27 CFR 478.11` and `27 CFR 478.50` so that one license can cover:
  - properties that physically adjoin each other, or
  - properties that are adjacent to each other and adjoin the same parking lot, sidewalk, or road.
- ATF says some FFLs currently need separate licenses for those kinds of layouts or must request a variance.
- The proposal does not eliminate licensing. It changes when one license is enough for more than one parcel.

## Why It Matters

- This is a deregulatory proposal, but it is still worth reading closely because the rule turns on line-drawing. The practical question is not whether adjoining parcels can ever function as one business site. The question is how clearly ATF defines which non-contiguous properties also qualify.
- ATF relies on ordinary legal meanings of `adjoin` and `adjacent`, plus examples like two sites across a road or two stores in the same shopping center sharing a parking lot.
- That helps, but the notice also says each case would still need to be evaluated “based on its circumstances.” That means some ambiguity will remain unless the final rule gives clearer boundaries.
- The economic support is also fairly thin from the text provided. ATF says a per-entity savings of about `$150` to `$300` may occur from avoiding duplicate license costs, plus about one hour of labor worth `$74.79`. But the notice also says “overall industry savings was not calculated.”
- That does not defeat the rule. It does mean commenters have a clear opening to ask for better support on how many FFLs are actually affected, how often these configurations arise, and whether any added review burden offsets some of the savings.

## Who Is Affected

- FFLs that operate from more than one parcel and want one license to cover the full business layout.
- ATF licensing and inspection personnel who will need to decide whether non-contiguous properties are close enough, and connected enough, to count as one business premises.
- Small businesses indirectly affected by licensing structure, including firms that may save on duplicate fees and administrative time.
- Commenters with real examples of shopping-center, road-separated, warehouse, or mixed-use layouts are especially well positioned to improve the record.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

1. The final rule needs a clearer boundary for `adjacent`.
- Why it matters: The notice uses ordinary-language terms like `near or close to`, which are understandable but still leave room for uneven interpretation.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: What specific factors will ATF use to determine when non-contiguous parcels are sufficiently close and operationally connected to count as one premises?

2. The visible savings analysis is limited.
- Why it matters: ATF says the rule reduces burden, but it does not show a full industry-wide estimate and does not disclose how many FFLs would actually benefit.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: How many current multiple-license or variance situations does ATF expect this rule to affect each year?

3. Alternatives are not explored in much detail.
- Why it matters: A narrower safe harbor for physically adjoining parcels, or more detailed criteria for road- or parking-lot-separated sites, might preserve flexibility while reducing ambiguity.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: Did ATF consider a more explicit safe harbor or example-based standard for non-contiguous properties?

4. Net administrative burden is unclear.
- Why it matters: Avoiding duplicate licenses may save money for FFLs, but broader eligibility may also create more case-by-case review if the final standard stays open-ended.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: Does ATF expect the amended rule to reduce or increase staff time spent evaluating complex premises layouts?
