# Public Summary: NHTSA-2026-1519-0001

## What This Document Does

NHTSA proposes to modernize lighting tests and driver-control requirements while preserving required external lighting. Integral-beam headlamps would be tested at their marked voltage; vertical high/low beam placement would become more flexible. Vehicles without manual driving controls could omit certain manual controls and routine driver telltales. Optional cyan ADS marker lamps would face uniform performance limits. This proposal does not authorize ADS driving generally or require every ADS vehicle to carry cyan lamps.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The modernization retains essential lighting functions, but its preamble and proposed visibility table reverse the intensity values for headlamps on and off. That narrow, repairable mismatch needs one consistent compliance requirement before finalization.

## Who Is Affected

Vehicle and lighting manufacturers, ADS fleets, test laboratories and State traffic authorities; passengers, pedestrians, cyclists and nearby drivers would encounter the resulting lights.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Resolve the visibility-table reversal.** The preamble and Table V-c assign opposite minima to headlamps on and off, so certification laboratories could use different requirements. **Question:** Which state-to-intensity mapping is intended, and will NHTSA align the table, explanation and test instructions?
- **Explain safety across permitted headlamp layouts.** Keeping existing height limits is a safeguard, but does not alone explain performance at the lowest allowed mounting positions. **Question:** What engineering evidence bounds seeing distance and glare for newly permitted layouts, including minimum-compliant lamps?
- **Clarify the transition for existing cyan lamps.** If-equipped limits may differ from current SAE-based implementations even though adding the equipment remains optional. **Question:** Would a prospective transition or different effective dates preserve flexibility while avoiding unexpected redesign burdens?
- **Supply practical lighting and cost evidence.** NHTSA explicitly seeks impact estimates and research on daylight rain, fog and marker signaling; these open questions are normal proposal design. **Question:** What test results and per-vehicle cost data would best support choosing the final brightness limits and SAE deviations?
