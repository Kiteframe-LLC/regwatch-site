## What This Document Does
- EPA proposes to undo part of its own July 22, 2020 San Joaquin Valley PM2.5 action.
- In 2020, EPA granted California a Clean Air Act section 188(e) extension that moved the Serious-area attainment date for the 2006 24-hour PM2.5 standard from December 31, 2019 to December 31, 2024.
- EPA now says that extension approval was erroneous after the Ninth Circuit's 2022 `Medical Advocates for Healthy Air` decision. The agency proposes to correct that action under CAA section 110(k)(6) and deny the extension request.
- EPA also proposes to find that the San Joaquin Valley failed to attain the 2006 24-hour PM2.5 standard by the original December 31, 2019 deadline.
- If EPA finalizes the proposal, California would have to submit a new SIP revision within one year under CAA sections 179(d) and 189(d), and contingency-measure requirements would also be triggered.

## Why It Matters
- This changes the legal status of the San Joaquin Valley's attainment deadline and resets what California must do next.
- EPA says the 2020 extension rested in part on an attainment demonstration and aggregate commitments that the Ninth Circuit later found inadequately supported, especially because EPA had not reasonably justified California's ability to meet incentive-based commitments given funding shortfalls.
- That matters because the extension was the reason EPA treated December 31, 2024 as the operative attainment date. If the extension is denied, the law snaps back to the original December 31, 2019 Serious-area deadline.
- EPA also says the valley still has not solved the underlying PM2.5 problem. The notice cites 2017-2019 regulatory monitoring data for the formal failure-to-attain finding and notes that 2022-2024 certified data still show a 2024 design value of 48 µg/m3.
- For commenters, the main leverage is whether EPA has clearly explained why the remand requires outright denial, whether the 2017-2019 monitoring record is presented transparently enough, and whether the final rule clearly explains what California must do next.

## Who Is Affected
- California Air Resources Board and San Joaquin Valley air regulators, because they would need to prepare a replacement SIP revision under sections 179(d) and 189(d).
- Residents of the San Joaquin Valley, especially communities exposed to high PM2.5 levels.
- Agricultural, industrial, freight, and other sectors whose future controls could be affected by a new attainment plan.
- Public-health groups, local governments, and environmental advocates that want a clearer final administrative record.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. Issue: EPA should explain more clearly why the Ninth Circuit remand requires denial of the extension request.
   - Why it matters: The most consequential move in the notice is EPA's decision that denial is now the right corrective endpoint.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Please explain why supplemental reasoning or a narrower correction would be legally insufficient, and why denial is the appropriate remedy under CAA section 110(k)(6).

2. Issue: The monitor-by-monitor basis for the 2019 failure-to-attain finding should be easier to audit.
   - Why it matters: EPA says the determination rests on regulatory monitors that meet siting, quality-assurance, and completeness rules, but the reviewed text does not provide a compact public-facing summary table.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Please provide a table listing each regulatory monitor used for the 2017-2019 determination, including completeness status, certifications, and resulting design values.

3. Issue: EPA should separate the formal 2019 attainment evidence from the later 2022-2024 context more clearly.
   - Why it matters: The later 2024 design value helps show the air-quality problem persists, but it is not the legal evidence for whether the area missed the December 31, 2019 deadline.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Please clarify in the final rule which findings rely on 2017-2019 data for the statutory missed-attainment determination and which later data are included only as context.

4. Issue: The practical consequences of the replacement SIP should be spelled out more clearly.
   - Why it matters: The NPRM says California must submit a new SIP revision within one year and implement contingency obligations, but readers will want clearer direction on what EPA expects from that next plan.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Please describe the main planning elements EPA expects in the follow-on SIP and how contingency-measure implementation interacts with that submission.
