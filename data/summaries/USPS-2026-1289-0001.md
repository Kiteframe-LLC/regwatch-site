## What This Document Does

USPS proposes new Domestic Mail Manual standards for mail-in and absentee ballots in federal general, special, and runoff elections. The rule would not cover primary elections or UOCAVA ballots.

The proposal would require outbound and return federal ballot envelopes to use the official Election Mail logo, be automation compatible, carry unique Intelligent Mail barcodes, and undergo USPS mailpiece design review. It would also create a Federal Ballot Mail Portal process. State-authorized users would submit each mail ballot recipient's name, address, outbound barcode, return barcode, and originating election-office state. USPS would compile that information into state-specific Mail-In and Absentee Participation Lists and provide each state's list to the chief election official on or about election day.

The rule also creates an acceptance-verification step. USPS would review outbound federal ballot mailings before acceptance to determine whether the mailing meets the proposed envelope and list-submission standards. Noncompliant outbound ballot mail would be returned to the authorized ballot mailer for correction.

## Why It Matters

The proposal is significant because it changes ballot-mail preparation from a set of USPS best practices into mandatory conditions for federal ballot mail acceptance. Some requirements, such as the Election Mail logo, automation compatibility, design review, and IMbs, fit closely with USPS mail-processing and tracking functions.

The more sensitive part is the proposed individualized list system. The rule would require election officials or authorized mail service providers to connect voter names and addresses with unique outbound and return barcodes. USPS says this will help law-enforcement efforts by allowing comparison of mailed ballots and received ballots. The notice does not yet provide much detail about privacy controls, data retention, access, correction procedures, portal reliability, or how ordinary election events such as spoiled ballots, replacement ballots, late additions, or nonmail ballot returns will be interpreted.

USPS also says technical instructions for barcode creation, service type identifiers, acceptance processes, file preparation, documentation submission, and portal entry will come with any final rule. Those details are central to whether the rule can work in real election calendars.

## Who Is Affected

- State chief election officials, who would authorize Ballot Portal Users and receive state-specific participation lists.
- Local election officials and authorized mail service providers that prepare and enter federal ballot mail.
- Voters who receive mail-in or absentee ballots for covered federal elections, especially if list or barcode errors affect outbound mailing.
- USPS acceptance personnel, who would review covered outbound ballot mail before acceptance.
- Privacy and election-security stakeholders concerned with voter-linked ballot-mail tracking data.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

1. Implementation details are deferred.
Why it matters: The notice says key technical instructions will arrive with the final rule, but those details determine whether election offices can comply.
Question to ask: Will USPS publish portal specifications, file formats, validation rules, testing procedures, and acceptance workflows for public comment before mandatory compliance?

2. Nonacceptance could create timing risk.
Why it matters: Returning noncompliant ballot mail may be reasonable for serious defects, but election-mail deadlines leave little room for late technical corrections.
Question to ask: What safe harbors, emergency escalation paths, and cure procedures will apply when barcode, portal, or certification errors are discovered near a mailing deadline?

3. Privacy and data governance are underdeveloped.
Why it matters: The proposal links individual voters to outbound and return ballot-mail barcodes. USPS notes that a Privacy Act system-of-records notice is forthcoming, but it is not in the available NPRM record.
Question to ask: What data fields are strictly necessary, how long will USPS retain them, who may access them, and what law-enforcement sharing limits will apply?

4. Alternatives are not meaningfully compared.
Why it matters: USPS does not analyze phased implementation, pilots, aggregate reporting, post-mailing reconciliation, data minimization, or special rules for small-volume mailers.
Question to ask: Why is pre-acceptance verification with individualized voter/barcode lists preferable to less disruptive alternatives?

5. The state-election boundary needs clarification.
Why it matters: USPS says states retain control over voter eligibility, but a mail-acceptance rejection can still affect the practical delivery of ballots.
Question to ask: How will USPS ensure that technical list or barcode errors do not operate as de facto voter-eligibility determinations?

6. The enforcement rationale needs more support.
Why it matters: USPS says participation lists will help compare mailed and received ballots, but normal election administration can create count differences for legitimate reasons.
Question to ask: How will USPS and election officials account for spoiled ballots, replacement ballots, undeliverable ballots, surrendered ballots, late-added voters, and ballots returned outside USPS?

## Evidence Quotes

- "Any state that intends to receive mail-in or absentee ballots from individual voters through the Postal Service must ensure that such individuals have been enrolled with the Postal Service for inclusion on the state's Mail-In and Absentee Participation List."
- "Mailings that do not comply with 24.5.1 and 24.5.2 will not be accepted and will be returned to the authorized ballot mailer."
- "Coinciding with publication of any final rule, to facilitate compliance by officials mailing ballots to voters, the Postal Service intends to provide further technical instructions regarding barcode creation, service type identifiers, acceptance processes, file preparation, documentation submission, and entry of data into the proposed portal."
- "The Postal Service does not purport to alter the eligibility of any individual to vote under state or federal law."
