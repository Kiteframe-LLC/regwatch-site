## What This Document Does

- EPA proposes significant new use rules (`SNURs`) for two proposed `40 CFR part 721` entries covering battery-related mixed metal oxide substances generically described as cobalt lithium manganese nickel oxide, metals-doped.
- The proposal would not ban the substances outright. Instead, it would require a significant new use notice (`SNUN`) at least 90 days before anyone manufactures or processes the substances in ways EPA treats as a significant new use.
- EPA says these chemicals were already reviewed through premanufacture notices (`PMNs`) and TSCA section `5(e)` orders. The new rule would extend those order-style protections beyond the original PMN submitters to any future manufacturer, processor, recycler, or reclaimer working with the same substances.
- The proposed restrictions are substantial. They include battery-only use, labels on batteries or packaging, `99%` dust-control efficiency, no release to water, hazardous-waste landfill or incineration disposal, a property-boundary air limit, hazard communication requirements, and a tiered respirator matrix tied to worker exposure levels.
- EPA also says some significant new uses are confidential. A prospective manufacturer or processor may need to use the bona fide intent process in `40 CFR 721.11` to learn whether a confidential use trigger applies.

## Why It Matters

- This is a classic TSCA section `5` move: EPA is taking restrictions it already imposed on specific PMN submitters and trying to make them the default rule for everyone else dealing with the same new substances.
- The practical effect is strong even though the action is framed as a notice rule. A firm can stay inside the order-style conditions or file a `SNUN` and wait for EPA review before doing something different.
- The visible record is mixed. The notice gives the core restrictions and the broad hazard rationale, including concerns about carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity, specific target organ toxicity, dermal and respiratory sensitization, and aquatic toxicity at very low concentrations. But much of the deeper support appears by reference to docket materials rather than full explanation in the preamble.
- That matters because the proposal uses exact thresholds and categorical limits. EPA does not just say “use controls are needed.” It says `99%` capture and control efficiency is required, releases to water are not allowed, and worker respirator requirements escalate across specific exposure bands.
- The public-facing economic discussion is also thin. EPA estimates about `$45,496` per `SNUN` for large businesses, `$14,976` for small businesses, and roughly `$106` per export notification, but it also says it cannot estimate how many export notifications are likely.
- The best comment opportunities are therefore about explanation and administrability: how EPA derived the exact thresholds, why battery-only use is the right boundary, how confidential significant new uses work in practice, and whether the burden discussion is too abstract.

## Who Is Affected

- Battery-material manufacturers and processors that might want to work with the covered mixed metal oxide substances.
- Importers, because TSCA import-certification rules can attach when a chemical is subject to TSCA controls.
- Recyclers and reclaimers, because EPA expressly says “processing” includes reclaiming substances from batteries or other items containing the PMN substance.
- Exporters, because TSCA section `12(b)` export-notification requirements apply to substances subject to proposed or final SNURs.
- Workers who could face inhalation or dermal exposure during manufacture, processing, recycling, packaging, or waste handling.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

1. EPA should explain how the technical evidence supports the exact thresholds it chose.
Why it matters: The notice gives precise requirements such as `99%` dust control, a `1.3E-4 mg/m3` boundary-air cap, and a detailed respirator matrix, but it does not fully walk readers through how the underlying assessment leads to each cutoff.
Practical question a commenter can ask: What parts of the standardized assessment or PMN record support each major numeric threshold in the proposed SNURs?

2. EPA should explain why battery-only use is the right use boundary.
Why it matters: The proposal treats any non-battery use as a significant new use, which is a major scope choice. The notice gives only a compressed explanation for why that line is appropriate.
Practical question a commenter can ask: What evidence shows that non-battery uses create materially different or less manageable exposure and release concerns than the proposed permitted battery use?

3. EPA should address how confidential significant new uses work for outside firms in practice.
Why it matters: If part of the rule depends on confidential use triggers, new entrants may face planning delays or uncertainty before they can know what EPA considers restricted.
Practical question a commenter can ask: How quickly and predictably can a prospective manufacturer or processor obtain a confidential significant-new-use determination through the bona fide process?

4. EPA should better explain why two separate CFR entries are needed for substances with the same generic chemical description.
Why it matters: Outside readers can see that the entries correspond to different PMN sets, but the notice does not fully explain the practical distinction between the two rule sections.
Practical question a commenter can ask: What substantive differences between the two PMN groupings justify separate SNUR entries rather than one combined rule treatment?

5. EPA should make the burden discussion more concrete.
Why it matters: Unit-cost estimates are useful, but they do not tell the public much if EPA cannot estimate how often firms are likely to face those burdens.
Practical question a commenter can ask: What information does EPA have about the likely number of future `SNUN`s or export notifications, even if it cannot provide a precise forecast?

## Evidence Quotes

- “The proposed SNURs extend the protective measures from the signed consent orders... to any person intending to manufacture, process, use, distribute in commerce, or dispose of the same new chemical substances.”
- “EPA identified concerns for carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity, specific target organ toxicity, and dermal and respiratory sensitization...”
- “No use of the PMN substance other than in the manufacture of batteries.”
- “No release of the PMN substance, or any waste stream containing the PMN substance, to water.”
- “While EPA is unable to make any estimate of the likely number of export notifications... the estimated cost of the export notification requirement on a per unit basis is approximately $106.”
