# Public Summary: SEC-2026-5617-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The SEC proposes to rescind rule 206(4)-5, which limits compensation and solicitation after certain political contributions, and to remove related books-and-records requirements in rule 204-2.

**Affected Entities**
- Investment advisers and covered associates
- Government entities and public pension plans
- Elected officials and candidates
- Investors, taxpayers, and public-sector beneficiaries
- Placement agents, broker-dealers, municipal advisers, and state procurement authorities

**Statutory Authority:** The notice invokes the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, including antifraud, fiduciary, compliance, code-of-ethics, and recordkeeping authorities, and discusses First Amendment considerations.

**Key Regulatory Change:** A prescriptive two-year compensation ban, solicitation restriction, coordination restriction, exceptions, exemptions, and related records would be removed and replaced by existing principles-based obligations.

**Observed Facts**
- The proposal retains federal antifraud and fiduciary obligations.
- The proposal would amend rule 204-2 to remove records specific to rule 206(4)-5.
- The notice includes economic analysis, alternatives, PRA, and RFA sections.

**Inference:** The proposal shifts primary pay-to-play prevention from an objective contribution-triggered rule toward adviser-designed compliance and enforcement after conduct is identified.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The Commission is proposing to rescind the political contribution rule ... in its entirety.
- Other existing requirements of the Advisers Act and its associated rules ... are likely sufficient to address pay-to-play practices.
- The Commission also is proposing to amend the rule under the Advisers Act pertaining to books and records consistent with the proposed rescission.
