# Public Summary: EPA-HQ-OPPT-2026-5974-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** Proposed TSCA section 5 SNURs would require a SNUN at least 90 days before a designated manufacture, import, or processing activity.

**Affected Entities**
- manufacturers, importers, processors, and downstream users
- exporters subject to TSCA section 12(b) notification
- small businesses facing SNUN fees and preparation costs
- workers, potentially exposed or susceptible subpopulations, and communities

**Statutory Authority:** TSCA sections 5(a)(2), 5(a)(1)(B), 5(e), 5(f)(4), 5(g), 12(b), and 13, with implementing regulations in 40 CFR part 721.

**Key Regulatory Change:** Deviations from underlying TSCA Order conditions would become significant new uses, extending pre-manufacture notification and EPA review beyond original PMN submitters.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice estimates per-SNUN costs of $45,496 for large businesses and $14,976 for small businesses.
- It estimates export notification cost at approximately $106 per unit but cannot estimate notification volume.
- It describes CBI significant new uses and a bona fide-intent identification process.
- It states that a SNUR is not itself a risk determination; EPA acts after receiving a SNUN.

**Inference:** The proposal is a notification and review gate whose fairness and feasibility depend on accessible, precise substance-specific Orders, evidence, and confidential-use procedures.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The issuance of a SNUR is not a risk determination itself, only a notification requirement.
- EPA believes it is necessary to follow a TSCA order with a SNUR.
- EPA is unable to estimate the likely number of export notifications.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal creates a pre-commencement gate for order-derived chemical controls, but the supplied record does not fully expose the substance-specific evidence, notice boundaries, or aggregate burden supporting the gate.
