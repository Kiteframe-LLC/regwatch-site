# Public Summary: OPM-2026-0166-0001

## What This Document Does

### Observed Facts
OPM proposes a ten-part conforming and modernization package. It would align schedules E, Policy/Career, and G with recent Executive orders; replace legacy part 302 lists and orders with agency procedures for identifying and considering priority candidates; authorize specified Pathways conversions; and update ALJ appointment and RIF rules. The notice identifies Federal agencies, applicants, employees, preference-eligible veterans, restoration-rights holders, Pathways participants, and ALJs as affected.

### Inference
The practical shift is from formalized, centralized procedural landmarks toward agency-level implementation judgment. The proposal is legally and administratively coherent on its face, but its benefits depend on common documentation, transition, and oversight controls.

### Evidence Quotes
- The proposed rule would replace legacy employment-list and order-of-consideration procedures in part 302 with a more direct framework.
- The annual consolidated notice is a more complete and useful transparency mechanism because it provides a single authoritative listing of agency-specific authorities.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is structurally coherent, but it shifts important visibility and priority-protection functions toward agency procedures whose minimum standards and public audit trail are not fully specified.

## Who Is Affected

- Federal agencies and their human-resources, staffing, and selecting officials
- Applicants and employees seeking excepted-service appointments or priority consideration
- Preference-eligible veterans, employees affected by RIF or furlough, and employees with compensable-injury restoration rights
- Pathways participants and recent graduates eligible for conversion
- Administrative law judges and agencies administering ALJ appointments and reductions in force

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **High — evidence request.** Demonstrate functional equivalence for priority candidates: provide a model workflow and minimum notice, eligibility, evaluation, exception, timestamp, retention, and review fields.
- **High — accountability safeguard.** Preserve timely public traceability after eliminating monthly notices: retain annual consolidation but add interim notices or a dated change log for new, modified, limited, and revoked authorities.
- **Moderate — evidence request.** Strengthen the implementation-cost analysis with the basis for the 100-hour average and a range covering agency size, HR-system complexity, training, coding, and legacy-list reconciliation.
- **Moderate — alternatives push.** Compare phased, hybrid, or pilot implementation options with a governmentwide minimum procedure to reduce reliance and cross-agency inconsistency.
