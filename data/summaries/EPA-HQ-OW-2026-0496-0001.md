# Public Summary: EPA-HQ-OW-2026-0496-0001

## What This Document Does

EPA proposes to let Utah issue and enforce Class VI permits for underground carbon-dioxide storage outside Indian lands. It would amend 40 CFR 147.2250 and incorporate specified state laws and rules. EPA would retain oversight and its enforcement authorities; individual projects would still need permits, technical review and public participation. Approval would transfer program administration, rather than authorize a particular storage project or establish a new national carbon-storage standard.

## Who Is Affected

Storage developers and operators would deal primarily with Utah DOGM. Groundwater users, nearby residents, pore-space and surface owners, and neighboring or Tribal governments have interests in site-specific notice, plume boundaries and long-term care. EPA retains primacy on Indian lands; nearby off-reservation projects may still require transboundary notification. Utah would manage the program and eventually qualifying closed facilities.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Which commitments control?** The notice cites older description and inconsistent MOA dates. A definitive inventory makes the approval reviewable. **Question:** Will EPA identify the approved versions and correct the docket number, CFR cross-reference and MOA dates?
- **Explain closure and liability release.** State law transfers responsibility after certification; R644 first requires approved closure and non-endangerment. **Question:** Will EPA explain how those provisions preserve every applicable federal obligation and require adequate long-term reserves?
- **Show startup capacity and reporting assignments.** The plan has fees and contractor roles, but startup contingencies and overlapping reporting schedules deserve clarity. **Question:** Will EPA publish committed specialist availability, a low-revenue contingency and a table of who reports what and when?
- **Make permit priority transparent.** The state favors Utah-produced CO₂ alongside timing and completeness. Applicants need predictable treatment. **Question:** Will EPA explain the preference’s scope and require published queue criteria, reasons, review paths and unchanged technical safeguards?
