# Public Summary: FTC-2026-1453-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The notice invites comments on a petition asking the FTC to consider rulemaking or other policy action concerning transparency, explanations, automated decision systems, record preservation, corrections, and meaningful review in material consumer decisions.

**Affected Entities**
- Consumers subject to material decisions
- Businesses and service providers within possible FTC jurisdiction
- Operators of automated or technology-assisted decision systems
- Data, fraud-prevention, compliance, and customer-service functions
- The FTC and other regulators that might share jurisdiction

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites 15 U.S.C. 46, 15 U.S.C. 57a, FTC Rule 1.31(f), and 5 U.S.C. 601 note. The supplement asks for action only within statutory authority.

**Key Regulatory Change:** No regulatory change is proposed by the FTC in this notice. The attached supplement proposes model provisions requiring explanations, automated-decision notice, human review, records, corrections, and truthful descriptions of review rights.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice is a petition receipt and comment invitation.
- The proposed text defines covered consumer decisions and material adverse decisions broadly.
- The proposed text includes exceptions for legally protected information, privacy, security, law enforcement, trade secrets, and entities outside FTC jurisdiction.

**Inference:** The most useful comment record would separate the policy case for usable explanations from the distinct questions of jurisdiction, covered decisions, evidence, burden, and remedy design.

**Unknown**
- Whether the FTC will initiate rulemaking, refer the issues, issue guidance, or deny the petition.
- Which sectors, decision types, and existing statutes the Commission believes are within reach.
- The prevalence and measurable effects of the practices described in the petition.

**Evidence Quotes**
- This document is not intended to start, stop, cancel, or otherwise affect rulemaking proceedings in any way.
- A covered consumer decision means a decision materially affecting a consumer's payment obligation, refund, eligibility, account access, consumer financial information, purchase or service access, or another material consumer economic interest within the Commission's jurisdiction.
