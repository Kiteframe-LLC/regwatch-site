# Public Summary: USCIS-2026-0529-0001

## What This Document Does

DHS proposes to remove 8 CFR 214.1(c)(5), ending the required deference posture for Form I-129 extension requests while allowing officers to consider prior approvals where relevant. The notice cites INA sections 103(a), 214, 235(d)(3), 287(b), and 291, plus related Homeland Security Act provisions.

**Observed Facts:** The rule applies to all Form I-129 benefit requests; its principal quantitative evidence concerns deference-eligible petitions and selected H-1B/L-1A data. It estimates 6,044 additional RFEs and $133,987 in annual petitioner opportunity costs, while leaving delay and other costs unquantified.

**Inference:** The practical change concerns the starting posture and intensity of extension review more than the formal eligibility standard.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is structurally coherent, but its universal scope and removal of a review posture are supported by evidence and alternatives analysis that remain incomplete, with limited safeguards for delay and inconsistent implementation.

## Who Is Affected

Employers and other petitioners filing Form I-129 extension requests; nonimmigrant workers and beneficiaries whose employment or authorized stay depends on those petitions; USCIS adjudicators; and, indirectly, small entities and employers that rely on timely extensions.
