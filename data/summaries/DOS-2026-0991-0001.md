# Public Summary: DOS-2026-0991-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory action:** The Department proposes to narrow selected USML controls, revise ITAR classification and lifecycle definitions, clarify defense-article exclusions, and add § 123.26 for specified temporary exports to an OEM or authorized service provider.

**Statutory authority:** The notice relies on section 38 of the Arms Export Control Act and 22 U.S.C. 2778.

**Observed Fact:** The notice says the USML review uses interagency, public-feedback, commodity-jurisdiction, advisory-opinion, and technology-monitoring inputs; it also says quantitative estimates are unavailable.

**Inference:** The main comment leverage is whether the proposed boundaries are reproducible and whether the servicing exemption has enough accountability.

**Evidence:** The notice says the changes would remove items from the USML, and that quantitative analyses are not available.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is structurally coherent but materially under-specifies the public evidence, transition rules, and accountability mechanisms supporting a broad high-stakes reclassification package.

- Defense manufacturers, suppliers, exporters, reexporters, and technology developers

- OEMs and authorized service providers handling foreign defense articles
