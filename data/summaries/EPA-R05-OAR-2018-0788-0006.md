## What This Document Does

- EPA proposes to approve the remaining visibility-related part of Indiana's infrastructure SIP for the `2015 ozone` air-quality standard.
- The proposal is limited to `Prong 4` under Clean Air Act section `110(a)(2)(D)(i)(II)`. That prong is about whether emissions from one state interfere with visibility-protection measures required in another state's SIP.
- EPA says Indiana's second-period regional haze SIP was fully approved on `January 26, 2026`, and that this approval now lets EPA conclude Indiana satisfies Prong 4 for the `2015 ozone NAAQS`.
- The notice does not propose new emission limits, new monitoring duties, or a new technical program for Indiana sources. It mainly closes a legal gap left open when EPA finalized most of Indiana's broader `2015 ozone` infrastructure SIP action in `2022`.
- EPA's logic is straightforward: a fully approved regional haze SIP that meets `40 CFR 51.308`, including interstate consultation requirements, is enough to satisfy Prong 4 under EPA's infrastructure-SIP guidance.

## Why It Matters

- This is a narrow but real approval. It matters because it completes a missing piece of Indiana's interstate-visibility showing for the `2015 ozone` standard.
- The notice appears legally routine. EPA is not redoing a major modeling exercise here. Instead, it is leaning on an earlier action: Indiana's regional haze SIP approval.
- That means the main question for commenters is not whether EPA has authority to act at all. The better question is whether the notice explains the link between the haze approval and this Prong 4 approval clearly enough for outside readers to audit.
- EPA says its `2013 Guidance` allows Prong 4 to be satisfied when a state has a fully approved regional haze SIP meeting `40 CFR 51.308`. That is the core premise of the whole proposal.
- The notice also says Indiana's regional haze SIP was fully approved on `January 26, 2026`. If that earlier approval really did resolve interstate visibility obligations, then this proposal is probably a clean follow-through step.
- The weakness is explanatory compression. The current notice gives only a brief statement of the bridge from the `2026` regional haze approval to this docket's conclusion. It does not spend much space identifying the specific findings from the haze action that now justify Prong 4 approval.
- That does not necessarily make the proposal wrong. It does mean the final rule would be stronger if EPA explained the connection more directly and stated plainly why the issue remained unresolved in `2022` but is approvable now.

## Who Is Affected

- Indiana air regulators, because the action would complete EPA's review of the remaining visibility piece of the state's `2015 ozone` infrastructure SIP submission.
- EPA Region 5, which is using Indiana's regional haze approval as the basis for the Prong 4 determination.
- Communities, state agencies, and organizations in downwind or visibility-sensitive areas that want the interstate visibility logic explained clearly in the public record.
- Commenters who focus on SIP completeness, interstate consultation, and whether EPA has made the administrative record easy to follow across related actions.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

1. EPA should identify the specific findings from the `January 26, 2026` regional haze approval that it believes satisfy Prong 4 here.  
Why it matters: The proposal's central reasoning depends on that prior approval, but the current notice gives only a short summary of the bridge.  
Practical question a commenter can ask: Which findings in the regional haze approval demonstrate that Indiana emissions will not interfere with visibility-protection measures in other states' SIPs?

2. EPA should explain the sequencing more clearly.  
Why it matters: The public-facing record is easier to evaluate when EPA explains why Indiana's `2018` infrastructure SIP could not be fully approved in `2022` but can be approved now after the later haze action.  
Practical question a commenter can ask: What changed between the `2022` no-action posture on visibility and this `2026` proposal, and why is that change sufficient to resolve Prong 4?
