# Public Summary: FS-2026-0100-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** Notice of intent to prepare an EIS and consider revisions to Forest Service travel-management regulations, including a national access presumption, revised local designation procedures, and specified vehicle and use exemptions.

**Affected Entities**
- National Forest System visitors and recreation users
- motorized and nonmotorized recreation groups
- forest-dependent and rural communities
- Tribes and Alaska Native Corporations
- conservation and wildlife interests
- Forest Service responsible officials and field staff
- state, local, and federal cooperating agencies
- landowners and holders of access or valid existing rights

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites 16 U.S.C. 551 and, as applicable, 7 U.S.C. 1011(f), and references NEPA, NFMA, the Clean Water Act, Clean Air Act, ESA, National Trails System Act, NHPA, Wilderness Act, the EXPLORE Act, and valid existing rights.

**Key Regulatory Change:** No final regulatory change is made by this notice. It opens the record for an EIS and prospective rulemaking that would replace or revise parts of the existing Travel Management Rule and related provisions.

**Observed Facts**
- The proposal would remove national minimization criteria and establish access and closure criteria tied to documented conditions, safety, conflicts, and capacity.
- Existing MVUM and OSVUM designations would remain in effect until revised, except for expressly self-executing exemptions.
- The notice lists alternatives involving route evidence, public involvement, monitoring, e-bikes, off-route uses, over-snow travel, and airfields.
- No separate public scoping process or scoping meetings are planned.

**Inference:** The notice is an early, access-oriented policy solicitation whose strongest leverage points are baseline evidence, safeguards, alternatives, and implementation criteria.

**Evidence Quotes**

- specific, documented and justifiable reasons
- annual review of all trails, roads, airfields, trailheads, and other access routes and points
- No separate public scoping process or scoping meetings are planned
