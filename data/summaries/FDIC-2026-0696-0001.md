## What This Document Does

- This proposal would create the FDIC's main implementing rule for stablecoin activities under the GENIUS Act for FDIC-supervised issuers and certain custodians.
- It would set requirements for reserve assets, capital, liquidity, risk management, disclosures, and custody/safekeeping practices for FDIC-supervised permitted payment stablecoin issuers.
- It would also add custody rules requiring customer assets, including private keys and stablecoin reserves, to be treated as the customer's property and protected from commingling or creditor claims.
- The notice also makes two especially important legal clarifications. First, reserve deposits backing a payment stablecoin would be insured to the issuer as a corporate depositor, not to stablecoin holders on a pass-through basis. Second, tokenized deposits would still be treated as deposits rather than as payment stablecoins.
- The FDIC says it has tried to align this proposal with related federal stablecoin rulemakings where relevant and specifically asks for comment on whether the final rules should be more closely harmonized.

## Why It Matters

- This is a foundational banking-law proposal for how FDIC-supervised institutions could issue and custody payment stablecoins. It is not a narrow housekeeping amendment.
- The proposal matters because it draws legal boundaries that market participants may otherwise misunderstand, especially around reserve-account insurance and the difference between a stablecoin and a tokenized bank deposit.
- The notice is strongest on legal authority and overall structure. It explains that the GENIUS Act requires implementing rules and that the FDIC has separate authority to clarify deposit-insurance treatment.
- The record is weaker on implementation proof. The FDIC provides some burden estimates and labor assumptions, but the notice does not do much to show why a principles-based regime is the best calibrated approach for a technically new market.
- That creates useful comment leverage. Commenters do not need to oppose the whole rule to make a valuable submission. They can ask for clearer examples, tighter burden support, and a more explicit explanation of how the FDIC's approach fits with parallel OCC and other federal rules.

## Who Is Affected

- FDIC-supervised permitted payment stablecoin issuers are affected directly because the proposal would govern reserves, liquidity, capital, governance, disclosures, and related compliance expectations.
- Insured depository institutions that act as custodians, sub-custodians, or reserve banks are also affected because the proposal addresses customer-asset segregation, private-key control, commingling limits, and reserve-deposit treatment.
- Stablecoin holders and customers are affected indirectly because the rule shapes how institutions describe reserves, custody, and insurance status.
- Institutions operating across multiple charters or affiliates may be especially affected if the FDIC's final approach does not line up cleanly with OCC, FRB, or NCUA stablecoin rules.
- Commenters with practical experience in custody operations, bank resolutions, reserve-account design, consumer disclosures, or digital-asset controls are well positioned to add useful evidence.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- The proposal relies heavily on a "principles-based" structure for novel stablecoin activity.
Why it matters: That may give the FDIC flexibility, but it can also leave institutions uncertain about what concrete controls, disclosures, and reserve practices will satisfy supervisors.
Question a commenter can ask: Can the FDIC add examples or more specific standards for reserve management, custody controls, and disclosure practices?

- The notice says reserve deposits would be insured to the issuer, not to stablecoin holders on a pass-through basis.
Why it matters: This is legally important and easy for the public or even market participants to misunderstand, especially in stress or failure scenarios.
Question a commenter can ask: Can the FDIC provide examples showing how different reserve-account structures would be treated if an insured bank failed?

- The burden analysis may be too thin for a rule this operationally complex.
Why it matters: The notice includes burden assumptions, but reserve reporting, audit readiness, disclosure production, and custody-control changes could require more work than the rule suggests.
Question a commenter can ask: What worked examples support the FDIC's burden estimates for issuers, custodians, and related insured institutions?

- The proposal asks about interagency alignment but does not fully map differences.
Why it matters: Stablecoin businesses and affiliated banking groups may face avoidable compliance fragmentation if federal regulators adopt materially different approaches.
Question a commenter can ask: Where does the FDIC intentionally diverge from the OCC proposal or other federal stablecoin rules, and why?

- The RFA discussion may not fully address smaller institutions involved as custodians or reserve banks.
Why it matters: Even if the FDIC expects no small PPSIs, smaller insured institutions could still face implementation work under related custody or reserve arrangements.
Question a commenter can ask: Should the final rule separately analyze burdens on smaller institutions participating in custody or reserve functions?

## Evidence Quotes

- "The proposed rule aims to establish a tailored, principles-based regulatory regime for FDIC-supervised PPSIs and FDIC-supervised custodians, consistent with the GENIUS Act, to support the responsible growth and use of digital assets and related technologies in the banking sector."
- "the FDIC seeks comment on the extent to which the primary Federal payment stablecoin regulators should further align in their final rules to promote consistency of regulations applicable to all PPSIs subject to the GENIUS Act."
- "deposits held as reserves backing a payment stablecoin would be insured to the PPSI under the FDIC's coverage rules for corporate deposits, but would not be insured to payment stablecoin holders on a pass-through basis."
- "The FDIC estimates that none of these PPSIs will be small for purposes of the Regulatory Flexibility Act."
- "FDIC-supervised custodians are expected to incur approximately one hour of burden per year."
