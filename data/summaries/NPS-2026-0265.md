# Public Summary: NPS-2026-0265-0001

## What This Document Does

The NPS proposes to open Castle Mountains and the original Craters of the Moon monument to hunting under state and federal law. Hunting already occurs in adjoining lands and the Craters preserve; those existing opportunities are distinct from this opening. Proposed protections include a ban on hunting listed species, a Castle Mountains watering-place buffer and intended mine buffer, and short-range weapons plus a northern closure at Craters. Superintendents could impose additional protective restrictions. These are proposed changes, not current permission to hunt.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposed opening has a recognizable management rationale, but the Castle Mountains text does not consistently implement it. The named unit, mine-buffer sentence, and retrieval cross-references need correction before hunters and staff can rely on the safeguards.

## Who Is Affected

- Hunters seeking new access, and non-hunting visitors sharing newly opened lands.
- NPS staff and volunteers, mine workers, and California and Idaho wildlife managers implementing restrictions.
- Tribes with cultural-resource or treaty interests; nearby recreation businesses may experience indirect effects.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Correct the operative text.** The CAMO authorization names a Preserve, the mine-buffer sentence is incomplete, and retrieval references do not match the spatial restrictions. These affect clear permissions and safety. **Question:** Will NPS correct the unit name, complete the mine prohibition, and align the retrieval paragraphs before finalizing?
- **Make pending reviews usable.** Environmental documentation and formal consultation remain prospective in the notice. Their findings could affect protective conditions, especially under the shortened schedule. This does not establish a procedural violation. **Question:** When will NPS release the categorical-exclusion basis and consultation-informed changes, and allow comment on material new information?
- **Show local effects.** Unchanged regional quotas and nearby hunting opportunities do not show where hunters will relocate. Local wildlife disturbance, visitor encounters, and staffing needs could differ from regional totals. **Question:** What local use estimates and overlap information support minimal effects, and what monitoring would trigger additional restrictions?
- **Explain protective choices.** Short-range weapons and the northern closure have clear qualitative rationales. Seasonal or spatial refinements may address remaining visitor overlap without frustrating the opening. The one-mile mine boundary also needs a measurement reference. **Question:** Will NPS explain residual risks, compare feasible seasonal refinements, and publish usable boundary guidance?
