## What This Document Does

- This proposal would clean up NCUA's share-insurance regulations by deleting many sections in 12 CFR part 741 that mostly act as pointers to requirements already written elsewhere in the NCUA rulebook.
- It does not appear to change the underlying compliance obligations for federally insured credit unions. The agency says the goal is to make the regulations shorter and easier to navigate.
- Two pieces of language would be moved rather than simply removed:
- The 35-day notice requirement when a federally insured credit union's fidelity bond coverage is terminated would be placed directly into § 713.3(c).
- Consultation language for federally insured state-chartered credit unions (FISCUs) when officials or senior executive officers change would be placed into § 701.14(c)(4).
- The Board is also specifically asking for feedback on whether deleting § 741.213 and § 741.218 would leave too much uncertainty about which part 747 and part 709 provisions apply to FISCUs.

## Why It Matters

- This is a housekeeping NPRM, but housekeeping rules can still matter if they remove text that people use as a map.
- Part 741 has historically helped FISCUs identify which rules outside part 741 apply to them. If those signposts disappear, the rulebook may become shorter but not necessarily easier to use.
- The strongest public-interest question is not whether NCUA can streamline its regulations. It is whether the agency should pair that streamlining with an official crosswalk, reference list, or clarifying amendments so users are not left to reconstruct applicability on their own.
- The record here is thin but not suspiciously so. There are no supporting technical documents in the export, and this kind of drafting-focused proposal does not obviously require a cost model or engineering appendix. Still, the notice gives little concrete evidence for the claim that deleting the cross-reference provisions will improve navigability in practice.

## Who Is Affected

- Federally insured state-chartered credit unions are the most directly affected because part 741 often serves as their guide to which NCUA requirements apply to them.
- Federal credit unions are less central to the proposal, but some moved or clarified provisions still matter to them.
- State supervisors, trade groups, compliance vendors, auditors, and internal compliance staff may need to update manuals, crosswalks, and citation references if the rule is finalized.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- Issue: The proposal says deleting cross-reference sections will make the rules easier to navigate, but the notice does not show how NCUA reached that judgment.
Why it matters: A shorter rulebook is not automatically a clearer one, especially for FISCUs that may use part 741 as a centralized applicability guide.
Question a commenter can ask: Will NCUA publish an official crosswalk or maintained reference list showing where each deleted part 741 provision now points?

- Issue: The notice treats §§ 741.213 and 741.218 as removable, but it also acknowledges that those sections may currently provide meaningful clarity about which provisions apply to FISCUs.
Why it matters: If those sections disappear without substitute text, readers may have more trouble determining whether certain liquidation or hearing provisions apply to FISCUs or only to FCUs.
Question a commenter can ask: Which specific clarifying amendments to parts 709 and 747, if any, does NCUA expect to adopt in the final rule to preserve FISCU applicability clarity?

- Issue: The Regulatory Flexibility Act certification is plausible, but the notice barely discusses transition costs from reorganizing the rule text.
Why it matters: Even where substantive duties do not change, small institutions and service providers may still need to revise manuals, training, compliance software, and citation references.
Question a commenter can ask: What is NCUA's basis for concluding that transition burdens on small credit unions and related compliance actors will be minimal?

- Issue: NCUA says it may create a reference list outside the regulations, but that possibility is left informal and undefined.
Why it matters: Informal guidance can help, but commenters may want assurance that any replacement aid will be durable, complete, and easy to find.
Question a commenter can ask: If NCUA chooses an off-code reference list instead of retaining a regulatory crosswalk, how will that document be maintained and communicated to affected institutions?
