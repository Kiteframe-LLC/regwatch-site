# Public Summary: IRS-2026-1420-0001

## What This Document Does

**Observed Facts:** Proposed section 36B rules make PTC treatment depend on Exchange verification and revise mixed-status reporting, repayment, and family-size SEP rules. **Inference:** Request data, transition, and correction safeguards.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is generally coherent, but it makes cross-system verification and data quality consequential for health-coverage subsidies without fully specifying safeguards, transition rules, or supporting impact evidence.

- **Verification:** The proposal makes Exchange verification a condition of PTC coverage-month treatment while deferring much Exchange implementation analysis to HHS. Verification may improve program integrity, but an uncorrected data or timing failure could reduce a household's subsidy or increase tax liability. **Worth a comment:** high. **Comment posture:** evidence request.
- **Family-size SEP waiver:** The notice estimates that roughly 50,000 newborn families benefit, then extends the waiver concept to several other family events. The record should show why newborn assumptions apply across those categories or compare conditional and staged alternatives. **Worth a comment:** high. **Comment posture:** alternatives push.
- **Distribution and reporting:** The proposal affects about 15.6 million Exchange enrollees but limits some revised reporting to taxpayers for whom APTC was paid. The agency should compare system savings with taxpayer and issuer burden outside that population, especially for mixed-status households. **Worth a comment:** moderate. **Comment posture:** collaborative clarification.

## Who Is Affected

- Individuals and families enrolled in Exchange coverage, including households seeking or receiving APTC
- Families with members whose immigration or eligible-alien status affects PTC computation
- Individuals affected by Medicaid community-engagement determinations
- State-Based Exchanges and issuers that verify eligibility and report Form 1095-A data
- Tax preparers and the IRS systems that reconcile Forms 1095-A and 8962

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Issue:** Verification should not convert routine data latency into a tax-credit loss without measurable safeguards.
  
  **Why it matters:** The proposal makes Exchange verification central to coverage-month treatment, but does not state error rates, service standards, or a complete correction pathway.
  
  **Ask:** Publish performance evidence and add provisional treatment, notice, reason codes, and correction protections for administrative failures.
- **Issue:** Treasury should coordinate the tax consequences with the forthcoming CMS implementation record.
  
  **Why it matters:** The notice defers Exchange operational costs and effects to HHS even though Exchange procedures determine whether a month qualifies for PTC treatment.
  
  **Ask:** Publish a joint transition crosswalk, implementation milestones, and an interim rule for periods when CMS and Exchange procedures are changing.
- **Issue:** The broad family-size waiver needs category-specific support.
  
  **Why it matters:** The principal quantitative analysis concerns newborns, while the waiver covers several other events with potentially different timing and data risks.
  
  **Ask:** Provide event-specific evidence or adopt a conditional, staged, or narrower waiver with later verification.
- **Issue:** The narrower eligible-alien reporting rule may shift burden to taxpayers outside the APTC population.
  
  **Why it matters:** The notice quantifies Exchange burden for a broader alternative but not taxpayer-side burden under the selected scope.
  
  **Ask:** Compare total system and taxpayer burden and provide a standardized worksheet or issuer correction process if the narrower rule is retained.
