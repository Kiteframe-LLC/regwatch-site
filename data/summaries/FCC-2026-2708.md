# Public Summary: FCC-2026-2708-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The FCC seeks comment on multi-party interstate NG911 interoperability testing and on a framework for Direct Video Calling (DVC) into NG911.

**Affected Entities**
- NGCS and ESInet providers
- 911 Authorities and PSAPs
- Originating service providers and other network operators
- Deaf and hard-of-hearing callers, ASL users, and small entities that must support the new workflow

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites the Communications Act, the Wireless Communications and Public Safety Act, and section 106 of the Twenty-First Century Communications and Video Accessibility Act.

**Observed Facts**
- The FCC says NG911 interoperability remains foundational to the transition.
- The notice proposes testing transfer of 911 traffic to facilities in at least two other states and with three similar providers.
- The FCC is not aware of any 911 Authorities using DVC to answer calls today.
- The notice asks for comment on costs, benefits, technical capabilities, operational standards, and small-entity burden.

**Inference:** The document is a comment-stage proposal that tries to turn interoperability and accessibility into testable, cross-jurisdictional requirements, but the DVC side is still exploratory and depends on later record development.

**Evidence Quotes**
- "propose requiring NGCS and ESInet providers"
- "define a 'successful transfer'"
- "signal their readiness"
- "not aware of any 911 Authorities"

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- The FCC should ground DVC readiness in actual 911 deployment data before treating it as a general 911 option.
  Why it matters: a readiness framework without deployment evidence risks authorizing a feature before the operating model is ready.
  Ask the agency to: show the current DVC-to-911 deployment baseline, routing prerequisites, and the minimum readiness checklist.
  Practical question: what evidence shows a jurisdiction can safely accept DVC-to-911 calls today?
- The FCC should explain how the interoperability test will detect real failures rather than only nominal compliance.
  Why it matters: if the test is too procedural, it may not expose the routing and metadata problems the rule is supposed to solve.
  Ask the agency to: define failure cases, verification steps, and how different provider architectures will be compared.
  Practical question: what will cause a provider to fail the proposed successful-transfer test?
- The FCC should compare the proposed timetable against a phased or pilot-based rollout.
  Why it matters: the notice keeps the implementation schedule open, which is the right moment to ask for a narrower or lower-risk path.
  Ask the agency to: analyze phased compliance, pilots, and risk-tiered alternatives before locking the final timetable.
  Practical question: why is a single three-year frame better than a phased rollout for harder-to-serve jurisdictions?
- The FCC should quantify staffing and small-entity burden before finalizing DVC-to-911 expectations.
  Why it matters: the rule could be easy for large systems and difficult for smaller jurisdictions or providers that lack ASL-capable staffing.
  Ask the agency to: provide a burden table by provider class and explain what staffing and training level is actually required.
  Practical question: what evidence shows smaller providers can absorb the new workload without service disruption?
