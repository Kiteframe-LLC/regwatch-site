# Public Summary: CMS-2026-3631-0001

## What This Document Does

This proposal would remove selected training, OB quality-improvement, vaccine-education and reporting requirements; add consent and release procedures for birth tissues from live births; update incorporated fire, facility and emergency-power codes; and revise CAH location and nursing-home physical-environment rules. It retains general quality and infection-control duties, resident vaccine protections, and ICF/IID direct-care compensation reporting. Embedded RFIs ask about transplant-program entry, CRNA supervision and hospital infection-data reporting; they do not themselves change those policies.

Observed Fact: CMS proposes changes to participation and payment-transparency rules under provider-specific Social Security Act authorities. Inference: benefits depend on whether remaining safeguards deliver comparable protection and saved time actually reaches patient care.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal has a concrete burden-reduction rationale and retains substantial safeguards. The clearest structural concerns are loss of comparable oversight information before replacement methods are validated, and future safety-code substitutions through guidance without a fully defined public review process.

## Who Is Affected

Patients giving birth and their families; nursing-home residents and direct care workers; people with intellectual disabilities; hospitals and CAHs; nursing facilities and ICFs/IID; State Medicaid agencies; Ombudsman offices; and safety-code users across hospices, ASCs, PACE, REHs, dialysis facilities and religious nonmedical health care institutions.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Keep outbreak readiness while cutting reporting work.** Voluntary data and PHE-only reporting may leave gaps before a declaration and after staff turnover. The notice already considers reduced frequency, making a practical comparison possible. **Question:** Could automated or lower-frequency reporting preserve representative coverage, and what activation-time and coverage benchmarks would CMS require?
- **Validate payment-transparency replacements.** CMS retains ICF/IID direct-care reporting but would remove three other categories. Existing data may reduce burden; feasibility work does not yet establish accuracy or timely public access. **Question:** Will CMS publish a facility-level validation pilot, error estimates and a timetable before ending the nursing-facility measures?
- **Make later-code waivers reviewable.** Newer safety provisions could offer useful flexibility. Guidance-based adoption needs transparent findings, version-compatibility checks and a clear way to challenge or withdraw unsafe substitutions. **Question:** What public findings, notice, review and transition procedures will govern each equal-or-superior safety determination?
- **Use actual eligible deliveries to estimate consent costs.** The model allocates delivery hospitalizations using shares of all hospital beds. This may undercount encounters while existing consent practices could reduce marginal work. Both effects matter to reliable costs. **Question:** Can CMS replace bed shares with delivery counts by participating facility and separately measure existing consent coverage and tissue-release demand?
- **Retain measurable maternal-care protection.** General QAPI, accreditation and educational resources may not reach the same staff or preserve subgroup outcome review. Removing paperwork could help rural access, but equivalent coverage needs evidence. **Question:** What minimum competency and OB outcome checks would remain, and how do narrower training or rural exemptions compare with full repeal?
