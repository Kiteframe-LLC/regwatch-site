# Public Summary: CMS-2026-1024-0002

## What This Document Does
- Updates Medicare payment rates for inpatient rehabilitation facilities (IRFs) for federal fiscal year 2027.
- Proposes lowering the high-cost outlier threshold from `$10,141` to `$8,689` to keep outlier payments at about 3 percent of total IRF payments.
- Proposes several operational rule changes: all therapy treatments or evaluations must begin within 36 hours from midnight on the day of admission; the preadmission screening must document current functional status; and the first interdisciplinary team meeting must occur by Day 4 of admission.
- Proposes IRF Quality Reporting Program deadline changes beginning with FY 2029.
- Includes requests for information on future IRF payment reform, possible alternative data sources for an IRF-specific wage index, and future IRF QRP measure concepts.

## Why It Matters
- The notice estimates a **$355 million increase** in aggregate FY 2027 payments to IRFs, so the annual payment update is financially meaningful even though many of the mechanics are routine.
- The main comment opportunities are not broad attacks on CMS authority. They are narrower questions about whether CMS has fully justified the exact timing and documentation requirements it chose.
- CMS offers some support for those choices. It says **2.1 to 3.8 percent** of IRF patients discharged between FY 2015 and FY 2023 had **zero** interdisciplinary team meetings during their stay under the current weekly-meeting structure, and that requiring the first IDT meeting by Day 4 would reduce that to **1 percent**.
- CMS also says **99.08 percent** of IRF-PAI assessments were already submitted within about 45 days, and that only **0.72 percent** would be affected by the faster QRP deadline. That still leaves an open question about whether the burden is concentrated in smaller or lower-capacity facilities.

## Who Is Affected
- Inpatient rehabilitation hospitals and inpatient rehabilitation units paid under the IRF PPS.
- Clinical and administrative staff involved in therapy scheduling, preadmission screening, interdisciplinary care planning, and IRF QRP reporting.
- Facilities with complex admission timing, lean staffing models, or weaker reporting capacity, because the therapy, IDT, and deadline changes may be harder for them to absorb than for larger systems.
- Unknown from available text: the full facility-by-facility distribution of burden from the Day-4 IDT requirement, the therapy-initiation clarification, and the accelerated reporting deadlines.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. The Day-4 interdisciplinary team meeting rule may rest on simplified burden assumptions.
- Why it matters: CMS estimates benefits from earlier coordinated review, but its cost model assumes about 5 minutes per patient and a roughly `$399.06` cost per extra 60-minute meeting. That may understate real staffing and scheduling burden.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: What happens to the estimated cost if meeting length, patient complexity, or required staff participation is higher than CMS assumed?

2. The all-therapies-within-36-hours clarification may be more rigid than necessary.
- Why it matters: CMS says older guidance created ambiguity about whether one therapy or all therapies had to begin within 36 hours. Clarifying that point may improve consistency, but commenters can ask whether some admissions need narrow exceptions.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: Should CMS allow documented exceptions for weekend admissions, late-day admissions, or clinical situations where one therapy cannot reasonably start within the same window?

3. The new preadmission-screening documentation requirement may need stronger evidence.
- Why it matters: CMS says documenting current functional status will help build a more complete picture of a patient's rehabilitation trajectory. That is plausible, but the public-facing text does not strongly quantify the current problem.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: What evidence shows that the current rule leads to poor admission decisions or weaker plans of care because current functional status is not already being documented often enough?

4. The QRP deadline change may have uneven effects even if the aggregate burden looks small.
- Why it matters: CMS says only 0.72 percent of IRF-PAI assessments would be affected by the shorter submission window, but the notice does not clearly show whether those late submissions cluster in certain types of facilities.
- Practical question a commenter can ask: Please provide subgroup analysis showing whether rural, smaller, or lower-capacity IRFs account for a disproportionate share of the submissions that miss the proposed faster deadline.

## Evidence Quotes
- “The overall economic impact of this proposed rule is an estimated $355 million increase in payments from the Federal Government to IRFs during FY 2027.”
- “We propose to update the outlier threshold amount from $10,141 for FY 2026 to $8,689 for FY 2027 ...”
- “Sub-regulatory guidance that was posted by CMS in 2010 may have created ambiguous policy interpretation as to whether only one therapy or all therapies must be initiated within 36 hours ...”
- “If we assume that IRFs hold a formal IDT meeting on a weekly basis ... an estimated range of 2.1 to 3.8 percent of IRF patients ... experience zero IDT meetings during their stay.”
