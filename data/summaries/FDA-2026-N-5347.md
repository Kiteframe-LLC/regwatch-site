# Public Summary: FDA-2026-N-5347-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** FDA proposes method-neutral terminology and definitions for selected nonclinical-study provisions in 21 CFR parts 312, 314, 315, 361, and 601.

**Affected Entities**
- Sponsors and applicants for human drugs and biological products
- Developers of new approach methodologies and other nonanimal testing methods
- Patients and the public who depend on reliable preclinical and nonclinical safety evidence

**Statutory Authority:** FDA cites FD&C Act sections 505 and 701, FDORA section 3209, PHS Act section 351, and delegated HHS authority.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The rule replaces animal, preclinical, and in vitro references with nonclinical terminology while retaining existing FDA adequacy review and adding no testing mandate.

**Observed Facts**
- The proposed definition expressly lists cell-based assays, organ chips and microphysiological systems, computer modeling, other nonhuman or human biology-based methods such as bioprinting, and animal tests or studies.
- The notice states that FDA will continue to determine whether data from nonanimal methods adequately support the relevant clinical or safety purpose.

**Inference:** The proposal may lower terminology barriers, but its practical effect depends on how FDA applies adequacy and scientific-validity judgments across methods and review contexts.

**Evidence Quotes**
- FDA will examine any toxicological data from nonanimal nonclinical studies to determine whether they adequately support the clinical studies identified in the IND.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal's purpose, authority, operative text, and retained FDA adequacy review fit together, but the broad definition is more specific than the implementation and cost analysis supporting it.
