## What This Document Does
- Proposes a special electronic-delivery rule for Form 1099-DA statements that digital-asset brokers must furnish to customers.
- Would let brokers obtain customer consent to electronic delivery without offering a paper-delivery alternative.
- Would specifically allow brokers to terminate the business relationship if a customer does not consent to electronic delivery.
- Would not require brokers to let customers withdraw a consent they already gave.
- Tries to offset those changes with added disclosure, notice, access, and retention requirements, including access through at least October 15 of the following year and seven-year retention.
- Applies only to 1099-DA statements, not to other broker statements such as 1099-B.

## Why It Matters
- This is a targeted but important change in how tax information for digital-asset sales would reach customers. It affects whether customers have a practical fallback if they do not want electronic delivery or later lose access to the delivery method they originally accepted.
- The IRS and Treasury say the current paper-default model is too burdensome for brokers because digital-asset trading is highly electronic and some customers may generate very large numbers of transactions. That concern is plausible, but the visible record in the supplied materials is thinner than ideal on the size of the burden and on whether narrower options could solve most of the problem.
- The biggest commenter leverage point is not whether electronic delivery should be allowed. It already is. The more important question is whether the agency has adequately justified removing two safeguards at once: paper fallback for non-consent and the ability to withdraw consent later.
- The rule also matters because tax statements are not just convenience documents. They affect filing accuracy, audit response, and the customer’s ability to understand what the IRS has been told about the customer’s transactions.

## Who Is Affected
- Digital-asset brokers that must furnish 1099-DA statements.
- Customers whose digital-asset sales are reported on 1099-DA statements.
- Compliance, legal, and product teams designing onboarding, disclosure, and account-access systems.
- Tax preparers and customer-support teams that help users retrieve prior-year records.
- Customers with weaker digital access, changing email addresses, lost account credentials, kiosk-only interactions, or accessibility needs.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. Issue: The rule removes paper fallback and allows account termination for non-consent.
   - Why it matters: This is the sharpest substantive change in the proposal and may make consent less meaningfully voluntary in practice.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: What evidence shows that allowing termination is necessary, rather than using narrower tools such as paper-on-request hardship exceptions or limited fallback rules for existing customers?

2. Issue: The rule does not require brokers to permit withdrawal of previously given consent.
   - Why it matters: Customers may later lose digital access, change devices, change email addresses, or need paper records for tax preparation or audit response.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Why would a prospective withdrawal right with reasonable timing limits fail to preserve most of the claimed burden reduction?

3. Issue: The record is thin on the size of the paper-burden problem.
   - Why it matters: The main policy justification is that mailing 1099-DA statements is unnecessarily burdensome, especially for high-volume users, but the supplied materials do not show detailed cost ranges or transaction-volume distributions.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Please publish the cost estimates, customer-volume assumptions, and any stakeholder submissions or broker data the agency relied on to conclude paper delivery is impractical.

4. Issue: The agency’s substitute protections may not fully replace the safeguards it removes.
   - Why it matters: Enhanced notice, retention, and access requirements help, but they do not automatically solve lost-account, bounced-email, or accessibility problems.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: What evidence shows that the proposed notice and access rules are enough to prevent missed statements and tax-filing problems when customers lose access to their accounts?

5. Issue: The 1099-DA-only carveout needs clearer line drawing.
   - Why it matters: The proposal says digital-asset statements are different, but the visible record is thinner on why the same burden logic would not apply to at least some other electronically mediated broker statements.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: What feature of digital-asset brokerage makes this market uniquely appropriate for a no-paper, no-withdrawal consent regime?

## Evidence Quotes
- “Stakeholders have provided comments indicating that transactions involving digital assets ... are almost exclusively conducted electronically.”
- “The Treasury Department and the IRS acknowledge that the cost of furnishing 1099-DA statements on paper ... may be unnecessarily burdensome for brokers...”
- “These proposed regulations propose alternative rules that would allow digital asset brokers to obtain customer consent ... without having to offer customers the choice of receiving the 1099-DA statements on paper.”
- “The rules proposed in these proposed regulations would not require the broker to furnish paper payee statements if the customer does not consent but, instead, would specifically permit brokers to terminate their business relationship with these customers.”
- “Additionally, unlike the existing rules, the rules proposed in these proposed regulations would not require brokers to permit customers to withdraw a previously provided consent.”
- “To ensure that customers are made aware that an important tax return document has been furnished in an electronic format ... these proposed alternative rules would ... require these brokers to meet certain enhanced electronic notice and delivery requirements and to provide customer access to the statements for a longer period of time.”
