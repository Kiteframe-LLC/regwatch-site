## What This Document Does
- EPA proposes to approve part of Wisconsin's Moderate ozone SIP package for three Wisconsin areas under the 2015 ozone standard: Kenosha County, the Milwaukee area, and Sheboygan County.
- The proposed approvals cover five main pieces: the `2017` base-year emissions inventory, the `15%` reasonable-further-progress demonstration, the `2023` motor-vehicle emissions budgets, the motor-vehicle inspection and maintenance program certification, and the nonattainment new source review certification.
- EPA is also starting the transportation-conformity adequacy process for the `2023` budgets. If EPA later finds those budgets adequate, transportation agencies will have to use them when testing whether future plans and projects conform to the SIP.
- EPA is not deciding everything in Wisconsin's Moderate-area package here. The notice says the attainment demonstration, RACM demonstration, and contingency measures will be handled in a separate action.

## Why It Matters
- This is a partial approval with real practical consequences. Even though EPA is postponing some of the most outcome-oriented issues, the current notice would still approve the inventories and progress demonstration that support the plan, plus the budgets that can shape transportation conformity.
- The Sheboygan supporting plan makes clear that this is not a simple local-emissions story. It describes a Lake Michigan ozone problem driven by both regional transport and lake-breeze meteorology. That matters because commenters can reasonably ask EPA to explain more clearly what the approved local planning elements can do, and what their limits are, in a transport-heavy setting.
- The strongest concern is record clarity, not absence of analysis. The supplied materials show substantial technical work for Sheboygan, including emissions inventories and a state claim that NOx falls by about `31%` and VOC by about `13%` between `2017` and `2023`. But EPA is proposing the same approval posture for three areas at once, while the surfaced support in the review package is much fuller for Sheboygan than for the other two areas.
- The split structure also matters. EPA is asking the public to comment now on inventories, RFP, budgets, I/M, and NNSR, while saying attainment, RACM, and contingency measures will come later. That may be administratively normal enough, but it can make it harder for ordinary commenters to see the whole planning picture in one place.
- One detail worth noticing is that the Sheboygan plan's own contingency table shows a simple `2023` to `2024` reduction comparison that does not meet the contingency target. EPA is not approving contingency measures here, so that does not by itself defeat this NPRM. But it gives commenters a fair reason to ask EPA to explain how the deferred contingency action fits with the approvals proposed now.

## Who Is Affected
- Residents of shoreline and near-shore communities in the three Wisconsin ozone nonattainment areas, because the proposal affects the planning framework for ozone control.
- Metropolitan planning organizations, state transportation agencies, and federal transportation agencies, because the `2023` budgets may become the conformity benchmarks they must use.
- Facilities that may need permits under Wisconsin's ozone-related permitting program, because EPA is relying on the existing SIP-approved NNSR program as satisfying Moderate-area requirements.
- State and local air regulators, because this action would lock in EPA's approval of several planning elements while leaving other Moderate-area obligations for later.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. EPA should explain the split action more clearly.
   - Why it matters: The notice approves several important planning elements now but postpones attainment, RACM, and contingency measures, which makes it harder to understand how the full package fits together.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Why is it appropriate to approve these pieces now, and could the later action on attainment, RACM, or contingency measures change the practical meaning of the approvals proposed here?

2. EPA should surface equal area-specific support for all three areas.
   - Why it matters: The supplied package gives much clearer technical visibility into Sheboygan than into Milwaukee or Kenosha.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Will EPA provide a short area-by-area explanation or table showing the main technical basis for each approved element in Milwaukee, Kenosha, and Sheboygan?

3. EPA should justify the transportation-budget assumptions in more plain language.
   - Why it matters: The budgets have direct conformity consequences, and the NPRM says Wisconsin added a `15%` VMT increase for Sheboygan and `7.5%` for Milwaukee and Kenosha.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: What is the practical basis for those VMT cushions, and did EPA or Wisconsin test alternative assumptions or sensitivity cases?

4. EPA should explain how older I/M and NNSR approvals map to current Moderate-area obligations.
   - Why it matters: EPA is relying on legacy approved programs to satisfy present requirements after reclassification and revised nonattainment boundaries.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Can EPA provide a clear crosswalk showing how the earlier approved I/M and NNSR provisions satisfy the current Moderate-area requirements for each of the three areas?

5. EPA should connect the transport discussion to the approved local planning tools more directly.
   - Why it matters: The Sheboygan plan emphasizes that Lake Michigan transport and mesoscale meteorology are major drivers of ozone problems, which may limit what local planning tools can accomplish by themselves.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: How does EPA account for the transport-heavy nature of these ozone problems when concluding that the approved inventories, RFP showing, budgets, and legacy program certifications are sufficient for this stage?
