# Public Summary: EPA-R05-OAR-2025-0222-0001

_Source file: `summaries/EPA-R05-OAR-2025-0222-0001.md`_

## What This Document Does
- Proposes to approve Ohio's plan for the Canton, Ohio lead (Pb) nonattainment area under the 2008 Pb National Ambient Air Quality Standards.
- Proposes to determine that the area has attained the 2008 Pb NAAQS.
- Proposes to approve Ohio's Pb emissions inventory and first 10-year maintenance plan.
- Proposes to redesignate the Canton area from nonattainment to attainment.
- Relies heavily on the permanent shutdown of the Republic Steel-Canton Plant, termination of associated air permits, ambient monitoring data, and AERMOD modeling for potential future cleanup or remediation activity.

## Why It Matters
- The area was designated nonattainment after monitoring near Republic Steel showed a 2019-2021 Pb design value above the 2008 Pb NAAQS.
- EPA says Pb levels later fell sharply after the Republic Steel-Canton Plant was idled and permanently closed. The notice states that by the end of 2024 monitored Pb levels had declined to 0.00 micrograms per cubic meter and that the 2022-2024 design value met the 0.15 micrograms per cubic meter standard.
- Redesignation would mark the area as attaining the standard, but the record still matters because lead can remain in dust, soil, structures, and roads after a source shuts down.
- The key practical issue is whether the maintenance plan, monitoring network, contingency triggers, and dust-control requirements are strong enough for future demolition, deconstruction, cleanup, remediation, or site reuse.
- EPA's record appears substantial for this type of SIP action. The docket includes a Technical Support Document, Ohio's maintenance-plan submittal, implementation guidance, monitoring material, and related supporting documents.

## Who Is Affected
- Residents, workers, schools, and businesses near the former Republic Steel-Canton Plant and the Canton Pb nonattainment area.
- Ohio EPA and the Canton City Public Health Department Air Pollution Control Division.
- Republic Steel and any future owner or operator of the former plant site.
- Contractors involved in demolition, deconstruction, cleanup, remediation, material handling, or site redevelopment.
- United Rolls, Inc. is identified in the emissions-inventory discussion, but Republic Steel is the principal source driving the nonattainment history.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. Modeling assumptions for future cleanup or remediation.
   - Why it matters: EPA cites AERMOD modeling that predicts a maximum three-month rolling average Pb concentration of 0.099440 micrograms per cubic meter, below the 0.15 standard. The preamble does not summarize the controlling assumptions in much detail.
   - Practical question to ask: Will EPA include a final-rule table showing the cleanup/remediation scenario, emissions inputs, receptor grid, background concentration, meteorology, fugitive-dust assumptions, and any sensitivity checks?

2. Contingency trigger levels.
   - Why it matters: The maintenance plan depends on monitor-triggered actions if Pb concentrations rise. The notice describes daily and monthly trigger levels, but the final rule should explain how those levels protect a rolling three-month Pb standard.
   - Practical question to ask: How did EPA determine that the 0.75 micrograms per cubic meter daily trigger and 0.10 micrograms per cubic meter monthly trigger provide timely protection against a future NAAQS problem?

3. Future-owner and contractor obligations.
   - Why it matters: The proposal says obligations apply to Republic Steel or any subsequent owner or operator, but future demolition or remediation may involve owners, operators, and contractors with different roles.
   - Practical question to ask: Will EPA explain how the consent order, Ohio findings and orders, NSR requirements, dust-control plans, and contingency measures apply during sale, demolition, remediation, and contractor-led work?

4. Public access to monitoring and response information.
   - Why it matters: Local residents may need timely access to Pb monitoring data, dust-control plans, trigger exceedances, and response actions during site activity.
   - Practical question to ask: Will EPA or Ohio commit to clear public posting of monitoring data, dust-control plans, and contingency-response actions while major site work is occurring?

5. Scope of the "no additional RACM" conclusion.
   - Why it matters: EPA agrees with Ohio that no additional reasonably available control measures would appreciably reduce fugitive dust or expedite attainment at this time. That makes sense after permanent shutdown, but it should not obscure dust-control duties for future site disturbance.
   - Practical question to ask: Will EPA clarify that the RACM conclusion is based on the end of permitted production and does not reduce requirements for demolition, deconstruction, cleanup, or remediation controls?

## Evidence Quotes
- "The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) is proposing to approve the State of Ohio's attainment plan for the Canton Nonattainment Area for the 2008 Lead (Pb) National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS)."
- "By the end of 2024, ambient air monitoring demonstrated that airborne Pb levels had declined to 0.00 micrograms per cubic meter and that the three-year design value for 2022-2024 for the Canton Nonattainment Area had achieved 0.15 micrograms per cubic meter."
- "Ohio EPA performed an atmospheric dispersion modeling analysis using AERMOD which accounted for potential cleanup and/or remediation efforts at the site in the future."
- "The model predicted a maximum three-month rolling average Pb concentration for the Canton Nonattainment Area of 0.099440 micrograms per cubic meter."
- "These contingency measures would be triggered if monitored Pb concentrations at either the Republic Steel or Republic Community monitoring sites exceed certain levels."
