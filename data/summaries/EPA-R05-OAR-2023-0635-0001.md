## What This Document Does

- EPA proposes to redesignate part of St. Clair County, Michigan, from `SO2 nonattainment` to `attainment` for the `2010 1-hour sulfur dioxide standard`.
- EPA also proposes to approve Michigan's maintenance plan for the area and to add Belle River's tighter SO2 permit limits to the federally enforceable SIP.
- The record says the area now meets the standard for two reasons:
  1. the two DTE near-source monitors showed attainment through `2022`, and
  2. Michigan's air-dispersion modeling shows a maximum concentration of `62.8 ppb`, below the `75 ppb` standard.
- EPA says the key changes were the `2022` shutdown of the St. Clair Power Plant and lower SO2 limits at the Belle River Power Plant under `Permit to Install 51-22`.
- EPA's TSD also tries to answer a likely objection: a Port Huron monitor outside the nonattainment area had exceedances, but EPA says trajectory analysis shows those polluted air masses moved away from the St. Clair nonattainment area rather than into it.

## Why It Matters

- Redesignation matters because it changes the area's legal status and shifts the focus from getting into attainment to staying there.
- The notice's immediate attainment case is fairly well supported. EPA is not relying on a single sentence or a bare conclusion. The record includes a state redesignation package, a later state supplement, and a TSD that addresses the most obvious technical complication.
- The strongest support is straightforward: monitored values at the two DTE sites remained below the standard through `2022`, Belle River's revised permit limits are concrete, St. Clair shut down, and the modeling result stays below the standard even after adding background concentrations.
- The bigger questions for commenters are about `durability`, not the basic present-tense attainment finding.
- Michigan's maintenance plan projects `0` SO2 tons per year from both major plants by `2033`. That is easy to read as reassuring, but one part of that projection depends on Belle River's expected future conversion from coal to natural gas around `2027`. The record does not spend much time explaining what happens if that timeline slips.
- The other weak spot is verification. The two DTE near-source monitors stopped operating after `2022`, and Michigan's promise to “periodically reevaluate” modeling assumptions is fairly general rather than trigger-based.
- The best public comments will probably aim to tighten the maintenance record rather than relitigate the whole attainment showing.

## Who Is Affected

- Communities in and around the former St. Clair SO2 nonattainment area, because redesignation changes the area's regulatory status while leaving Belle River's permit limits important to continued air-quality protection.
- Michigan air regulators, because they are asking EPA to approve both the redesignation and the maintenance plan and will remain responsible for tracking whether assumptions stay valid.
- DTE's Belle River facility, because its tighter SO2 permit limits are part of the redesignation logic and would become federally enforceable through SIP incorporation.
- Commenters focused on air monitoring, source operations, and maintenance-plan reliability, especially anyone concerned about what replaces the discontinued DTE near-source monitors.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

1. EPA should make the maintenance-verification trigger more specific.  
Why it matters: Michigan promises “periodic” reevaluation of modeling assumptions, but the record does not say what event actually requires a refreshed review after redesignation. That matters more because the near-source DTE monitors ended after `2022`.  
Practical question a commenter can ask: What specific changes in emissions, permits, operating patterns, or source status would trigger updated modeling or another formal technical review?

2. EPA should explain how the maintenance plan stays protective if Belle River's expected fuel conversion is delayed.  
Why it matters: The `2033` projected inventory shows zero emissions from Belle River because the state expects a future conversion to natural gas. That is plausible, but the record should say more clearly what happens if the conversion does not occur on the current timeline.  
Practical question a commenter can ask: If Belle River continues coal-fired operations longer than expected, what mechanism ensures the maintenance demonstration remains current and adequate?

3. EPA should summarize the Port Huron explanation more directly in the final rule.  
Why it matters: EPA's TSD appears to address the issue by showing the relevant air masses moved away from the St. Clair nonattainment area and by documenting invalid wind-direction data for part of `2024`. But readers should not have to do all of that reconstruction themselves.  
Practical question a commenter can ask: Can EPA add a short plain-language summary of the Port Huron trajectory and instrument-error findings to the final rule preamble?

## Evidence Quotes

- “Michigan's modeling analysis demonstrates... a maximum SO2 concentration of 62.8 ppb, which is below the 2010 SO2 NAAQS of 75 ppb.”
- “The trajectories demonstrate that the air mass containing the higher SO2 concentrations did not disperse into the St. Clair nonattainment area.”
- “EU-BOILER1-BR and EU-BOILER2-BR are to adjust to an SO2 emission limit of 0.680 pounds per million British thermal units... for a 30-day rolling average emission rate and 1.2 pounds... based upon any 3-hour average.”
- “Because there are no large sources of SO2 projected to be constructed or resume operations in the NAA, projected emissions to 2033 for the Belle River facility will be zero...”
- “To track the progress of the maintenance plan, EGLE commits to periodically reevaluate the modeling assumptions and input data used in the redesignation SIP development...”
