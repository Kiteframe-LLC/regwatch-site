# Public Summary: EPA-HQ-OW-2025-0322-15447

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** This SNPRM supplements the November 2025 WOTUS proposal with options for relatively permanent waters and continuous surface connection, and adds a perennial definition in 33 CFR 328.3 and 40 CFR 120.2.

**Affected Entities**
- EPA and Department of the Army/Corps of Engineers
- States and Tribal governments
- landowners, farmers, developers, and infrastructure operators
- wetland and stream consultants and permit applicants
- communities affected by Clean Water Act coverage

**Statutory Authority:** Federal Water Pollution Control Act sections 301, 304, 311, 401, 402, 404, and 501; Sackett v. EPA and the Rapanos plurality.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposed text defines perennial, relatively permanent, and continuous surface connection using daily presence and specified interruption rules.

**Observed Facts**
- In this SNPRM, the agencies are seeking comment on supplemental regulatory options for the revised definitions of relatively permanent and continuous surface connection, and adding a definition of the term perennial.
- Relatively permanent means perennial bodies of water.
- Perennial means having standing or continuously flowing water every day of the year during ordinary conditions.
- The agencies would interpret drought to extend continuously across no more than a five-year timeframe, after which the drought condition would be nullified for purposes of identifying relatively permanent waters.
- A temporary interruption in the surface water connection lasting no more than a single period of up to 30 consecutive days in any given year may also occur.
- The notice states that approximately 220,000 comments were received on the November 2025 NPRM.
- The notice identifies an October 9, 2026 deadline; supplied metadata displays October 10, 2026 in UTC-normalized form.

**Inference:** The principal comment leverage is the translation of broad legal language into bright-line temporal rules and the adequacy of implementation, economic, federalism, and reliance analysis.
