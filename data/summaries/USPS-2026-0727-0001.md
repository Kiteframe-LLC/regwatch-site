## What This Document Does

USPS proposes a large set of Domestic Mail Manual updates tied to mailing services price adjustments scheduled for July 12, 2026. The proposal changes preparation, classification, labeling, pricing-structure, and documentation rules across First-Class Mail, USPS Marketing Mail, Periodicals, Bound Printed Matter, Market Dominant parcels, and a new Heavy Printed Matter category.

Major changes include:

- removing ADC/AADC rates and replacing them with 3-Digit or Mixed categories;
- restructuring Outside County Periodicals prices to look more like USPS Marketing Mail;
- increasing Marketing Mail flat weight limits to 20 ounces, and Carrier Route flat limits to 24 ounces;
- creating Heavy Printed Matter for certain Marketing Mail parcels up to 15 pounds;
- extending some direct-container and SCF pallet discounts to Heavy Printed Matter;
- allowing First-Class cards and letters to meet a combined 500-piece minimum when mailed together;
- adding a barcode quality noncompliance fee for some Market Dominant parcels;
- changing BPM presorted and carrier route pricing from piece-plus-pound pricing to weight-increment piece prices;
- eliminating bundling for Marketing Mail flats prepared in flat trays;
- setting up 2027 mailing promotions, including two new promotions.

USPS says it is exempt from APA notice-and-comment requirements under 39 U.S.C. 410(a), but it is still inviting public comments.

## Why It Matters

The rule is operationally important because it changes how mailers prepare, document, classify, and price mail. It is not just a price notice; it rewrites many DMM provisions that mail owners, presort bureaus, software vendors, publishers, and nonprofit mailers use in day-to-day compliance.

The available Regulations.gov record is thin. It identifies the DMM text changes, but it does not include supporting analysis for price effects, threshold choices, implementation burden, barcode fee design, or small-mailer impacts. The notice says proposed prices will be available in PRC Docket No. R2026-1, but those PRC materials were not included in the supplied export.

That makes comments most useful when they focus on implementation clarity: crosswalks, examples, threshold explanations, transition timing, fee process, and category boundaries.

## Who Is Affected

Affected groups include:

- commercial First-Class Mail, USPS Marketing Mail, Periodicals, and BPM mailers;
- publishers using Periodicals Outside County rates;
- nonprofit and commercial mailers that may use Heavy Printed Matter;
- presort bureaus and mail service providers;
- mailing software vendors;
- mailers using ADC/AADC categories, flat trays, bundles, pallets, or standardized documentation;
- Market Dominant parcel mailers subject to barcode quality rules;
- mailers planning campaigns around 2027 promotions.

Small mailers, nonprofit mailers, publishers, and vendors may face concentrated transition costs even if the overall standards are intended to simplify products.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

### 1. Price-to-DMM crosswalk

USPS references PRC Docket No. R2026-1 for proposed prices, while this proposal contains the DMM implementation text. A clear crosswalk would help mailers connect each DMM change to the related price category and effective date.

Why it matters: mailers and software vendors need to implement the rule quickly and consistently.

Practical question: Will USPS publish a table mapping each DMM amendment to the related PRC price category and implementation date?

### 2. Threshold rationale

The proposal sets important thresholds, including 20 ounces, 24 ounces, 15 pounds, and a combined 500-piece minimum for certain First-Class mailings.

Why it matters: thresholds shape eligibility, mailpiece design, preparation, equipment use, and pricing.

Practical question: What operational or pricing evidence supports each threshold, and did USPS consider intermediate thresholds or phased adoption?

### 3. Heavy Printed Matter boundaries

HPM is a new category for certain commercial and nonprofit non-fulfillment Marketing Mail parcels up to 15 pounds.

Why it matters: mailers need to know which pieces qualify and how HPM differs from other Marketing Mail or Package Services options.

Practical question: Can USPS provide examples of qualifying and non-qualifying HPM pieces, including how "non-fulfillment" will be applied?

### 4. Barcode fee process

USPS proposes a Market Dominant parcel barcode quality noncompliance fee that mirrors the Competitive parcel fee, with exclusions for Periodicals parcels and HPM.

Why it matters: fee rules need clear measurement, notice, correction, and dispute procedures.

Practical question: What barcode criteria, measurement period, notice process, and cure or appeal pathway will apply before a fee is assessed?

### 5. Transition burden

The changes affect rate categories, documentation, labeling, bundling, pallets, trays, and software logic, with an effective date tied to July 12, 2026.

Why it matters: rapid implementation may be harder for smaller mailers, publishers, and vendors with legacy systems.

Practical question: Will USPS provide a transition guide, grace period, or temporary error-tolerance policy for documentation and labeling changes?

### 6. Periodicals restructuring

USPS proposes to simplify Outside County Periodicals pricing by making it more like USPS Marketing Mail and eliminating bundle and container rates.

Why it matters: Periodicals mailers may have different operating patterns from Marketing Mail users.

Practical question: Can USPS provide representative examples showing how different Periodicals mailings would be affected before and after the restructuring?

- "The Postal Service is proposing to remove ADC/AADC rates for First-Class Mail, USPS Marketing Mail, and Periodicals and replacing with 3-Digit rates."
- "The Postal Service is proposing to simplify the Periodicals pricing structure to more closely resemble the USPS Marketing Mail pricing structure."
- "Heavy Printed Matter (HPM) will be introduced for Marketing Mail parcels with a weight limit of 15 pounds for Commercial/Nonprofit non-fulfillment pieces."
- "The Postal Service is proposing to implement a package barcode quality noncompliance fee for Market Dominant parcels, excluding Periodicals parcels and Heavy Printed Matter, that mirrors the existing fee for Competitive parcels."
