## What This Document Does

- EPA proposes to redesignate the Cleveland, Ohio area to attainment for the 2015 ozone standard.
- The proposal also would approve Ohio's maintenance plan through `2038`, approve `2032` and `2038` motor-vehicle emissions budgets for VOC and NOx, adjust the deadline for Ohio's Serious-area SIP revisions to `December 5, 2026`, and approve Ohio's Enhanced I/M, Clean Fuel Vehicle Program, and Enhanced Monitoring Program certifications.
- The affected area includes `Cuyahoga`, `Geauga`, `Lake`, `Lorain`, `Medina`, `Portage`, and `Summit` Counties.
- EPA says the area's `2023-2025` design value is `0.070 ppm`, which meets the ozone standard.
- Ohio's own transmittal letter adds an important qualifier: the attainment showing is contingent on EPA concurrence with Ohio's separate exceptional-events demonstrations for wildfire-related ozone days.
- EPA also says it will not finalize redesignation if the clean-data determination is not finalized or if a monitor violates the standard before final approval.

## Why It Matters

- Redesignation is a major status change under the Clean Air Act. It means EPA is not just saying the area currently looks better; it is saying the area has met the legal conditions to move out of nonattainment status and stay there under an approved maintenance plan.
- This proposal matters because Cleveland was reclassified from `Marginal` to `Moderate` and then to `Serious` after earlier failures to attain. EPA is now proposing to redesignate the area anyway by combining a current attainment showing with a maintenance case and a deadline extension for remaining Serious-area obligations.
- The strongest public-interest issue is not whether redesignation exists as a legal tool. It is whether this particular record is explained clearly enough. The area's design value lands exactly at the standard, not well below it, and Ohio says that result depends on separate exceptional-events treatment for some wildfire-related days.
- EPA also proposes to extend the deadline for Ohio's Serious SIP revisions to `December 5, 2026` and says that if redesignation is finalized before then, those Serious planning requirements would not count as due for redesignation purposes. That is a consequential timing move, so it deserves a clear explanation.
- The proposal also locks in transportation-conformity-relevant budgets for `2032` and `2038`. Those budget decisions matter for future transportation planning, so commenters have a legitimate interest in how Ohio and EPA chose the assumptions behind them.

## Who Is Affected

- Residents and local governments in the seven-county Cleveland area.
- Transportation planners and metropolitan planning organizations that rely on approved motor-vehicle emissions budgets for conformity.
- Ohio EPA and local air agencies that will continue monitoring and implementing the maintenance framework.
- Businesses and developers whose future projects may rely on the area's attainment status and PSD-based future-control assumptions.
- Community and advocacy groups focused on ozone exposure, wildfire-related data treatment, and long-term air-quality reliability.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

1. EPA should explain the exceptional-events dependency more directly.
Why it matters: Ohio says the redesignation request depends on EPA concurrence with separate exceptional-events demonstrations for wildfire-related ozone days, but the public-facing NPRM only partly highlights how central that dependency is.
Practical question a commenter can ask: Which wildfire-related days are material to the attainment showing, and how would the redesignation analysis change if EPA does not concur with some or all of those exclusions?

2. EPA should justify redesignation on a design value that sits exactly at `0.070 ppm`.
Why it matters: Meeting the standard is legally meaningful, but an exact-threshold result can raise obvious questions about sensitivity, especially when the area is being redesignated and given a long maintenance horizon at the same time.
Practical question a commenter can ask: Why does EPA believe a `0.070 ppm` design value is robust enough to support redesignation and maintenance approval through `2038` rather than only showing current minimal compliance?

3. EPA should make the `2032` and `2038` budget assumptions easier to evaluate.
Why it matters: The notice says EPA is approving future VOC and NOx motor-vehicle budgets, and the supporting materials reference MOVES5-based inventories, but the key assumptions are not summarized very clearly for ordinary readers.
Practical question a commenter can ask: What traffic, growth, meteorology, and control assumptions are most important to the `2032` and `2038` budget calculations?

4. EPA should explain the Serious-deadline timing logic more concretely.
Why it matters: EPA proposes to extend Ohio's Serious SIP deadline and then rely on that extension so additional Serious planning requirements are not yet due for redesignation purposes.
Practical question a commenter can ask: Why is this sequencing appropriate here, given that the redesignation case is both narrow-margin and contingent on separate exceptional-events treatment?

5. EPA should explain the fallback path if monitoring changes before final action.
Why it matters: EPA expressly says it will not finalize redesignation if a monitor violates before final approval, but the public-facing record says less about what that would mean for the rest of the package.
Practical question a commenter can ask: If a monitor exceeds the standard before final action, what happens to the proposed maintenance-plan approval, budget approval, and related certifications?

- "The maintenance plan is designed to keep the Cleveland area in attainment of the 2015 ozone NAAQS through `2038`."
- "The EPA is initiating the adequacy process and proposing to approve the newly established `2032` and `2038` motor vehicle emissions budgets for the Cleveland area."
- "Providing the EPA finalizes this SIP submittal deadline adjustment and the redesignation of the Cleveland area prior to `December 5, 2026`, Serious SIP requirements would not be applicable requirements for purposes of redesignation."
- "The EPA will not take final action to redesignate the Cleveland area to attainment if the EPA fails to finalize the CDD or if the design value of a monitoring site in the area violates the NAAQS prior to final approval."
