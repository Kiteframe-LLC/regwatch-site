# Public Summary: EPA-R03-OAR-2026-1685-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** EPA proposes to redesignate Warren County, Pennsylvania from nonattainment to attainment for the 2010 1-hour primary sulfur dioxide NAAQS, approve the area maintenance plan, and replace unredacted URC SIP language with a title V excerpt.

**Affected Entities**
- People living and working in the Warren County nonattainment area
- United Refining Company and other nearby stationary sources
- Pennsylvania DEP and EPA Region III
- Local air-quality planners and permit administrators
- Commenters interested in SO2 monitoring, maintenance, and SIP record clarity

**Statutory Authority:** CAA sections 107(d)(3)(E), 110(k), 175A, 110, and part D; 40 CFR parts 52 and 81; EPA guidance in the 2014 SO2 Guidance and Calcagni Memo.

**Key Regulatory Change:** If finalized, the area would move to attainment status and the SIP would be cleaned up to reflect the corrected URC source-specific language.

**Observed Facts**
- The rule says the EPA is proposing to approve a SIP revision, redesignation request, and maintenance plan.
- The rule reports recent design values below the 75 ppb standard and says the Warren East monitor is not suitable for compliance assessment.
- The rule says the area was already the subject of a 2017 attainment plan approval and that the current control strategy has been implemented.
- The rule says the URC permit excerpt replaces an unredacted COA and that no emissions increase is expected.

**Inference**
- The redesignation rests on a mix of current monitoring data, earlier attainment modeling, and the claim that the control strategy is now embedded in enforceable limits.
- The URC correction looks like a record-cleanup action, but it still matters because it changes the SIP text that future commenters and regulators will rely on.

**Core Tradeoff:** The proposal would reward a region that now appears to be attaining the standard, but it depends on a maintenance showing and a permit-text cleanup that commenters can only test if the supporting record is sufficiently transparent.
