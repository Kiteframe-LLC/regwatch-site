# Public Summary: NRC-2025-0478-0001

## What This Document Does

**Observed Facts**

- The notice proposes to revise 10 CFR part 51 across NRC licensing and related regulatory functions.
- The rule would narrow NEPA review to effects within the NRC's substantive statutory authority, create new categorical exclusions, and give licensees, applicants, and petitioners more flexibility to submit environmental information.
- The NRC also issues draft guidance NUREG-2270 and a draft regulatory analysis and invites comment on both.

**Inference**

- This is a broad procedural rewrite, not a narrow cleanup. Its main effect is to relocate part of the environmental-review architecture from regulation into guidance and application-specific documentation.

**Statutory authority**

- NEPA as amended by the Fiscal Responsibility Act and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, plus the AEA, Energy Reorganization Act, Uranium Mill Tailings Radiation Control Act, and the cases and executive directives cited in the notice.

**Affected entities**

- NRC licensees, applicants, petitioners for rulemaking, Tribal governments, state agencies, and the public that comments on NRC licensing actions.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Issue:** Operative detail is moving into guidance.
  
  **Why it matters:** The binding line has to stay visible.
  
  **Practical question:** What stays in the CFR, and what moves to guidance?
- **Issue:** Narrowing NEPA to authority-bound effects may exclude close-causal impacts.
  
  **Why it matters:** That boundary controls what the public can challenge.
  
  **Practical question:** Which effects remain in scope, and why are the rest excluded?
- **Issue:** The cost-savings claim depends on hidden inputs.
  
  **Why it matters:** A savings estimate is only as strong as its assumptions.
  
  **Practical question:** Please publish the appendix assumptions and sensitivity runs.
