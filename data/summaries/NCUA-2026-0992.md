## What This Document Does

NCUA proposes to update its management interlocks rule under the Depository Institution Management Interlocks Act. The proposal would:

- raise both major-assets prohibition thresholds to $10 billion;
- replace the current $2.5 billion and $1.5 billion thresholds with one simpler line;
- leave the community and relevant metropolitan statistical area interlock prohibitions in place;
- remove a rebuttable presumption for depository organizations controlled or managed by persons who are members of a minority group or women.

The agency says the old thresholds no longer reflect the size of large depository organizations after market growth since 1996. It also points to a 2019 rule by the OCC, Federal Reserve, and FDIC that moved their comparable DIMIA thresholds to $10 billion.

## Why It Matters

This is a deregulatory proposal, but it still raises important record-building questions. NCUA says the change would reduce exemption requests and help credit unions below $10 billion find qualified directors. It reports that, as of December 31, 2024, 309 credit unions had more than $1.5 billion in assets, 190 had more than $2.5 billion, and only 20 had more than $10 billion.

The strongest support for the proposal is that the existing thresholds are old and other banking regulators already use $10 billion. The weakest part of the record is that NCUA does not show much threshold sensitivity analysis. It does not compare $10 billion with an inflation-adjusted number, an indexed threshold, an intermediate threshold, or continued use of two different thresholds.

The removal of the minority/women presumption also deserves targeted comment. NCUA cites Equal Protection concerns, but the notice does not explain whether a race- and sex-neutral replacement factor could preserve flexibility for institutions with real director-recruitment or community-service challenges.

## Who Is Affected

- Credit unions and other depository organizations with management officials who may serve at unaffiliated institutions.
- Credit unions between the current thresholds and $10 billion, which may no longer need exemptions from the major-assets prohibition.
- Large credit unions and other depository organizations above $10 billion, which would remain subject to the major-assets prohibition.
- Community-based, newly chartered, low-income-area, or recruitment-constrained institutions that may care about how exemption standards work after the presumption is removed.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- Threshold justification. NCUA gives a plausible reason to update the thresholds, but the record does not show why $10 billion is the best line. This matters because DIMIA allows adjustments for inflation or market changes, and commenters can help the agency connect the final threshold to evidence. Practical question: Can NCUA provide a table comparing institution counts and likely exemption effects at $5 billion, $7.5 billion, $10 billion, indexed, and asymmetric threshold options?

- Burden relief evidence. The proposal says the change would reduce regulatory burden, but the available text does not quantify exemption-application burden. This matters because actual filing counts, processing times, and approval rates would show how much relief the rule provides. Practical question: How many major-assets interlock exemption applications has NCUA received in recent years, and what were the average applicant and agency burdens?

- Alternatives to a fixed threshold. A fixed $10 billion line may become stale over time. This matters because the proposal is partly a catch-up rule responding to old thresholds. Practical question: Should the final rule include automatic indexing or a scheduled review so the thresholds continue to reflect market changes?

- Competition risks below $10 billion. The proposal says smaller institutions generally operate in regional markets and remain subject to community and RMSA prohibitions. This matters because some competitive relationships may not fit neatly within those geographic tests. Practical question: Does NCUA have evidence that interlocks involving institutions below $10 billion rarely present competition concerns outside the community and RMSA prohibitions?

- Removal of the presumption. NCUA says the current presumption raises Equal Protection concerns, but it does not discuss neutral replacement criteria. This matters because some institutions may still face genuine governance recruitment challenges. Practical question: Should NCUA clarify that applicants may submit race- and sex-neutral evidence about recruitment difficulty, community-service mission, low-income-area service, or newly chartered status?

## Evidence Quotes

- "This proposal would increase both major assets prohibition thresholds to $10 billion to account for changes in the United States banking market since 1996."
- "Based on regulatory reporting, total assets at depository organizations have grown by more than 341 percent in the 29 years between the fourth quarter of 1996 and the fourth quarter of 2024."
- "Raising the $1.5 billion asset threshold to $10 billion would exempt 289 credit unions from the major assets prohibition as of December 31, 2024."
- "As of December 31, 2024, only 20 credit unions reported total assets greater than $10 billion and would remain subject to the major assets prohibition."
- "The NCUA believes this presumption is overly broad and raises Equal Protection issues under the U.S. Constitution."
