# Public Summary: FDA-2025-N-6224-0011

## What This Document Does

FDA proposes to move seven categories of medical-device accessories into Class I, distinct from their parent devices, generally relying on general controls and applicable premarket-notification exemption limits. The notice is tentative and requests comments before any final classification action. The central question is whether FDA's item-specific risk, compatibility, and control analysis is sufficiently visible to support the proposed burden reduction.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is structurally coherent and has a clear statutory and administrative pathway, but the available record leaves the item-specific risk and compatibility showing largely implicit.

## Who Is Affected

- Manufacturers and sponsors of noninvasive blood-pressure, cardiac pacing, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, defibrillator, urological catheter, smoke-evacuation, and battery-powered surgical-instrument accessories
- Manufacturers whose accessories are currently authorized through a parent-device submission
- FDA classification, premarket-review, compliance, and postmarket programs
- Clinicians, health-care facilities, and patients who rely on accessory compatibility and safety
- Commenters proposing additional accessories or objecting to a listed category

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Make the statutory test auditable.** Ask FDA for an item-by-item table addressing all three eligibility factors and identifying the supporting evidence. This would distinguish supported classifications from category-level assumptions.
- **Define the device-specific boundary.** Ask FDA for operational examples and a reviewable decision tree for generic, hybrid, and device-specific accessories, because compatibility-dependent products may need design controls.
- **Compare narrower and staged options.** Ask FDA to compare the proposed list with product-code-specific, phased, conditional, or review-triggered alternatives where product variation is unresolved.
- **Preserve postmarket learning.** Ask FDA to describe monitoring, recordkeeping, and reassessment if safety or compatibility signals emerge after distinct Class I treatment.
