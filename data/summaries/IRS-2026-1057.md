# Public Summary: IRS-2026-1057-0001

## What This Document Does

**Observed Fact:** The proposal would make the refunded portion of the adoption tax credit, child tax credit, American opportunity tax credit, and earned income credit a Federal public benefit for PRWORA purposes. Non-U.S.-citizens, non-U.S.-nationals, and aliens who are not qualified aliens would be ineligible for that refunded portion, subject to the rule's definitions and exceptions.

**Inference:** The proposal converts part of the tax-return calculation into an immigration-status eligibility gate while leaving ordinary credit eligibility otherwise governed by the Internal Revenue Code.

**Regulatory Action:** Add rules under 26 CFR part 1 specifying the refunded portion of covered credits, eligible status categories, joint-return treatment, filing-date status, and examples.

**Affected Entities**
- Taxpayers claiming the adoption, child, American opportunity, or earned income credit
- Families with mixed immigration or citizenship status
- Tax return preparers and software providers
- IRS processing, compliance, and taxpayer-service personnel
- States, localities, and organizations assisting eligible taxpayers

**Statutory Authority:** Internal Revenue Code sections 23, 24, 25A, 32, and 7805(a); PRWORA sections 401, 404, and 431, codified in 8 U.S.C. 1611, 1614, and 1641.

**Key Regulatory Change:** Eligibility for the refunded excess of covered credits would depend on being a U.S. citizen, U.S. national, or qualified alien, with status generally fixed when the return first claims the credit.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal has a coherent legal premise and mechanism, but its status gate and potential deprivation are more developed than its correction, transition, impact, and review safeguards.
