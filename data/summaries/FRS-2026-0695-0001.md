## What This Document Does

- This proposal would add a new Federal Reserve rule stating that the Board may not use "reputation risk" in its examination programs or supervisory materials.
- It would also say the Board may not encourage or compel supervised banking organizations to deny or condition financial services because of a person's or business's constitutionally protected political or religious beliefs, associations, speech, or conduct, or because of involvement in politically disfavored but lawful business activities.
- The proposal applies to a broad set of Board-supervised entities, including bank holding companies, savings and loan holding companies, state member banks, subsidiaries, and combined U.S. operations of foreign banking organizations.
- The Board also includes a rule of construction saying the proposal does not limit its ability to enforce other laws, including anti-money-laundering, sanctions, fair-lending, and banking statutes.
- In practical terms, this is both a codification of the Board's June 2025 policy change and a statement about how supervision should avoid politically tinged pressure on banks.

## Why It Matters

- The notice deals with a real supervisory-policy question: whether "reputation risk" is too vague and too easy to misuse in examinations.
- The Board argues that reputation risk is hard to quantify, hard to communicate, and unnecessary because other risk categories already cover legitimate safety-and-soundness concerns.
- If that reasoning is sound, the rule could reduce ambiguity and narrow examiner discretion in a way that makes supervision more predictable.
- If the reasoning is too thin, the final rule could simply swap one vague concept for another. The proposal uses phrases like "encourage," "compel," and "politically disfavored but lawful business activities" without much detail on where the lines are.
- The economic discussion is also important because the Board says benefits outweigh costs, but it mostly offers high-level assertions rather than measured estimates.

## Who Is Affected

- Board-supervised banks and holding companies would be affected directly because examiner materials and supervisory expectations would change.
- Foreign banking organizations with covered U.S. operations are also included.
- Businesses or individuals who believe banking access has been restricted because of political, religious, or controversial but lawful activity may see this proposal as relevant to future supervisory boundaries.
- Examiners and supervisory managers would be affected internally because the Board says it would train staff and revise materials to comply with the rule.
- Commenters with experience in bank supervision, compliance, lawful high-risk industries, fair lending, sanctions, or anti-money-laundering programs are especially well positioned to supply useful evidence.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- The proposal says other risk categories already cover any legitimate safety-and-soundness concerns formerly associated with reputation risk.
Why it matters: That is one of the rule's core premises, but the notice does not show much evidence for how those substitute categories will work in edge cases.
Question a commenter can ask: What specific examples show that credit, liquidity, operational, or legal risk frameworks fully replace any valid supervisory use of reputation risk?

- The economic analysis says benefits are likely to outweigh costs, but it gives little quantification.
Why it matters: A final rule is easier to defend if burden reduction, implementation cost, and market-access claims are backed by more than general assertions.
Question a commenter can ask: Can the Board provide bounded estimates or worked examples for training costs, manual revisions, compliance savings, and any expected effect on supervisory outcomes?

- The operative language may still be ambiguous even while removing a vague supervisory concept.
Why it matters: Terms such as "encourage," "compel," and "politically disfavored but lawful business activities" may create future disputes unless the Board adds definitions or examples.
Question a commenter can ask: What conduct would count as prohibited supervisory pressure, and what conduct would remain ordinary, permissible supervisory feedback?

- The notice asks about alternatives, but it does not analyze them in detail.
Why it matters: Commenters can still shape the final rule by proposing narrower or clearer options, especially because this is an NPRM and solicitation of alternatives is normal.
Question a commenter can ask: Why is this combined codification-plus-policy approach better than a narrower deletion rule, a more definitions-heavy rule, or phased implementation tied to updated manuals?

## Evidence Quotes

- "Safety and soundness concerns that motivated the Board's prior inclusion of reputation risk in supervision are adequately addressed through other existing risk types."
- "The proposal's benefits and costs since June 2025 are expected to be de minimis, as there has been no further change in policy since that time."
- "These entities may be able to maintain or establish profitable relationships that may have been previously discouraged due to reputation risk concerns."
- "The Board shall not use reputation risk as a component of its examination programs or in materials used for the supervision of banking organizations."
- "The Board shall not encourage or compel banking organizations to deny or condition the provision of banking or other financial products or services" for the specified protected reasons.
