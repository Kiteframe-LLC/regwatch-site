# Public Summary: FCC-2026-1519-0001

## What This Document Does

**Observed Facts**
- The FCC is proposing rules for providers that use foreign call centers or related offshore customer-service operations.
- The proposed framework covers telecommunications services, CMRS, interconnected VoIP, cable television, DBS, and related affiliates, with possible expansion to other services.
- The proposal would require American Standard English proficiency, a cap on offshore call handling, customer notice and transfer rights, U.S.-only handling of sensitive-information communications, and a ban on representatives in foreign adversary nations.
- The notice also seeks comment on extending some or all of the rules to emails, texts, and online chats.
- The notice includes a PRA discussion, a Small Business Paperwork Relief Act request, and an IRFA.

**Affected Entities**
- Telecommunications, CMRS, interconnected VoIP, cable, and DBS providers
- Affiliates that provide internet access service
- Providers using offshore customer-service vendors or foreign call centers
- Small entities that may need new testing, routing, and reporting systems
- Consumers who rely on customer service by phone, text, chat, or email

**Inference**
- This is a consumer-protection and data-security proposal that tries to move customer service closer to the United States while also making offshore operations more transparent and more constrained.
- The key public-comment questions are not whether customer service should improve, but whether these specific tools are measurable, administrable, and proportionate.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The Commission proposes actions to encourage and facilitate the onshoring of foreign call centers.
- The proposal would improve customer service communications and better protect consumers' sensitive personal information.
- Customers would have the right to have the call transferred to a customer representative located within the United States.
- The proposal would bar use of customer representatives located in foreign adversary nations.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- The FCC should justify the percentage cap and explain how it will be measured.
  Why it matters: the cap is the main quantitative constraint, but the notice leaves the key measurement choices open.
  Ask the agency to: define the denominator, time window, and treatment of transfers.
  Practical question: why is this cap the right line instead of a different threshold or a performance standard?
- The FCC should explain how the English-proficiency standard will be tested and audited.
  Why it matters: a language rule can become arbitrary if the test measures the wrong thing or is applied inconsistently.
  Ask the agency to: identify the test, scoring threshold, retesting rules, and who bears the cost.
  Practical question: what evidence shows that this standard improves customer service rather than simply reshuffling staffing?
- The FCC should separate mixed channel rules from sensitive-data rules.
  Why it matters: calls, email, texts, and chats are different workflows, and one rule may not fit all of them.
  Ask the agency to: explain which steps of a customer interaction must stay domestic.
  Practical question: can the FCC give examples showing how a mixed-purpose call or chat would be classified?
- The FCC should show that the remedy bundle is proportionate to the harms it identifies.
  Why it matters: the notice supports several distinct remedies with one broad service-quality rationale.
  Ask the agency to: tie each remedy to a distinct harm and show why less intrusive alternatives would not work.
  Practical question: which problem does each tool solve?
- The FCC should provide a real small-entity burden analysis.
  Why it matters: smaller providers may face training, routing, testing, and reporting costs that the notice only sketches.
  Ask the agency to: quantify expected cost categories and explain whether exemptions or phase-ins are warranted.
  Practical question: what concrete burden numbers justify the claim that the proposal will be manageable for small entities?
