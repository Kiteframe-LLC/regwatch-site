## What This Document Does
- EPA proposes to approve Maricopa County Air Quality Department Rule 205 into the Arizona SIP.
- The rule would let fleet owners or operators create mobile emission reduction credits by replacing or retrofitting lower-emitting on-road vehicles, then transfer those credits to major stationary sources that need offsets under the county's nonattainment new source review program.
- EPA says those credits can be used only if they are real, surplus, quantifiable, permanent, and federally enforceable.
- This is a revised proposal path. EPA had previously proposed conditional approval of an earlier 2023 version, then received revisions and support materials tied to a 2025 resubmittal.
- The available docket record is not thin. It includes a current EPA technical support document, prior EPA review history, state submittal materials, and guidance documents on economic incentive programs and MOVES modeling.

## Why It Matters
- This proposal is about offset supply in a nonattainment area. If approved, it could make it easier for some major stationary sources to obtain offsets for new or modified emissions by purchasing credits generated from mobile-source reductions.
- That matters because offset programs only work if the credited reductions are genuine and are not being counted somewhere else already.
- The hardest issue here is not whether EPA has identified the right legal test. EPA states the legal standard clearly. The harder issue is whether the county and regional planning agencies can carry out the program in a way that prevents double counting and preserves surplus reductions over time.
- The record shows EPA and the county are aware of that problem. The supporting materials say MCAQD will use the latest MOVES model inputs, coordinate with the Maricopa Association of Governments, adjust planning inventories, and revise certificate amounts if needed to maintain surplus integrity.
- That is a meaningful answer, but it is also the main place where commenters can still press for more clarity. A public comment could help push EPA to explain the anti-double-counting steps, permit conditions, and revalidation process more concretely in the final rule.

## Who Is Affected
- Fleet owners or operators that may want to generate mobile emission reduction credits.
- Major stationary sources in Maricopa County that need offsets for nonattainment permitting.
- MCAQD, which would review applications, issue certificates, and enforce permit conditions.
- MAG and other planning entities that handle transportation conformity and inventory accounting.
- Communities affected by ozone and particulate-matter nonattainment, because weak crediting rules could undercut real emission reductions.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. Anti-double-counting safeguards depend heavily on execution
- Why it matters: The proposal relies on coordination between MCAQD and MAG to keep the same mobile-source reductions from being counted both in planning inventories and as offset credits.
- Practical question to ask: Please explain the exact sequence of notice, inventory adjustment, conformity treatment, and public documentation for each MERC project.

2. Baseline and quantification assumptions deserve clearer explanation
- Why it matters: Credited reductions can be overstated if historic vehicle miles traveled, replacement assumptions, or ordinary fleet turnover are not handled carefully.
- Practical question to ask: Please explain how baseline mileage, replacement assumptions, and ordinary turnover are verified so that credited reductions remain truly surplus.

3. Permit-condition detail is still fairly abstract in the notice
- Why it matters: EPA's enforceability conclusion depends on permit revisions and ongoing conditions, but the public notice does not show much detail on what those conditions look like in practice.
- Practical question to ask: Please provide example permit or certificate conditions that make MERCs permanent, enforceable, and adjustable if later information changes the credit amount.

4. The record is stronger on revisions than on alternatives
- Why it matters: The docket shows EPA pushed for revisions to the earlier version of Rule 205, but it says less about whether tighter or simpler safeguard options were considered and rejected.
- Practical question to ask: Did EPA consider tighter caps, periodic recertification, or more explicit rule-text anti-double-counting provisions, and if so, why were those options not adopted?

## Evidence Quotes
