# Public Summary: FCC-2026-2942-0001

## What This Document Does

**Observed Facts**
- The notice is a Further Notice of Proposed Rulemaking for the FCC's alerting systems proceeding.
- The proposal reaches CAP authentication, legacy EAS authentication, WEA geotargeting, duplicate-alert identifiers, EAS software, symbology, text-to-speech, and 90-character WEA messages.
- The notice cites public-safety, Paperwork Reduction Act, small-entity, and legal-authority sections and asks for comment on both benefits and risks.

**Inference:** The agency is trying to harden emergency-alert integrity and modernize message delivery while keeping the system usable in the field, but the legacy-EAS path is still the hardest part of the design.

**Regulatory Action:** Modernize EAS and WEA through authentication, targeting, content, software, and burden-reduction changes.

**Affected Entities**
- EAS Participants
- Participating CMS Providers
- state and local alerting authorities
- small entities that rely on the alerting ecosystem
- people who receive emergency alerts on phones, radios, and other devices
- equipment manufacturers and software vendors

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites multiple Communications Act sections and WARN Act authority, including 47 U.S.C. 151, 152, 154(i), 154(n), 301, 303, 307, 309, 316, 335, 403, 544(g), 606, 613, 1201, 1203, 1204, and 1206.

**Key Regulatory Changes**
- Reject CAP EAS messages without a valid digital signature.
- Consider whether legacy EAS alerts should also be digitally signed.
- Update WEA geotargeting rules and location-data protections.
- Add a universal message identifier and standardized symbology.
- Permit EAS software and retire the 90-character WEA requirement.

**Evidence Quotes**
- We propose to require EAS Participants to reject CAP EAS messages that do not include a valid digital signature.
- The Commission proposes to improve the accuracy of WEA geotargeting by eliminating outdated regulatory exceptions.
- Finally, the Commission proposes to minimize burdens on all alerting participants... by removing unnecessary alerting requirements.
