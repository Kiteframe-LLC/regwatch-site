# Public Summary: CFTC-2026-2213-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory action:** The CFTC proposes to add a broad, non-exhaustive class of event contracts to the swap definition in 17 CFR 1.3(2)(i)(G).

**Affected entities:** DCMs, SEFs, swap dealers, major swap participants, platforms, state-regulated operators, and market makers.

**Statutory authority:** CEA section 1a(47) and Dodd-Frank sections 712(d)(1), 712(d)(2)(A), and 721(b).

**Observed Fact:** The notice cites conflicting state actions and appellate decisions, reports $1.5 billion monthly, and identifies exclusions for futures, SEC instruments, and casino-style products.

**Inference:** The clarification may affect federalism, entry, competition, transition costs, and retail behavior.

**Evidence:** The proposed text adds event contracts to § 1.3; the notice says the list is non-exhaustive and that no viable alternatives were identified.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is structurally coherent but leaves material uncertainty about the scope of a broad federal classification, the fit of the remedy to the stated problem, and the consequences the notice itself identifies.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Issue:** Reconcile the negligible-cost conclusion with acknowledged indirect effects.
  
  **Why it matters:** State revenue, fixed compliance costs, entry barriers, retail behavior, and transition friction may be material.
  
  **Ask the agency to:** Quantify or bound those effects and explain how they inform CEA section 15(a) and related analyses.
- **Issue:** Define the boundary of the non-exhaustive event-contract category.
  
  **Why it matters:** Clear examples and limiting principles would reduce fair-notice risk and distinguish swaps from futures, SEC instruments, and casino-style products.
  
  **Ask the agency to:** Publish a product taxonomy, identify the statutory prong for each category, and explain treatment of borderline products.
- **Issue:** Compare the broad definition with narrower, phased, interpretive, and coordination alternatives.
  
  **Why it matters:** The proposal may have significant jurisdictional and market effects despite its clarification framing.
  
  **Ask the agency to:** Provide an alternatives matrix addressing certainty, transition, federalism, retail protection, competition, and enforceability.
- **Issue:** Pair classification certainty with safeguards and monitoring for retail risk.
  
  **Why it matters:** The notice warns that federal classification may be perceived as a safety signal and may increase loss-chasing or addictive-risk exposure.
  
  **Ask the agency to:** Explain disclosure, education, monitoring, and corrective mechanisms if participation increases.
