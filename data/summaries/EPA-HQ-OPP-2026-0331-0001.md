## What This Document Does

- EPA is giving public notice that it received pesticide petitions seeking residue tolerances under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act.
- The docket-specific petition asks EPA to establish a tolerance for residues of the fungicide mancozeb on longan at `15 ppm`.
- The petitioner is USDA's Foreign Agricultural Service.
- EPA is not yet saying the tolerance should be granted. The notice says EPA has determined that the petitions contain required data or information, but EPA has "not fully evaluated" whether the data are sufficient or support granting the petitions.
- The supporting petition summary says the request relies on Joint FAO/WHO Meeting on Pesticide Residues (`JMPR`) review materials and notes that Codex has a `15 ppm` maximum residue limit for mancozeb on longan.
- The supporting summary identifies mancozeb's metabolite `ETU` as a key toxicology concern and discusses aggregate exposure, drinking water, cancer risk, infants and children, and international tolerances.

## Why It Matters

- This is an early-stage petition notice, but it matters because it is the public's chance to shape the record before EPA makes a final tolerance decision.
- EPA's notice is procedurally careful: it says additional data may be needed and that any final tolerance may vary from the petitioned request.
- The strongest comment opportunities are not about claiming EPA has already made an unsupported final decision. They are about asking EPA to make the residue, exposure, and enforcement basis clear before any final tolerance is established.
- The petition summary includes important technical claims, including that Codex has a `15 ppm` MRL, that the Keppel colorimetric method measures mancozeb by degradation to `CS2`, and that EPA risk assessments dated February 10, 2023 support the petitioner's safety conclusion.
- The available record does not show the full JMPR residue evaluation, the cited EPA risk assessments, or a longan-specific enforcement-method discussion. Those are reasonable targets for public comments.

## Who Is Affected

- Importers and handlers of longan that may be treated with mancozeb outside the United States.
- Consumers, including population subgroups EPA must consider under FFDCA and FQPA.
- Laboratories and enforcement staff who would need to test for mancozeb residues.
- Growers and trading partners relying on Codex-aligned residue limits.
- Public-health, food-safety, and agricultural commenters interested in pesticide tolerance decisions.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

1. EPA should identify and explain the residue evidence supporting the `15 ppm` level.  
Why it matters: The petition relies on JMPR review materials, but the available record mainly shows the petitioner's summary.  
Practical question a commenter can ask: Which residue trials and JMPR findings support a `15 ppm` U.S. tolerance for longan, and how do they map onto U.S. FFDCA requirements?

2. EPA should explain aggregate mancozeb and `ETU` exposure before final action.  
Why it matters: The petition summary identifies `ETU` as a metabolite of concern and a probable human carcinogen for risk-assessment purposes.  
Practical question a commenter can ask: How would imported longan affect aggregate mancozeb and `ETU` exposure for the general population, infants, and children?

3. EPA should clarify enforcement-method performance for longan.  
Why it matters: The notice identifies the Keppel colorimetric method, which measures mancozeb by degradation to `CS2`. Commenters may need to know whether that method is sensitive and specific enough for this commodity.  
Practical question a commenter can ask: What validation supports use of the Keppel colorimetric method for longan at the proposed tolerance level?

4. EPA should distinguish petition assertions from EPA's own conclusions.  
Why it matters: The supporting summary makes safety claims, but EPA's notice says it has not fully evaluated data sufficiency.  
Practical question a commenter can ask: In any final action, will EPA identify which safety, exposure, and residue conclusions are EPA's own findings?

5. EPA should explain Codex harmonization and any U.S.-specific adjustments.  
Why it matters: Codex alignment may support international consistency, but EPA still must apply U.S. statutory safety standards.  
Practical question a commenter can ask: Does EPA expect any U.S.-specific differences in consumption, import patterns, commodity definitions, or related commodities to affect the final tolerance?
