# Public Summary: DOE-HQ-2025-0603-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** DOE proposes to add sunset provisions to 28 covered parts of title 10 and a new Part 1061 that would govern extensions and expiration mechanics.

**Affected Entities**
- DOE contractors and subcontractors
- workers covered by DOE safety programs
- nuclear energy and classified-information programs
- legacy cleanup, waste, and compensation stakeholders
- DOE field offices and designees who would make extension decisions
- public commenters who may need to monitor future RFI-based extension reviews

**Statutory Authority**
- Executive Order 14270
- 5 U.S.C. 301
- 42 U.S.C. 7254
- part-specific authorities in the listed CFR parts

**Key Regulatory Change**
- Most covered sections would automatically expire one year after the effective date of the final rule unless extended.
- Certain parts tied to classified information, human reliability, worker health and safety, and nuclear material production would receive a five-year conditional sunset date.
- A new Part 1061 would set the extension process and require public comment on costs and benefits before expiration.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice uses a blanket sunset structure across a large set of DOE parts rather than a part-by-part substantive rewrite.
- The text carves out some excluded regulations and some five-year exceptions, but the rationale is stated at a high level.
- The notice says comments on costs and benefits of extensions will occur later through a request for information or other means.

**Inferences**
- The proposal is mainly an administrative control architecture for future regulatory survival, not a narrow edit to one technical standard.
- The main record question is whether DOE can administer recurring review and extension decisions without letting important protections lapse by default.

**Evidence Quotes**
- Sections in this part will automatically expire... unless the sunset provision is extended.
- The Secretary has preliminarily determined that an extension... for a period of five years is warranted.
- DOE will give the public an opportunity to comment on the costs and benefits of extending... before allowing any regulation to expire.
