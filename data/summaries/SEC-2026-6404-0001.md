# Public Summary: SEC-2026-6404-0001

## What This Document Does

**Observed Facts:** The proposal would expand rule 17a-7 to Level 2 securities, allow next-NAV or unaffiliated-source current-market pricing, require adviser best-interest determinations, shift detailed review to CCOs with board reporting, and add aggregate N-PORT/N-MFP reporting. It excludes Level 3 and non-leveled securities. **Inference:** The design seeks lower internal-trading costs while controlling affiliated-fund value transfer; its success depends on pricing-source validation and retrospective review. **Authority:** The notice relies on sections 6(c) and 17(b) of the Investment Company Act and preserves separate Advisers Act and ERISA duties.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is structurally coherent, with ordinary weaknesses around evidence calibration, pricing-source discretion, and retrospective oversight design.

## Who Is Affected

Registered investment companies, business development companies relying on rule 17a-7, their advisers, CCOs, fund boards, eligible affiliates, pricing providers, service providers, and fund shareholders. The IRFA identifies approximately 62 registered funds and 5 BDCs as small entities as of December 31, 2025.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Test the Level 2 eligibility boundary.** Ask for asset-class examples and evidence linking Level 2 inputs to executable independent prices in normal and stressed markets.
- **Set current-market pricing standards.** Ask for objective indicators, conflict checks, review frequency, and records for stale, conflicting, or model-based prices.
- **Improve the economic analysis and reporting design.** Request sensitivity analysis and a comparison with confidential or anonymized trade-level reporting.
- **Make CCO oversight reproducible.** Request minimum expectations for trade-to-disposition matching, sampling, escalation, and board response.
