## What This Document Does
- Publishes notice that DOE has received a petition from America First Legal Foundation asking DOE to rescind the DEAR clause at 48 CFR 970.5226-1 ("Diversity Plan").
- Seeks public comment on whether DOE should grant the petition and open a rulemaking to repeal that clause.
- States DOE has not taken a position on the petition’s merits and is inviting evidence and views to inform a potential rescission rulemaking.
- Notes DOE previously issued a class deviation (Feb. 3, 2025) directing contracting officers to stop enforcing the clause and to remove it from solicitations and contracts as soon as practicable.

## Why It Matters
- Practical vs. formal change: DOE already stopped enforcing the clause by class deviation, but the clause remains codified. Commenters should help DOE evaluate whether formal repeal is necessary to eliminate residual uncertainty or risk.
- Operational impacts: Management & operating (M&O) contractors, contracting officers, and procurement staff may have experienced implementation burdens or uncertainty; formal rescission could change contract requirements and compliance obligations.
- Legal and administrative record: If DOE proceeds to rescind, the agency will need a reasoned administrative record explaining why repeal (rather than amendment or continued deviation) is appropriate. Evidence now can shape that record.
- Alternatives exist: Narrowed amendments, codifying the deviation, or replacing subjective planning language with objective compliance requirements might address concerns without full repeal. DOE’s notice does not yet weigh those alternatives in detail.

## Who Is Affected
- Management & operating (M&O) contractors at DOE sites previously subject to 48 CFR 970.5226-1.
- DOE contracting officers and contracting offices that issued solicitations and managed affected contracts.
- Prime contractors, subcontractors, and vendors that responded to or complied with the clause when it was in use.
- Compliance officers, legal counsel, and workforce/DEIA program managers who implemented or interpreted the clause.
- Stakeholders interested in the balance between procurement administration and DEIA-related contract language.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. Issue: Is formal rescission necessary given the existing class deviation?
   - Why it matters: If the deviation already removes practical effects, rescission may be redundant; if not, codified repeal may be needed to remove uncertainty.
   - Practical question to ask DOE: Provide data or examples showing whether the clause’s codified presence continues to create operational or legal uncertainty for contracting officers or contractors.

2. Issue: Scope and magnitude of past implementation and burdens
   - Why it matters: Decisions should be informed by how many contracts/solicitations used the clause and what burdens followed.
   - Practical question to ask DOE: How many contracts or solicitations contained the clause prior to the deviation, and how many were modified after Feb. 3, 2025?

3. Issue: Viable alternatives short of full repeal
   - Why it matters: Amendment or replacement with objective compliance language might address vagueness or legal concerns while retaining anti-discrimination protections.
   - Practical question to ask DOE: Did DOE consider narrower revisions (or codifying the deviation) and why would those be insufficient compared to repeal?

4. Issue: Evidence that the clause produced tangible benefits or harms
   - Why it matters: Petitioners claim the clause lacked benefit and imposed burdens; DOE’s future decision should weigh concrete examples on both sides.
   - Practical question to ask DOE: Provide examples of how the clause was applied in practice, including any demonstrated procurement or workforce benefits or documented harms.

5. Issue: Criteria DOE will use to decide the petition
   - Why it matters: Transparency about decision thresholds will shape useful public input.
   - Practical question to ask DOE: What legal, operational, and evidentiary criteria will DOE apply to determine whether to grant the petition and proceed to rescission?
