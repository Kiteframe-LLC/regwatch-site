# Public Summary: DOJ_FRDOC_0001-0334

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** DOJ proposes to amend 28 CFR 16.79 to exempt records in JUSTICE/OPA-002, the Firearms Rights Restoration Electronic Records Database, from specified Privacy Act accounting, access, amendment, relevance, direct-collection, and accuracy provisions when the statutory law-enforcement exemption predicates apply.

**Affected Entities:** Applicants seeking restoration of federal firearms rights; individuals whose information is collected or supplied during investigations; PARDON and DOJ officials; other federal, state, tribal, and local agencies contributing information; and representatives or third parties seeking to understand or challenge records used in adjudication.

**Statutory Authority:** The notice relies on 5 U.S.C. 552a(j)(2), 5 U.S.C. 552a(k)(2), 5 U.S.C. 553, and the Attorney General's authority under 5 U.S.C. 552a and related delegations.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal would list JUSTICE/OPA-002 in § 16.79(c), specify nine categories of Privacy Act protections from which qualifying information may be exempt, and state that DOJ may waive an exemption in its sole discretion when compliance would not interfere with the system's purpose or law-enforcement operations.

**Observed Facts**
- The covered system will receive, track, and adjudicate applications and prepare recommendations for the Attorney General under 18 U.S.C. 925(c).
- The proposed exemptions cover disclosure accounting, notice of amendment to recipients, individual access and amendment, maintaining relevant and necessary information, collecting directly from the subject, and maintaining accurate, relevant, timely, and complete records.
- The notice gives subsection-specific rationales involving confidential sources, ongoing investigations, third-party privacy, the official adjudicatory record, and multi-source data.

**Inference:** The strongest comment leverage concerns tailoring, accuracy safeguards, and accountable waiver administration.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is coherent at a high level but leaves the boundaries, accuracy safeguards, and oversight of broad record exemptions materially under-specified.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Id:** a1
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Issue:** Limit the exemption to defined record categories and genuinely qualifying information.
  
  **Why It Matters:** The public can evaluate a tailored exemption more reliably than a system-level restriction justified by generalized sensitivity.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** Add a record-category matrix, mixed-record segregation rules, and examples of accessible or correctable information.
- **Id:** a2
  
  **Priority:** high
  
  **Issue:** Preserve a controlled method for applicants to identify and challenge inaccurate information.
  
  **Why It Matters:** The system will support a consequential rights-restoration decision and may rely on non-federal source data.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** Create dispute flags, source verification, correction review, and notice before material adverse information is used.
- **Id:** a3
  
  **Priority:** moderate
  
  **Issue:** Make the waiver a reviewable safeguard rather than an opaque internal option.
  
  **Why It Matters:** Sole-discretion language can undercut the limiting clause unless decisions are governed and recorded.
  
  **Ask Agency To:** Specify standards, approval levels, written reasons, supervisory review, and aggregate reporting.
