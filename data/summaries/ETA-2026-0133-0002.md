# Public Summary: ETA-2026-0133-0002

## What This Document Does

**Observed facts:** DOL proposes to end Schedule A and special sheepherder procedures, replace newspaper ads with modern recruitment, generally require at least 90 days of advertising, bar filings after relevant layoffs during the previous two years, and expand notices, documentation and oversight. Electronic filing is largely existing practice; the proposal codifies it with mail and disability accommodations. These changes are proposed, not current requirements.

**Authority:** INA § 212(a)(5)(A), 8 U.S.C. 1182(a)(5)(A), governs worker availability and adverse wage/working-condition effects. Teacher and science/arts exceptional-ability cases retain the special equally-qualified comparison.

**Affected entities:** Sponsoring employers, U.S. applicants, laid-off and sponsored workers, staffing clients, universities, attorneys and agencies; nurses and physical therapists would lose the Schedule A route.

**Inference:** Broader visibility plausibly helps workers, but the cutoffs and filing mechanics need stronger support or correction.

**Evidence:** Proposed § 656.17(c) requires advertisements to be active “for a period of no less than 90 calendar days”; § 656.13(e)(1) prohibits relevant layoffs “within 2 years prior to the filing of the application.”

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The worker-protection purpose fits the general recruitment reforms. Material concerns are incompatible filing clocks, a broadly restrictive layoff screen with limited comparative evidence, and an unreliable low-cost recruitment baseline. These are specific, potentially repairable defects; the record does not establish bad faith or a wholesale failure of the program.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Make the filing calendar workable.** A 30-day filing deadline conflicts with a 30–60-day advertising quiet period and a 1–29-day preamble example. Fixed transition dates also need correction. **Question:** Which filing dates are valid? Align the provisions, define recruitment completion, and use publication-relative transition dates.
- **Reconcile recruitment costs and savings.** 108,786 × $1,362 equals $148.17 million, not $174.47 million. Free websites do not establish zero costs for customary paid channels or recruitment staff time. **Question:** Will DOL publish corrected formulas and realistic cost ranges, including small employers and former Schedule A cases?
- **Test the two-year layoff bar against worker availability.** A past layoff can block filing even when former employees are no longer available. Post-filing rules already use documented offers and lawful rejection reasons. **Question:** Would shorter screens plus documented recall protect workers as effectively, including at staffing-client sites?
- **Explain Schedule A repeal and manage the transfer.** The proposal adds individualized testing for nurses, physical therapists and exceptional-ability cases. Uncertain national methods do not establish that local shortages disappeared. **Question:** How do categorical certification and exemption differ legally, and what staffing, capacity and reliance scenarios support the transition?
- **Measure recruitment effectiveness across occupations.** DOL provides method and duration data, but the marginal benefit of 90 days and mandatory fair/campus/placement recruitment is less clear. It considered shorter small-employer periods. **Question:** What qualified-applicant and hiring results favor this design over 30/60-day or equivalent-channel alternatives?
