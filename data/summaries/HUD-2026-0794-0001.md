# Public Summary: HUD-2026-0794-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** Amend the definitions and construction, installation, and transportation provisions in 24 CFR parts 201, 3280, 3285, and 3286 so upper-floor sections of multistory manufactured homes need not be built or transported on permanent chassis, while the lowest floor remains subject to the chassis requirement.

**Affected Entities**
- manufactured-home manufacturers, including small manufacturers
- design approval and inspection agencies
- transporters and installers
- lenders, insurers, and purchasers of multistory manufactured homes
- state and local officials and communities
- HUD program administrators

**Statutory Authority:** National Manufactured Housing Construction and Safety Standards Act, including 42 U.S.C. 5401 et seq., 5403, 5404, 5409, 5415, and 5424; related authority for Title I manufactured-home loans.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The definition would expressly state that only transportable sections designed for the lowest floor must be built on a permanent chassis; related provisions would address serial numbers, structural fastening, transportation scope, and braking axles.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice proposes chassis-free upper floors and retains a lowest-floor chassis.
- The notice cites $4,800-$6,700 per-unit consumer cost effects and $3,300-$4,600 production estimates.
- MHCC recommendations were unanimously approved and incorporated according to the notice.

**Inference:** The proposal is intended to reduce cost and expand design flexibility while preserving safety through integrated-structure, fastening, approval, and transport requirements.

**Evidence Quotes**
- The permanent chassis requirement on the upper floors of a multistory manufactured home provides no practical benefit to homeowners or manufacturers.
- The statute does not specify whether each individual transportable section must independently be constructed on a permanent chassis.
- Each story must be securely fastened to the story above and/or below to provide continuity and resist design loads in this part, except that upper floors do not need to be fastened to a chassis when a chassis is not present.

Core structural concern: The proposal has a coherent affordability and innovation objective, with a targeted material concern about whether engineering and implementation evidence fully supports removing upper-floor chassis requirements.
