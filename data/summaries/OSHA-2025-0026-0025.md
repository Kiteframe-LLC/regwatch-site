# Public Summary: OSHA-2025-0026-0025

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The current notice reopens the comment period for OSHA's Formaldehyde rulemaking while the agency considers comments on the underlying proposal to revise the standard.

**Observed Facts**
- The reopening notice gives the public an additional 30 days to comment.
- The notice references ACCSH consultation on April 1 and May 19, 2026.
- The underlying NPRM seeks to revise the Formaldehyde standard and, in the substantive proposal, would remove initial medical-evaluation requirements for workers using filtering facepiece respirators and loose-fitting powered air-purifying respirators.

**Inference**
- The reopened record does not materially change the underlying policy theory; it extends the same proposal's comment window and keeps the substantive question open.
- The core dispute remains whether OSHA has enough evidence to convert uncertainty into a categorical exemption for two lower-burden respirator types.

**Affected Entities**
- workers required to use filtering facepiece respirators
- workers required to use loose-fitting powered air-purifying respirators
- employers that administer respirator programs and medical-evaluation paperwork
- occupational-health providers that review questionnaires or perform follow-up evaluations
- industries covered by the Formaldehyde standard and related respirator requirements

**Statutory Authority**
- Occupational Safety and Health Act sections 4, 6, and 8 (29 U.S.C. 653, 655, and 657)
- 5 U.S.C. 553
- 29 CFR part 1911
- Secretary of Labor's Order No. 07-2025

**Key Regulatory Changes**
- The substantive proposal would remove the initial medical-evaluation requirement for FFR and loose-fitting PAPR users.
- The proposal would keep the rest of the respiratory-protection program in place, including hazard assessment, respirator selection, fit testing, training, and maintenance requirements.
- The proposal is framed as a simplification and alignment with OSHA's Respiratory Protection Standard rather than a full repeal of respirator-program obligations.

**Evidence Quotes**
- OSHA is re-opening the record for this rulemaking to provide an additional 30 days for public comment.
- OSHA consulted with ACCSH on April 1 and May 19, 2026, and is now reopening the rulemaking record to allow for public comment for an additional 30 days.
- OSHA is proposing an update to the Respiratory Protection Standard to amend the medical evaluation requirements specified in paragraph (e) where an employee is required to wear either a filtering facepiece respirator (FFR) or loose-fitting powered air-purifying respirator (PAPR).
- All other required provisions under the Respiratory Protection Standard ... continue to apply under this proposal.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The proposal is structurally coherent, but it relies on uncertainty, low average burden, and a broad respirator-type line rather than a more targeted safeguard for higher-risk workers.
