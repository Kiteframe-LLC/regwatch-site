# Public Summary: CMS-2026-3037-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** This RFI asks interested parties to provide information that CMS may use to establish future standards for reasonable and relevant Part D pharmacy contract terms and conditions. It does not itself impose standards or change contracts.

**Core Questions**
- What reimbursement, dispensing-fee, pricing, reconciliation, and disclosure practices affect pharmacy economics and predictability?
- How should CMS evaluate similarly situated pharmacies, specialty networks, preferred and non-preferred networks, and vertical integration?
- What contract-change, dispute, audit, quality-measure, and payment-suspension safeguards are used in practice?
- What standards would be transparent, objective, enforceable, and operationally feasible?

**Affected Entities:** Part D plan sponsors, Medicare Advantage prescription drug plans, stand-alone prescription drug plans, PBMs, pharmacies, PSAOs, specialty and mail-order pharmacies, long-term-care and home-infusion providers, manufacturers, Medicare Part D enrollees, and CMS.

**Stage Limit:** The notice is an information and planning document. It does not announce a proposed formula, contract term, reimbursement rate, or enforcement mechanism.

- **Title:** Record-quality brief
  
  **Worth Commenting:** yes
  
  **Why:** The RFI is unusually broad and gives commenters a meaningful chance to shape the evidence, definitions, and safeguards that will support future Part D contracting standards.
  
  **Minimum Useful Comment Package:** Submit one or two de-identified contract or payment examples, identify the data period and denominator, describe the practical access or burden effect, and answer the relevant RFI sections with concrete definitions and questions.
  
  **Main Caution:** Do not treat the RFI as a proposed rule or assume that a future standard has already been selected.
- **Title:** Best single contribution
  
  **Worth Commenting:** yes
  
  **Why:** A well-documented comparison between claim-level reimbursement and final net reimbursement would help CMS evaluate transparency, predictability, and the effects of aggregate guarantees.
  
  **Minimum Useful Comment Package:** Provide a de-identified timeline from dispensing through reconciliation, explain each adjustment, and state how the example differs by pharmacy type, drug type, or line of business.
- **Title:** Public-interest brief
  
  **Worth Commenting:** yes
  
  **Why:** Part D pharmacy contracting can affect network participation, prescription access, pharmacy viability, enrollee cost exposure, and the balance of bargaining power across a national program.
  
  **Minimum Useful Comment Package:** If time is limited, address access and distributional effects, contract-change and dispute safeguards, and the evidence CMS should require before adopting future standards.
