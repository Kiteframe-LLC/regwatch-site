## What This Document Does
- EPA issued a supplemental proposed rule under the SNAP program to add `HFO-1234yf` as an acceptable refrigerant, subject to use conditions, for retrofit motor vehicle air conditioning systems in heavy-duty pickup trucks and complete heavy-duty vans.
- The notice also corrects the scope of an earlier `2025` proposal by saying the proposed `R-444A` retrofit listing was meant to apply only to complete heavy-duty vans, not incomplete heavy-duty vans.
- EPA says the supplemental proposal responds to comments on the earlier NPRM that pointed out the omission of `HFO-1234yf` from this end-use even though EPA had proposed similar listings for other refrigerants.
- EPA is not reopening the whole 2025 proposal. It is reopening only the new `HFO-1234yf` listing and the specific clarifications discussed in this supplemental notice.

## Why It Matters
- This proposal affects which low-global-warming-potential refrigerants may be used to retrofit older heavy-duty vehicle air-conditioning systems. That matters to vehicle fleets, repair businesses, manufacturers, and communities concerned with both ozone-depleting substances and safety risks from substitute refrigerants.
- The notice is not claiming `HFO-1234yf` is risk-free. EPA's argument is narrower: the refrigerant can be listed for this limited retrofit use if subject-to-use-conditions safeguards apply and if the risk record supports that conclusion.
- The available record here is stronger than a bare notice. The export includes the preamble plus five supporting technical documents, including a `2026` heavy-duty technical support document and older `Gradient` risk assessments that report very low modeled ignition, fire, and hydrogen fluoride exposure probabilities for `R-1234yf`.
- The main weakness is transparency. EPA repeatedly relies on a new docketed `2026b` risk screen for this exact end-use, but that risk screen is only summarized in the preamble rather than clearly unpacked. Readers can see the conclusion, but not enough of the scenario design and assumptions behind it.
- EPA also asks whether more mitigation strategies are needed beyond the existing Appendix D retrofit requirements. That is important because the proposal would allow retrofits from systems originally designed for nonflammable refrigerants to a flammable A2L refrigerant. EPA is treating that as manageable, but not so settled that extra safeguards are off the table.

## Who Is Affected
- Heavy-duty pickup and van owners and fleets considering retrofit options for vehicle air-conditioning systems.
- Repair shops and technicians who would have to follow fittings, labeling, and other retrofit requirements.
- Refrigerant manufacturers and related suppliers that benefit or lose from whether another substitute is listed.
- Commenters focused on public safety and administrative-record quality, because the proposal depends on whether EPA has shown enough heavy-duty-specific retrofit risk analysis.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)
1. EPA should summarize the new heavy-duty risk screen more clearly.
   - Why it matters: the notice cites the `2026b` risk screen as the main support for the new listing, but does not clearly show its assumptions.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Will EPA provide a table or summary describing the leak scenarios, ignition assumptions, and mitigation assumptions used in the `2026b` risk screen?

2. EPA should explain why general Appendix D safeguards are enough for this heavy-duty retrofit use.
   - Why it matters: retrofitting a system designed for a nonflammable refrigerant to a flammable refrigerant raises obvious practical safety questions.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Why did EPA conclude that existing retrofit conditions are sufficient, and what additional mitigation strategies did EPA consider before making that judgment?

3. EPA should explain the omission history more directly.
   - Why it matters: EPA says comments showed the agency should have included all four refrigerants in the earlier proposal, but the notice does not explain why `HFO-1234yf` was omitted in the first place.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: What analytical or procedural reason led to the omission of `HFO-1234yf` from the 2025 NPRM, and what specifically resolved that concern now?

4. EPA should sharpen the complete-versus-incomplete heavy-duty van distinction.
   - Why it matters: the supplemental notice narrows the earlier `R-444A` proposal, but the practical boundary still reads as compressed.
   - Practical question a commenter can ask: Can EPA provide a clearer explanation or examples showing why incomplete heavy-duty vans are treated differently from complete heavy-duty vans in this context?

## Evidence Quotes
- "The EPA reviewed these comments and agrees that the Agency should have included all four refrigerants for this end-use consistent with other MVAC listings in the 2025 NPRM and is issuing this supplemental proposal to add this listing."
- "The risk screen also found that HFO-1234yf in HD pickup trucks and complete HD vans does not pose a significant risk of end-use exposure, provided systems are installed in appropriate spaces with proper engineering controls, emergency response plans, and according to guidelines from the manufacturer, standards, and the safety data sheet."
- "The EPA seeks comment on whether additional strategies to mitigate the flammability risk of A2L refrigerants beyond those required by appendix D to part 82, subpart G are necessary and suggestions of what those strategies may be."
- "The EPA is not reopening the comment period for any portions of the November 10, 2025, proposal which are not explicitly addressed in this supplemental proposal."
