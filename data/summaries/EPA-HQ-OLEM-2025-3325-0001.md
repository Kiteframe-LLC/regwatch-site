## What This Document Does

- EPA proposes to approve Virginia's **partial** coal combustion residuals (CCR, or coal ash) permit program under RCRA.
- If finalized, Virginia would administer the approved portion of the CCR program through its own permitting system instead of the federal self-implementing program.
- The approval is only partial because Virginia did not adopt every later federal CCR amendment. EPA says some federal requirements would still apply directly in Virginia even after approval.
- The record includes a real technical support document, state regulations, EPA's 2017 state-program guidance, and an appendix showing which federal provisions Virginia would replace and which would remain federal.

## Why It Matters

- This is a governance rule with real on-the-ground consequences for coal ash units. It affects who regulates which requirements, when permit shield protections apply, and how communities can track compliance.
- Virginia's program is not being approved as a clean substitute for the whole federal CCR rule. EPA is proposing a split system:
  - Virginia permits and state regulations would govern the approved portion.
  - Federal rules would still govern omitted areas, including legacy CCR surface impoundments, CCR management units, and several post-2020 federal amendments.
- EPA and Virginia both say existing Virginia permits would later need major modification so they become permits issued under an approved state program. That means approval alone does not instantly simplify compliance.
- Virginia also has some stricter state requirements. The biggest example in this record is mandatory closure by removal for certain CCR impoundments in the Chesapeake Bay watershed and in Giles and Russell counties.

## Who Is Affected

- Electric utilities and independent power producers operating CCR landfills or surface impoundments in Virginia.
- Communities near those units, especially where closure, groundwater monitoring, or permit changes are contested.
- Virginia DEQ, which would become the lead permitting authority for the approved portion of the program.
- EPA, which would still enforce the federal provisions that remain directly applicable.
- Tribal governments and other public participants who want to understand which regulator controls which requirements.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- Issue: The split federal/state regime may be legally valid but still hard to follow in practice.
  - Why it matters: EPA says omitted federal provisions will continue to apply directly, while approved Virginia provisions will operate in lieu of federal law. That can create confusion for facilities and the public unless the boundary is explained clearly.
  - Practical question a commenter can ask: Can EPA add a simple facility-facing summary showing which requirements remain federally applicable for active units, legacy impoundments, and CCR management units after approval?

- Issue: Existing Virginia permits still need to be reviewed and modified after approval.
  - Why it matters: The record says Virginia will initiate review of existing permits and issue major modifications after federal approval. Until that happens, the transition to permit-shield protection may be less clear than the proposal suggests.
  - Practical question a commenter can ask: What is the expected timeline and sequencing for reviewing and modifying existing Virginia CCR permits after final approval?

- Issue: Some "at least as protective" conclusions are stronger in outcome than in explanation.
  - Why it matters: The docket has meaningful support, but several conclusions rely on EPA concurrence stated at a high level. That leaves room for comments asking EPA to show more of its reasoning, especially for omitted post-2020 federal amendments.
  - Practical question a commenter can ask: Please explain in more detail why the approved Virginia provisions remain at least as protective where later federal rules changed definitions, closure obligations, or groundwater-related requirements.

- Issue: Public accountability may suffer if overlapping authority is not communicated clearly.
  - Why it matters: Coal ash regulation often matters most when residents are trying to understand closure plans, groundwater obligations, or permit enforcement at a specific facility. A mixed regime can make that harder.
  - Practical question a commenter can ask: How will EPA and Virginia identify, in public documents for each facility, whether a disputed requirement comes from an approved state permit, a still-applicable federal rule, or both?

- Issue: The record says future program review will handle some implementation questions.
  - Why it matters: Future review is normal, but commenters can still ask EPA to explain why the current record is enough now despite acknowledged transition work that still lies ahead.
  - Practical question a commenter can ask: What implementation questions does EPA believe can safely wait for later program reviews, and which should be clarified in the final approval record now?
