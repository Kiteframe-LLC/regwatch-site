## What This Document Does

- The Surface Transportation Board proposes to repeal 49 CFR part 1144, its long-standing regulations on "Intramodal Rail Competition."
- Part 1144 currently narrows how the Board handles requests for reciprocal switching, through routes, and through rates. The Board says those regulations added an anticompetitive-conduct screen that is stricter than the underlying statutes require.
- If the rule is finalized, the Board would stop using that rule-based screen and would instead evaluate future requests case by case under the broader statutory standards in 49 U.S.C. 10705 and 11102(c).
- The Board frames this as a deregulatory move. It argues that the current regulation narrows the agency's discretion too much and may be blocking competitive relief that Congress meant the Board to be able to grant.
- The notice also asks a narrower question: should repeal apply to all of part 1144, or only to the provisions that affect reciprocal switching?

## Why It Matters

- This proposal is about more than cleanup. It would change the practical gateway for rail shippers and carriers seeking Board-ordered competitive access remedies.
- The Board's main argument is that part 1144 has been too restrictive. It says the rule has been rarely invoked in 40 years and has never produced a prescription, which it treats as evidence that the rule operates as a barrier rather than a workable standard.
- The legal basis for reconsidering the rule appears fairly substantial from the text provided. The notice cites court decisions saying the Board had discretion to adopt the current narrowing rule and therefore can also decide to remove it, so long as it gives a reasoned explanation.
- The thinner part of the record is the forward-looking policy case. The NPRM says repeal will remove an unnecessary barrier to competition, will not negatively affect operations or investment decisions, and will be net deregulatory. But the available materials do not show much quantified support for those predictions.
- The notice also acknowledges that repeal could initially increase the number of matters brought before the Board, with added administrative costs for carriers, shippers, and the agency. That makes the missing analysis more important. If repeal increases disputes but does not materially improve access to competitive remedies, the claimed benefits become less certain.
- Another important point is scope. The Board gives some reasons for repealing part 1144 as a whole, but it also specifically asks whether repeal should be limited to reciprocal switching rather than also sweeping in through routes and through rates. That creates a clear opening for comments focused on a narrower alternative.

## Who Is Affected

- Freight rail shippers, especially those seeking more competitive options where one or two large carriers dominate service.
- Rail carriers that may face more petitions for reciprocal switching, through routes, or through rates if the current screening rule is removed.
- Smaller and medium-sized businesses that rely on rail service and may have limited transportation choices.
- The Board itself, because a repeal could increase case-by-case adjudication volume and pressure it to define standards through precedent rather than regulation.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- Issue: The NPRM does not do much structured comparison between full repeal and narrower repeal.
Why it matters: The Board itself identifies limited repeal as a live option, which suggests the scope question is important and unresolved.
One practical question a commenter can ask: What evidence shows that through-route and through-rate provisions should be repealed along with reciprocal-switching provisions, rather than addressed separately?

- Issue: The historical case for repeal is asserted more than demonstrated.
Why it matters: Saying the rule was rarely used and never produced a prescription is important, but commenters need more detail to know whether that means the rule was too strict, seldom needed, or simply operating in a different market setting.
One practical question a commenter can ask: Can the Board provide a complete case history of part 1144 petitions, outcomes, and reasons for denial or non-use so commenters can evaluate whether the rule actually functioned as a barrier?

- Issue: The net-deregulatory claim is not very transparent.
Why it matters: The Board says competition benefits will outweigh increased administrative costs, but the available record does not show a clear method for reaching that conclusion.
One practical question a commenter can ask: What analysis supports the statement that repeal will improve competition enough to outweigh added adjudication and compliance costs?

- Issue: Reliance interests are flagged legally but not discussed much factually.
Why it matters: The notice correctly says an agency changing position should consider serious reliance interests, yet the available text provides little concrete discussion of who relied on the current framework.
One practical question a commenter can ask: What reliance interests, if any, has the Board identified among carriers, shippers, or smaller railroads that organized their expectations around the existing part 1144 rules?

- Issue: The NPRM removes a threshold without explaining what practical guardrails will replace it.
Why it matters: A purely case-by-case regime may broaden access, but it can also create uncertainty about burden of proof, evidence, and operational-feasibility showings.
One practical question a commenter can ask: If part 1144 is repealed, what guidance does the Board expect parties to use on evidence, burdens, and case management in future prescription proceedings?

## Evidence Quotes

- "The approach set out in the regulations, which narrows the Board's statutory discretion, may no longer be appropriate on an industrywide basis..."
- "In the 40 years since its adoption, part 1144 has been rarely invoked, and the agency has never issued a prescription under its framework."
- "The Board is choosing to restore its discretion here to the full extent provided by the statute in order to better effectuate Congressional intent."
- "Accordingly, the Board seeks comment on whether its repeal of part 1144 should be so limited."
- "This will remove an unnecessarily high barrier to competition in freight rail transportation without negatively impacting operations or investment decisions by carriers."
- "The Board anticipates that this rule will be net deregulatory, as the benefits of a more competitive market resulting from removing these regulatory barriers will outweigh any increase in administrative or other costs..."
