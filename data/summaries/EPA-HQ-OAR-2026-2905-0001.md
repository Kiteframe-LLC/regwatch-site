## What This Document Does

- EPA proposes to exempt road and intermodal container transport refrigeration units (TRUs) from the HFC leak repair requirements in `40 CFR 84.106`.
- The agency says this is a narrow clarification of the 2024 HFC management rule, not a broader rewrite of that rule.
- If finalized, all road and intermodal TRUs would be exempt from these leak repair requirements, even if they carry more than `15 pounds` of refrigerant.
- EPA is also asking for comments and data on leak rates, charge sizes, repair patterns, costs, and whether rail TRUs should also be exempted.

## Why It Matters

- This proposal would reduce compliance obligations for refrigerated trucking and intermodal fleets, maintenance providers, lessors, and related businesses.
- EPA says the 2024 analysis assumed road/intermodal TRUs were all at or below the `15-pound` threshold, but newer stakeholder information indicates many units are actually above it.
- That matters because EPA’s original leak-repair program applied to appliances with a full charge of `15 pounds or more`, so a bad assumption about charge size changes who is covered.
- EPA gives a preliminary estimate of roughly `$90 million` per year in cost savings from the exemption, but it also admits it has not completed a broader market-impact analysis for this proposal.
- The environmental side of the explanation is thinner. EPA suggests the exempted TRUs represent a relatively small share of total installed charge compared with the broader regulated universe, but it does not fully quantify the emissions tradeoff in the notice.

## Who Is Affected

- Trucking fleets and refrigerated trailer operators using road TRUs.
- Intermodal container operators using refrigerated containers.
- Equipment owners, lessors, service contractors, and maintenance providers handling these units.
- Food, cold-chain, and pharmaceutical shippers that depend on refrigerated transport.
- Potentially rail TRU operators, because EPA specifically asks whether rail TRUs should also be treated the same way.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

1. EPA’s revised fleet assumptions need clearer support.
Why it matters: EPA’s case for the exemption depends heavily on the idea that about `360,000` road/intermodal TRUs fall in the `15-18 pound` range. If that number is wrong, the cost and scope analysis changes.
Practical question a commenter can ask: What exact datasets, manufacturer submissions, or fleet records support the `360,000`-unit estimate and the `15-18 pound` charge-size range?

2. The proposal explains cost savings better than environmental tradeoffs.
Why it matters: EPA gives a rough burden-reduction estimate but does not provide the same level of clarity on foregone leak-repair benefits or emissions consequences.
Practical question a commenter can ask: Can EPA quantify the expected emissions effect of exempting these units, or explain with supporting data why that effect is small?

3. A categorical exemption is not the only possible fix.
Why it matters: The notice moves straight to exempting the whole road/intermodal TRU class, but the Carrier petition also raised a different option: increasing the applicability threshold.
Practical question a commenter can ask: Why is a full class exemption better than a higher threshold or a tailored compliance rule for transport refrigeration equipment?

4. The road/intermodal versus rail boundary is not fully explained.
Why it matters: EPA is already asking whether rail TRUs should also be exempted, which suggests the scope line may still be unsettled.
Practical question a commenter can ask: What features make road/intermodal TRUs different from rail TRUs for leak-repair purposes, and should the final rule address both categories together?

5. The “clarification of original intent” claim should be pinned to the record.
Why it matters: EPA says it never intended to subject these TRUs to the leak repair rule. That may be true, but commenters can reasonably ask the agency to show exactly where the earlier record demonstrates that point.
Practical question a commenter can ask: Which passages in the 2024 rulemaking record show that EPA intended to exclude road and intermodal TRUs rather than simply overlooking a subset above the threshold?

## Evidence Quotes

- “The EPA did not intend to subject refrigerant-containing road and intermodal container TRUs to the leak repair requirements.”
- “Under this proposal, all road and intermodal container TRUs would be exempt from the leak repair requirements, regardless of their charge size.”
- “The EPA incorrectly assumed that road and intermodal container TRUs have an average charge size of 10 pounds and that all these units have a charge size of 15 pounds or less.”
- “The EPA estimates cost savings of this rule of $90 million per year, reflecting 360,000 appliances with per-unit annual costs of $250.”
- “The EPA has not assessed market impacts of this proposal outside of the preliminary engineering cost estimates described above.”
- “The EPA is requesting comment on whether rail TRUs (e.g., TRUs used exclusively on rail cars) should also be explicitly exempted from the leak repair requirements.”
