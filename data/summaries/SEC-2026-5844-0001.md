# Public Summary: SEC-2026-5844-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** The SEC proposes to rescind Rule 14a-8, which currently requires companies to include qualifying shareholder proposals in proxy materials, and to amend Rule 14a-4(c) so companies may exercise discretionary voting authority over certain timely received proposals submitted outside Rule 14a-8, subject to disclosure and a shareholder check box.

**Affected Entities:** Public companies subject to the federal proxy rules, registered investment companies and business development companies, shareholder proponents, non-proponent shareholders, proxy solicitors, proxy-administration and voting-platform providers, States, and companies that may develop private shareholder-proposal frameworks.

**Statutory Authority:** The notice cites sections 3(b), 14, and 23(a) of the Exchange Act and sections 20(a) and 38 of the Investment Company Act. It also discusses section 14(a), section 2(b) of the Securities Act, and section 3(f) of the Exchange Act for public-interest and economic considerations.

**Key Regulatory Change:** Federal Rule 14a-8 inclusion and exclusion standards would be removed, leaving proposal inclusion primarily to State law and permitted company governing documents. For timely proposals submitted outside Rule 14a-8, a company could use discretionary voting authority if it provides a brief description and voting position, a proxy-card cross-reference, and a check box allowing each shareholder to withhold that authority for the returned card.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice estimates 5,357 potentially affected section 12 companies, 184 identified lead proponents in 2025, and about 502 unique lead proponents from 2022 through 2025.
- The economic analysis estimates 107 hours per Rule 14a-8 proposal and approximately $49,000 in cost savings per proposal, while identifying approximately $39.6 million in aggregate annual monetized savings from rescission.
- The notice recognizes that the direction and magnitude of proposal-composition, negotiation, and governance effects are uncertain.
- The notice considers full rescission, a State-law-referential alternative, a reversed check-box default, and separate check boxes for each proposal.

**Inference:** This is a structurally consequential but analytically developed NPRM. Its strongest comment leverage is not that it lacks any analysis; it is that the legal and cost-savings case may not fully establish why full rescission plus expanded management discretion is preferable to a narrower process-preserving alternative, particularly for less-resourced proponents and shareholders who rely on proxy voting.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Narrower baseline:** Compare a sunset-and-review federal process rule with full rescission, using the same access, cost, and governance measures.
- **Check-box design:** Publish usability testing and consider affirmative consent or separate controls for materially distinct omitted proposals.
- **Distribution:** Disaggregate issuer savings and access costs by company size, proponent resources, and proposal type, with a transition safeguard.
- **Recordkeeping:** Specify minimum notices, records, and reconciliation logs for omitted proposals and multiple proxy cards.
