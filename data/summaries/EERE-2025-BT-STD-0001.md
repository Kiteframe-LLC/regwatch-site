# Public Summary: EERE-2025-BT-STD-0001-0044

## What This Document Does

**Observed Facts**
- DOE proposes revisions to the Process Rule in 10 CFR part 430, subpart C, appendix A for consumer products and certain commercial/industrial equipment.
- The proposal is framed as both a procedural update and a response to EPCA, Loper Bright, Michigan v. EPA, and E.O. 14154.
- The notice says DOE wants the changes to provide certainty and better inform the Secretary when deciding whether to regulate and what standard level to select.

**Inference**
- This is not mainly a product standard; it is a rules-of-the-road proposal for how DOE will develop future appliance and equipment standards.
- The main practical effect is to change the decision architecture that later commentors and regulated entities must work through.

**Evidence Quotes**
- “make Appendix A binding on DOE for certain actions”
- “add a definition of ‘significant energy savings’”
- “re-instate the comparative analysis requirement”

## Why It Matters

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Issue:** The notice does not yet justify the threshold architecture with enough specificity.
  **Why it matters:** The threshold can decide whether DOE reaches the substantive standards stage at all.
  **Ask:** Show why the proposed threshold design is preferable to a narrower, phased, or class-specific option.
- **Issue:** Binding Appendix A needs a clearer explanation of scope and exceptions.
  **Why it matters:** A binding process rule can improve discipline, but only if DOE explains when it can step outside the rule and why.
  **Ask:** Identify the actions covered by the binding language and the conditions for departure.
- **Issue:** The restored comparative-analysis step should be operationalized, not just named.
  **Why it matters:** Without a usable template, the “walk up” requirement may be hard to audit or challenge in later rulemakings.
  **Ask:** Provide a concrete model of the comparative analysis and explain how it will be used in the record.
- **Issue:** Thresholds may need class-specific treatment.
  **Why it matters:** Consumer products, commercial equipment, and ASHRAE-covered equipment may not fit one threshold equally well.
  **Ask:** Explain whether one threshold should govern all categories or whether DOE should use tailored lines.
