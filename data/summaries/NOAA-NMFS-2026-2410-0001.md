# Public Summary: NOAA-NMFS-2026-2410-0001

## What This Document Does

NMFS proposes to reopen commercial fishing in the roughly 50–200 nautical mile expansion waters around Wake and Jarvis Islands and Johnston Atoll. Existing fishery permits, catch limits, gear rules and protected-species safeguards would apply. The inner monument closures would remain. **Observed Fact:** the proposal changes the prohibition, definitions and permit provisions. **Inference:** most activity would be longlining near Johnston and purse seining near Jarvis. The agency expressly requests comment on whether existing rules provide proper care for monument resources.

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The rule has a coherent mechanism and substantial supporting analysis. Its main structural weakness is treating a planned 2028 monitoring transition as sufficient reason to reject enhanced coverage now, without a clear interim comparison.

## Who Is Affected

U.S. longline and purse-seine operators, crews and fishing communities in Hawaiʻi and American Samoa; cultural practitioners, researchers and people concerned with monument wildlife. NMFS expects little small-boat activity because of distance and operating barriers.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Explain monitoring before 2028.** The EA rejects enhanced coverage partly because fleetwide electronic monitoring is planned. Existing observers remain, but equipment installation and video review are different safeguards. **Question:** What interim coverage, review rate and response triggers will apply if the rollout is delayed? (EA §2.4.)
- **Test effort relocation and local exposure.** The EA supplies historical activity and stock analysis, but unchanged vessel caps do not alone fix hooks, days or location. Concentrated effort could change encounter rates. **Question:** Can NMFS publish a higher-effort and seasonal-concentration sensitivity case tied to monument resources? (EA §§4.3–4.5.)
- **Make fee savings reproducible.** The $1.37 million fleetwide estimate is an upper bound. The analysis moves from sets to daily fees, while transit and location choices also affect net benefits. **Question:** What sets-to-days conversion and travel-cost assumptions produce the upper bound and a likely case? (RIR §5.)
- **Improve the cultural-use baseline.** The EA recognizes offshore voyages that need no special-use permit and may be unknown to NMFS. Few permit requests cannot fully measure use or experiential effects. **Question:** How will practitioner evidence on routes, timing and cultural values inform the final assessment? (EA §4.6.2.)
- **Clarify what comments can change.** The notice describes repeal as mandatory while seeking comment on proper care. A clear account of retained management authority would help focus useful submissions. **Question:** Which monitoring, gear or other safeguards remain open to change, and can NMFS verify the retained-closure cross-references? (NPRM p. 70829 and proposed §665.933.)
