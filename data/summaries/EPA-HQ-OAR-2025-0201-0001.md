## What This Document Does

EPA proposes to change how ozone nonattainment-area State Implementation Plan (SIP) requirements apply after an area is reclassified to a higher classification. The proposal would revise EPA's January 2025 rule and replace the "leftover SIP elements policy" with a "reclassified area SIPs policy."

In plain terms, EPA would treat the area's current ozone classification as controlling. If an area moves from Moderate to Serious, EPA would require the State to meet the Serious-area SIP requirements, including the lower-classification elements incorporated into that Serious-area plan, on the schedule for the current classification. EPA says a State would not need to submit separate prior-classification SIP elements as if the area were still in the old classification.

EPA says the proposal would apply nationwide to past and future reclassifications under the 2008, 2015, and future ozone NAAQS. EPA is not reopening the rest of the January 2025 rule, including the default reclassified-area SIP deadlines in 40 CFR 51.1402.

## Why It Matters

The proposal is legally important because EPA is reversing a recent interpretation from January 2025. The earlier policy preserved some prior-classification SIP obligations even after an area moved to a higher ozone classification. EPA now says that reading is not the best reading of Clean Air Act section 182.

The practical importance is timing and enforcement. EPA explains that some prior failure-to-submit findings, including the October 2023 findings for Moderate-area SIP elements, could become moot for areas later reclassified to Serious. That could affect sanctions clocks, Federal Implementation Plan obligations, and the schedule for State planning submissions.

EPA frames the action as deregulatory and says it would reduce duplicative planning requirements. The key question for commenters is whether the proposal avoids duplicative paperwork while still preserving timely emissions-control planning and enforceable progress toward ozone attainment.

## Who Is Affected

- States with ozone nonattainment areas that have been or will be reclassified.
- Areas reclassified under the 2008 and 2015 ozone standards, especially Moderate areas recently reclassified to Serious.
- Local governments, air agencies, and planning organizations involved in SIP development.
- Regulated sources affected by ozone SIP control strategies, including RACT, RFP, inspection and maintenance, contingency measures, and related planning elements.
- Communities in ozone nonattainment areas that may be affected by changes in SIP timing and enforcement leverage.

## Issues Worth Commenting On (0-6)

- **Area-by-area impacts are not fully shown.** EPA says 18 Moderate areas in 15 States were reclassified to Serious in 2024 and 2025, but the notice does not provide a detailed transition table. This matters because commenters need to know which findings, sanctions clocks, FIP deadlines, and SIP elements would change. A practical question: Will EPA provide an area-by-area table identifying every affected SIP obligation and its treatment under the final rule?

- **Mootness of prior findings needs clearer procedure.** EPA says the October 2023 failure-to-submit findings would be moot if the rule is finalized as proposed. This matters because automatic mootness could affect enforcement timing and public expectations. A practical question: Would prior findings become moot automatically on the effective date, or would EPA issue area-specific determinations?

- **Reliance interests may be broader than EPA assumes.** EPA says reliance interests are minimal because the proposal relieves State obligations and does not directly affect the public. This matters because communities, regulated sources, and agencies may have relied on existing SIP deadlines or sanctions clocks. A practical question: How will EPA evaluate reliance evidence from affected communities, States, local agencies, and regulated entities?

- **Substance and timing should be separated.** EPA says higher classifications still include lower-classification substantive elements. This matters because preserving the substance of a SIP element is different from preserving its original due date or implementation timing. A practical question: Will EPA show whether the proposal delays submission, review, or implementation of any control measure in affected areas?

- **Alternatives are thinly analyzed.** EPA compares the January 2025 policy with the new current-classification approach, but does not deeply analyze intermediate options. This matters because a narrower transition rule might avoid duplicative submissions without weakening near-term accountability. A practical question: Did EPA consider preserving prior enforcement consequences until a complete current-classification SIP is submitted?
