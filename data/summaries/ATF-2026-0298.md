# Public Summary: ATF-2026-0298-0001

## What This Document Does

**Regulatory Action:** ATF proposes to revise 27 CFR part 479 so that manufacturers who produce NFA firearms for the USG can rely on a codified relief provision, including a late-registration path if those firearms later fall out of contract.

**Affected Entities**
- NFA firearms manufacturers and importers
- Type 10 SOT licensees and other qualified manufacturers
- government-contract firearm producers
- ATF registration and inspection staff
- law-enforcement users of the NFRTR
- small businesses that perform marking or related services

**Statutory Authority:** The preamble relies on 26 U.S.C. 5841, 5851(a), and related NFA implementing authority in 27 CFR part 479.

**Key Regulatory Change:** The proposal would separate the special occupational tax exemption from the Director's relief authority, add a formal letter request process for relief, and create a 15-day late-registration path for rejected, residual, or repurposed USG firearms.

**Observed Facts**
- The notice says the Director has long applied the relief authority to firearms manufactured for or pursuant to a USG contract.
- The notice says these firearms currently may have to be destroyed or exported if they fall out of contract before registration.
- The proposal would keep other marking, recordkeeping, and explosives requirements in force.

**Inference:** The practical effect is likely to be reduced compliance friction for government-contract manufacturers, while keeping a paper trail through other records and late registration.

**Evidence Quotes**

## Why It Matters

Core structural concern: The rule is coherent and legally grounded, but it still leans on a generalized batch-processing rationale and a procedurally gated late-registration path that is not calibrated with detailed operational data.
